- **[ResWAM: Learning What Changes, Not What Stays, for World-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19559)**  `arXiv:2611.19559`  `cs.RO`  
  _Chongyu Fan, Gaowen Liu, Ramana Rao Kompella, Jason Corso, Sijia Liu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  World-action models (WAMs) augment vision-language-action policies (VLAs) with future prediction so that the policy can anticipate how its actions will affect the physical world. Existing WAMs supervise complete future observations. However, observations typically change little over an action chunk, so the future loss is dominated by copying static background content rather than learning task-relevant robot and object motion, limiting policy learning. Moreover, existing WAMs rely on large video generation models or separate world models, incurring heavy training and inference compute that hinders closed-loop deployment. To improve policy performance, we propose visual residual learning, supervising only the feature change from the current frame to the future frame in a frozen DINOv3 space. Compared with complete future features, this residual learning target yields a higher feature rank with richer and more diverse representations of scene changes, and spatially concentrates on the robot arm and manipulated objects rather than the background. To further improve computational efficiency, we present ResWAM, a residual world-action model with 2.3B parameters in total, including a lightweight 0.5B dual-branch DiT that jointly trains an action branch and a residual branch via conditional flow matching. Without robot pretraining (i.e., pretraining on external robot action trajectories beyond the benchmark demonstrations), ResWAM achieves top average success rates on LIBERO-Plus (81.1%), RoboTwin 2.0 (93.8%), and RoboDojo (9.40% success, 13.41 score) at the lowest training (2.7-4.0 TFLOPs) and inference compute (1.0-1.4 TFLOPs), and leads real-robot Open, Put, and Stack (80.0% average, +10.0% over $\pi_{0.5}$) at 92.7 ms latency.

- **[ODEWorld: A Continuous Predictive Architecture via Physical-Time Flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27924)**  `arXiv:2607.27924`  `cs.LG` `cs.CV` `cs.RO`  
  _Dongxiu Liu, Haoyi Niu, Peng Cheng, Yuan Gao, Xirui Kang, Sangli Teng, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  In the physical world we inhabit, space and time are fundamentally continuous. However, existing machine learning paradigms for world modeling are largely confined to discrete-time prediction, thereby exhibiting significant inefficiency in capturing the dynamics of physical world. We introduce Physical-Time Flow (PT-Flow), a novel approach that learns a continuous latent velocity field operating in physical time. Crucially, the underlying dynamics of sequential data are parameterized by an ordinary differential equation (ODE) embedded in a well-structured representation space. Under this paradigm, the prediction of future can be recast as temporal integration via an ODE solver in the compressed latent space. Building upon PT-Flow, we construct ODEWorld, a continuous-time latent world model that is both efficient and versatile. By extracting time-variant features and enforcing ODE properties on both the dynamical representation space and the latent velocity field, ODEWorld effectively addresses the long-standing representation collapse issue in latent world model literature. This also enables high-quality image reconstruction even after long-horizon prediction. Moreover, its continuous nature allows for arbitrary temporal resolution and even backward prediction, which is impossible for most discrete-time models. Lastly, ODEWorld can provide rich planning-oriented information to facilitate downstream policy learning. Comprehensive experiments demonstrate that ODEWorld successfully reconciles planning-conducive dynamics abstraction with visual realism, excelling in both video generation and robotic control. - **[PoCoAttn: Position-Content Collaborative Sparse Attention for Fast and High-Fidelity Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20307)**  `arXiv:2611.20307`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Jianwen Wu, Meiling Wang, Junkang Zhou, Yipeng Shen, Haoran You_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video diffusion transformers rely on 3D full attention over tens of thousands of spatiotemporal tokens, and its quadratic cost dominates inference latency. Sparse attention reduces this cost, but existing methods face a dilemma: static methods apply predefined masks and cannot capture input-dependent attention, whereas dynamic methods reconstruct the sparse mask repeatedly, paying online to rediscover positional structure that is largely stable across inputs. To this end, we propose PoCoAttn, a training-free sparse attention framework that separates input-stable from input-dependent attention and resolves the former offline and the latter online. PoCoAttn features: (1) A relative-offset analysis of 3D query-key pairs, which shows that video attention decomposes into an input-stable positional prior that carries most of the attention mass and a small content-dependent residual. (2) A position-content collaborative sparse attention mechanism, which retrieves positional keys from offline-calibrated tables, selects content keys online with a head-adaptive ratio between the two sources, and compensates for the discarded keys to approximate full attention. (3) A step-adaptive budget allocation algorithm, which models how early-step sparse attention errors are amplified along the denoising trajectory and derives the optimal sparsity budget per step. Extensive experiments across four video diffusion models and three tasks show that PoCoAttn consistently achieves a better speed-fidelity Pareto frontier than state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods, improving PSNR by 1.9 to 5.0 dB over baselines and delivering up to 4.1$\times$ attention and 2.0$\times$ denoising speedups over dense FlashAttention-3 attention.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Long video generation should keep the subject and background consistent while supporting rich, natural motion. However, autoregressive models are often distilled with distribution matching distillation (DMD) over short windows, which does not train them to keep this consistency over long ranges. As a result, the subject and background can drift over time. Methods that manage the key-value (KV) cache retain or retrieve history to reduce drift. Yet this history may itself have drifted. We propose Reference Forcing, which uses the initial frames as an appearance reference. Building on this formulation, we introduce Mem2Fair, a plug-and-play framework that maintains concept-conditioned Fair Memory. Using generated videos at inference time as feedback, Mem2Fair selects prompt-level attribute interventions based on the deviation from each concept's prescribed fairness target, without training or separate pre-deployment sampling. Experiments demonstrate that Mem2Fair consistently improves fairness over the unmodified generator and existing baselines across multiple demographic attributes, prompt settings, and target distributions, while maintaining comparable prompt-video alignment and video quality. We further demonstrate its effectiveness on commercial black-box T2V models.

  This study reports findings from a cross-sectional survey (n = 72) of higher education practitioners examining beliefs, behaviors, and institutional conditions related to artificial intelligence (AI) integration in teaching and learning.
