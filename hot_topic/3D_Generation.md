- **[Self-Distilling Future Video Representations for World Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30320)**  `arXiv:2610.30320`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Huiwon Jang, Suhyeok Jang, Hyeonmo Kang, Seungku Kim, Jinwoo Shin, Sihyun Yu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  World Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as a promising approach to learning generalist robot policies using rich spatiotemporal priors from pre-trained video diffusion models. A recent line of WAMs improves inference efficiency by conditioning a separate action decoder on intermediate future video representations extracted from a single denoising step, avoiding costly iterative denoising required for video generation. This design makes action prediction depend directly on the quality of these future representations. In this paper, we introduce Future REpresentation learning via self-Distillation with temporal context (FRED), a framework that trains WAMs to improve the quality of future video representations from noise in a single denoising step. Specifically, FRED uses self-distillation to predict future representations from noise, using representations extracted from ground-truth future observations as targets. We further condition the video model on historical observations, providing temporal context for predicting future representations better. Across diverse real-world manipulation scenarios, FRED consistently outperforms competing WAMs. It achieves particularly large gains on long-horizon and out-of-domain tasks under zero-shot environment generalization, and substantially improves human-to-robot skill transfer, reaching 50.0% task success on tasks absent from robot training data and demonstrated only in human videos, compared with 19.4% for the best competing WAM.

  _Chaoyang Wang, Sicheng Gao, Junan Hu, Kunyang Li, Dongyang Chen, Yiyang Zhou, et al._

  Recent years have witnessed remarkable progress in video generation in terms of visual quality and temporal consistency. However, generating videos with precise spatial structure, coherent motion dynamics, and controllable scene composition remains challenging. In this paper, we introduce Video-Coder, the first attempt to train a code-driven video generation agent, which autonomously writes and executes code to construct a dynamic 3D scene (Draft Video) that provides structure and motion signals to downstream video generators. To support this paradigm, we design a dedicated data pipeline and curate two high-quality datasets, Video-Coder-SFT-6k and Video-Coder-RL-2k, which contain diverse prompts and ground-truth reference videos. We then train Video-Coder with SFT fol- lowed by harness-native agentic reinforcement learning with dual reward feedback, which combines execution-based and video-based rewards. Additionally, we design an annealing training strategy that gradually reduces the execution reward over time, allowing the model to rely more on the video reward in the later training stages. Experiments show that Video-Coder achieves significant gains, improving Minimax-H3 by approximately 7 points on PhyGenBench and 24 points on VBench-2.0. We hope this work can serve as an open foundation for coding agents in video generation, and we fully open-source our data, models, and code

- **[Trajectory Forcing: Exploiting Diffusion Trajectories for Autoregressive Long Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09096)**  `arXiv:2611.09096`  `cs.CV`  
  _Bohan Wang, Shuo Chen, Yunzhi Li, Zhaozheng Chen, Junzhe Zhang, Hanwang Zhang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Autoregressive (AR) modeling is arguably the only way to extend a fixed-length video diffusion model to longer or even streaming videos. However, existing AR diffusion methods use only the previously predicted chunk as context for the next, discarding the intermediate predictions that constitute the chunk's generation history. In contrast, conventional AR models such as LLMs condition on the entire history of preceding tokens. To this end, we propose Trajectory Forcing (TraF), an AR diffusion method that exploits the denoising trajectories of previous video chunks as context. TraF operates under two training regimes: (1) with ground-truth videos, where pseudo-trajectories are constructed from Gaussian interpolations and re-predicted by the model, and (2) with a teacher model, where real trajectories are obtained from the student's own denoising rollout. The first regime naturally provides a strong initialization for the second. On the 5-second VBench benchmark, TraF achieves a Total score of 85.15, outperforming Self Forcing (+0.84) and Causal Forcing (+1.11), with Quality and Semantic improving simultaneously. On 30-second generation---6$\times$ the training horizon---TraF achieves the highest overall quality among all compared methods.

- **[Emo-LiveSync: Real-Time Universal Lip Synchronization with Explicit Emotion Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09074)**  `arXiv:2611.09074`  `cs.CV`  
  _Sangjoon Yu, Seungeun Lee, JoonHeyok Shin, Ji-Su Kang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Lip synchronization, the task of generating realistic lip movements synchronized with audio signals, is fundamental to applications such as film dubbing, virtual avatars, and digital content creation. Recent GAN-based methods offer computational efficiency but often lack visual fidelity, while diffusion-based approaches achieve high-quality results at the cost of substantial computational overhead. Moreover, most existing methods are limited to photo-realistic human faces, restricting their applicability to stylized characters and non-human entities. In this paper, we propose an efficient universal lip-sync video generation framework that achieves high-quality results without compromising computational efficiency. We build on LivePortrait, a warp-based portrait animation model, and introduce a training strategy that aligns audio signals to an implicit motion space by distilling knowledge from a pre-trained video-to-motion model. This enables real-time inference while maintaining high visual fidelity, and naturally generalizes to diverse stylistic characters beyond photo-realistic humans. Furthermore, we introduce an expressive control gate that enables explicit manipulation of emotional expressions for content enrichment. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance in both visual quality and lip-sync accuracy while ensuring real-time inference.

- **[MotionCore: A Latent Neural Simulator for Video Generation with Rigid-Body Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08904)**  `arXiv:2611.08904`  `cs.CV`  
  _Zheng Wang, Zhengwei Gong, Yuanzhi Liang, Chi Zhang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video generation models can produce highly realistic videos while generating physically inconsistent object motion. This limitation suggests that visual modeling alone may not provide a transferable representation of physical dynamics. We introduce MotionCore, a framework for learning physical dynamics via a learnable partial differential equation solver and focuses on rigid-body motion. We introduce a latent neural simulator learning to predict the evolution of object-level physical states from observed motion. Its predictions are then converted into spatially aligned latent controls for a controllable video diffusion model. The generator serves as a visual interface: it provides appearance and scene detail, while the learned dynamics model specifies the physical trajectory. To preserve this trajectory through the interface, MotionCore combines a diffusion-matching objective with a low-capacity decodability constraint on object identity and position. We evaluate MotionCore on public physical-consistency benchmarks and on paired data with known trajectories. Results indicate that MotionCore improves physical consistency and trajectory obedience in the evaluated rigid-body settings, while maintaining comparable visual quality. A controlled ablation protocol compares learned dynamics with an analytic prior and studies the choice of latent evolution processor.

  _Chenliang Zhou, Yifan Wang, Andrzej Banburski-Fahey_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We present Align3D, an image-conditioned diffusion model that generates 3D indoor layouts that are both faithful to a single input photograph and physically plausible beyond the observed view. From the photograph we infer a confidence-calibrated scene graph and train a set-diffusion layout denoiser with image-structure co-alignment where confidence-weighted modality fusion balances image and scene-graph alignment using predicted confidences, while observability-aware fidelity-preserving guidance projects conflicting scene-quality gradients and reweighs them with an object-level confidence mask so under-observed degrees of freedom absorb physical priors without undoing the image-graph match. On 3D-FRONT, Align3D improves image-alignment and scene-quality metrics over recent baselines; once trained, it maps previously unseen photographs to layouts in a single feed-forward pass.

- **[InstaGen: Group-Shared Importance Sampling for Fast Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08610)**  `arXiv:2611.08610`  `cs.CV`  
  _Kislay Aditya Oj, Kumar Krishna Agrawal, Sri Surya Varma Datla, Aditya Desai_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) are the dominant architecture for video generation, but their quadratic attention complexity results in substantial computational cost and latency. Permuted block-sparse attention (PBSA) offers a promising approach to reducing this cost by selectively computing blocks of a semantically permuted attention matrix. However, existing approaches typically select only the top few key blocks for each query block, following a top-$p$ paradigm. We find that top-$p$ selection can be highly sample inefficient in high-entropy attention regimes, which are common in video generation. In these regimes, we show that sampling can provide substantially better approximations under the same computational budget. More generally, we cast block selection as importance sampling, where the top-scoring keys form an exact set that is computed in full and the remaining keys are represented by a sample drawn in proportion to their estimated attention and reweighted by inverse sampling probability. Building on this formulation, we present InstaGen, which can be parameterized by any PBSA method: it replaces the method's block selection with an exact set plus a weighted sample. Each query cluster draws one sample shared by all its queries, so the sampled keys form blocks amenable to efficient GPU execution. In this paper, we take the SVG family of PBSA methods and show that, on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B and Wan2.1-T2V-14B, InstaGen parameterized by SVG2 improves PSNR over SVG2 by 3.4 dB and 2 dB, respectively, at matched attention density, and by 1.6 dB and 2.2 dB at matched wall-clock time. On Wan2.1-T2V-14B, InstaGen also beats SVG-EAR by 1.1 dB PSNR at nearly the same density while running faster (1.65x against 1.48x over dense).

- **[Training-Free Predictive Spatio-Temporal Token Pruning for Few-Step Autoregressive Video Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07551)**  `arXiv:2611.07551`  `cs.CV`  
  _Jia Li, Xurui Peng, Xing Wang, Hayden Kwok-Hay So_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Few-step autoregressive (AR) video diffusion models generate videos chunk by chunk and have made efficient video generation practical. However, further accelerating these models toward real-time generation remains challenging, as few-step distillation leaves little cross-step redundancy for existing training-free caching methods to exploit. We revisit this regime and identify two token-level redundancy patterns: cross-frame redundancy from static content shared across adjacent frames, and cross-step redundancy from tokens that converge early during denoising. Crucially, we find that early clean estimates along the model's own denoising trajectory provide an effective online signal for locating both patterns, requiring no retraining, auxiliary predictor, or reference video. Based on these findings, we propose PreSTP (Predictive Spatio-Temporal Token Pruning), a training-free framework that constructs a unified token-wise redundancy mask from cross-frame similarity and cross-step convergence. Tokens marked as redundant skip subsequent computation under a consistent last-computed-value restoration rule, and a content-adaptive mask reuse strategy further reduces dense probing cost. On 5-second generation, PreSTP achieves 1.52x-3.20x speedups and a better efficiency-fidelity trade-off than coarse-grained caching baselines such as TeaCache and FlowCache. On 30-second generation, it reaches up to 1.96x speedup while improving VBench-Long and suppressing long-horizon drift. Notably, it continues to reduce inference cost even in the extreme one-step regime, where cross-step redundancy vanishes entirely and coarse step-wise caching is no longer applicable.

- **[TriTD: Tri-Partite Trajectory-Distribution Distillation for Real-Time Autoregressive Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26165)**  `arXiv:2610.26165`  `cs.CV`  
  _Jia Li, Xurui Peng, Haowei Zhu, Tianyu Zhao, Jiexi Wang, Youwei Zheng, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Autoregressive (AR) video diffusion has emerged as a leading paradigm for efficient video generation, yet its practical deployment remains bottlenecked by the trade-off between generation quality and inference efficiency. We attribute this bottleneck to three error sources in AR rollouts: the base model error, the train-inference distribution mismatch, and the upstream error propagation. Building on this decomposition, we derive closed-form upper bounds on the per-step and cumulative errors, providing a principled basis for jointly optimizing generation quality and inference efficiency. Guided by this analysis, we propose TriTD, a Tri-Partite Trajectory-Distribution co-optimized distillation framework with three complementary modules. First, for ODE initialization, we adopt a Next-Node Trajectory Distillation (NTD) objective, which shortens the supervision horizon and admits a lower regression-error floor. Second, we introduce Chunk-Graded Noising (CGN), a monotonically non-decreasing per-chunk noising schedule that helps narrow the expected train-inference noise mismatch and removes redundant KV-cache updates, yielding substantial speedups at no quality cost. Third, we propose Orthogonal-Parallel DMD (OP-DMD), a distribution-matching loss that retains the original DMD supervision while introducing an additional regression constraint in the orthogonal subspace to suppress off-axis drift, improving both training stability and final generation quality. Extensive experiments show that TriTD consistently surpasses existing baselines in visual quality while reaching a real-time throughput of 26.8 FPS on a single H100 GPU, delivering a balanced quality-efficiency solution for interactive streaming video generation.
  </details>

- **[I2V-DETACH: Source Grounding Detachment for Unauthorized Image-to-Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23287)**  `arXiv:2610.23287`  `cs.CV`  
  _Chanhui Lee, Yeonghwan Song, Yewon Kang, Jeany Son_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent diffusion models have advanced image-conditioned video generation, but their accessibility raises risks of unauthorized manipulation. Image immunization has emerged as a promising defense for Image-to-Video (I2V) protection, yet existing methods often rely on degrading visual quality or temporal coherence. In this work, we revisit I2V immunization as reducing source-content preservation, shifting the focus from output degradation to weakening source-conditioned content propagation. To this end, we propose I2V-DETACH, an image immunization method that prevents unauthorized I2V generation through source grounding detachment. I2V models preserve source content by propagating source-image cues into video latents during denoising. Accordingly, I2V-DETACH disrupts this process in two complementary ways: it suppresses source-video grounding to reduce source cue propagation, and strengthens intra-video interactions to shift denoising toward video-side dynamics with reduced reliance on the source image. To obtain informative optimization signals, we initialize auxiliary video latents with source-oriented states that remain within the source-conditioned generation regime while deviating from source-preserving trajectories. Extensive experiments across diverse diffusion architectures show that I2V-DETACH substantially reduces source-content preservation, improves over prior immunization methods, and exhibits strong black-box transferability.
  </details>

- **[VideoGen-Agent: Reinforcing Video Generation Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24997)**  `arXiv:2609.24997`  `cs.CV`  
  _Binxu Li, Haoyi Duan, Yuhui Zhang, Yaohui Zhang, Zihao Lin, Kaituo Feng, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent advances in video generative models have enabled high-fidelity, temporally coherent video generation. However, these models often struggle to satisfy prompts requiring specialized knowledge, specific identities, physical consistency, or ordered events. In this paper, we present VideoGen-Agent, a multimodal agent trained through multitask agentic reinforcement learning to use external tools for video generation. The agent coordinates augmentation, generation, and verification tools through multi-turn interactions, using the prompt and intermediate observations to guide its decisions. We train a shared policy on a category-balanced dataset spanning six tasks. Supervised fine-tuning on teacher-generated trajectories establishes tool-use behavior, which is then refined through reinforcement learning. A category-aware hybrid reward evaluates tool-call validity, task-appropriate tool use, and generated video quality. We further introduce VABench, a held-out benchmark of 600 prompts covering procedural knowledge, single- and multi-entity identity preservation, physical consistency, scene composition, and multi-shot temporal structure. On VABench, VideoGen-Agent improves over its base text-to-video generator by 19.1 points, from 56.5 to 75.6. Upgrading the generation tools further raises the score to 86.1 without additional agent training. Human raters prefer the upgraded configuration over the strongest standalone baseline in 84.3% of comparisons.
