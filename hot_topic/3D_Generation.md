- **[Self-Distilling Future Video Representations for World Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30320)**  `arXiv:2610.30320`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Huiwon Jang, Suhyeok Jang, Hyeonmo Kang, Seungku Kim, Jinwoo Shin, Sihyun Yu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  World Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as a promising approach to learning generalist robot policies using rich spatiotemporal priors from pre-trained video diffusion models. A recent line of WAMs improves inference efficiency by conditioning a separate action decoder on intermediate future video representations extracted from a single denoising step, avoiding costly iterative denoising required for video generation. This design makes action prediction depend directly on the quality of these future representations. In this paper, we introduce Future REpresentation learning via self-Distillation with temporal context (FRED), a framework that trains WAMs to improve the quality of future video representations from noise in a single denoising step. Specifically, FRED uses self-distillation to predict future representations from noise, using representations extracted from ground-truth future observations as targets. We further condition the video model on historical observations, providing temporal context for predicting future representations better. Across diverse real-world manipulation scenarios, FRED consistently outperforms competing WAMs. It achieves particularly large gains on long-horizon and out-of-domain tasks under zero-shot environment generalization, and substantially improves human-to-robot skill transfer, reaching 50.0% task success on tasks absent from robot training data and demonstrated only in human videos, compared with 19.4% for the best competing WAM.

- **[RGraph-Eval: Task-Grounded Scene Graphs for Evaluating Robot Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31121)**  `arXiv:2610.31121`  `cs.RO`  
  _Aleksandr Lazarev, Sofia Zimina, Roman Kuznetsov, Tatiana Zemskova, Iaroslav Koshelev, Dmitry Yudin_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Generated robot videos can look convincing while depicting the wrong interac- tion: a different object is moved, a grasp disappears, or a placement ends out- side its destination. We introduce RGraph-Eval, a reference-based framework that makes these discrepancies explicit through task-grounded temporal scene graphs. Unlike approaches that construct separate evidence representations for different evaluation dimensions, we organize task entities, interactions, and motion within a unified, interpretable scene graph representation. A foundation-model pipeline identifies manipulation roles, grounds persistent object masks, infers and verifies state transitions and action intervals, and reconstructs manipulated- object motion in three dimensions. Applying this representation to a generated video and a reference demonstration supports an interpretable RGraph-Eval score with visible-object, relation-change, and 3D path-shape components. We also introduce ShareRobot-SG, a collection of 1,000 manipulation scenes from ten ShareRobot sources annotated with the multimodal representation. Our eval- uation separates graph-generation quality from agreement with human video judgments, and includes comparisons with adapted SVG2 and SG-Ego, a bench- mark of three video generators. The framework connects robot-video assessment to the observable entities and interactions underlying a task, while making the graph annotations reusable beyond evaluation. - **[Disentangled Sparse Representations for Concept-Separated Diffusion Unlearning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12122)**  `arXiv:2605.12122`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Hyeonjin Kim, Hangyeol Jung, Heechan Yun, Sungjun Yun, Dong-Jun Han_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Unlearning specific concepts in text-to-image diffusion models has become increasingly important for preventing undesirable content generation. Among prior approaches, sparse autoencoder (SAE)-based methods have attracted attention due to their ability to suppress target concepts through lightweight manipulation of latent features, without modifying model parameters. However, SAEs trained primarily for sparse reconstruction do not explicitly enforce concept-wise separation, allowing semantically related concepts to share latent features. To address this, we propose SAEParate, which explicitly separates semantically overlapping concepts in the SAE latent space through concept-aware contrastive learning, supported by cross-attention-guided spatial aggregation that emphasizes concept-relevant regions. In addition, we enhance the encoder with a nonlinear transformation to increase its expressive capacity under this separation objective, enabling more concept-discriminative latent representations. Experiments on UnlearnCanvas demonstrate state-of-the-art performance, with particularly strong gains in joint style-object unlearning, a challenging setting where existing methods suffer from severe interference between target and non-target concepts.

- **[Direct Forcing: A Single-Stage Training Recipe for Few-Step Autoregressive Text-to-Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30861)**  `arXiv:2610.30861`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Haojun Xu, Jie Huang, Xin Lu, Mingchen Zhong, Zihao Fan, Linjiang Huang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Few-step autoregressive video generation enables low-latency streaming, yet prevailing training pipelines separate causal adaptation from distribution matching. Recent representation-based text-to-video methods retain this separation by starting from ODE-initialized checkpoints, even when the target distribution is defined directly by reference videos. We introduce Direct Forcing, a single-stage framework that uses reference videos both as paired examples of how a history continues and as a target population for self-generated rollouts. Causal Continuation Alignment learns paired spatiotemporal correspondence under reference histories, while Rollout Distribution Matching uses maximum mean discrepancy to align the representation distributions of self-generated and reference videos. Together, these complementary signals allow a pretrained video model to learn causal continuation and the target video distribution in a single training stage. Dynamic Gradient Balancing adapts the relative gradient contributions of continuation alignment and distribution matching during training. Starting from bidirectional Wan2.1-1.3B, Direct Forcing learns a four-step autoregressive generator in 250 updates without a separate initialization stage or online diffusion-score supervision, achieving 84.63 VBench Total and 86.01 Quality. Ablation studies further demonstrate the benefits of dynamic gradient balancing and reveal distinct representation preferences: deeper features benefit continuation alignment, whereas intermediate features yield better performance for rollout distribution matching.

- **[WorldForge V2: Eliciting Camera-Trajectory Alignment in Video Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31640)**  `arXiv:2610.31640`  `cs.CV`  
  _Chenxi Song, Yanming Yang, Tong Zhao, Ruibo Li, Joey Tianyi Zhou, Chi Zhang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video diffusion models have learned rich 3D/4D priors, making them promising for scene generation and video re-rendering. However, their use in spatial tasks is limited by imprecise camera control, spatial and temporal inconsistencies, and entangled camera motion with scene dynamics. Existing approaches, such as task-specific fine-tuning and post-process warping, often introduce visual artifacts, generalize poorly, or incur high computational costs. We propose WorldForge V2 to achieve accurate camera control while preserving the generative capabilities of pretrained video models. We first develop a training-free guidance framework using warped references to iteratively refine generation along a target camera path. Optical-flow analysis identifies motion-related latent channels for selective guidance, helping separate motion control from appearance. A dual-path strategy further compares guided and unguided predictions to correct artifacts from imperfect references. Together, these components guide viewpoint changes while preserving scene appearance and object motion. To reduce refinement costs, v-cache approximates the correction using the preceding denoising prediction, avoiding an additional model evaluation. We further extend motion localization from latent channels to network blocks and introduce Trajectory LoRA, which concentrates adaptation on blocks most responsive to trajectory guidance. A two-stage training strategy teaches these adapters to denoise the states encountered during guided generation, using limited supervision effectively. This enables reliable camera control with very little training data, with a single training scene already supporting generalization to unseen scenes. Quantitative and qualitative evaluations across multiple datasets show that our method achieves state-of-the-art generation quality and trajectory accuracy in camera-controlled video generation.

- **[Making MLLM Latents Matter: Factor-Aware Predictive Learning for Cinematic Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30926)**  `arXiv:2610.30926`  `cs.CV`  
  _Songlin Yang, Zhe Wang, Xiaotong Zhao, Xi Wang, Ran Zhang, Shuai Li, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Cinematic video generation requires both temporal inference from limited inputs (i.e., sparse rather than frame-by-frame guidance) and factor-aware control of character, scene, action, and camera. Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) offer useful priors for both capabilities, but their representations are not automatically actionable for video generation: a Video Diffusion Transformer (DiT) may bypass them through native conditioning pathways, and using them does not ensure either factor-aware control or generation-compatible temporal prediction. We propose Factor-Aware Predictive Learning (FAPL), which establishes effective MLLM latent use as a shared prerequisite and then targets the two capabilities with complementary learning signals. First, latent-only generator grounding trains the DiT with a frozen MLLM and connector, strengthening the DiT's use of MLLM latents for generation. Second, for factor-aware control, paired videos specify a target-factor change and non-target preservation, and factor-span replacement trains the DiT to realize this relationship in its output. Third, for temporal inference, text-only and text-plus-complete-video observations share a video target, and the trained DiT is then frozen while the MLLM and connector are optimized through it. This final stage trains limited-input representations for generation while retaining complete-observation reconstruction as a complementary training mode. Extensive experiments demonstrate our consistently better performance on three cinematic video generation tasks (i.e., sparse-to-video, factor-aware editing, and multi-shot generation) over baselines and validate FAPL's contributions to MLLM feature use, temporal control, and factor coordination.

- **[Verus-Insight: Towards Explainable Artifact Detection in AI-Generated Videos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30866)**  `arXiv:2610.30866`  `cs.CV`  
  _Zhuoran Zhang, Yang Shi, Qixun Wang, Tengfei Liu, Yuqi Tang, Xuanyu Zhu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent advances in video generation have made synthetic videos increasingly realistic, placing greater demands on reliable AI-generated video detection. Existing artifact-based detectors typically identify generation artifacts as evidence for source prediction, yet they may either fail to recognize subtle artifacts or reach the correct source decision without faithfully identifying the evidence underlying it. In this work, we systematically investigate how to design and train effective artifact detectors and formulate artifact detection as a structured process comprising three complementary capabilities: Inspection Planning, which determines what should be examined; Evidence Verification, which verifies whether the inspected content exhibits genuine generation artifacts; and Evidence Aggregation, which integrates the verified evidence into the final source decision. Building on this formulation, we introduce Verus-Insight, trained with human-anchored full-process supervision and progressive capability training to strengthen both fine-grained artifact detection and video-level detection. We further introduce Verus-Bench, a benchmark with fine-grained human annotations of perceptible artifacts for evaluating artifact detection. Extensive experiments across diverse benchmarks demonstrate that Verus-Insight achieves strong performance in both fine-grained artifact analysis and AI-generated video detection, with substantial gains in recovering human-perceived artifacts. Further analyses reveal that the three capabilities are complementary yet distinct, and that Verus-Insight more reliably converts relevant inspection targets into supported artifact evidence without increasing spurious artifact claims.

  Before acting on an object, a robot needs to anticipate how its hand motion will change the physical world and what the resulting scene will look like. Existing approaches typically entangle action-conditioned dynamics, camera motion, and visual synthesis within a video generation model, or represent dynamics as point trajectories without explicit object structure. We introduce DeXplicit, a dexterous manipulation world model that instead predicts how the world evolves explicitly in metric 3D before rendering its visual consequences.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent advances in text-to-video generation have substantially improved visual fidelity, yet existing models still struggle to understand the physical evolution implicit in user prompts, making it difficult to generate videos with continuous physical state evolution. PhyEvolve consists of three agents: a Director, a Physical Evolver, and a Composer. - **[An Attribute-Based Measure of Video Complexity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00640)**  `arXiv:2606.00640`  `cs.CV`  
  _Aditya Sarkar, Yi Li, Zihao Wang, Hao Yuan, Sai Vidyaranya Nuthalapati, Aashu Singh, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  A new framework for the estimation of the complexity posed by video-question pairs to video-LLMs, Video Attribute-Based Complexity (VideoABC), is proposed. Video complexity is defined as the probability of failure of a video-LLM for a given video-question pair. VideoABC is a non-parametric complexity measure, using a reference video dataset and a pre-defined vocabulary of video attributes informative of complexity, e.g. the scene complexity or the speed of the video event relevant to the question. In a training phase, reference videos are projected into the space of these attributes, which is then quantized. The expected ABC of each quantization cell is then computed. Given a new video and its projection into the attribute space, complexity is estimated by the expected ABC of the associated quantization cell. To enable the use of VideoABC with small reference video datasets, two quantizers are combined: a k-means quantizer that enables accurate complexity estimates for samples in the distribution of the reference dataset and a universal lattice quantizer that guarantees generalization to out-of-distribution samples. A synthetic video generation procedure, inspired by target-distractor manipulations of psychophysics studies, is proposed to populate the cells of the lattice quantizer during training, enabling the computation of their expected ABCs. Experimental results show that VideoABC is effective even with very low-dimensional attribute representations, substantially outperforming approaches like 'video-LLM as judge' with much less complexity. Finally, the explainable nature of the VideoABC score, in terms of well-defined attributes, is shown to provide insights on how the attribute composition of benchmarks affects their complexity.
