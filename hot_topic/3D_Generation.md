- **[UGG: Unified Generative Geometry Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14440)**  `arXiv:2611.14440`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG` `cs.RO`  
  _Amani Kiruga, Zhiyi Li, Ruojin Cai, Hansen Lillemark, Xiaoshan Wu, Ayush Tewari, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Understanding 4D scenes is a core challenge in visual intelligence, requiring models to jointly capture scene geometry, appearance, and dynamics from partial observations. However, existing approaches are subdivided into feedforward models for direct geometric prediction, and generative models for visual synthesis. We introduce UGG, a unified framework for 4D scene understanding that jointly models RGB video and geometric representations such as depth and camera pose. By assigning different noise levels across modalities and time, UGG formulates visual generation and geometric prediction as conditional completion within a single generative model. This flexible formulation enables the model to learn from diverse, heterogeneous datasets with different subsets of geometric and visual annotations. Furthermore, our generative formulation enables inference-time search over multiple plausible scene hypotheses, using geometric evidence extracted from observed RGB frames to improve geometric prediction without ground truth geometry. We demonstrate UGG's effectiveness over non-unified baselines across camera-controlled video generation, depth estimation, and camera pose prediction, including in challenging sparse observation settings. Code and visualizations are available atthis https URL

- **[Few-Step Streaming Video Generation via Condition Shifting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14033)**  `arXiv:2611.14033`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Deyuan Liu, Jungang Li, Kaiwen Zheng, Kai Jiang, Jintao Chen, Yaole Wang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Few-step sampling reduces the cost of streaming video generation. Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) supervises few-step generators with a separate fake-score network, which adds training memory and computation and must track the evolving generator. We extend APEX to streaming video by using condition shifting to estimate the velocity field of the generated distribution within the generator. The original text condition selects generation, while a shifted condition selects fake estimation using the same parameters. This removes the independent fake network while retaining S2-Avatar's frozen reference teacher, Self-Replay Forcing, and perceptual regularization. We evaluate APEX on 180-second videos using three backbone evaluations per block and a one-step spatial Refiner, with a 70-case human study and a 320-case StreamAV-Bench comparison. Compared with S2-Avatar, APEX achieves higher video instruction fulfillment on the Progressive track (3.856 versus 3.831) and higher visual aesthetics across both tracks (0.641898 versus 0.632606). Human raters favor APEX most strongly on instruction following, with +30.0% net preference, followed by consistency and video quality at +4.3% each. Motion quality favors S2-Avatar, audiovisual synchronization is predominantly tied, and usability is unchanged. Separate measurements under matched training settings show 4.4% lower peak allocated GPU memory per GPU and 9.2% shorter mean iteration time than DMD.
  </details>

- **[PhyRubric: Rubric-Grounded Diagnostic Evaluation of Physical Commonsense in Text-to-Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13576)**  `arXiv:2611.13576`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Huiyi Chen, Weitai Kang, Shicheng Fan, Xinshuo Wang, Zhixiang Shen, Yan Yan, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  As text-to-video (T2V) models are increasingly positioned as world simulators, it is essential to evaluate whether their videos conform to physical commonsense. However, existing benchmarks rely on annotators who rate each video with a single score based on intuition, yielding ratings that are subjective and do not tell which physical principle is violated. We introduce PhyRubric, a rubric-grounded diagnostic framework for evaluating physical commonsense in T2V generation. For each caption, PhyRubric constructs a caption-specific rubric before any video is seen, decomposing the caption into semantic and physical items, and derives scores from item-level verdicts grounded in timestamped evidence. We instantiate this framework as PhyRubric-Bench, which spans 34 categories across five physical domains, and scale it up to PhyRubric-Train with 3,788 videos, on which we fine-tune the open-source PhyRubric-Judge. Empirical results across ten T2V models show that physical commonsense remains far from solved, and that PhyRubric-Judge correlates more closely with human judgments than existing open-source judges while matching the frontier proprietary Gemini 4 Argon. Further analysis provides actionable insights for T2V models.
  </details>

- **[OnlyAct: Extracting Action Representations from Foundation Models for World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14420)**  `arXiv:2611.14420`  `cs.CV`  
  _Runjia Li, Minghao Chen, Junyu Xie, Philip Torr, Andrea Vedaldi, Tomas Jakab_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video world models promise general-purpose interactive simulators, but their training is limited by action supervision: action labels are scarce, fragmented across incompatible specifications, and do not scale with the unlabeled video pretraining corpus. A common workaround jointly trains an action encoder to compress consecutive frames into a latent action and a decoder to reconstruct the next frame from the previous frame and that latent, forcing the latent to capture only new information. This has two limitations: learning the action space from scratch can limit generalization to unseen domains, and the bottleneck trades expressiveness for separation, with tighter settings losing nuance and looser ones risking scene leakage under domain shifts. We argue for a different starting point: rather than learning actions from scratch, we start from a pretrained foundation model (V-JEPA 2), whose embeddings of a frame pair already carry substantial action information after large-scale, diverse video pretraining. The remaining challenge is to extract the action while discarding the scene. We propose a label-free contrastive adaptation framework that pulls together frame pairs sharing the same motion under a different scene and pushes apart pairs from the same scene with different motion, reshaping the geometry of the pretrained space without imposing a reconstruction bottleneck. We introduce a unified evaluation protocol along three axes (action expressiveness, scene invariance, and action-conditioned video generation quality) across robot manipulation, camera navigation, game, and driving benchmarks, most of which are unseen during adaptation. Compared with annotation-free latent-action methods, our representations are substantially more expressive while matching their scene invariance, and they yield world models that transfer demonstrated actions to new scenes more faithfully.
  </details>

- **[TaoMate-R1: Temporal Role Specialization for Streaming Multi-Reference Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14307)**  `arXiv:2611.14307`  `cs.CV`  
  _Qijun Gan, Chenwei Zhang, Xinyang Li, Meiguang Jin, Junfeng Ma_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Streaming multi-reference video generation establishes a scene from specified people and objects, then continues it under changing instructions. We introduce TaoMate-R1, a streaming multi-reference generator that organizes adaptation parameters through temporal role specialization. It assigns separate low-rank adapters to scene establishment and continuation on a frozen backbone. Each adapter performs both denoising and context writing for its role; Self Gradient Forcing jointly trains the roles through the history they construct. This allocation contrasts with operation-based specialization, which separates denoising from context writing across all chunks. A fixed-replay gradient analysis characterizes the update directions admitted by the two sharing schemes. On 32 multi-reference cases, we compare the schemes at the same two-adapter budget. Across ten approximately two-minute streams with changing instructions and shared teacher-audio conditioning, TaoMate-R1 reduces the mean boundary luminance jump from 1.96 to 0.613, with higher boundary feature similarity. It also achieves higher mean CLIP and DINO temporal similarity across short interactions, long streams, and public reference compositions. These results support grouping adaptation parameters by scene establishment and continuation for streaming multi-reference generation. Our code and model are released atthis https URL.
  </details>

- **[iCATS: Fast Video Generation via Interaction-Aware Sparse Attention and Timestep-Adaptive Sparsity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11302)**  `arXiv:2610.11302`  `cs.CV`  
  _Chengfeng Han, Ang Wang, Baole Ai, Xianlu Bian, Jie Yao, Zilong Huang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Training-free sparse attention offers a practical acceleration solution to Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) via reducing computations without fine-tuning. It typically involves estimating the importance of query-key regions and deriving sparse masks to compute only the important candidates, which inevitably introduces approximation errors that may degrade generation quality. To better balance the efficiency-quality trade-off, we propose iCATS, integrating improved importance estimation and sparse mask construction with an efficient hardware execution strategy. Specifically, for importance estimation, unlike previous works that perform independent clustering over query and key tokens based on feature similarity to estimate attention scores, iCATS demonstrates that clustering based on query-key dot-product interactions is more accurate and further reformulates this objective as a simple quadratic form for low-cost computation. For sparse mask construction, instead of using a fixed top-p rule, we observe that tolerance to sparse approximation errors varies across denoising timesteps and therefore introduce an SNR-guided sparsity schedule to adjust sparsity dynamically, leading to higher accuracy. Finally, for hardware execution, we devise a tail-merging strategy to reduce padding overhead caused by irregular cluster sizes, improving GPU kernel utilization. Extensive experiments show that iCATS achieves $2.03\times$ acceleration with 31.017 dB PSNR on HunyuanVideo-T2V-13B and $1.55\times$ acceleration with 29.301 dB PSNR on Wan2.1-T2V-14B, delivering a state-of-the-art efficiency-quality trade-off.
  </details>

- **[Epipolar Geometry Improves Video Generation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21615)**  `arXiv:2510.21615`  `cs.CV`  
  _Orest Kupyn, Théo Uscidda, Marta Tintore Gazulla, Fabian Manhardt, Federico Tombari, Christian Rupprecht_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video generation models have advanced significantly through the latent diffusion transformers trained with rectified flow techniques. Yet these models still struggle with geometric inconsistencies, unstable motion, and visual artifacts that break the illusion of realistic 3D scenes. 3D-consistent video generation could significantly impact numerous downstream applications in generation and reconstruction tasks. We explore how epipolar geometry constraints improve modern video diffusion models. Despite using massive training data, these models fail to capture fundamental geometric principles. We align diffusion models using pairwise epipolar geometry constraints via preference-based optimization, directly addressing unstable trajectories and geometric artifacts through mathematically principled geometric enforcement. Our approach efficiently enforces geometric principles without requiring end-to-end differentiability. Evaluation demonstrates that classical geometric constraints provide more reliable preference signals than the video-quality and geometry metrics based on foundational models. We construct preferences from static scenes with camera motion, where the epipolar metric is most reliable, while the aligned model generalizes to dynamic scenes. By bridging data-driven learning with classical computer vision, we reduce epipolar error by 31% and improve human-rated consistency from 54% to 72% without compromising visual quality.
  </details>
