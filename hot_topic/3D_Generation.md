  The ability to reason, adapt, and creatively solve problems under unexpected challenges is essential for robots operating in real-world environments.   _Hai Zhang, Siqi Liang, Yanjun Guo, Shoufeng Zhang, Yunhao Wang, Hangyu Du, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Embodied navigation requires agents to achieve persistent spatial awareness and long-horizon execution stability in open-world environments. However, existing navigation architectures struggle to sustain both capabilities, remaining effective only over a handful of recent frames. Without altering the underlying architecture, we introduce History Representation, which integrates sparse visual keyframes with an explicit History Representation Map to preserve long-term spatiotemporal context. Compass 1achieves a dominant overall success rate of 79.4% across open-world evaluations, compared to 17.8% for the strongest baseline. It demonstrates exceptional zero-shot execution exceeding 90% on foundational tasks, a 250% boost in exploration, and an unparalleled leap from 0% to 58% in challenging persistent history backtracking.

- **[ForeAct: Geometry-Guided Action Learning from World Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11500)**  `arXiv:2611.11500`  `cs.CV` `cs.RO`  
  _Wei Lu, Jiaxi Zhang, Yunheng Wang, Taowen Wang, Yuetong Fang, Zhenhui Zhang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robotic manipulation requires representations that capture both scene geometry and its evolution during interaction. Existing approaches have largely treated these two aspects separately. World action models capture such dynamics by coupling action prediction with future video generation, but their reliance on iterative diffusion or rectified-flow inference leads to high computational cost, while their representations lack explicit spatial structure. Explicitly modeling scene geometry typically requires auxiliary modules that increase both parameter count and computational cost. Conversely, geometry-centric policies primarily recover observed spatial structure and often lack explicit supervision for anticipating future geometric changes during interaction. To address these limitations, we introduce ForeAct, which repurposes a pretrained video world model as a single-pass representation backbone for low-latency control. ForeAct directly processes the clean latent of the current observation in one forward pass, eliminating future-frame synthesis and iterative denoising while retaining the backbone's pretrained spatiotemporal priors. Rather than introducing a separate geometry backbone, ForeAct uses learnable tokens supervised by next-frame relative depth to shape the shared video representation through bidirectional self-attention. This compact interface turns the spatial knowledge already encoded in the backbone into a future-aware representation for action generation, with little parameter overhead. The lightweight geometry readout is used only during training and removed at deployment, enabling low-latency, geometry-aware control without depth input or an external geometry backbone. Without additional large-scale pretraining, ForeAct achieves 96.6% average success on LIBERO, 63.5% on SimplerEnv-Bridge, and a 74.4% success rate across 90 real-world rollouts.

- **[Semantically Aligned KV Cache Compression for Efficient Agentic Autoregressive Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11452)**  `arXiv:2611.11452`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Weizhong Huang, Huaijia Kou, Tianyu Xie, Xiawu Zheng, Liujuan Cao_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Agentic autoregressive video generation combines semantic planning, sequential generation, and visual verification for controllable synthesis. Growing visual histories enlarge the key-value (KV) cache, increasing memory use and attention costs across candidate rollouts. Existing compression methods primarily optimize individual generators without the planner's semantic constraints, risking low priority for states supporting requested attributes or actions. We introduce SemanticKV, a training-free method for semantically aligned KV cache compression. To our knowledge, SemanticKV is the first KV cache compression method for agentic autoregressive video generation that explicitly uses planner-extracted semantic constraints to guide retention. Text cross-attention identifies constraint-related video positions, whose self-attention queries are collected into separate groups. Attention weights are averaged within each group and maximized across groups, giving each cached entry a high score when strongly used by any represented constraint. A single top-k selection retains high-scoring entries under the memory budget, leaving their keys, values, and positions unchanged. For fixed query groups and equal entry costs, this selection minimizes an additive upper bound on worst-group discarded attention. The method operates independently within each candidate without retraining or additional video generations. We evaluate four AR video backbones on three benchmarks for 5-, 30-, and 60-second generation. On Causal Forcing at a compression ratio of 0.7, SemanticKV obtains a VBench Total Score of 84.10, compared with 84.05 for the strongest compressed baseline and 83.99 for Full KV. It achieves a 1.22x speedup over Full KV and reduces KV-cache memory from 6.04 GB to 1.81 GB, a 3.35x reduction.
  </details>

To address this limitation, we propose Curved Ray Expectation Positional Encoding (CRePE). - **[PhysOPSD: Code as Privilege for Physically Plausible Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12411)**  `arXiv:2611.12411`  `cs.CV`  
  _Harold Haodong Chen, Rui Peng, Lingteng Qiu, Kejie Qiu, Chao Xu, Zilong Dong_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  While modern video generative models achieve remarkable visual fidelity, they remain noticeably brittle when generating physically plausible dynamics and complex interactions. Targeted post-training is crucial, yet supervised fine-tuning suffers from offline state mismatch, and reinforcement learning is hindered by sparse, ill-defined physical reward functions. On-Policy (Self-)Distillation (OPSD) reconciles on-policy rollouts with dense corrective feedback, shifting the core challenge to designing effective privileged context. However, conventional modalities fall short: text prompts are inherently declarative, whereas real reference videos heavily entangle physical laws with visual appearance without parametric control. To this end, we propose PhysOPSD, introducing executable code as privileged physical context for video generation. Unlike static visual observations, executable code instantiates an editable physical world with intrinsic abstraction and controllability. Powered by PhysOPSD-Engine, we translate real-world physical scenarios into verifiable simulation programs. PhysOPSD then incorporates (i) dual-level physical privilege to bridge the information gap via an intermediate bottleneck, alongside (ii) controlled physical variations under shared rollout noise to expose causal dynamic shifts. Extensive experiments demonstrate that PhysOPSD substantially improves physical plausibility across diverse backbones, outperforming SFT and RL baselines with only $3$K samples, while preserving general video generation quality.

- **[MPEG-Diffusion: Mixed-Resolution Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12069)**  `arXiv:2611.12069`  `cs.CV`  
  _Dennis Menn, Tianpei Gu, Guande He, Aryan V S, Yanke Song, Nan Liu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video diffusion transformers (DiTs) generate realistic videos, but their heavy computational cost limits broader real-world applications. Mixed-resolution generation reduces this cost by generating the visually important region at high resolution (HR) and the rest at low resolution (LR), yet existing methods suffer a severe quality drop on videos and require knowing the HR region in advance. We show that these failures are not inherent to mixed resolution, and propose $\textbf{MPEG-Diffusion}$ to address their three root causes: (1) dual-resolution attention to mitigate the curse of mixed resolution, where each query reads the K/V of its own resolution, (2) a mixed-resolution decoder to blend the HR and LR latents before decoding and resolve distortion, and (3) a self-selected HR region using the DiT's knowledge to find the visually important region without requiring it in advance. Processing only $30\%$ of full-resolution tokens, MPEG-Diffusion speeds up denoising by $4.8\times$ on Wan2.1 14B ($3.8\times$ on 1.3B), outperforming prior methods on nearly every metric. In the user study, we demonstrate the high quality of MPEG-Diffusion, with raters preferring it over prior mixed-resolution methods in over $90\%$ of pairs and rating it close to full resolution.

- **[GenRM-Flow: Generators are Process-aware Reward Models in Flow Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11421)**  `arXiv:2611.11421`  `cs.CV`  
  _Siming Fu, Ruizhe He, Zheming Fu, Zeyue Xue, Fengkai Liu, Yufei Jiao, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Aligning video generation models with human preference depends critically on the quality of the reward model that scores generated videos. Current video reward models, however, source their reward signal from a model's representational rather than generative capacity: they either repurpose vision-language models as pixel-space scorers, or train auxiliary scoring heads on top of frozen generator features. The generator's actual ability to predict velocities, denoise latents, and produce videos plays no role in scoring its own generations. We argue that this overlooks a structural property of offline preference learning. Any preference optimization objective that approximates an offline preference dataset mathematically targets a strictly defined KL-constrained optimal policy. By isolating the reward term from this analytical solution and mapping the exact generation log-likelihood to its flow-matching surrogate, the empirical reward emerges deterministically as the relative difference in continuous-time velocity-prediction errors. We formalize this equivalence into GenRM-Flow, a framework where a preference-tuned video generator inherently operates as a process-aware reward model directly in the noisy latent space. We further show theoretically that distinct fine-tuning objectives, whether contrastive like DPO and IPO or non-contrastive like RWR, are alternative optimization pathways toward the same optimal policy, each inducing a reward readout from the generator's velocity-prediction errors. Furthermore, the extracted process reward seamlessly substitutes external VLM scorers in FlowAWR. Operating purely on native latent outputs without auxiliary reward networks, this generative reward drives measurable improvements in downstream video generation.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video generation models can produce highly realistic videos while generating physically inconsistent object motion. This limitation suggests that visual modeling alone may not provide a transferable representation of physical dynamics. We introduce MotionCore, a framework for learning physical dynamics via a learnable partial differential equation solver and focuses on rigid-body motion. We introduce a latent neural simulator learning to predict the evolution of object-level physical states from observed motion. We evaluate MotionCore on public physical-consistency benchmarks and on paired data with known trajectories. Results indicate that MotionCore improves physical consistency and trajectory obedience in the evaluated rigid-body settings, while maintaining comparable visual quality. A controlled ablation protocol compares learned dynamics with an analytic prior and studies the choice of latent evolution processor.

- **[Next Forcing: World Action Modeling with Multi-Chunk Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11187)**  `arXiv:2606.11187`  `cs.CV`  
  _Gangwei Xu, Qihang Zhang, Jiaming Zhou, Xing Zhu, Yujun Shen, Xin Yang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Autoregressive video generation has emerged as a powerful paradigm for World Action Models (WAMs). However, existing approaches suffer from slow training convergence and limited converged accuracy, particularly at high frame rates, as the training supervision is confined to the current chunk without explicit signals about future dynamics; they also suffer from slow inference due to iterative video denoising. In this paper, we present Next Forcing, a multi-chunk prediction (MCP) framework for world action modeling that enables faster training, higher accuracy, and accelerated inference. Inspired by multi-token prediction in large language models, Next Forcing introduces an MCP training objective that augments the main model with lightweight auxiliary MCP modules to simultaneously denoise video chunks at multiple future temporal horizons (next$^1$, next$^2$, next$^3$ chunks). These MCP modules form a causal chain across prediction depths, where intermediate features fused from multiple layers of the main model are leveraged to predict future dynamics, allowing near-future predictions to inform farther-future ones and providing dense multi-scale temporal supervision back to the main model. During training, the MCP modules significantly accelerate convergence and improve converged accuracy, especially at high frame rates: at 50 fps, Next Forcing achieves a 93.1% relative improvement over LingBot-VA at 5k training steps and 2.3$\times$ faster convergence, and establishes new state-of-the-art results on the RoboTwin benchmark (94.1/93.5% on Clean/Random). At inference, the MCP modules can be retained to predict the next video chunk in parallel with the current one, achieving 2$\times$ inference acceleration. Beyond World Action Models, Next Forcing also improves physical-law adherence on PhyWorld and reduces FVD by over 50\% in action-free general video pretraining.

  _Lin Zhao, Yushu Wu, Yifan Gong, Yanzhi Wang, Pu Zhao_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Autoregressive (AR) video generation extends videos by producing latent chunks sequentially, but scaling to long videos requires repeated access to a growing historical KV cache. Existing methods reduce this cost by truncating the KV cache or compressing it into implicit memory, but both lose explicit access to query-relevant historical details. We propose OmniMem, an explicit full-range memory retrieval framework that performs sparse KV retrieval over the historical cache. To make this practical for chunk-based AR video generation, OmniMem addresses two issues: (i) local bias in sparse KV selection and (ii) Union Explosion in memory access. Adaptive Window Exclusion removes local-window blocks from the selection candidates when sufficient long-range history is available, preserving the sparse budget for informative long-range retrieval. Query-Shared KV Selection reduces cross-query diversity, while Per-Head Scattered KV Access avoids expanding head-specific selections into a large selected KV buffer. This allows each attention head to retrieve non-contiguous KV blocks according to its own selection pattern. Experiments on long-video generation show that OmniMem improves Dynamic Degree by 52.3% and preserves strong consistency over strong baselines, while maintaining comparable memory usage.
