- **[VTAM: Efficient Visuo-Tactile World-Action Models for Contact-Rich Manipulation without Tactile Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23481)**  `arXiv:2603.23481`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Haoran Yuan, Weigang Yi, Zhenyu Zhang, Wendi Chen, Yuchen Mo, Jiashi Yin, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Tactile vision-language-action models (VLAs) and tactile world-action models (WAMs) are a promising new paradigm for contact-rich manipulation, but existing models either underutilize touch, rely on large-scale tactile pretraining, or incur high inference latency due to iterative video generation. We present VTAM, an efficient visuo-tactile world-action model for contact-rich manipulation that requires no tactile or action pretraining. RGB and tactile observations share a frozen video VAE and the pretrained video transformer weights. VTAM first adapts the video backbone to jointly predict future visual and tactile latents, then fine-tunes it end-to-end with a randomly initialized action expert that predicts actions, proprioceptive states, and a light flow-force proxy computed from tactile optical flow. Trained only on task demonstrations, VTAM outperforms all baselines on four real-robot tasks across two platforms with different tactile sensors, outperforms all baselines without tactile pretraining in average success on eight UniVTAC simulation tasks, and is comparable to tactile VLAs and WAMs pretrained on over 30K hours of largely visuo-tactile robot data (83.4% vs. 84.5% for N0-TWAM). Without full video denoising at inference, VTAM is approximately 6x and 16x faster than N0-TWAM and LingBot-VA without compilation, with compiled latency comparable to Fast-WAM. Ablations show that feeding frozen-VAE tactile features directly to the action expert, bypassing tactile world modeling, fails on every tested task, that Stage I visuo-tactile world-model fine-tuning improves UniVTAC success on most tasks, and that flow-force proxy keeps the policy from fully ignoring touch. The tactile encoder transfers zero-shot across GelSight Mini and Xense Photon sensors.

- **[RoboCurate: Harnessing Diversity with Action-Verified Synthetic Data for Robot Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18742)**  `arXiv:2602.18742`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Seungku Kim, Suhyeok Jang, Byungjun Yoon, Dongyoung Kim, John Won, Jinwoo Shin_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Synthetic data generated by video generative models has shown promise for robot learning as a scalable pipeline, but it often suffers from noisy action labels due to imperfect video generation or inaccurate action prediction. Recently, vision-language models (VLMs) have been leveraged to validate video quality, but they cannot directly evaluate the generated actions themselves. To tackle this issue, we introduce RoboCurate, a novel framework for action-verified synthetic robot data that addresses inconsistencies between generated videos and annotated actions. Specifically, RoboCurate executes the actions predicted by an inverse dynamics model (IDM) in a simulator and verifies action consistency by measuring the motion agreement between the simulator rollout and the generated video. To better leverage such a framework, we further expand the scene diversity of synthetic data beyond the available dataset through off-the-shelf generative models with structured editing axes. We then use a VLM to pre-filter physically implausible generated videos before verifying their action labels. Across diverse manipulation scenarios, RoboCurate yields substantial relative improvements in success rates compared to using only real data, achieving +90.3% on GR-1 Tabletop (300 demos), +29.1% on DexMimicGen in the pre-training setup on simulation benchmarks, and +179.9% and +42.9% in the co-finetuning setup on challenging real-world ALLEX and OpenArm humanoid benchmarks, respectively.

- **[HyperVAttention: Efficient Sparse Attention with Spatio-Temporal Clustering for Video Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03012)**  `arXiv:2607.03012`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI` `cs.LG`  
  _Dongyeun Lee, Amir Zandieh, Vahab Mirrokni, Junmo Kim, Insu Han_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video Diffusion Transformers (VDiTs) have demonstrated significant capabilities in high-fidelity video generation. However, their ability to produce long-duration videos is fundamentally constrained by the quadratic complexity of the self-attention mechanism. Recent clustering-based sparse attention methods improve the quality-speed trade-off by grouping semantically similar tokens, but their practical efficiency remains limited by two bottlenecks: substantial clustering overhead and low CTA utilization caused by irregular cluster-induced blocks. We propose HyperVAttention (HVA), a training-free sparse attention framework that addresses both bottlenecks jointly. To reduce clustering overhead, we introduce 3D local-window clustering, which exploits the spatio-temporal locality of video tokens to restrict centroid search to fixed local neighborhoods, and implement it with a custom Triton kernel for efficient execution. We further propose a hybrid clustering strategy that performs full clustering only at anchor steps and updates only subset tokens at intermediate steps, leveraging the temporal stability of cluster assignments across denoising steps. To improve CTA utilization, we present hardware-aware cluster merging that minimizes CTA-aligned execution cost through parallel agglomerative merging, improving block density and approximation fidelity by utilizing idle tile capacity. Together, these components reduce clustering overhead, avoid redundant updates, and better align sparse attention with the fixed tile structure of modern GPU kernels. Experiments on video generation show that HVA advances the Pareto frontier of training free sparse attention, achieving higher fidelity than SVG2 at comparable latency and a $2.13\times$ speedup over full attention on HunyuanVideo, or $2.23\times$ with its faster variant HVA-Flash.

- **[PickMoment: Continuous-Time Single-Image-to-Video via Learning Deblurring and Blur-to-Video](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01279)**  `arXiv:2610.01279`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Junseong Shin, Hyeonsu Jo, Daehyun Kim, Tae Hyun Kim_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Motion blur arises from the temporal integration of a continuous sharp signal over a finite exposure window, yet existing learning-based methods sidestep this physical model and predict only the sharp signal itself: most single-image deblurring methods recover a single frame at the exposure center, while blur-to-video methods predict a fixed set of frames. We introduce PickMoment, a continuous-time reformulation that directly learns the interval-mean blur over arbitrary sub-intervals of the exposure with a single deterministic model. Drawing an analogy to MeanFlow's average-velocity formulation, we train the model with three supervisions derived from the blur integral: an empirical reconstruction loss from available subframes, an additivity loss that enforces self-consistency across overlapping sub-intervals, and a sharp-frame loss anchored at the zero-interval limit. A single trained model unifies single-image deblurring, blur-to-video generation, and continuous-time pick-a-moment recovery as different queries to the same network, with no separate training for each task. Our PickMoment achieves state-of-the-art performance among generative-based deblurring methods on GoPro and HIDE while competitive against restoration-based methods on RealBlur, and the highest per-frame fidelity on GoPro-7 blur-to-video, all in a single forward pass without iterative sampling.

  _Le Jiang, Xiangyu Bai, Bishoy Galoaa, Shayda Moezzi, Caleb James Lee, Tooba Imtiaz, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We present PanoWorld, a panoramic video world model that generates geometry-consistent 360$\degree$ video from a single image and a caption. Existing panoramic video methods optimize primarily for visual realism and do not explicitly constrain the underlying 3D scene state, producing outputs that appear plausible yet exhibit inconsistent depth, broken correspondences, and implausible motion across the spherical surface. We address this gap by framing panoramic video generation as a geometry- and dynamics-consistent latent state modeling problem rather than pure visual synthesis. Building on a pre-trained perspective video world model, we introduce two lightweight regularizers: a depth consistency loss against pseudo ground-truth panoramic depth, and a trajectory consistency loss that supervises the 3D world-frame positions of tracked points across time. We further apply spherical-geometry-aware adaptation to the conditioning and positional encoding. We additionally introduce PanoGeo, a unified geometry-aware panoramic video dataset with consistent depth, trajectory, and prompt annotations across diverse real and synthetic sources, used for both training and stratified evaluation. Experiments show that PanoWorld improves geometric consistency over prior panoramic generation methods while maintaining competitive visual realism, establishing that panoramic video generation must be treated as a geometric modeling problem to support the holistic spatial understanding requirements of embodied AI applications.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>

Rendering does not directly identify the code changes needed to correct these visual failures, so feedback-driven revision is needed. Existing agentic frameworks use iterative rendering and revision but typically rely on large models, often proprietary. Existing Manim datasets primarily contain final programs and often lack quality verification and the trajectories of feedback and revisions needed to learn this refinement process.   _Hongye Hou, Shuai Zhang, Hongjie Li, Zhuoxiao Li, Haiping Wang, Yuan Liu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Generating compositional 3D scenes from a single image requires recovering detailed object geometry and coherent spatial arrangements. Existing object-to-scene methods, including SAM3D and MIDI, extend object generation through image-context reasoning or inter-instance modeling. Coordinating objects remains challenging with limited scene-level supervision. Directly extending object-centric generators to entire scenes can degrade geometry and fail to preserve instance scale, orientation, and pose. We propose Bri3D, a framework bridging scene-level structural reasoning and object-level generative priors through instance-aware voxel scene anchors. Bri3D predicts a color-coded scene scaffold encoding coarse object geometry and spatial arrangements. Bri3D predicts scene layouts efficiently; full scene generation is faster than SceneGen and SAM3D.

- **[Guidance Is a Field, Not a Scalar: Interval-Integrated Classifier-Free Guidance for One-Step Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07566)**  `arXiv:2611.07566`  `cs.CV`  
  _Zijie Meng, Xixin Cao_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  One-step generators built on MeanFlow synthesize a video in a single function evaluation, yet inherit a rigid treatment of classifier-free guidance (CFG): the guidance scale is frozen during training or, at best, exposed as a single scalar. This conflicts with mounting evidence that the optimal guidance is not a constant but a field that varies with the noise level, with spatial content, and, for video, with frame position. Every existing remedy presupposes a multi-step sampler along which guidance can be re-scheduled--an assumption one-step models violate by construction. We resolve this tension through a single observation: because the MeanFlow target is an integral over a noise interval, an arbitrary guidance field can be absorbed into the target itself and compiled into one evaluation. We formalize this as the interval-integrated CFG identity, prove that the compiled guidance carries no schedule-discretization error and can thus match or exceed any finite multi-step sampler, and instantiate it as AGAVE, which equips the average-velocity field with a learnable temporal schedule, a free motion-saliency spatial modulation, and a frame-position asymmetry. Adding fewer than ten scalars at zero inference cost, AGAVE surpasses all one-step baselines on VBench, UCF-101 and MSR-VTT while matching multi-step guided sampling. - **[Training-Free Predictive Spatio-Temporal Token Pruning for Few-Step Autoregressive Video Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07551)**  `arXiv:2611.07551`  `cs.CV`  
  _Jia Li, Xurui Peng, Xing Wang, Hayden Kwok-Hay So_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Few-step autoregressive (AR) video diffusion models generate videos chunk by chunk and have made efficient video generation practical. However, further accelerating these models toward real-time generation remains challenging, as few-step distillation leaves little cross-step redundancy for existing training-free caching methods to exploit. We revisit this regime and identify two token-level redundancy patterns: cross-frame redundancy from static content shared across adjacent frames, and cross-step redundancy from tokens that converge early during denoising. Crucially, we find that early clean estimates along the model's own denoising trajectory provide an effective online signal for locating both patterns, requiring no retraining, auxiliary predictor, or reference video. Based on these findings, we propose PreSTP (Predictive Spatio-Temporal Token Pruning), a training-free framework that constructs a unified token-wise redundancy mask from cross-frame similarity and cross-step convergence. Tokens marked as redundant skip subsequent computation under a consistent last-computed-value restoration rule, and a content-adaptive mask reuse strategy further reduces dense probing cost. On 5-second generation, PreSTP achieves 1.52x-3.20x speedups and a better efficiency-fidelity trade-off than coarse-grained caching baselines such as TeaCache and FlowCache. On 30-second generation, it reaches up to 1.96x speedup while improving VBench-Long and suppressing long-horizon drift. Notably, it continues to reduce inference cost even in the extreme one-step regime, where cross-step redundancy vanishes entirely and coarse step-wise caching is no longer applicable.

- **[ArtiVidRM: Verifiable Artifact-as-Rubric Reward Modeling for Explicit Quality Articulation in Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07301)**  `arXiv:2611.07301`  `cs.CV`

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Reinforcement learning has become an effective approach for improving video generation, but its success depends heavily on reliable reward models. Existing methods either map videos directly to opaque scalar scores or generate free-form rationales that are not explicitly optimized as the basis of the final judgment. We propose ArtiVidRM, a verifiable, artifact-as-rubrics video reward model that follows an observe-then-score paradigm. It first identifies visible generation artifacts and organizes them into a structured artifact list, then uses these artifacts as explicit rubrics for quality assessment. - **[Householder Motion Embedding: Noise Modulation for Motion-Controllable Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07294)**  `arXiv:2611.07294`  `cs.CV`  
  _Tao Jun Lin, Jinhong Ni, Ruyi Zha, Yifu Wang, Xibin Song, Pan Ji, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion models for video generation have achieved remarkable success, yet precise motion control under explicit user guidance remains challenging. Recent noise-warping methods introduce temporal correlations by warping the noise according to the user-provided motion signal. However, standard spatial warping generally disrupts the noise's i.i.d. Gaussian structure, requiring distribution correction, such as Jacobian-based correction, but at a substantial computational overhead. We propose Householder Motion Embedding (HME), which encodes the motion signal into the noise through a sequence of Householder reflections that together form an orthogonal transformation applied to each location's channel vector. HME exactly preserves the spatial Gaussian structure within each frame while introducing cross-frame correlations that promote temporally consistent video. HME is agnostic to the network architecture and integrates directly into standard video diffusion fine-tuning pipelines. Experiments on different scenarios show that HME matches or outperforms the state of the art, while achieving a 2.7x speedup over noise warping. These results demonstrate that channel-space Householder transformations offer a principled and efficient alternative to noise warping for motion-controllable video generation.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We introduce TrackTok, an efficient video tokenization framework that produces object-centric tokens, i.e., tokens that consistently encode the same semantic objects across time, even as it moves, deforms, or undergoes partial occlusion. In contrast, our approach learns temporally persistent object-centric representations that bind visual features across frames into stable semantic units. - **[A 3D-Driven Framework for Moving-Camera Perspective-to-360° Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25395)**  `arXiv:2610.25395`  `cs.CV`  
  _Michael Wakeham, Mrinmoy Kumar Das, Wenqi Wang, Ayush Tewari, Boqing Gong, Deepti Ghadiyaram_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Turning a moving-camera perspective video into a 360° video means generating content outside the input frame under several constraints: observations revealed over time must be preserved, unseen regions plausibly completed, and the resulting scene kept consistent as the camera viewpoint changes. Existing methods assign these different requirements to a single conditional video model, requiring them to be jointly learned from panoramic video data. We instead present a modular, 3D-driven framework that distributes these three responsibilities across specialized components, all connected through a shared scene representation. Input observations are reconstructed explicitly in 3D, while a pretrained panoramic image model completes unobserved content from a selected panoramic render. This generated content is lifted into the shared 3D scene, where it remains spatially anchored across camera viewpoints. Without requiring any additional panoramic-video training, our framework achieves state-of-the-art input-view fidelity, perceptual quality, and geometric consistency on a static-scene subset of the Argus evaluation set. These results support our decomposed, 3D-driven formulation as a promising alternative to direct video generation in this setting.
