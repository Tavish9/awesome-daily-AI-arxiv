- **[PDRVLA: A Vision-Language-Action Model with Physical Dynamics Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37381)**  `arXiv:2610.37381`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Hanqing Wang, Zhaotian Cai, Ruichuan An, Bohan Zeng, Tao Zhang, Yilin Chen, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision--Language--Action (VLA) models generate robot actions from visual observations and language instructions, but their understanding of physical dynamics remains limited. Video world models can capture scene dynamics, yet generating future videos introduces high latency into the control loop. We introduce PDRVLA, whose lightweight Physical Dynamics Module extracts physical tokens from vision--language features to condition action generation without online video generation. The module learns through direct prediction of future RGB, depth, and optical-flow features, with auxiliary supervision through a frozen pretrained video world model. Training relies entirely on publicly available data, expanded through human-to-robot synthesis, subtask annotation, and embodied chain-of-thought (ECoT) annotation. Across four simulation benchmarks, PDRVLA achieves success rates of 99.3% on LIBERO, 89.9% on LIBERO-Plus, 93.6% on RoboTwin~2.0, and 81.5% on SimplerEnv. Ablations show improved future prediction with world-model supervision and evaluate how subtask conditioning, future object-pose supervision, and ECoT training affect action success.
  </details>

- **[TAPESTRY: A Single Backbone for Video Generation and Robot Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36823)**  `arXiv:2610.36823`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Jeremy A. Collins, Seungjae Lee, Rachanon Wachakorn, Krishnan Srinivasan, Animesh Garg, Vitor Campagnolo Guizilini, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Pretrained video generation models encode rich priors about physical interactions, but leveraging them for robot control typically requires either resource-intensive video generation at test time or action heads trained from scratch that do not fully utilize the pretrained backbone. We introduce Tokenizing Actions as Video Patches in a Shared Transformer Policy (TAPESTRY), a world action model that repurposes a pretrained video diffusion transformer into a robotic policy without requiring video generation at inference. Our key idea is to treat robot actions as single-patch tokens in the video model's latent space. A lightweight linear projection maps each action timestep to the patch dimensions of the pretrained VAE, and the resulting tokens are processed by the same patchifying layers and transformer backbone used for video. The shared backbone is optimized through three diffusion objectives sampled per minibatch element: Video diffusion uses VAE latents from robot and human videos to establish dynamics priors. Joint video+action diffusion trains on robot demonstrations using a coupled diffusion timestep and an asymmetric attention mask. Action-only diffusion denoises actions conditioned on clean image latents. At test time, the model generates a compact action token sequence in a single DDIM step from pure noise, producing smooth action chunks at 84 ms per action chunk on consumer hardware and avoiding the cost of full video generation. Across simulation benchmarks and real-robot experiments, TAPESTRY achieves strong performance with substantially lower inference cost than comparable joint video--action policies. - **[ReferStream: Interactive Subject Injection for Streaming Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37566)**  `arXiv:2610.37566`  `cs.CV`  
  _Shuolin Xu, Guanyuan Li, Boyu Li, Kelu Yao, Xiaogang Xu, Hongchuan Yu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Injecting new subjects naturally during streaming video generation requires integrating newly supplied visual references into the existing video while preserving established subjects and the scene. Encoding references independently of the current scene provides little guidance for this integration, while keeping their features in persistent memory can interfere with established context. We propose ReferStream, a framework that requires no additional training and combines reference construction using the current scene with transient cache injection. Joint Latent places reference latents in target regions of the latest scene latent, allowing reference features to be extracted in context. Cache Restoration uses these features to condition the injection chunk, then restores the overwritten global cache entries while retaining the newly generated history. This procedure incorporates new visual conditions without retraining the generator or revising emitted frames. We further introduce ReferStream Bench, a new benchmark with scheduled reference arrivals, evolving instructions, and explicit annotations of subject presence for InShot and MultiShot generation. Evaluation follows the event schedule and measures both the fidelity of new subjects and the preservation of established subjects, alongside video quality, text adherence, and count accuracy. Experiments on ReferStream Bench show higher scores for text adherence, subject similarity, and count accuracy than the compared baselines, including offline methods given all references in advance. - **[ScIntBench: Physical Interaction Plausibility in 3D Scene Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37326)**  `arXiv:2610.37326`  `cs.CV`  
  _Wendi Ying, Daochang Liu, Leonid Sigal, Ajmal Saeed Mian_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Existing evaluations of text-conditioned 3D indoor scene generation largely emphasize semantic fidelity and physical validity at rest, leaving interaction-time physical reliability insufficiently assessed. To address this gap, we introduce ScIntBench, a unified benchmark centered on interaction readiness: it executes prescribed manipulation actions (e.g., pushing, opening, and surface probing) in physics simulation and measures task success, capability and progress, post-interaction integrity, and dynamic plausibility. Multi-view semantic and static physical evaluations complement these metrics and help explain interaction outcomes. We systematically evaluate 12 state-of-the-art scene generation systems across 10 room types. Our results reveal that strong semantic and static physical performance does not necessarily translate into interaction readiness: a system with near-perfect static plausibility completes only 11.54% of interactions, 7 of the 12 systems produce scenes that cannot support any interaction, and even the best system succeeds in only 36.92% of prescribed tasks. These findings highlight two fundamental challenges in generating interaction-ready scenes: providing the necessary physical interaction capabilities and ensuring reliable physical behavior under active manipulation.
  </details>

- **[VibeVideoBench: Evaluating Multimodal Agents on Code-Driven Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35864)**  `arXiv:2610.35864`  `cs.CV`  
  _Muxi Diao, Wenbin Xu, Lele Yang, Xuyang Ma, Yuzhe Chen, Jiankang Chen, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion video models produce striking clips, but they cannot reliably render an exact formula, a correct chart, a rule-consistent game or narration synchronized to on-screen events. A different route is emerging: multimodal agents that write code to render every frame and synthesize every sound. We introduce VibeVideoBench, a benchmark for evaluating agents on code-driven video generation. Its 239 self-contained tasks in English and Chinese span 12 categories, from data stories and music-driven pieces to classroom lessons and paper explainers; each asks an agent, running in its native harness inside a sandbox with no image or video generation model, to deliver a finished MP4 with sound within one hour. Evaluation is hierarchical and evidence-based: program checks of the delivery spec, about 13k task-specific rubric checks traced to the requirements they enforce, and five quality dimensions anchored per task, scored by three VLM judge panels. Across nine frontier agent systems and 2,151 scored rollouts, GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 rank first and second with both the judges and human annotators, and the strongest system satisfies every core requirement on 76.3% of tasks. Delivery is a second axis: counting missing videos as zero changes the ranking, and three quarters of the lost videos are final renders that started too late, ran too slowly or never started, a failure of how agents plan their hour. Agents build their own rendering pipelines and inspect their own frames, yet how much they look does not predict quality. The judges' ranking of the systems closely matches blind human preferences.
  </details>

- **[OctWorld: Long-Range World-Consistent Video Generation with Octree-Based 3D Mapping](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03919)**  `arXiv:2609.03919`  `cs.CV`  
  _Zelong Lv, Sicheng Xu, Jianfeng Xiang, Ruicheng Wang, Yue Dong, Yu Deng, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We present OctWorld, a video diffusion framework with persistent 3D memory for generating explorable, world-consistent, and high-fidelity visual scenes. Given a single image, OctWorld performs stable autoregressive world generation along user-specified camera trajectories. We focus on long-range generation, characterized by extended camera paths and wide viewpoint coverage, where preserving spatial consistency is particularly challenging when previously generated regions are revisited. To address this problem, we introduce OctMap, an extensible and spatially adaptive 3D memory that progressively fuses generated visual observations and their corresponding depth maps into a global representation. OctMap employs TSDF fusion within a dynamic sparse octree whose spatial resolution adapts to image evidence. This design preserves geometric and appearance details across diverse scene scales while maintaining low memory overhead. Experiments demonstrate that OctWorld generates long-range, spatially consistent videos and outperforms prior methods on both existing benchmarks and challenging long-range generation settings. OctMap also provides clear advantages over point-based caches and fixed-resolution TSDF volumes. Project page:this https URL

- **[Memorize When Needed: Decoupled Memory Control for Spatially Consistent Long-Horizon Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18215)**  `arXiv:2604.18215`  `cs.CV`  
  _Yanjun Guo, Zhengqiang Zhang, Pengfei Wang, Xinyue Liang, Zhiyuan Ma, Lei Zhang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Spatially consistent long-horizon video generation aims to maintain temporal and spatial consistency along predefined camera trajectories. Existing methods mostly entangle memory modeling with video generation, leading to inconsistent content during scene revisits and diminished generative capacity when exploring novel regions, even when trained on extensive annotated data. To address these limitations, we propose a decoupled framework that separates memory conditioning from generation. Our approach significantly reduces training costs while simultaneously enhancing spatial consistency and preserving the generative capacity for novel scene exploration. Specifically, we employ a lightweight, independent memory branch to learn precise spatial consistency from historical observations. We first introduce a hybrid memory representation to capture complementary temporal and spatial cues from generated frames, then leverage a per-frame cross-attention mechanism to ensure each frame is conditioned exclusively on the most spatially relevant historical information, which is injected into the generative model to ensure spatial consistency. When generating new scenes, a camera-aware gating mechanism is proposed to mediate the interaction between memory and generation modules, enabling memory conditioning only when meaningful historical references exist. Compared with the existing method, our method is highly data-efficient, yet the experiments demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance in terms of both visual quality and spatial consistency. - **[DART: Differentiable Dynamic Adaptive Region Tokenizer for Vision Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10390)**  `arXiv:2506.10390`  `cs.CV`  
  _Shicheng Yin, Kaixuan Yin, Yang Liu, Weixing Chen, Liang Lin_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  The content-agnostic, fixed-grid tokenizers used by standard large-scale vision models like Vision Transformer (ViT) and Vision Mamba (Vim) represent a fundamental performance bottleneck, creating a trade-off between capturing fine-grained detail and suffering from redundant computation. To resolve this dilemma, we introduce DART, a fully differentiable Dynamic Adaptive Region Tokenizer. DART employs learnable region scores and quantile-based partitioning to create content-aware patches of varying sizes, intelligently allocating a higher token density to information-rich regions. The impact of this approach is profound: it unlocks a more intelligent scaling paradigm, where a DART-equipped DeiT-Small (22M parameters) matches the performance of a DeiT-Base (86M) with nearly double the inference speed by efficiently capturing high-resolution details in key regions. Furthermore, the principle of adaptive tokenization proves its generality with clear benefits in dense prediction and spatiotemporal video tasks. We argue that by resolving the tokenizer bottleneck at its source, adaptive tokenization is a key component for building the next generation of more efficient and capable foundation models for multimodal AI, robotics, and content generation.
