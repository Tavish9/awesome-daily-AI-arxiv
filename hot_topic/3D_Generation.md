- **[ReWorld: Multi-Dimensional Reward Modeling for Embodied Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12428)**  `arXiv:2601.12428`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Baorui Peng, Wenyao Zhang, Liang Xu, Bohan Li, Hu Zhu, Zekun Qi, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Current embodied video generation models primarily emphasize visual generative quality while overlooking physical fidelity, dynamic consistency, and task logic, especially for contact-rich manipulation tasks, which limits their applicability to downstream tasks. In this paper, we introduce ReWorld, a framework aimed to employ reinforcement learning to align the video generation with physical realism, task completion capability, embodiment plausibility and visual quality. Specifically, we first construct a large-scale (~235K) video preference dataset and employ it to train a hierarchical reward model designed to capture multi-dimensional reward consistent with human preferences. We further propose a practical alignment algorithm that post-trains flow-based video generators using this reward through a computationally efficient PPO-style algorithm. Comprehensive experiments and theoretical analysis demonstrate that ReWorld significantly improves the physical fidelity, logical coherence, embodiment and visual quality of generated rollouts, outperforming previous methods.

- **[RoboSnap: One-Shot Real-to-Sim Scene Generation for Generalizable Robot Learning and Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06699)**  `arXiv:2607.06699`  `cs.RO`  
  _Shujie Zhang, Jingkun Yi, Weipeng Zhong, Zirui Zhou, Yangkun Zhu, Hanqing Wang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recovering real-world scenes as interactive simulation environments can enable generalizable robot learning and reproducible policy evaluation. However, constructing scenes that are both physically stable and visually faithful remains slow and expensive. In this work, we present RoboSnap, a real-to-sim framework that turns a single RGB image into a simulation-ready scene. The key idea is a layered design that separates the physics-critical interaction area from the surrounding visual context: collision-aware foreground assets are refined for stable robot interaction, while a 3D Gaussian splatting visual layer preserves faithful background appearance under novel views. Experiments on DROID scenes and real-robot tasks show that RoboSnap achieves reliable trajectory replay in the recovered scenes, supports task-specific synthetic data generation for policy training, and yields meaningful sim-real correlation for policy evaluation. To further support real-to-sim research, we introduce DROID-Sim, a real-to-sim companion dataset constructed from 564 real-world scenes in DROID. Extensive experiments suggest that the value of real-to-sim methods lies not only in high-fidelity visual reconstruction, but in turning real environments into reusable infrastructure for robot learning and evaluation.

- **[Disen-Forcing: Disentangling Semantic Anchoring from Motion for Autoregressive Video Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02822)**  `arXiv:2611.02822`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Zhengyang Yu, Akio Hayakawa, Masato Ishii, Qingtao Yu, Xuesong Li, Takashi Shibuya, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Autoregressive video diffusion models (AR-VDMs) enable real-time, long-horizon video generation but degrade rapidly once the inference horizon outruns the training horizon, due to compounding exposure bias. To stabilize long rollouts, recent methods heuristically adopt frame sink, keeping initial frames as a persistent model context, inspired by attention sinks in LLMs. We revisit this design and find that the mechanism by which initial frames stabilize long-range consistency in AR-VDMs is not an LLM-style attention sink; instead, long-range consistency is anchored through a sparse subset of attention heads, while the majority of heads predominantly capture local temporal dynamics. This observation reveals an over-conditioning pathology in existing frame-sink strategies: by universally exposing every head to the pinned initial-frame context, the model becomes prone to collapsing motion toward the low-dynamic modes of the teacher during model distillation. We propose Disen-Forcing, a distillation-time plug-in for AR-VDMs that disentangles and reinforces this head specialization through disentangled context routing and motion-disentangling regularization. On long-horizon video generation benchmarks, Disen-Forcing simultaneously improves long-range consistency and motion dynamics of base AR-VDMs, a trade-off that sink-based methods fail to balance.
  </details>

- **[TaoMate-HD: Progressive-Resolution Autoregressive Audio-Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03394)**  `arXiv:2611.03394`  `cs.CV`  
  _Qijun Gan, Chenwei Zhang, Xinyang Li, Meiguang Jin, Junfeng Ma, Shen Qiu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Streaming digital humans produce detailed video while continuing speech as new text arrives. High-resolution denoising is costly, and text updates can cause repeated or interrupted speech. We present TaoMate-HD, an autoregressive model for high-resolution audio-video streaming. For each short segment, or chunk, two denoising steps establish coarse video structure at low resolution. A final step refines the video to high resolution, with audio generated throughout the same trajectory. Each stage passes its audiovisual predictions to the same stage of the next chunk as history. Across text updates, the model retains this history to infer what has already been spoken. Continuation training utilizes asymmetric text conditioning, with separate text windows for generation and distillation. Experiments show a 1.6x speedup over direct high-resolution generation with the same model and hardware. Optimized stage-parallel deployment provides a further 1.9x overall speedup over serial progressive generation. Our continuation training reduces character error rate by 11.18 percentage points relative to the baseline. The model also supports hour-long audiovisual generation. - **[History Forcing: Distilling History-Dependent Continuations for Long-Horizon Video World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02782)**  `arXiv:2611.02782`  `cs.CV`  
  _Hangyi Kuang, Chengzhi Wang, Yefei He, Weijie Wang, Yuke Zhao, Zhuokun Chen, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Few-step autoregressive video diffusion enables efficient generation, yet maintaining consistency over long rollouts remains challenging, especially when subjects or scenes reappear after going out of sight. Despite progress in fidelity and rollout robustness, existing distillation methods do not fully exploit richer historical information to constrain the student's current prediction. To address this gap, we introduce \textbf{History Forcing}, a unified on-policy distillation framework that strengthens historical supervision for few-step autoregressive video generators. The framework uses the residual between teacher predictions with and without informative historical context to decouple this supervision. Such a historical context can be obtained using earlier generated chunks with less accumulated drift or a fixed-view teacher rollout that keeps the scene visible while the student looks away. The residual is then weighted and added to the score or velocity prediction used for distillation, forming a corrected target. By aligning the student with this corrected target through distillation, History Forcing incorporates long-range historical constraints into autoregressive generation. Our method achieves state-of-the-art performance among 1.3B models, with a VBench quality score of 85.07 for 15s video generation and a VBench-Long aggregate score of 79.82 for 60s generation. For out-of-sight world modeling, evaluations further demonstrate more plausible scene evolution and more consistent states when the camera returns, with Evolution Quality improving from 2.35 to 2.70 on PlayWorld and Object Consistency from 58.43 to 62.18 on MemoBench.

- **[Learning-to-Memorize: Dynamic Context Management for Long-Horizon Autoregressive Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01858)**  `arXiv:2611.01858`  `cs.CV`  
  _Haowei Zhu, Qijie Wang, Jia Li, Xing Wang, Tianyu Zhao, Jiexi Wang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Autoregressive video generation has achieved impressive results on short clips, yet generating long videos remains challenging due to error accumulation over extended horizons, where each predicted frame depends on potentially imperfect previous frames. Existing approaches typically rely on static context management strategies, such as sliding-window caches that discard early frames or fixed sink frames that permanently preserve initial content. These designs either lose critical long-range context or anchor generation to outdated information, which can lead to degraded fidelity, motion stagnation, and reduced diversity. In this paper, we propose Learning to Memorize (L2M), a learned context memory management framework that dynamically retains, evicts, and evolves historical frames according to predicted importance. The framework consists of an importance prediction router that estimates the relevance of each historical frame, degradation-aware training that improves robustness to noisy or corrupted context, and a dynamic memory initialization and evolution mechanism that updates long-term memory using exponential moving average (EMA) importance scores. This allows high-quality frames to replace stale anchors and enables memory to adapt naturally to scene evolution. Trained with a teacher-student distillation objective and sparsity regularization, L2M makes efficient use of a fixed KV-cache budget while preserving the most informative contextual information. Extensive experiments demonstrate that L2M improves long-horizon video generation quality and temporal consistency over existing baselines, providing a learned strategy for context memory management in long autoregressive video generation.
  </details>

- **[RIFT: Residual-Informed Flow Transfer for One-Step Generative Video Compression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01791)**  `arXiv:2611.01791`  `cs.CV`  
  _Qizhe Wang, Jiaye Fu, Feng Ye, Haocheng Tang, Zetian Song, Chuanmin Jia, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent advances in video generation have enabled generative video compression to reconstruct details lost during coding using pretrained spatiotemporal priors. However, a substantial gap remains between codec-induced degradation and the perturbation trajectories learned by flow-matching models. In this paper, we identify a compression-dependent directional mismatch that degradation-strength-based timestep calibration alone cannot resolve. Compressed latents remain strongly aligned across codec operating points, while their reconstruction residuals vary substantially in magnitude and direction. To bridge this gap, we propose RIFT, a Residual-Informed Flow Transfer framework for one-step generative video compression. At its core, RIFT introduces a Residual Geometry Inducer (RGI) that converts the pretrained flow prediction into a content-adaptive restoration update using decoder-visible codec cues. RGI integrates flow-timestep conditioning with a learned full-band residual-strength estimate and content-conditioned direction and gain modulation, aligning the orientation and magnitude of the update with the compression-dependent residual geometry. Extensive experiments on three public video compression benchmarks show that RIFT outperforms competing generative video compression methods. The code is publicly available on RIFT.

- **[IMPACT: Image-Conditioned Motion with Physics-Aware Collision and Trajectory Guidance for Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01372)**  `arXiv:2611.01372`  `cs.CV`  
  _Shiwei Yang, Yunlong Yu, Zhao Wang, Jungong Han_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Visually realistic video generation does not guarantee physically consistent motion. Generated objects may exhibit implausible collision responses or rebounds without energy dissipation, while even physically valid motion predictions may not be faithfully realized during synthesis. We propose \textbf{IMPACT}, a physics-state-driven image-to-video framework that addresses these challenges by coupling explicit dynamics prediction with complementary sparse--dense physical guidance. A learnably calibrated Physics State Generator unifies continuous free-flight dynamics with discrete collision responses, linking impact timing, post-impact velocity, rebound attenuation, and eventual settling within a coherent physical-state sequence. To translate this evolution into effective generative constraints, IMPACT derives sparse trajectories and dense region-level motion priors from the same physical rollout. Sparse trajectories constrain global object displacement, while dense priors guide diffusion sampling to encourage coherent object-region motion and suppress unwanted background changes. Their shared physical basis coordinates global and local motion guidance to reduce prediction--generation mismatch. Experiments on 100 controlled sphere--plane collision scenes spanning different material combinations and visual environments demonstrate improved physical plausibility over representative general-purpose, physics-grounded, and motion-controllable baselines. - **[What Helps Denoising Can Hurt Memory: Rethinking History in Long Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00937)**  `arXiv:2611.00937`  `cs.CV`  
  _Yubin Jing, Ziyang Zhang, Meng Chen, Yuyao Li, Haofan Wang, Yichen Gong_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Autoregressive video generators produce long videos block by block, using historical context to maintain temporal consistency. After denoising, a committing pass encodes each clean block into keys and values (KV) that later blocks read, and the same clean block can be encoded into different KV depending on the history it reads. We therefore study historical context not only as a condition for denoising but also as an input to committing. Older historical context that supports denoising can harm subsequent generation when read during committing. At fixed context size and temporal positions, older historical context lowers mean clip-to-clip Subject Consistency. Across 5 generator configurations supporting longer contexts, consistency rises, plateaus, and then declines as committing context expands. Motivated by these findings, we propose Commit Forcing, a training-free rule that organizes committing into a chain. During committing, each block reads only the preceding block's stored KV, so older historical context reaches new entries only through the chain. We apply this rule to 6 pretrained generators. On 60-second VBench, Commit Forcing improves Quality and Total on all 6 generators, by up to 1.27 Quality points and 1.10 Total points. Long-range consistency gains extend to 240-second videos. In a blinded comparison on 120-second LongLive videos, Commit Forcing achieves win rates of 83.5% for subject and scene consistency and 82.9% for overall preference against the base generator, excluding ties.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent advances in video generation have achieved impressive visual quality, yet they often fail to produce physically consistent dynamics due to the lack of explicit modeling of underlying physical processes. Existing approaches either rely on simulators with strong assumptions or use coarse semantic guidance, and further struggle to effectively inject physical priors into generation models. In this work, we propose Seeing the Unseen, a unified framework for physics-grounded video generation. Our approach decomposes the problem into two stages: learning physical dynamics and injecting them into video synthesis. First, we introduce a lightweight Particle-based Graph Dynamics Simulator (PGDS) that learns generalizable physical interactions from data and predicts plausible 3D motion trajectories. This representation enables more complete and structured motion guidance compared to conventional physical signals. Extensive experiments demonstrate improved motion accuracy, interaction consistency, and reduced physical artifacts compared to strong baselines.

- **[iCATS: Fast Video Generation via Interaction-Aware Sparse Attention and Timestep-Adaptive Sparsity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11302)**  `arXiv:2610.11302`  `cs.CV`  
  _Chengfeng Han, Ang Wang, Baole Ai, Xianlu Bian, Jie Yao, Zilong Huang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Training-free sparse attention offers a practical acceleration solution to Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) via reducing computations without fine-tuning. It typically involves estimating the importance of query-key regions and deriving sparse masks to compute only the important candidates, which inevitably introduces approximation errors that may degrade generation quality. To better balance the efficiency-quality trade-off, we propose iCATS, integrating improved importance estimation and sparse mask construction with an efficient hardware execution strategy. Specifically, for importance estimation, unlike previous works that perform independent clustering over query and key tokens based on feature similarity to estimate attention scores, iCATS demonstrates that clustering based on query-key dot-product interactions is more accurate and further reformulates this objective as a simple quadratic form for low-cost computation. For sparse mask construction, instead of using a fixed top-p rule, we observe that tolerance to sparse approximation errors varies across denoising timesteps and therefore introduce an SNR-guided sparsity schedule to adjust sparsity dynamically, leading to higher accuracy. Finally, for hardware execution, we devise a tail-merging strategy to reduce padding overhead caused by irregular cluster sizes, improving GPU kernel utilization. Extensive experiments show that iCATS achieves $2.03\times$ acceleration with 31.017 dB PSNR on HunyuanVideo-T2V-13B and $1.55\times$ acceleration with 29.301 dB PSNR on Wan2.1-T2V-14B, delivering a state-of-the-art efficiency-quality trade-off.
