- **[Support2Sim: Visually Faithful Real2Sim with Stable Object Supports](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32727)**  `arXiv:2611.32727`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Samarth Chopra, Ankit Prabhu, Akshayan Nagarajah, Sumer Murthy, Pratik Chaudhari, Vijay Kumar_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Reconstructing a scene for physics simulation requires recovering its spatial layout and the support relationships that govern its behavior. We present Support2Sim, a Real2Sim system that reconstructs simulation-ready scenes from a single RGB image. Our design objective is to maximize source fidelity, support realization, and stability within a practical time budget for scene generation. Support2Sim infers a support graph, which a contact-guided pose initializer uses to propose poses for objects with multiple supports. The initializer generates and ranks candidates by geometric fit and source-image agreement. A coarse-to-fine refiner allocates a fixed search budget across supported objects and evaluates pose updates with physics rollouts. After an accepted update, it searches from the new pose at a finer translation and rotation scale. We benchmark our method on 50 scenes spanning four datasets. Compared with the strongest baseline, Support2Sim improves source-mask IoU after 10 seconds by 21.4 pp, realizes 87.1% versus 73.2% of intended supports, and reduces mean object drift from 79.6 to 45.3 mm.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>

- **[Contact Geometry for Generative Models: An Unbalanced Optimal Transport Formulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32547)**  `arXiv:2611.32547`  `cs.LG`  
  _Andrea Testa, Søren Hauberg, Andras Gabor Kupcsik, Tamim Asfour, Leonel Rozo_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Real-world generative problems in biology, medical imaging, or robotics, rarely come with perfectly paired source and target distributions. While unbalanced Optimal Transport (uOT) provides a robust framework to bridge unpaired datasets via minimum-length paths in probability space, existing formulations based on the Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao (WFR) geometry introduce a structural bias toward high density regions, often triggering mode collapse. We introduce Contact unbalanced Optimal Transport (CuOT), a novel uOT formulation grounded in contact geometry that decouples density transport direction from mass growth to eliminate structural bias. This allows CuOT to adapt sampling steps to the local data structure, naturally reducing step sizes in high-density regions to prevent overshooting and ensuring stable convergence. The result is a stable, scalable path-length minimization solver that consistently outperforms WFR-based methods on biological dynamics reconstruction, unpaired image-to-image translation, and video generation.

- **[PhysMend: Verification-Guided Program Refinement for Physics-Aware Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32821)**  `arXiv:2611.32821`  `cs.CV`  
  _Meilong Xu, Ziyi Zhao, Bhagyashree Puranik, Shayda Moezzi, Yixiao Zhang, Kushan Thakkar_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video generative models synthesize realistic scenes, yet their interactions often violate physics. A ball dropped onto sand should displace grains and settle into a crater, whereas a generated video may show it passing through the surface or leaving the sand flat. Post-hoc methods typically score generated videos with a vision-language model (VLM), but such scores offer limited guidance on which object or parameter to modify, and each correction costs another synthesis. Simulation-based pipelines instead run fixed stages before synthesis, so simulation errors reach the video. To address these limitations, we present PhysMend, a training-free framework for verification-guided program refinement. PhysMend describes the target event as an editable physical program of grounded objects, events, and required relations, paired with tunable execution parameters. Our key insight is to verify and repair the event on its simulated rollout and on the depth video that conditions the generator (depth control) rather than on generated videos, enabling quantitative violation measurement, targeted editing, and fast re-verification before synthesis. A hybrid verifier evaluates semantic event consistency, physical plausibility, solver stability, and visibility in the depth control. VLM judgments are confined to event semantics and cannot override failed physical measurements, which limits the impact of VLM hallucinations. Each failed check localizes the program fields eligible for repair, and its measured violation scales a bounded update of their parameters. Only accepted depth control reaches the generator, which runs once per input. Experiments on the commonly used benchmarks Physics-IQ, PhyGenBench, and VBench-2.0 demonstrate substantial gains across different generator backbones; for example, PhysMend raises the overall Physics-IQ score of Wan-VACE from 27.33 to 39.40.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Self-attention over tens of thousands of tokens dominates the generation time of video diffusion transformers, because its cost grows quadratically with sequence length. The FP4 matrix products of modern GPUs can speed up attention, but the 4-bit rounding of the queries Q and the keys K introduces large errors. We show that the exponential in softmax amplifies the error of their product QK, which therefore dominates the attention error. We propose DeCo-Attention, a co-design of algorithm and system for low-precision attention.
