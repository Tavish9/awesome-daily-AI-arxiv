- **[3D Generation for Embodied AI and Robotic Simulation: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.26509)**  `arXiv:2604.26509`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Tianwei Ye, Yifan Mao, Minwen Liao, Jian Liu, Chunchao Guo, Dazhao Du, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Embodied AI and robotic systems increasingly depend on scalable, diverse, and physically grounded 3D content for simulation-based training and real-world deployment. While 3D generative modeling has advanced rapidly, embodied applications impose requirements far beyond visual realism: generated objects must carry kinematic structure and material properties, scenes must support interaction and task execution, and the resulting content must bridge the gap between simulation and reality. This survey reviews 3D generation for embodied AI and organizes the literature around three roles that 3D generation plays in embodied systems. In Data Generator, 3D generation produces simulation-ready objects and assets, including articulated, physically grounded, and deformable content for downstream interaction; in Simulation Environments, it constructs interactive and task-oriented worlds, spanning structure-aware, controllable, and agentic scene generation; and in Sim2Real Bridge, it supports digital twin reconstruction, data augmentation, and synthetic demonstrations for downstream robot learning and real-world transfer.   _Yunye Gong, Indranil Sur, Jared Claypoole, Pritish Sahu, Ajay Divakaran_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Powerful generative models including large language and vision models have demonstrated success across a wide range of content generation, editing and manipulation tasks via end-to-end prompt-based generation. However, current assessment of the quality of generative content manipulation remains subjective, limiting scientific understanding of model capabilities. Inspired by rate-distortion theory from Shannon information compression theory, in this work we formalize hierarchical content summarization as an information encoding problem. - **[Online Temporal Rotations for 2 Bit KV-Cache Quantization in Video Diffusion Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26334)**  `arXiv:2611.26334`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Ryan Lucas, Petros Prastakos, Rahul Mazumder_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video diffusion transformers reuse key--value (KV) caches across denoising steps, which quickly becomes a bottleneck for long video generation. We find that quantizing temporally distant cache vectors leads to a large range and high quantization error, but that nearby video tokens in the KV-cache share a persistent pattern per channel. These patterns also vary across videos, motivating rotations that are adapted to each video's unique cache. We propose Temporal Rotation Quantization (TRQ), a method that rotates frames in the cache that are adjacent in time before quantization. For each block of temporally adjacent cache vectors, TRQ fits an orthogonal transform that maps the block's per-channel means to a vector that is close to a constant across channels. To keep the quantization format lightweight, we parameterize this rotation as a tree of $2 \times 2$ planar rotations, each stored with only two bits, followed by a fixed Hadamard transform shared across all blocks. The rotations are fit without gradients or changes to model weights, and we prove that a simple greedy fit is globally optimal for our 'mean-flattening' objective. The transforms are fit online for each new video, and we release fused Triton kernels to make this process efficient at inference. At 2-bit precision and matched compression, TRQ improves VBench subject consistency over the state-of-the-art method QVG from 90.38 to 92.02 on LongCat-Video-13B and from 86.33 to 87.61 on HY-WorldPlay-8B, as well as improving other metrics.

- **[Posterior Matching Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26629)**  `arXiv:2611.26629`  `cs.CV`  
  _Hidir Yesiltepe, Julius Berner, Arash Vahdat, Pinar Yanardag_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion distillation accelerates image and video generation by compressing a pretrained teacher's iterative sampling process into a few network evaluations. Distribution-based methods achieve this by matching the student's output distribution to the teacher's. This matching is often driven by reverse-KL objectives, whose mode-seeking bias favors dominant teacher modes at the expense of diversity across samples. We introduce Posterior Matching Distillation (PMD), a new member of the distribution-based distillation family that combines mode-covering posterior sampling with any-step generation. At zero population loss, PMD recovers the teacher's full posterior of clean images at each trained noise level. Its stochastic student supports any-step sampling budgets within the trained timestep grid without further training. To learn these posteriors, PMD matches exponentially reweighted averages of student samples conditioned on a fixed noisy state to corresponding teacher posterior means. Under Gaussian corruption, exponentially tilted posterior mean is a single teacher evaluation at a shifted input. We prove that, matching tilted means over an open neighborhood uniquely identifies the full posterior. PMD thus supervises the entire conditional distribution through a family of mean constraints, without any auxiliary student-score network. Experiments on SDXL and FLUX.2 [klein] 4B demonstrate that PMD achieves the best FID among 4-step methods on both models, while maintaining competitive or leading performance across prompt-alignment and perceptual-quality metrics.
  </details>

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Reference-to-video diffusion transformers pack the variational autoencoder (VAE) latents of all references and the generated tokens into one full-attention sequence, in which reference tokens make up about half. Because attention cost grows quadratically with sequence length, these tokens take up an even larger share of the attention computation, yet most of them are static, smooth or irrelevant to the output. To accelerate reference-conditioned generation, we propose pruning and caching of reference tokens for video generation (PCRG), a training-free, plug-and-play method that uses two signals the model already computes to assign each reference token to one of three tiers, namely pruned, cached or kept. Pruned tokens leave the sequence before the first step with all rotary position embedding positions unchanged. Cached tokens have their keys and values computed once and reused, and kept tokens are processed as usual. PCRG shortens the sequence rather than changing how attention is computed or which steps are run, so it also stacks on step caching and sparse attention.
