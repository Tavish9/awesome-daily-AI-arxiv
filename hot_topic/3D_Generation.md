  _Harold Haodong Chen, Sirui Chen, Yingjie Xu, Wenhang Ge, Ying-Cong Chen_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  The scalability of robotic manipulation is fundamentally bottlenecked by the scarcity of task-aligned physical interaction data.   _Jialei Chen, Kai Wang, Kang Chen, Shuaihang Chen, Feng Gao, Wenhao Tang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) leverage large-scale vision-language pretraining for semantic robot control, but often lack explicit foresight into how robot actions change the scene. World-Action Models (WAMs) address this limitation by conditioning policies on predicted futures, yet existing approaches typically rely on computationally expensive video generation with substantial pixel-level redundancy. We present LaWAM, a Latent World Action Model that exposes predictive dynamics to robot policies through compact latent visual subgoals instead of reconstructed future video. At the core of LaWAM is a latent-action-conditioned Latent World Model (LaWM). LaWAM then conditions action generation on these predicted latent visual subgoals to enable dynamics-aware robot control. LaWAM achieves state-of-the-art or competitive success rates (SRs) across LIBERO (98.6% SR), RoboTwin (91.22% SR), and real-world manipulation tasks while retaining low-latency inference. LaWAM runs in 187 ms per action-chunk prediction and achieves up to 24x lower wall-clock latency than pixel-space WAMs.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>

We observe, however, that DMD fundamentally relies on an output-space objective, lacking explicit regularization on the model's internal representations.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Camera-controlled video generation has attracted increasing interest for its applications in simulation, gaming, and world modeling. However, current models often deform rigid objects and scene structure as the viewpoint changes. We investigate whether this failure is related to a measurable internal property: the view consistency of diffusion representations. Across a diverse collection of camera-controlled image-to-video diffusion models, we find that models exhibiting more view-consistent representations generate videos with superior 3D consistency. We show that the ViCoDR loss improves output 3D consistency more reliably than the alternatives and remains effective across design variants, while exhibiting camera-control trade-offs and requiring additional training computation.
