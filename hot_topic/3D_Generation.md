- **[What Remains in Sight? Autoregressive Video Decoding as Representation-Guided Context Rewriting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04164)**  `arXiv:2611.04164`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI` `cs.LG`  
  _Lixuan He, Jihyeon Je, Yang You, Leonidas Guibas_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Long-horizon video generation is increasingly moving toward autoregressive rollout, where each newly generated segment becomes part of the evidence used for future prediction. Recent causal decoders, long-context training, and KV-cache mechanisms have extended feasible duration, but most inference pipelines still decide the visible past implicitly through recent windows, compressed caches, or external memory. This raises a direct question: \textbf{can the choice of which past frames or segments to show to the decoder be treated as an explicit test-time decision?} We answer this question with \textsc{ReCR}, a training-free method that formulates autoregressive video decoding as representation-guided context rewriting. At each step, \textsc{ReCR} builds a selected visible context from the generated history by using internal decoder representations to favor context units that match the global rollout state, avoid redundancy with the recent continuation, preserve temporal boundary coverage, and widen the range of spatial layouts in view. The selected units are then mapped onto a compact causal axis before decoding the next block. Across long-horizon text-to-video benchmarks, \textsc{ReCR} consistently improves multiple autoregressive backbones under matched visible-token budgets. Empirical analyses such as representation-space diagnostics and transition-count prompt switching further show that improving what remains visible is an effective path toward more stable long-video decoding.
  </details>

- **[FloMo: 3D Scene Flow as a World Action Model Intermediate for Learning from Human Video](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05062)**  `arXiv:2611.05062`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Jeremy Collins, Namra Patel, Ayush Agarwal, Shitij Govil, Animesh Garg_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Pretrained video generation models distill internet-scale motion priors into robot policies, but full video is a redundant signal for action prediction. We argue that dense 3D motion is the medium through which video pretraining best transfers to robot actions, and that predicting motion rather than video affords better sample-efficiency for action prediction. FloMo is a 5B video-generation model fine-tuned to predict 3D scene-flow and action tokens. Rendering scene flow as RGB and encoding it into the same latent space as video enables a pretrained video generation backbone to efficiently adapt to predict 3D scene flow. Co-trained on action-free human video and action-labeled robot teleoperation data, our model benefits from human-video pretraining and generalizes to unseen objects, motion primitives, and tasks. Replacing motion prediction with a video prediction objective degrades out-of-distribution generalization, while adding a video prediction target alongside motion lowers policy success rather than improving it. When motion, video, and action are predicted jointly, action tokens attend far more to motion than to video, suggesting that predicting video wastes model capacity. Together, these results indicate that motion effectively mediates the transfer from video pretraining to action. More information can be found atthis http URL.

- **[Route Anything: A Study of Model and Policy Routing for General-Purpose AI Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05275)**  `arXiv:2611.05275`  `cs.CV`  
  _Pengfei Zhou, Yixing Ma, Hexin Wang, Weidong Tang, Zhenglin Wan, Wangbo Zhao, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Artificial general intelligence (AGI) requires a method that can understand, generate, reason, and act across diverse domains and modalities. Rather than relying on a single model to master every capability, a promising path toward AGI now is to orchestrate a heterogeneous ecosystem of specialized models, tools, and policies (candidates) into a unified AI system. At the heart of this vision lies a fundamental question: how can an AI system dynamically select and coordinate the most capable candidate for each task while balancing performance and computational cost? In this work, we present Route Anything, a unified framework for studying model and policy routing across general-purpose AI systems. We develop a unified theoretical foundation that characterizes optimal routing under quality--cost trade-offs and establishes how the information available to a router determines its attainable performance. To systematically investigate routing across diverse capabilities, we first construct an omni-modal trajectory matrix spanning 25 benchmarks across language understanding, image, video, and speech generation, coding and workplace agents, and robotic manipulation, comprising 374,401 scored runs from 452 candidate configurations. Building on this framework, we train and test different routers to investigate the potential of omni-modal model routing, the capabilities of learned routers, and the conditions under which routing improves efficiency. Our results reveal substantial opportunities for capability orchestration: per-input oracle routing outperforms the best fixed candidate by 5.4 points, and learned routers outperform it by 0.3 points across 16 benchmarks, by 2.1 points on video generation, and by 1.1 points on robot manipulation while using 24% less policy time. More importantly, routing raises efficiency by 8.82x on average compared with the reference model.

- **[Behind Video Generation: Observations and Insights on Multimodal Understanding from Kling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04916)**  `arXiv:2611.04916`  `cs.CV`  
  _Kling Team, Dong Chen, Jialu Chen, Xinlong Chen, Yifan Dai, Haotian Dong, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video generation has progressed from isolated clips to coherent short-form audiovisual sequences, and the bottleneck is shifting from rendering to semantic control. This report summarizes the observations and insights on multimodal understanding gained from successive generations of Kling. We argue that generation quality is the projection of multimodal understanding through the entire data--conditioning--alignment--evaluation chain, and we examine this chain module by module. Kling-MLLM, a shared multimodal foundation model that unifies image, video, audio, synchronized audio--video, and interleaved inputs, is specialized for each downstream module. Because a DiT is trained on far fewer training samples than a language model, we treat captioning as a semantic contract that maximizes description density, and extend it to reference-grounded captioning that makes reference bindings explicit. Prompt enhancement translates user intent into this caption distribution: basic PE is derived from captions of compelling videos and refined with text-level and generator-in-the-loop reinforcement learning, while agentic PE refines the story and retrieves compelling examples. We show that a reliable text encoder comes from perceptual training rather than from stronger language ability, and that candidates can be screened from text alone before end-to-end training. Reward modeling pairs a general reward model for explainable, localized critiques with a decision reward model for fast multidimensional scoring, treating perceptual bandwidth as a first-class design parameter. Finally, we review task-specific automatic evaluation, focusing on protocols that expose scoring shortcuts and help trace failures to their upstream source. We hope these lessons offer practical guidance for building the understanding stack behind controllable video generation.

- **[Pixel-space video generation with U-JiT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04764)**  `arXiv:2611.04764`  `cs.CV`  
  _Théophane Vallaeys, Jakob Verbeek, Matthieu Cord_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Latent diffusion and flow matching models are ubiquitous in state-of-the-art generative video models. While highly effective, relying on pretrained autoencoders to compress the data into a lower-dimensional latent space also comes with drawbacks. First, training proceeds in two stages: autoencoder pretraining, with compression and reconstruction objectives, followed by latent generative model training, precluding fully end-to-end training. Second, autoencoders also lead to an inherent loss of signal, and the trade-off between reconstruction quality and generative performance in the latent space is still an active area of research. In this paper we explore the viability of generative video models directly in pixel space. We analyze recent approaches in pixel-space generative modeling of images that rely on basic transformer designs, and identify several shortcomings that limit their effectiveness for the high-dimensional pixel-space video generation. To overcome these limitations, we propose a UNet-like transformer architecture along with improvements to the training recipe and sampling strategy. We conduct controlled experiments, training latent and pixel-space models from scratch on Kinetics-600. For the standard frames-to-video task at 128 resolution, our U-JiT model improves generative FVD by 27% over prior state-of-the-art results, and by 14% against the JiT pixel-space baseline. Scaling to higher 256 resolution, we find the improvement over pixel-space and latent-based models grows as we increase the spatiotemporal compression rate up to $64 \times 64 \times 8$. Finally, we find that U-JiT improves per-frame FID by 27% over latent models and 61% over pixel-space models on class-to-video generation, with respectively 9% and 56% reduction in FVD.

- **[CounterSketch: Physics-Grounded Counterfactual Reasoning for Sketch-to-Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04244)**  `arXiv:2611.04244`  `cs.CV`  
  _Hien Trinh, Tan-Hiep To, Dinh-Khoi Vo, Tam V. Nguyen, Minh-Triet Tran, Trung-Nghia Le_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Counterfactual sketch animation explores how a static sketch evolves under a hypothetical intervention. We present CounterSketch, a physics-grounded framework that transforms natural-language ``What if...?'' queries into physically plausible sketch animations. The framework integrates VLM-guided scene grounding, causal event-chain reasoning, constraint-based trajectory generation, and verification. It models physical transitions to update the scene graph, computes continuous object trajectories, and validates them against physical, geometric, and semantic constraints. The verified trajectories are then projected into object-level motion bounding boxes to guide downstream sketch-video synthesis. Experiments on CounterSketchBench (80 questions) demonstrate that physics-grounded trajectories provide stronger motion guidance than direct VLM predictions.

- **[Compression Forcing: Hierarchical History Tokenization Enables Flexible World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04028)**  `arXiv:2611.04028`  `cs.CV`  
  _Siqiao Huang, Partha Kaushik, Michael Chen, Hengkai Pan, Kaiwen Geng, Omar Chehab, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Effective world modeling requires compressing salient history information that facilitates future prediction. However, most existing approaches inherit tokenization and conditioning mechanisms from video generation, where objectives are centered around perceptual realism rather than predictive utility. This discrepancy limits the world model's effectiveness, especially in environments dominated by visual nuisances or task-irrelevant background details. To this end, we introduce Compression Forcing (CF), a general framework that aligns history compression with the predictive objective of world modeling. Compression Forcing features Dynamics-Aware Tokenization (DAT), a novel tokenization method that compresses history observations into a sequence ordered by decreasing dynamics saliency. Built on a diffusion backbone, this structure enables Hierarchical History Guidance (HG-H), allowing selective amplification of dynamics-salient information during inference. Extensive experiments across various environments demonstrate improved prediction fidelity, physical realism, and downstream decision-making performance, establishing Compression Forcing as a highly flexible and effective approach for world modeling.

- **[SlotMemory: Object-Centric KV Memory for Streaming Long-Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31033)**  `arXiv:2605.31033`  `cs.CV`  
  _Weijia Dou, Hui Li, Jiahao Cui, Lei Zhou, Jingdong Wang, Siyu Zhu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Streaming video generation models typically rely on temporal-centric memory, organizing historical context as latent queues, compressed context tokens, or unclustered token sets. This often causes identity drift and semantic inconsistency when entities exit the frame or during interactive prompt transitions. To address these limitations, we propose SlotMemory, an object-centric Key-Value (KV) memory mechanism for streaming video diffusion. Our approach shifts memory abstraction from ``when'' an event occurred to ``what'' is represented by decomposing the transformer's KV manifold into discrete, reusable semantic slots. Because the slots serve only as routing addresses and the stored content remains the original transformer KV tokens, the mechanism adds entity-level persistence and prompt-aware retrieval across long horizons. Evaluated on 60-second interactive narratives using the Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B backbone, SlotMemory achieves the highest Dynamic Degree among evaluated streaming methods and the second-highest aggregate Quality Score, preserving prompt adherence rather than trading it away for consistency. At the 30-second horizon, SlotMemory leads in Total, Quality, and Dynamic Degree, improving Dynamic Degree by 31.2% over MemFlow, the strongest memory-based baseline. Our results support treating structured semantic representation, rather than raw temporal capacity, as a critical primitive for persistent long-form video synthesis.
  </details>

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  High-fidelity surgical video generation holds great promise for medical training and AI development, yet precise, controllable editing of such videos remains a formidable challenge. Modifying surgical attributes such as instrument, tissue interactions or procedural phases is particularly difficult due to the strict anatomical and temporal constraints of ophthalmic procedures. We introduce OphEdit, the first training-free framework for text-guided editing of ophthalmic surgical videos. Our approach uses a deterministic second-order ODE inversion pipeline to capture attention Value tensors from the source video and selectively injects them into the conditional branch of classifier-free guidance during denoising, preserving the intricate anatomical geometry of the eye while mapping text-driven semantic modifications onto the video. Clinical evaluation by an ophthalmologist shows that OphEdit handles complex transformations, such as instrument swaps and procedural variations, with superior structural fidelity and temporal consistency compared to adapted general-domain video editors, offering a scalable route to diverse surgical training data. - **[HumanScore: Benchmarking Human Motions in Generated Videos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20157)**  `arXiv:2604.20157`  `cs.CV`  
  _Tiange Xiang, Yusu Fang, Tian Tan, Narayan Schuetz, Scott Delp, Li Fei-Fei, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent advances in model architectures, compute, and data scale have driven rapid progress in video generation, producing increasingly realistic content. Yet, no prior method systematically measures how faithfully these systems render human bodies and motion dynamics. In this paper, we present HumanScore, a systematic framework to evaluate the quality of human motions in AI-generated videos. HumanScore defines six interpretable metrics spanning kinematic plausibility, temporal stability, and biomechanical consistency, enabling fine-grained diagnosis beyond visual realism alone. Through carefully designed prompts, we elicit a diverse set of movements at varying intensities and evaluate videos generated by thirteen state-of-the-art models. Our analysis reveals consistent gaps between perceptual plausibility and motion biomechanical fidelity, identifies recurrent failure modes (e.g., temporal jitter, anatomically implausible poses, and motion drift), and produces robust model rankings from quantitative and physically meaningful criteria.

- **[Compact Attention: Exploiting Structured Spatio-Temporal Sparsity for Fast Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12969)**  `arXiv:2508.12969`  `cs.CV`  
  _Qirui Li, Guangcong Zheng, Qi Zhao, Jie Li, Bin Dong, Yiwu Yao, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  The quadratic computational complexity of self-attention mechanisms imposes a critical constraint on transformer-based video generation, particularly for synthesizing ultra-long and high-definition sequences. While most sparse attention techniques employ fixed or heuristic patterns, they often struggle to accommodate the heterogeneous and layer-specific redundancies inherent in video diffusion transformers (VDiTs). We find that different attention heads consistently concentrate most of their attention mass in different coarse query-key regions, a behavior we call functional specialization. Although individual attention values vary with the generated content, these regions remain similar across prompts and random seeds. Leveraging these insights, we introduce Compact Attention, a hardware-aware sparse attention framework for efficient long-video synthesis. We quantify this stability and use it to construct searchable, reusable, head-specific masks. Based on the observation, we propose a deformable dual-window sparse pattern mechanism that flexibly approximates local and axial attention regions within one tile-aligned mask family. To avoid manually choosing a mask for each head, we develop a recall-driven offline auto-search algorithm that identifies high-quality sparse masks while leveraging temporal stability for mask reuse. Extensive evaluations on Wan2.1 (14B) and Hunyuan-Video demonstrate that Compact Attention achieves 2.25x/3.03x attention speedups over FlashAttention-2 and 1.20x/1.74x over FlashAttention-3, respectively, while maintaining visual quality and temporal consistency comparable to full-attention baselines.
