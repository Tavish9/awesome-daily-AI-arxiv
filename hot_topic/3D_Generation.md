- **[PDRVLA: A Vision-Language-Action Model with Physical Dynamics Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37381)**  `arXiv:2610.37381`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Hanqing Wang, Zhaotian Cai, Ruichuan An, Bohan Zeng, Tao Zhang, Yilin Chen, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision--Language--Action (VLA) models generate robot actions from visual observations and language instructions, but their understanding of physical dynamics remains limited. Video world models can capture scene dynamics, yet generating future videos introduces high latency into the control loop. We introduce PDRVLA, whose lightweight Physical Dynamics Module extracts physical tokens from vision--language features to condition action generation without online video generation. The module learns through direct prediction of future RGB, depth, and optical-flow features, with auxiliary supervision through a frozen pretrained video world model. Training relies entirely on publicly available data, expanded through human-to-robot synthesis, subtask annotation, and embodied chain-of-thought (ECoT) annotation. Across four simulation benchmarks, PDRVLA achieves success rates of 99.3% on LIBERO, 89.9% on LIBERO-Plus, 93.6% on RoboTwin~2.0, and 81.5% on SimplerEnv. Ablations show improved future prediction with world-model supervision and evaluate how subtask conditioning, future object-pose supervision, and ECoT training affect action success.
  </details>

- **[Adaptive-WAM: Quality-Guided Early-Exit Planning from Intermediate Video-Diffusion Features](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06008)**  `arXiv:2608.06008`  `cs.RO`  
  _Sining Ang, Yuguang Yang, Yan Wang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Large video diffusion models provide rich spatiotemporal priors for autonomous driving, but existing world-action models often inherit the cost of iterative future-video generation even though deployment only requires an ego trajectory. We ask a more basic question: how much of a video diffusion model must be executed to make a reliable driving decision? Through a controlled study of video denoising timesteps and Diffusion Transformer (DiT) depth, we find that planning performance is largely insensitive to the tested video-noise levels, whereas strong trajectories can already be decoded from intermediate layers. Based on this observation, we introduce Adaptive-WAM, a quality-aware multi-exit planner built on a Wan2.2-5B backbone. Trajectory diffusion heads are attached to selected DiT blocks, and a lightweight trajectory-quality scorer terminates inference once the best trajectory decoded so far satisfies a quality threshold; otherwise, computation continues from the cached hidden state to a deeper exit. The deployed planner therefore avoids the iterative classifier-free denoising loop and VAE decoding required for future-video synthesis, while dynamically allocating backbone depth according to trajectory quality. On NAVSIM, the adaptive single-trajectory planner achieves 90.8 PDMS; a separate fixed-exit variant reaches 92.6 PDMS with 64 proposals. It further obtains 89.9 EPDMS on NAVSIM v2, yielding the best reported results among the compared front-view video world-model planners. Without target-domain fine-tuning, Adaptive-WAM transfers to nuScenes with 0.88 m average L2 error and a 0.08\% collision rate. On an A100, adaptive routing improves PDMS from 90.62 to 90.79 while averaging 170 ms end-to-end planning latency, approximately 10\% below the 190 ms fixed block-15 planner and 47\% below the 320 ms fixed full-depth planner. Code will be released.

- **[4-Tensor Attention Model for Semantic Physical Reality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11716)**  `arXiv:2610.11716`  `cs.LG` `cs.CL`  
  _Jongwook Kim, Sangheon Yun_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We describe a 4-tensor attention model that predicts the next semantic state of a scene, for video generation and robot planning. A window of states has positions (x, t) and two fibers, a semantic fiber and a temporal-context fiber, and one softmax normalizes attention jointly over the window. Frames and an agent's situation are written as those states; the encoder, the renderer, and the planner remain outside the update. To test the update on its own, we train on ROCStories, where each window poses the same next-sentence task at the semantic layer. On the validation split, with one seed per setting, the last-sentence cross-entropy on the three matched settings is lower for the 4-tensor model than for a free-running one-dimensional transformer by 5.3% at H=2, L=2, by 2.6% at H=4, L=2, and by 2.4% at H=4, L=3. At H=4, L=2 the parameter counts are nearly the same, 172.5M and 175.9M. On the same two GPUs that 4-tensor run finished in 2.4 hours and the baseline run in 45.2 hours; the baseline is trained by free-running decoding, one sequential forward pass per target token.

- **[A Better Critic Matters for Distribution Matching Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42135)**  `arXiv:2610.42135`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Zongze Wu, Xianbang Wang, Zhengyang Geng, Mingdeng Cao, Zhoutong Zhang, Richard Zhang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) trains a few-step student using the difference between a teacher's score and an online critic's estimate of the student distribution's score. We investigate what the critic needs, in capacity and in information, to provide a reliable training signal. On the capacity side, controlled experiments across seven SiT model sizes on ImageNet show that increasing teacher size while keeping a small critic fixed can degrade student quality and cause training collapse. Scaling the critic alongside the teacher, a strategy we call joint scaling, prevents collapse in our experiments and allows the student to benefit from larger teachers. On the information side, rather than forcing the critic to model a step-agnostic mixture of student outputs, we introduce a step-aware critic (STAC) that conditions on the student's generation step to estimate step-specific scores. Together, joint scaling and STAC consistently improve few-step distillation across class-conditional ImageNet generation, text-to-image generation with SDXL and FLUX.2, and text-to-video generation with Wan2.1.
  </details>

- **[GenDSR: Transferring Representations from Video Generation Models for Dynamic Spatial Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41314)**  `arXiv:2610.41314`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Ke Yang, Zhenyu Zhang, Jun Li_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Dynamic spatial reasoning is essential for connecting visual intelligence to thephysical world, yet remains challenging for vision-language models (VLMs), which must preserve scene state while tracking how spatial relations evolve over time. Meanwhile, video generation models (VGMs), trained to synthesize temporally coherent world dynamics, offer a rich source of spatiotemporal representations. We present GenDSR, a framework that transfers intermediate representations from VGM into VLM for dynamic spatial reasoning. Rather than treating a VGM feature as a monolithic prior, GenDSR constructs two complementary branches: a raw branch preserving holistic generative context and a delta branch emphasizing temporal variation. Task-conditioned tokenwise gates then fuse both signals and inject the result into the native visual representation. On DSR-Bench, GenDSR achieves 67.2%, surpassing the previous best result and establishing a new state of the art.

- **[Autoregressive Diffusion for Compositional Text-to-Scene Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16552)**  `arXiv:2604.16552`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Zhenggang Tang, Yuehao Wang, Yuchen Fan, Jun-Kun Chen, Yu-Ying Yeh, Kihyuk Sohn, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent text-to-scene generation approaches largely reduced the manual efforts required to create 3D scenes. However, their focus is either to generate a scene layout or to generate objects, and few generate both. The generated scene layout is often simple even with LLM's help. Moreover, the generated scene is often inconsistent with the text input that contains non-trivial descriptions of the shape, appearance, and spatial arrangement of the objects. We present a new paradigm of sequential text-to-scene generation and propose a novel generative model for interactive scene creation. At the core is a 3D Autoregressive Diffusion model 3D-ARD+, which unifies the autoregressive generation over a multimodal token sequence and diffusion generation of next-object 3D latents. To generate the next object, the model uses one autoregressive step to generate the coarse-grained 3D latents in the scene space, conditioned on both the current seen text instructions and already synthesized 3D scene. It then uses a second step to generate the 3D latents in the smaller object space, which can be decoded into fine-grained object geometry and appearance. We curate a large dataset of 230K indoor scenes with paired text instructions for training. We evaluate 7B 3D-ARD+, on challenging scenes, and showcase the model can generate and place objects following non-trivial spatial layout and semantics prescribed by the text instructions.
  </details>

- **[Any Forcing: Any-Step Generality and One-Step Stability for Autoregressive Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42031)**  `arXiv:2610.42031`  `cs.CV`  
  _Jiahua Xu, Yijie Fang, Nannan Wang, Xunhao Lin, Li Cao, Lei Hao, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Autoregressive video generation enables efficient inference through streaming prediction. However, existing methods are typically restricted to fixed sampling steps, limiting the flexibility to balance generation quality and efficiency, while one-step generation suffers from degraded visual quality and error accumulation over long sequences, leading to content drift and generation collapse. To address these issues, we propose Any Forcing, a unified framework for achieving Any-Step Generality and One-Step Stability. Specifically, we introduce a unified adversarial discriminator that jointly performs real-distribution alignment, inter-step consistency alignment, and first-frame quality enhancement, enabling flexible sampling while preserving one-step generation quality. Furthermore, we propose a training-free adaptive long-term memory selection mechanism that selects historical states based on global semantic relevance and local value contribution, while remapping their temporal positions to preserve temporal relationships within the memory cache, thereby enabling stable one-step long-video generation. Experiments demonstrate that Any Forcing achieves high-quality and stable one-step autoregressive video generation, achieving 20.9 FPS and 24.7 FPS for short and long video generation on a single H100 GPU, respectively.
  </details>

- **[GeoFlip: Benchmarking Geometry-Sensitive Physics in Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42002)**  `arXiv:2610.42002`  `cs.CV`  
  _Yuqi Li, Junming Liu, Bowen Hu, Ruize Niu, Huiran Duan, Yingli Tian, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video generation models synthesize realistic motion, but can they respond correctly to changes in initial geometry? We introduce GeoFlip, a video generation benchmark centered on physical outcome flips induced by geometry. With shared physical settings and textual prompts held fixed, GeoFlip applies controlled interventions to support extent, object placement, and mechanism configuration, constructing paired scenes with contrasting reference events, such as remaining stable versus falling or tipping. These pairs test whether models translate geometric differences in the first frame into correct physical evolution. The benchmark comprises 482 scene pairs and 964 reference videos across six physical categories, with reference outcomes established through procedural scene construction, physics simulation, and numerical validation and verification of physical events. We evaluate 16 models for image to video generation along three dimensions: event correctness, structural integrity, and similarity to reference videos. We examine evaluation consistency with independent vision language judges. On the 442 conditions shared by all models, the highest event correctness averaged across tasks is 27.98%, revealing substantial limitations in physical generation conditioned on geometry. Model strengths vary across physical mechanisms, and the three evaluation dimensions capture distinct capabilities. Typical failures include ignoring geometric changes, generating incorrect object motion, bending rigid objects, and introducing additional components. Rank correlations between judges reach 0.906 for event correctness and 0.929 for structural integrity. By combining controlled geometric interventions, verifiable physical references, and multidimensional evaluation, GeoFlip assesses progress from visual realism toward physical consistency conditioned on geometry.
  </details>

- **[TADP: Dual-Branch Temporally Adaptive Differential Privacy for Expression-Aware Video Anonymization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41642)**  `arXiv:2610.41642`  `cs.CV`  
  _Feng Xu, Xun Li, Lars Petersson, Yulei Sui, David Ahmedt-Aristizabal, Dadong Wang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We present TADP, a temporally adaptive differential privacy (DP) framework for inference-time privacy-preserving video generation under fixed-length clip-replacement adjacency. TADP uses a dual-branch design to extract identity and expression representations, clips and privatizes both branches with temporally adaptive Gaussian mechanisms whose noise scales depend only on preceding privatized releases within the same clip and are bounded below by history-uniform calibrated minima, and accounts for their joint release via adaptive Rényi Differential Privacy (RDP) composition. The clip-level RDP guarantee is converted to $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-differential privacy, while soft quantization and subsequent temporal processing are applied only after privatization as DP post-processing. Experiments on facial expression datasets show reduced identity leakage under evaluated attacks, usable perceptual quality and temporal stability, and high facial expression recognition utility relative to the evaluated privacy-preserving baselines. TADP formally satisfies clip-level DP and empirically provides a favorable privacy--utility trade-off under the evaluated threat models and datasets.

- **[VoxLution: Evolving Voxels for High-Fidelity 3D Scene Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41501)**  `arXiv:2610.41501`  `cs.CV`  
  _Daoyi Gao, Xinyi Zhang, Alexey Bokhovkin, Lei Li, Angela Dai_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  High-resolution 3D indoor scene generation is crucial for immersive applications such as virtual reality, spatial computing, and robotics simulation. We introduce VoxLution, a coarse-to-fine transformer-based approach that generates high-fidelity indoor scenes with rich details, overcoming the overly smooth geometry typical of existing generative models. First, we generate a low-resolution spatial layout; then, based on this structural information, we progressively refine the scene geometry locally in chunks. Key to our approach is formulating 3D super-resolution as a sequence modeling problem over multi-resolution sparse voxel grids. To achieve this, we introduce a training-free, resolution-invariant tokenization scheme that compactly encodes the geometric information of eight octants into a single decimal number. We formulate the transformer sequence to include multiple resolutions, allowing the coarse layout to guide the generation of fine local details. To ensure coherence across local chunks, we incorporate adjacent chunk boundary information directly into the transformer sequencing. VoxLution achieves scene generation at a finer voxel resolution than existing baselines, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance on standard benchmarks with 59.6% lower FPD and 51.1% lower FID on 3D-FRONT, and 70.5% lower FPD and 73.8% lower FID on Scannet++.

- **[DyCraft: An Agentic System for Long-Horizon 4D Scene Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40754)**  `arXiv:2610.40754`  `cs.CV`  
  _Jeff Tan, Hang Dai, Zeqian Long, Yanzhe Lyu, R. Kenny Jones, Shangzhe Wu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  While frontier coding agents can generate high-quality 3D scenes and articulated assets, they struggle to create coherent long-horizon animations. When equipped with general-purpose harnesses, they frequently produce physically implausible outputs, generating objects that interpenetrate, lack physical support, or move unnaturally. Such bare agents struggle to repair these errors because they default to scripting the full animation in one pass, making it difficult to verify their work and disentangle complex spatiotemporal constraints. Drawing on the practice of human animators, we introduce DyCraft, a system for animated 3D scene generation that breaks animation into steps that coding agents can more reliably solve. DyCraft first expands the user's prompt into a detailed plan and builds a static scene. It then creates a 3D storyboard of key animation poses, where each frame is a static 3D scene that the agent can build and debug in isolation. Storyboarding decomposes the problem into pairs of verified start and end states, allowing the agent to author local motion between them with a collection of motion authoring tools. We evaluate DyCraft on 24 long-horizon, multi-shot animation tasks covering humanoid robots, human and animal characters, and physical mechanisms. Compared with a native GPT-6 agent in Codex and prior agentic baselines, DyCraft substantially reduces physical violations and generates higher-quality animated 3D scenes that are more often preferred by human raters.
  </details>

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Modern game development faces significant challenges in creativity and cost due to predetermined content in traditional game engines. Recent breakthroughs in video generation models, capable of synthesizing realistic and interactive virtual environments, present an opportunity to revolutionize game creation. We term such engines Generative Game Engines (GGE), with IGV as the perceptual and world-generation core under designer control. - **[Optimizing Stochastic Policy Geometry for Few-Step Flow Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41949)**  `arXiv:2610.41949`  `cs.AI` `cs.LG`  
  _Zhuguanyu Wu, Congjia Chen, Ruihao Gong, Yang Yong, Shiqiao Gu, Yi Xu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Few-step flow models generate samples through a handful of large denoising transitions. Applying GRPO to such models requires introducing stochasticity into the denoising transitions, and when a single transition spans a wide range of noise levels, the choice of the resulting stochastic policy becomes a major factor in optimization. Recent constructions constrain the policy to preserve the target flow coefficients, but this constraint admits a family of policies and offers no criterion for choosing among them. We introduce Geo-FSRL (Geometry-Optimized Few-Step Reinforcement Learning), built on a selection principle: at a fixed conditioning state and timestep pair, choose the admissible policy that minimizes the conditional KL divergence induced by any given nonzero velocity-output update. Within a coefficient-preserving family of isotropic Gaussian transitions, this criterion reduces to minimizing a single scalar geometry factor that determines the conditional KL for every velocity-output update. The same factor governs velocity-space Fisher information and conditional transition log-likelihood-ratio variance. We derive the unique closed-form optimizer within this family, shared across all nonzero update magnitudes and directions, with transition coefficients determined by both the source and target noise levels. Because the minimized geometry factor still varies across timesteps, we further adopt gradient-scale matching from prior work and derive closed-form policy-gradient and KL weights that normalize explicit score scales and reference-KL curvature. Geo-FSRL trains on the original few-step schedule and retains the deterministic solver at inference. Our method achieves higher final reward scores than Flow-GRPO and Flow-CPS on both image generation and video generation models under the 4-step setting. Code is available atthis https URL
  </details>
