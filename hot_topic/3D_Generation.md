- **[Utility-WAM: Correcting Future-Induced Action Changes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39367)**  `arXiv:2610.39367`  `cs.RO`  
  _Feihong Zhang, Xi Lin, Yulong Shi, Zuxing Lu, Yanghong Mei, Zhaopeng Gu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  World Action Models (WAMs) can condition robot actions on generated visual futures, but this additional context is neither cost-free nor uniformly beneficial. We examine what a predicted future actually changes: the action delta $\Delta a$ between the future-conditioned and future-free execution paths of the same model. Under a shared WAM checkpoint, this delta barely shifts average benchmark success ($+0.8$ points on LIBERO, $-0.8$ on RoboTwin~2.0), while generating visual futures increases inference latency by 1.7--1.9$\times$. These aggregate metrics conceal a concentrated failure mode that we term \emph{future-induced negative transfer}: while predictive deltas benefit long-horizon tasks ($+2.4$ points on LIBERO-Long) and remain negligible on most routine manipulations, they induce severe degradation on precision tasks---with \emph{Press Stapler} and \emph{Place Can Basket} losing 54.0 and 34.0 points, recurring as top failure modes across independently trained WAM variants. We introduce \textbf{Utility-WAM} for in-place correction of future-conditioned control. Its primary learning component is a residual adapter that repairs future-induced velocity deltas under a frozen shared WAM by aligning them with ground-truth dynamic guidance and penalizing excess error beyond the future-free baseline. The adapter selectively eliminates the majority of severe task drops (reducing losses to 6.0 and 14.0 points) while preserving long-horizon foresight. To eliminate superfluous video computation, a lightweight pre-generation gate reuses counterfactual action outcomes to selectively bypass video generation on 75.5\% of RoboTwin queries, cutting latency by 38\% while improving average success to 93.3\% on RoboTwin~2.0 and 98.9\% on LIBERO. On physical bimanual manipulation across three real-world tasks, Utility-WAM succeeds in 53 of 60 trials, compared to 43 for FAST-WAM.

- **[DiTSG: Context-Aware Air Traffic Scene Generation with Multi-Agent Diffusion Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16959)**  `arXiv:2610.16959`  `cs.MA` `cs.LG` `cs.RO`  
  _Seokbin Yoon, Kyungmin Kim, Taejung Kwon, Keumjin Lee, Peng Wei_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Understanding how many aircraft, and which traffic configurations, can be safely and efficiently managed by air traffic controllers within a given airspace is fundamental to air traffic management. Air traffic controllers continuously modify aircraft trajectories through interventions such as speed advisories and radar vectoring, and the patterns of these interventions are difficult to capture effectively using existing air traffic simulators. To address this limitation, we propose Diffusion Transformers for Traffic Scene Generation (DiTSG), which learns to generate air traffic scenes from recorded multi-aircraft trajectories. DiTSG employs stacked multi-agent inverted Transformer layers as the denoising backbone of a diffusion transformer, jointly generates the trajectories of multiple aircraft within a traffic scene, and conditions the generation on agent-specific required times of arrival. The resulting trajectories generated by DiTSG preserve realistic inter-aircraft interactions observed in actual air traffic operations. Experiments show that the DiTSG generates diverse traffic scenes that reproduce the statistical properties of recorded traffic while preserving realistic inter-aircraft interactions.

- **[GlanceWAM: Sparse Test-Time Imagination for World-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23927)**  `arXiv:2608.23927`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI` `cs.RO`  
  _Linhan Wang, Zijian An, Mingyuan Zhang, Chen Dai, Yi Xu, Can Cui, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video generative models provide rich physical priors for robot learning, yet existing world-action models (WAMs) face a fundamental trade-off: synchronous video generation at control rate is latency-prohibitive, while abandoning test-time visual imagination sacrifices task success. We show that visual imagination achieves both real-time inference and superior success rates when generated asynchronously off the critical path and consumed directly in latent space. We introduce GlanceWAM, which decouples imagination from control on a single shared video DiT backbone: an asynchronous proposer glances ahead on a slow clock to imagine a single lookahead frame seconds into the future in the background, while an action head decodes action chunks at control rate (48 ms) purely in latent space without blocking. Enabled by a non-interfering attention mask that isolates video representations and staleness-robust horizon training that accommodates asynchronous lookahead aging, GlanceWAM breaks the speed-success dilemma. Trained purely on demonstrations, it attains 72.2% on the 24-task RoboCasa kitchen benchmark (vs. 67.1% for synchronous Cosmos Policy) and 99.0% on LIBERO while cutting per-chunk control latency 24x relative to synchronous world-action models (48 ms on one A100). In single-arm and bimanual real-robot manipulation, it achieves higher average success than $\pi_{0.5}$ without any robot-data pretraining. - **[MEV: A Multi-Event Video Dataset for Long-Take Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38241)**  `arXiv:2610.38241`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Peiyuan Zhu, Shaoan Xie, Yifan Shen, Wen Tian, Zijian Li, Guangyi Chen, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Current datasets for text-to-video generation are largely limited to short clips depicting a single event or coarse-grained captions. As a result, they provide limited supervision for modeling long-take temporal dependencies, often leading to temporally inconsistent generations when describing multiple events. In this paper, we introduce MEV, a multi-event video dataset designed for long-take video generation. MEV provides high-quality real-world videos where subjects can perform a sequence of distinct, non-overlapping events, featuring precise temporal boundaries and fine-grained procedural annotations. This event-centric discretization reduces confounding factors and enables models to support temporally localized conditioning and build long-take videos from atomic event units. Furthermore, we introduce a comprehensive annotation pipeline that segments videos into temporally grounded events, and generates structured captions at both the global level and the event level. In addition, under an event-centric evaluation protocol, we conduct multi-event generation experiments by fine-tuning on MEV with temporally grounded event conditioning, leading to improved long-take event transitions and more coherent content. We believe this work provides a publicly available resource for multi-event fine-tuning and event-centric evaluation, together with practical insights into learning from temporally structured video-text data.

- **[Focused Forcing: Content-Aware Per-Frame KV Selection for Efficient Autoregressive Video Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18346)**  `arXiv:2605.18346`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Peiliang Cai, Evelyn Zhang, Jiacheng Liu, Hao Lin, Ruiqi Zhang, Weile Mo, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent advances in autoregressive video diffusion have enabled sequential and streaming video generation. However, long-horizon generation requires increasingly large KV caches, making efficient compression without sacrificing quality challenging. Existing methods mostly select historical frames based on attention scores, but their context decisions remain coarse. When multiple frames are generated in the same chunk, these methods often apply a shared history selection to the whole chunk, score historical frames solely by attention, and assign head-wise budgets either uniformly or by attention-pattern heuristics rather than explicit head-importance estimation. We show that frames within the same generated chunk can depend on distinct historical frames, that the same historical frame can receive different attention scores as its relative temporal distance to the current frames changes, and that masking different heads induces unequal generation degradation. Motivated by these findings, we propose Focused Forcing, a training-free KV selection method that focuses cached history along both generated-frame and head dimensions. For each generated frame, Focused Forcing preserves the most relevant and distinctive historical frames by combining attention scores with diversity scores of historical frames, while assigning larger budgets to heads with higher estimated importance. Across multiple autoregressive generation paradigms, Focused Forcing achieves up to 1.48x end-to-end acceleration without training, while improving visual quality and text alignment. Our code is available atthis https URL.
  </details>

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>

Agentic frameworks further advance this direction by decoupling explicit game logic and controllable world representations from visual generation. - **[Video Generation Meets Agent Harness: Agentic Video Generation, Agentic World Modeling and Recursive Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40191)**  `arXiv:2610.40191`  `cs.CV`  
  _Liaoyuan Fan, Yuhao Zheng, Dongxing Mao, Hangyu Ran, Siyuan Hu, Weijia Mao, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video generation is shifting from end-to-end synthesis toward agentic generation, where planning, tool use, and feedback enable coherent, controllable multi-shot videos. In parallel, video world models provide action-conditioned observations and predicted futures, serving as interactive environments for agents. We study their intersection through the agent harness: an executable runtime that constructs context, exposes tools, maintains state, routes feedback, and manages budgets around video models. This report examines three directions. First, agentic video generation, where harnesses coordinate scripting, shot planning, asset management, production, and editing, alongside the evaluation challenges of these systems. Second, agentic world modeling, where harnesses mediate observation, control, execution, and state. We discuss hybrid designs in which game engines or agent-written programs maintain world state while video models render observations, and how agents and world models can mutually evaluate and improve one another. Third, recursive self-improvement (RSI) in video model development, distinguishing output revisions, updates to harness state, generator parameters, and evaluators, and changes to the improvement procedure itself. We identify how these improvements feed back into subsequent development. Across these directions, we analyze essential agent capabilities, including audiovisual perception, long-horizon planning, memory, code-based interaction, feedback-guided control, skill reuse, and multi-agent coordination. We conclude with challenges in autonomous video production, lifelong memory, programmable hybrid control, autonomous model development, provenance, cost accounting, and evaluation linking video quality and task performance to sustained improvements in models and harnesses.

- **[FutureWorld: A Plug-and-Play Causal Next-State Predictor for Interactive Video World Exploration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39619)**  `arXiv:2610.39619`  `cs.CV`  
  _Guangyuan Li, Kelu Yao, Shuolin Xu, Xinlei Yu, Wei Xing, Lei Zhao, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Interactive video world models aim to follow user actions while maintaining scene consistency over extended interactions. History-based methods reuse generated latents to preserve scene content, but three limitations of this reuse contribute to error recirculation. 1) The latent generated at each interaction is retained as history, potentially containing blurred details, geometric distortions, or semantic drift. 2) Although retrieved historical content matches the current scene, it can still contain errors that are carried into the next chunk. 3) New outputs then become history for later interactions, potentially reinforcing the same errors through repeated reuse. To limit this cycle, we aim to predict the future scene state from the current generated latent and the next action before generating the next interaction's video chunk. This future-state prior provides guidance for the next chunk, with the aim of reducing the errors carried forward from history. To this end, we propose a plug-and-play Causal Next-Chunk Predictor (C-NCP) with cross-model adaptability and prediction-guided modulation. We integrate C-NCP into four base generators and evaluate the augmented models across three benchmarks for interactive long-video generation and action-conditioned world modeling. Extensive experiments demonstrate reduced error accumulation and improved long-horizon consistency across diverse generation settings. The project page is atthis https URL.

- **[Edited Endpoints as Partial Goals for Image-to-Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39179)**  `arXiv:2610.39179`  `cs.CV`  
  _Sihyun Kim, Sanghyun Jo, Kyungsu Kim_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Edited endpoint images make the target of an image-to-video transition explicit and can improve transition completion, but also preserve context in the source image's layout. Full-frame endpoint conditioning turns this preserved context into requirements on the video's final state, potentially inducing temporal artifacts. We measure discontinuity with Hard-Cut (HC), late background reversal with Background Motion Reversal (BMR), and motion suppression with Dynamic Degree (DD). These artifacts appear across five video models spanning training-free and learned endpoint conditioning, in model-specific forms. We study Partial, a selective conditioning scheme that constrains only an edit-relevant region estimated from image-editor attention and leaves the remaining terminal content to the video model. Across three backbones on TC-Bench-I2V, Partial reduces temporal artifacts relative to full-frame conditioning, with no significant difference in Transition Completion Ratio (TCR); on Wan2.2-Turbo, uniformly weakening conditioning does not reproduce these reductions. A donor-context intervention keeps full-frame conditioning and replaces only the context outside the edit-relevant region with the final frame of a Partial video. It lowers HC and BMR on HunyuanVideo-1.5 and lowers BMR while restoring DD on Wan2.2-Turbo, with no significant difference in TCR, so constraint extent alone does not account for these artifacts. Together, these findings suggest that edited endpoints should serve as partial future goals, although edit relevance does not guarantee future validity. - **[Hierarchical Diffusion for Camera-controlled Autoregressive Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37983)**  `arXiv:2610.37983`  `cs.CV`  
  _Kaihua Chen, Yiming Gong, Deva Ramanan, Tarasha Khurana_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We address the task of long-video, camera-controlled generation from a single image. The dominant autoregressive paradigm suffers from two key limitations: (1) error accumulation over time, as distant frames are conditioned on previously generated outputs rather than ground truth, leading to drift; and (2) ambiguity in how to incorporate camera control. Standard solutions to both--such as self-forcing and conditioning on explicit 6-DOF poses--either incur significant computational overhead or produce drifting scene geometries over time. We propose a simple and lightweight framework that combines autoregressive generation (WanAR) with hierarchical long-context denoising (FixAR). To enforce scene geometry remains consistent with input cameras, we draw from feed-forward novel-view synthesis and reformulate video generation as a warping-and-inpainting problem. To mitigate drift, we introduce a hierarchical denoising strategy in which different stages condition on different temporal histories. Specifically, we first generate frames conditioned on the immediate past, and then refine them using a second denoiser conditioned on a temporally subsampled long-range history to enforce global consistency. This fine-to-coarse scheme enables each generated frame to effectively condition on the full temporal context without incurring a prohibitive cost. Experiments on RealEstate10k and DL3DV demonstrate that WanAR+FixAR outperforms prior methods for camera-controlled long-video generation, producing results that are both temporally stable and consistent with input camera trajectories.

- **[UniWorld: A Unified World Model for Real-Time Visual World Simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32381)**  `arXiv:2610.32381`  `cs.CV`  
  _Xin Zhang, Yabo Chen, Shuaiwei Wang, Zixuan Duan, Haibin Huang, Feng Xu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Continuous visual world simulation requires coupling evolving scene geometry with temporally persistent appearance. We present UniWorld, a unified framework that integrates depth trajectories and reference appearance within a shared video generation model. The central design maintains visual and geometric histories on a common timeline. Noise-aligned overlapping continuation places generated history at the prescribed sampling noise level and commits only newly generated latents. Rolling depth latent state preserves the corresponding geometric controls, incrementally reads newly available geometry, and keeps the conditioning fixed within each sampling window. Together, these mechanisms ensure exact preservation of latent history while bounding the active depth state by the window size. We evaluate continuous generation, paired history interventions, and long-horizon behavior across diverse scene categories. The same generator supports estimated, native, and on-demand simulator depth, while cached and incremental execution paths produce bitwise-identical outputs. Appearance changes and geometric control branches operate through the same interface without modifying committed history. The measured steady-state DiT execution path achieves real-time generation throughput, demonstrating the efficiency of UniWorld for continuous visual world simulation. Project page:this https URL.

- **[AnyGameRenderer: Entity-Consistent Generative Rendering Across Games and an Entity-Level Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31466)**  `arXiv:2610.31466`  `cs.CV`  
  _Ruicong Liu, Liangyang Ouyang, Fengbo Lan, Zhixiang Wang, Kaipeng Zhang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  AI coding agents can now build small playable games in minutes, but the results usually look like programmer art: gray blockouts, colored geometric primitives, and flat semantic blobs. We present AnyGameRenderer, a video-to-video generative renderer that turns such footage into high-quality visuals with characters, motion detail, and effects. Throughout, it keeps the layout, camera, and every entity's position and timing intact. Our AnyGameRenderer generalizes across a wide range of genres that agents build, such as tower defense, auto-battlers, top-down survivors, turn-based battles, kart racing, and crowd runners. AnyGameRenderer takes plain video as its game-state input, with no complex representations such as engine G-buffers, per-object class labels, or low-poly proxies. Gray blockouts, colored primitives, semantic maps, and wireframes all work as input. It also preserves entity correspondence: group counts stay correct, and entities appear and disappear on the right frame. We implement AnyGameRenderer by LoRA fine-tuning MiniMax-H3 ref2va, a 33B reference-video-conditioned video generation model. Training uses ~10k paired clips from three sources: (i) multi-stream rendering data from 38 agent-made games; (ii) two-stream rendering data from Unreal Engine, with programmatically controlled crowds, count changes, and entity scales; and (iii) real gameplay recordings, with pairs reconstructed via 3D-box detection. To evaluate the entity correspondence, we propose an entity-level benchmark of 59 held-out cases with 753 entities, scored against ground-truth boxes. Compared with the base model, AnyGameRenderer better preserves entity correspondence: temporal match rises from 50.9% to 62.9%, missed count changes fall from 34.7% to 21.3%, and fewer entities are hallucinated or left unrendered. We introduce Human-Participatory Multi-Agent Organization and instantiate it in Agentic University (AU).
