- **[LaLa-World: Scaling Latent World Models with Natural Language Actions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27851)**  `arXiv:2610.27851`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Junha Song, Geonmo Gu, Jeongeun Park, Heejeong Nam, Lucas Maes, Byeongho Heo, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  This paper shows that JEPA-style latent world models (LWMs) can learn across diverse domains and data sources by adopting the language-conditioned interface of video generation models. We establish this approach as an effective and efficient basis for planning with imagination and downstream policy fine-tuning. To this end, we introduce LaLa-World, a Language-conditioned Latent World model that learns from simulation and robot trajectories together with broader image and video data. Two designs make this work. First, we describe both numerical actions and visual changes in language, enabling joint training of a single future predictor across heterogeneous datasets. Second, we build the predictor on a language model, allowing it to interpret language conditions while predicting future latent states. The resulting predictor achieves higher average MPC planning performance than existing LWMs trained separately for each domain or task. Adding image and video data further improves average planning performance and robustness to action phrasing. In a matched comparison with an RAEv2-based video generation model baseline, LaLa-World reaches comparable planning performance with $6\times$ less training cost and $13.9\times$ faster planning. Beyond planning, the pretrained LaLa-World serves as an initialization for LaLa-Policy, which jointly predicts actions and future visual representations. LaLa-Policy performs competitively with Cosmos Policy on LIBERO and RoboCasa, with up to $5.5\times$ faster inference.

- **[Let It Be Simple: One-Step Action Generation for Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05737)**  `arXiv:2606.05737`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI` `cs.LG` `cs.RO`  
  _Yitong Chen, Shiduo Zhang, Jingjing Gong, Xipeng Qiu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  One-step generation from standard diffusion or Flow-Matching training is rarely effective for text-to-image or video generation without distillation or specialized one-step objectives. We find that VLA action generation behaves strikingly differently. Biasing only the training timestep distribution toward the noise endpoint yields strong one-step policies across LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-Pro, and real-world robot tasks. Moreover, for open-source VLA models with different architectures, simply replacing iterative decoding with a single forward pass often performs comparably to the original multi-step decoding on benchmarks such as LIBERO, CALVIN, and SimplerEnv. We attribute this difference to the condition-target structure of VLA generation, which is closer to image-to-text than text-to-image: rich observations strongly constrain a comparatively compact target. We formalize this intuition through the irreducible velocity loss $R_v(t,c)$ of standard Flow Matching, which becomes much smaller toward the noise endpoint for VLA, whereas image-generation models usually exhibit a U-shaped profile. The geometry induced by the learned generative map shows the same contrast: the Jacobian of this map with respect to the initial noise has a substantially smaller largest singular value for action generation than for image generation. Together, these results suggest that VLA's condition-target structure makes one-step generation unusually accessible to standard Flow Matching. Code and training configurations for this paper are available atthis https URL.

- **[Attn-Forcing: Learning Full-Context Attention Proxies for Streaming Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30036)**  `arXiv:2610.30036`  `cs.CV`  
  _Junyao Gao, Sibo Liu, Jiaxing Li, Yanan Sun, Hanxin Zhu, Weidong Zhang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Increasing the attention context length significantly improves streaming video generation quality, while incurring prohibitive memory and computational costs as the context grows. Existing methods attempt to capture this contextual information through attention selection or compression, but remain limited by bounded representations and lack explicit attention-level supervision to retain historical contributions to current queries. To address this, we introduce Attn-Forcing, a novel framework that preserves full-context attention contributions for streaming video generation under bounded memory and computation. We achieve this by introducing attention proxies that continuously approximate the attention responses induced by the historical context. Specifically, we implement the proxies as learnable visual latents and employ a Proxy Update Module to incorporate visual context from historical latents. Moreover, we propose an Attention Matching Loss to explicitly constrain the attention behavior of the learned proxies using full-context attention supervision. Extensive qualitative and quantitative experiments demonstrate that Attn-Forcing consistently improves streaming video generation quality and achieves state-of-the-art performance over existing baseline methods.
  </details>

- **[Looking Ahead: Modeling Future Evolution in Autoregressive Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29989)**  `arXiv:2610.29989`  `cs.CV`  
  _Ruopeng Gao, Yukun Huang, Jiarui Hu, Shuai Wang, Wenbo Hu, Ying Shan, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Autoregressive video diffusion models generate videos chunk by chunk, yet producing plausible continuations does not guarantee that they capture how scenes evolve. Under teacher forcing, strong temporal redundancy may allow local visual continuity to serve as a shortcut for current-chunk denoising, reducing the pressure to model visual evolution. We introduce Future Chunk Prediction (FCP), an auxiliary objective jointly optimized with teacher-forced denoising that predicts temporally separated chunks without access to the intervening visual context. Drawing supervision directly from training videos, FCP requires no additional pretrained visual encoder and leaves autoregressive inference unchanged. Our method achieves superior physical plausibility over standard teacher-forced autoregressive training with a lower overall training compute budget, highlighting the effectiveness and efficiency of future-chunk supervision for modeling visual evolution. Intriguingly, our visualizations suggest that FCP fosters a more structured representation space, which may be valuable for building autoregressive world models.
  </details>

- **[CausalAlign: Causal Alignment of Video Generation Models for Consistent Ego-Motion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29915)**  `arXiv:2610.29915`  `cs.CV`  
  _Kai Zhu, Yintianrun Zhang, Shuwei Jiang, Ruolin Zuo, Yixin Chen, Duomin Wang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion-based video generation models (VGMs) can produce visually plausible videos, yet their camera ego-motion often drifts over long horizons. Camera ego-motion determines how the viewpoint changes between frames and is essential for maintaining consistent geometry and trajectories. To address this problem, we propose CausalAlign, a causal alignment framework with two components. First, we diagnose how precise ego motion is represented, used, and retained within VGMs using linear probes and causal interventions, revealing a rise-and-fall pattern during denoising. Second, we strengthen ego-motion state in VGM by aligning the representations at the identified layers to a 3D foundation model through delta-token space. This space encodes the changes between consecutive frames, allowing the alignment to focus on ego-motion dynamics rather than static scene content. With Wan2.2 fine-tuned on DL3DV and RealEstate10K, CausalAlign can largely improve motion accuracy, geometric consistency, and temporal smoothness, while preserving visual quality. For example, it improves geometric consistency by more than 20% relative to the base model. Moreover, with the fine-tuned VGM, CausalAlign improves the success rate of world action models (WAMs) by 10 points on mobile manipulation tasks of RoboCasa365 benchmark. Overall, CausalAlign improves ego-motion consistency in diffusion-based VGMs and yields noticeable gains in embodied models.
  </details>

- **[Spatially Gated Demographic Steering for Debiasing Multi-Person Text-to-Image Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29651)**  `arXiv:2610.29651`  `cs.CV`  
  _Venkatesh Thirugnana Sambandham, Torsten Schon_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Text-to-Image models reproduce societal stereotypes, and existing mitigation applies one demographic correction to the whole scene. In multi-person scene generation this creates a trade-off: global steering improves aggregate balance only by homogenizing the people within an image. Steering-based methods like Embedding Arithmetic drive the intra-image diversity below the unsteered baseline. Text- and latent-projection approaches shift the aggregate skew with only marginal gains in intra-image diversity. We present Spatially Gated Steering (SGS), which steers the demographics of each person independently by extracting per-person masks from the Diffusion Transformer's (DiT) internal attention maps at a critical denoising timestep and applying a distinct demographic vector within each mask for the remaining steps. SGS needs no fine-tuning, inversion or layout specifications, and uses a lightweight segmentor over frozen DiT attention features to localize subjects at early timesteps. On skewed occupational prompts, SGS reaches high intra-image racial and gender diversity while keeping aggregate balance competitive and image quality near baseline, a result we validate on two architecturally distinct diffusion transformers, FLUX.1-Dev and SD3.5-Large. Codebase and model weights are available atthis https URL

- **[CineEvolve: An Agentic Self-Evolution Framework for Cinematic Multi-Shot Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29590)**  `arXiv:2610.29590`  `cs.CV`  
  _Jingzhe Zhang, Peixuan Zhang, Genying Li, Bing Ma, Shuchen Weng, Si Li, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Cinematic multi-shot video generation requires both narrative planning and iterative refinement. Existing methods either lack narrative reasoning or rely on feed-forward workflows without policy updates. In this paper, we propose CineEvolve, an agentic self-evolution framework for cinematic multi-shot video generation that unifies a Director and a Critic within a single vision-language policy. Emulating real-world filmmaking that couples iterative direction with critical feedback, we train CineEvolve via alternating reinforcement learning, where the Director surfaces flaws to sharpen the Critic, and the updated Critic guides the Director's targeted revisions. To stabilize co-evolution, we introduce Evolutionary GRPO (E-GRPO), leveraging historical anchoring and revision safeguarding to mitigate advantage inflation. Additionally, we contribute CineTraj, a 40K cinematic trajectory dataset spanning creation, critique, and revision. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CineEvolve outperforms existing approaches. Our code will be available atthis https URL.

- **[Pair-Free Coarse-to-Real Video Generation via Real-to-Coarse Image Supervision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28954)**  `arXiv:2610.28954`  `cs.CV`  
  _Gonzalo Gómez-Nogales, Daohan Lu, Peiye Zhuang, Tinghui Zhou, Yi Zhou_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Coarse-to-real (C2R) video generation converts coarse animation, simulation, or game-rendered videos into photorealistic videos while preserving scene layout, camera motion, and dynamics. Training such models requires aligned (coarse, realistic) video pairs, which are expensive to create and confine prior systems to narrow synthetic domains. We exploit a simple asymmetry: paired videos are hard to obtain, but paired real-to-coarse images are easy to construct with existing editing and 3D reconstruction tools. Using these image pairs, we train the DINO-R2C model to transfer realistic images to coarse images in DINO feature space, and apply it frame-wise to ordinary real videos to synthesize pseudo-coarse videos. The C2R# model then learns to generate each real video from its pseudo-coarse counterpart, free from needing (coarse, realistic) video pairs. At inference, the DINO-R2C model is discarded and actual coarse videos drive the C2R# model directly. Thus, our pair-free training scales with any real-video corpora rather than with paired synthetic videos. We will release the model and project webpage atthis https URL.

- **[Painting Change Map as guidance for generating painting processes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25369)**  `arXiv:2610.25369`  `cs.CV`  
  _Markus Pobitzer, Chang Liu, Niccolò Biondi, Nicu Sebe_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Digital art generation has undergone a massive paradigm shift, moving from static image synthesis to dynamic video generation that can mimic the human painting process. While existing models can synthesize visually stunning time-lapses of paintings being created or "unpainted," they largely treat the creative process as a black box. Current conditioning mechanisms are either too rigid, relying on static geometric cues like Canny edges or depth maps, or too holistic, utilizing simple scalar progress variables that offer no localized control. Consequently, users lack the agency to steer how a painting evolves over time. In this work, we argue that true control over the video generation of artwork requires a joint understanding of two fundamental dimensions: where an artist acts, and when those actions take place. To achieve this, we introduce the Painting Change Map, a novel spatio-temporal control mechanism that explicitly tracks normalized structural changes across a 1,000-timestep continuum, a resolution sufficient to capture fine-grained stroke-level changes while remaining computationally tractable. By mapping localized visual differences into an encoded RGB space, our representation enables fine-grained guidance for both the forward painting and reverse unpainting processes. Extensive benchmarking demonstrates the efficacy of our approach. We outperform existing control baselines and achieve competitive or superior results compared to state-of-the-art painting methods. Furthermore, under oracle conditions, our method surpasses all baselines by a significant margin. Code is available atthis https URL.

- **[Beyond Masks and Trajectories: Flow-Guided Latent Action Injection for Stable Surgical Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09800)**  `arXiv:2610.09800`  `cs.CV`  
  _Tsz-Yui Qin, Siyu Zhou, Chi-Keung Tang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Surgical video generation holds substantial potential for surgical education, simulation, and data augmentation, yet generating surgical videos with realistic and clinically plausible motion remains challenging. Most existing methods rely on auxiliary conditions, such as masks, trajectories, depth, or reference videos, to achieve visually plausible synthesis. Yet, these auxiliary conditions typically require additional manual annotation or specialized acquisition, making it difficult to scale such methods beyond small, curated datasets. This motivates the need for a reference-free architecture capable of generating high-quality surgical video without requiring auxiliary visual conditions at inference time. We propose FLAIR, a Flow-guided LatentAction Injection framework for Reference-free surgical video generation. FLAIR learns action priors from optical flow of real surgical videos, dynamically predicts corresponding latent action representation from an input prompt, and injects it into a frozen base model to generate surgical videos with improved action consistency. We further construct SurgActionClip-30K, the first large-scale surgical vision dataset comprising action-centric segmented clips and structured caption labels, addressing the persistent lack of fine-grained, action-centric surgical datasets. Lastly, we introduce SurgMetrics, the first surgical domain-specific evaluation metrics for quantifying the quality of generated surgical videos, addressing the persistent absence of clinically grounded evaluation standards in this domain. Extensive experiments demonstrate that FLAIR enables generating high-quality surgical videos using text-only inference without auxiliary conditions, and validation in SurgMetrics demonstrates its strength in alignment with human perception compared to traditional metrics.

- **[Sphinx: Efficiently Serving Novel View Synthesis using Regression-Guided Selective Refinement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18672)**  `arXiv:2511.18672`  `cs.CV`  
  _Yuchen Xia, Souvik Kundu, Mosharaf Chowdhury, Nishil Talati_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Novel View Synthesis (NVS) generates novel views of a scene from a sparse set of input images and is a key component in applications such as immersive rendering and 3D content generation. Existing NVS approaches broadly exhibit an efficiency--quality tradeoff: regression-based models generate novel views with relatively low latency but can struggle with perceptual quality, particularly in challenging regions or regions not visible in the input views (disoccluded regions), while diffusion-based models produce higher-quality and temporally consistent views at substantially greater computational cost. This motivates a hybrid approach that leverages the complementary strengths of regression and diffusion to bridge this efficiency--quality gap.We present Sphinx, a training-free hybrid inference and serving system for NVS that combines regression-level efficiency with diffusion-level quality. Sphinx first uses fast regression-based NVS to generate coarse novel views, which provide a strong initialization for diffusion and avoid expensive generation from pure noise. It then selectively applies diffusion refinement where additional computation is needed, reducing the denoising workload while preserving the quality benefits of diffusion-based NVS. To efficiently serve the resulting heterogeneous requests, Sphinx further introduces an SLO-aware serving framework that dynamically allocates GPU resources based on each request's remaining computation and urgency. Evaluated across diverse NVS models, datasets, and serving configurations, Sphinx achieves an average 2.3 times inference speedup (up to 2.7 times) with less than 10% perceptual degradation, while its SLO-aware scheduler achieves up to 10 times higher SLO attainment than FCFS scheduling across hardware platforms.

- **[Loomis Painter: Learning Painting Processes by Reversing Image-to-Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17344)**  `arXiv:2511.17344`  `cs.CV`  
  _Markus Pobitzer, Chang Liu, Chenyi Zhuang, Teng Long, Bin Ren, Nicu Sebe_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Step-by-step painting tutorials are used for learning artistic techniques, but existing video resources lack interactivity and personalization. While recent generative models have advanced artistic image synthesis, they still struggle to generate temporally and structurally consistent videos. Video models excel in consistent frame generation, yet fine-tuning an image-to-video model on forward painting sequences creates a long-range dependency on the first-frame reference, disrupting its pretrained attention pattern and hindering reconstruction. We address this issue by training on reversed sequences that better preserve the reference-attention behavior. Building on this, we present Loomis Painter, a framework for reconstructing coherent painting processes. We further introduce a media-transfer training strategy that decouples input appearance from the target process, enabling generation across different artistic media. Trained on a curated dataset of real-world painting processes, our method outperforms existing baselines across LPIPS, DINO, and CLIP metrics and is consistently preferred in expert and non-expert user studies. Code available atthis https URL.
