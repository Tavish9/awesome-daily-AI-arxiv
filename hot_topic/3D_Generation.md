- **[Learning Interaction Dynamics via Motion-Region Guidance for Robotic Video World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27344)**  `arXiv:2611.27344`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Yongkang He, Xiaofen Xing, Xiangmin Xu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Action-conditioned video world models imagine future observations under candidate robot actions, providing a promising visual tool for robot policy evaluation and interaction simulation. However, existing video world models often optimize global reconstruction errors instead of emphasizing interaction-relevant regions. As a result, they may preserve overall scene appearance but overlook local interaction details, leading to physically implausible outcomes such as hallucinated contact effects and inconsistent robot-object interactions. We introduce MoReWorld, a unified motion-region-guided framework that uses a shared motion region, the part of the environment that moves in response to the robot's actions, to focus dynamics learning and video generation on interaction-relevant regions. The same motion region guides local motion prediction, region-aware supervision, and motion-gated future-feature conditioning. At inference time, the motion region is predicted as a motion probability map from visual history and robot actions, without requiring future observations or ground-truth motion labels. On WorldSimProbe, MoReWorld achieves the highest overall simulator-faithfulness score, with the strongest performance on local consistency, global consistency, and interaction dynamics. These results indicate that motion-region guidance helps the model preserve scene-level consistency while more faithfully predicting action-conditioned interaction dynamics.
  </details>

- **[DIDO: Distilling Interaction-Centric Dynamics into One-Step Denoising for World Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15570)**  `arXiv:2609.15570`  `cs.RO`  
  _Jing Lyu, Shuanghao Bai, Runze Xiao, Zhenyu Liao, Wenxing Tan, Zihan Tang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  World Action Models (WAMs) use video generation models to predict future visual dynamics for robotic manipulation, but iterative denoising introduces additional latency for closed-loop control. We empirically find that visual content converges at different rates during denoising. Static background structure forms early, whereas the gripper and manipulated object remain blurry after the first step, with their interaction dynamics emerging only through subsequent denoising. Consequently, naively truncating a multi-step video model to one step preserves scene structure but loses the interaction-centric dynamics most critical for manipulation. To address this issue, we propose DIDO, which distills the converged dynamics of a multi-step video model into a single denoising step. DIDO combines distribution matching distillation with interaction-centric representation guidance. Beyond compressing multi-step generation into one forward pass, DIDO explicitly models the gripper, manipulated object, and their interaction using supervised bounding-box visual reasoning tokens. Additionally, DIDO aligns the target object's representations across multiple model layers with features from a pretrained DINOv3 encoder. This interaction-centric guidance helps the distilled model preserve both the relevant entities and their future dynamics in a single step, while substantially reducing inference latency. DIDO achieves an average success rate of 99.0\% on LIBERO, 76.6\% on LIBERO-Plus, and 92.0\% on RoboTwin, while also demonstrating effective transfer to long-horizon and generalization tasks in real-world robotic manipulation.
  </details>

- **[Efficient VLA Reinforcement Learning via Subchunk Rollouts in Latent World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27645)**  `arXiv:2611.27645`  `cs.LG`  
  _Nan Tang, Han-Tao Zhang, Jing-Cheng Pang, Hao-Nan Wang, Hao-Xiang Ren, Yi-Fei Sheng, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) can improve pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) policies through imagined experiences. However, existing VLA-MBRL methods primarily rely on large-scale image or video generation world models, incurring large computational overhead. Latent world models offer a more efficient alternative but face two challenges: compounding errors during recursive prediction and incompatibility with VLAs' visual input interfaces. In this work, we propose LS-MBPO, a lightweight residual MBRL framework that leverages latent world models to improve upon pretrained VLAs. LS-MBPO introduces two key techniques: (1) Hierarchical Forcing, which combines dense causal supervision with autoregressive feedback to mitigate compounding prediction errors; and (2) Latent Subchunk Rollouts, which leverage action chunks from a frozen VLA policy and a latent residual policy for successive subchunk refinement, bridging the gap between latent imagination and VLA policy execution. We further introduce a Progress-Conditioned Branch-Start Curriculum that progressively shifts rollout starting points from later to earlier task stages, enabling effective policy learning from short imagined rollouts. Extensive experiments on LIBERO, RoboTwin, and a real-world ALOHA robot show that LS-MBPO achieves superior task performance and training efficiency compared to existing methods.
  </details>

- **[Ego2Ego: Generative Visual Perspective Taking Across Collaborators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27459)**  `arXiv:2611.27459`  `cs.CV` `cs.RO`  
  _Chuhan Jiao, John Gideon, Anshul Gupta, Guy Rosman, Andreas Bulling_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Visual perspective taking, the ability to infer what another person sees from their position, is fundamental to human collaboration. However, prior works reduced perspective taking to state estimation, geometric reconstruction, or discrete question answering, whereas existing cross-view video generation methods either inferred the target view from the input video alone or required an external camera that observes the target person. We introduce generative visual perspective taking: Given an ego person's egocentric video, a scene point cloud, and a collaborator's camera trajectory, the task is to generate the continuous egocentric video from the collaborator's perspective. This task is challenging given extreme viewpoint shifts, dynamic occlusions, and sparse visual overlap. We address these challenges with Ego2Ego -- a set of backbone-agnostic geometric modules that provide a pretrained video diffusion model with the 3D scene geometry shared by both interactants. Ego2Ego renders dense, temporally consistent scene coordinate maps into every view as a shared spatial reference, aligns cross-view tokens in 3D ray space through relative geometric attention, and recovers regions outside the ego person's current view from the ego person's visual memory. Experiments on the CoMind dataset across three video diffusion backbones show that Ego2Ego consistently improves visual quality, Level-2 scene layout accuracy, and collaborator motion, outperforming state-of-the-art cross-view video generation baselines on every backbone. By generating the collaborator's egocentric video more accurately, Ego2Ego brings visual perspective taking to human-robot collaboration, where it can help infer what a human collaborator sees and does, and can turn single-person recordings into synchronised multi-perspective videos for robot learning.
  </details>

- **[RefDecoder: Enhancing Visual Generation with Conditional Video Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15196)**  `arXiv:2605.15196`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Xiang Fan, Yuheng Wang, Bohan Fang, Zhongzheng Ren, Ranjay Krishna_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video generation powers a vast array of downstream applications. However, while the de facto standard, i.e., latent diffusion models, typically employ heavily conditioned denoising networks, their decoders often remain unconditional. We observe that this architectural asymmetry leads to significant loss of detail and inconsistency relative to the input image. To address this, we argue that the decoder requires equal conditioning to preserve structural integrity. We introduce RefDecoder, a reference-conditioned video VAE decoder by injecting high-fidelity reference image signal directly into the decoding process via reference attention. Specifically, a lightweight image encoder maps the reference frame into the detail-rich high-dimensional tokens, which are co-processed with the denoised video latent tokens at each decoder up-sampling stage. We demonstrate consistent improvements across several distinct decoder backbones (e.g., Wan 2.1 and VideoVAE+), achieving up to +2.1 dB PSNR over the unconditional baselines on the Inter4K, WebVid, and Large Motion reconstruction benchmarks. Notably, RefDecoder can be directly swapped into existing video generation systems without additional fine-tuning, and we report across-the-board improvements in subject consistency, background consistency, and overall quality scores on the VBench I2V benchmark. Beyond I2V, RefDecoder generalizes well to a wide range of visual generation tasks such as style transfer and video editing refinement.
  </details>

- **[Verify Before You Generate: Sketch-Guided Efficient Motion-Planning for Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17450)**  `arXiv:2511.17450`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI` `cs.CL`  
  _Yidong Huang, Zun Wang, Han Lin, Dong-Ki Kim, Shayegan Omidshafiei, Jaehong Yoon, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent video generation approaches increasingly rely on planning intermediate control signals like object trajectories to improve temporal coherence and motion fidelity. However, these methods mostly employ single-shot plans that are typically limited to simple motion instructions, leading to suboptimal motion quality, or iterative refinement which requires multiple calls to the video generator and incurs high computational cost. To address this trade-off, we propose SketchVerify, a training-free framework whose key insight is to verify motion plans using lightweight video sketches before invoking the expensive video generator. This lightweight planning space enables efficient comparison of multiple candidate trajectories and bounded iterative refinement before a single final video-generation pass. Given a prompt and a reference image, SketchVerify samples multiple candidate trajectories and renders each as a lightweight video sketch by compositing the moving objects over a static background. A vision-language verifier then ranks these sketches according to instruction alignment and visual motion consistency, allowing unsuitable trajectories to be rejected before full video synthesis. The motion plan is iteratively refined until a satisfactory one is identified, then passed to a trajectory-conditioned generator for final synthesis. Experiments on WORLDMODELBENCH and PHYWORLDBENCH demonstrate consistent improvements in instruction following and temporally coherent, visually plausible motion. Using the same ATI-14B generator, SketchVerify improves the aggregate WorldModelBench score from 7.71 to 8.71. Compared with full-video iterative refinement, it achieves a 13.1x planning-stage speedup and a 3.6x end-to-end speedup.
  </details>

- **[Refrain: Learning Multi-Shot Consistency from Inconsistent References for A$^2$O Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27806)**  `arXiv:2611.27806`  `cs.CV`  
  _Hongru Yan, Yue Wu, Jian-Wei Zhang, Liangbin Xie, Mingzhe Zheng, Shenghua Gao_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  A comic-book character, a photorealistic cityscape, an oil-painted coffee cup -- such stylistically diverse references provide visual guidance across shots and frames, yet generating a coherent multi-shot video that unifies their visual styles while preserving subject identities and scene content remains challenging. Existing methods maintain cross-shot continuity through additional attention control or memory processing. However, they struggle to resolve disparate per-shot styles or handle reference-target duration mismatches within a single framework. We introduce Refrain, a unified model that learns cross-shot consistency through a self-supervised reconstruction task. Reference clips from the same original video are deliberately perturbed with distinct visual edits, and the model recovers the unedited source footage under a shared style prompt. The resulting pipeline yields DurMix-300K, which pairs independently edited references of varying durations with their original videos. Operating on the A$^2$O principle (Any Reference, Any Duration, One Timeline), Refrain maps image and video references onto a shared temporal canvas without auxiliary conditioning modules. For evaluation, experiments show that standard CLIP or DINO metrics frequently overlook stylistic clashes or misinterpret intended narrative cuts as inconsistency. We propose Shot-Debiased Consistency (SDC), which uses an extensive real-video bank to establish a baseline for style variation associated with content changes within shots. By discounting this baseline divergence, SDC evaluates cross-shot style shifts against real-world distributions, where smaller residual differences indicate higher stylistic continuity. Extensive experiments demonstrate that Refrain achieves state-of-the-art performance in cross-shot consistency, while excelling at shot timing, reference adherence, and text alignment.
  </details>

- **[GameScrub: Learning HUD Removal from Real Gameplay with Recovered Targets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27547)**  `arXiv:2611.27547`  `cs.CV`  
  _Mohammad Areeb Qazi, Yiyan Hu, Yanan Wang, Xuezhi Liang, Xin Li, Fan Yang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Gameplay videos are a rich source of experience for learning virtual-world dynamics, yet their frames contain heads-up displays (HUDs) that follow interface rules rather than scene dynamics. Removing HUDs would make this footage directly usable for learning world dynamics. However, training a removal model requires paired clips with and without HUDs, whereas real recordings contain only HUD-overlaid footage. Existing work synthesizes pairs by compositing HUD graphics onto HUD-free footage, requiring a separately curated clean corpus and producing HUDs that only approximate real ones. Models trained on such pairs leave 40 to 50% of real HUD elements untouched. We instead recover pseudo-clean targets directly from real recordings by inpainting HUD regions with a pretrained video diffusion model. Preventing HUD traces from leaking into recovered targets is challenging because latent video autoencoders mix information across mask boundaries. Our training-free recovery pipeline, GameScrub-Data, pre-fills HUD regions with flow-guided background estimates before encoding, excludes HUDs from scene conditioning, and confines restoration to masked regions through pixel-space compositing. The resulting pairs train GameScrub, a video diffusion removal model that handles diverse HUDs without inference-time masks or clean reference images. On the in-the-wild Game2World benchmark, GameScrub achieves an artifact-adjusted removal score of 71.95, compared with 57.86 for the strongest reference-free GameCleaner configuration, and removes 90.06% of annotated HUD elements versus 59.07%. These results demonstrate that real recordings with recovered targets can support effective HUD removal, advancing HUD-free gameplay data for world models and controllable video generation.
  </details>

- **[Budgeted Safety Verification for Streaming Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27268)**  `arXiv:2611.27268`  `cs.CV`  
  _Yuxin Cao, Ziqi Ding, Jiayu Zhang, Xianglin Yang, Wei Song, Jin Song Dong_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  A streamed video generator releases each chunk the moment that it is produced, so a safety decision that waits for the finished clip arrives after the harm has been seen. We measure a guardrail by exposure, the harmful frames a viewer is shown, and ask where a fixed moderation budget should be spent. The accurate detector reads decoded frames with a video language model and is too expensive for every chunk, so our guard reads hidden states the generator has computed already and spends a cheap score on deciding when to call it. On 599 streams from a distilled causal generator the undefended model shows 46.25 harmful frames of 81. The video model on every chunk leaves 0.91 frames at 80.1 scored per benign stream, and the same model triggered by our guard leaves 2.35 at 5.3. Spending that budget at random leaves 37.20 frames, so the gain is in the ordering, and a frame detector in the same role gives up almost six, so the judging stage must read the chunk as a clip. The ordering is worth more as the stream lengthens, and the guard carries to a second system. An independent guardrail model agrees with our oracle at $\kappa=0.74$, and a blinded audit by 50 participants puts its precision at 96.1% and recall at 82.8%. Warning. This paper contains examples of generated content that some readers may find disturbing or offensive in nature.

- **[PP-CFG: Polarity-Preserving Guidance for Controllable Few-Step Image and Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27266)**  `arXiv:2611.27266`  `cs.CV`  
  _Junyi He, Xu Peng, Chengming Xu, Sixiao Zheng, Donghao Luo, Chao Wen, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Few-step diffusion distillation reduces sampling cost but often weakens adjustable classifier-free guidance (CFG) and runtime negative prompting. We introduce PP-CFG, an architecture that keeps positive and negative text conditions separate and combines their attention responses with opposite signs within a shared visual backbone. Its Scale-Time Polarity Mixer refines each condition using the guidance scale and denoising timestep, allowing prompt influence to vary throughout generation. The student retains a continuous scale input and selectable negative prompts with one visual backbone pass per step. We evaluate PP-CFG on Wan2.1 for video generation with distribution matching distillation (DMD), and on Qwen-Image with both latent consistency model (LCM) distillation and DMD. PP-CFG achieves 81.46 VBench in four steps and lower video teacher-student distances across guidance scales than step-distilled DMD. On NegElement-1K, our benchmark of 1,000 matched image and video prompt cases, it achieves higher target-removal rates than the corresponding teachers. These experiments support the method's generality across attention architectures, generation modalities, and distillation objectives while retaining competitive generation quality.

  _Ziqi Ding, Yuxin Cao, Kai Ye, Yaobo Yang, Wei Song_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Inference-time methods that lift a small open video generator toward a proprietary one are compared through preference scores, which never say how much of the gap to a target video they close. We introduce the oracle control ladder, which hands a fixed student progressively richer information from a target clip and reads the closure of each fidelity metric at each rung as an empirical headroom reference for the methods that must synthesize that information.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Video generation models are increasingly important for robot learning, yet most generate each camera view separately or tile all views in a fixed layout. Robot demonstrations, however, are captured by synchronized cameras whose number, placement, and order vary across embodiments. We present ViewDreamer, a single world model that handles variable sets of cameras by disentangling per-view generation from cross-view interaction. Each view follows the generation path of a pretrained single-view model, and views interact only through shared slot tokens. Our WAM outperforms the FastWAM-Joint baseline on LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin 2.0, and RoboDojo, and on a real Franka robot. - **[Streaming-AAA: Streaming AAA Game Video Generation at High Resolution, High Frame Rate, and Low Latency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26040)**  `arXiv:2611.26040`  `cs.CV`  
  _Zhihao Hu, Jianqi Ma, Jun Zhang, Jiayang Bai, Haifeng Shi, Yanxiang Zhuang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Controllable streaming video generation offers a promising route toward interactive game simulation, yet existing methods remain far from practical gameplay. They often fail to jointly achieve high visual fidelity, high frame rates, and low interaction latency, resulting in poor responsiveness and degraded user experience. We present Streaming-AAA, a new paradigm for controllable infinite generation of AAA-quality game videos. Our system generates 1080p gameplay videos at 43 FPS with 198 ms end-to-end latency interaction on merely NVIDIA A800 GPUs, while supporting long-horizon play without severe temporal degradation or collapse. Streaming-AAA is built upon three key designs. First, we redesign the VAE architecture for efficient high-resolution decoding, enabling real-time synthesis at 1080p. Second, we pretrain the diffusion model using diffusion forcing and significantly extend the temporal training horizon, allowing the model to capture long-term dependencies and maintain persistent memory. Third, we perform post-training distillation and memory compression to improve inference efficiency while retaining visual quality and temporal consistency. Experiments show that Streaming-AAA substantially improves visual fidelity, frame rate, latency, and long-term stability over prior controllable video generation approaches, enabling a significantly more playable experience for interactive AAA game video generation.

- **[PCRG: Training-Free Pruning and Caching of Reference Tokens for Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22717)**  `arXiv:2611.22717`  `cs.CV`  
  _Siyuan Wang, Haoran Li, Yi Yue, Tak L. Fung, Quan Chen_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Reference-to-video diffusion transformers pack the variational autoencoder (VAE) latents of all references and the generated tokens into one full-attention sequence, in which reference tokens make up about half. Because attention cost grows quadratically with sequence length, these tokens take up an even larger share of the attention computation, yet most of them are static, smooth or irrelevant to the output. To accelerate reference-conditioned generation, we propose pruning and caching of reference tokens for video generation (PCRG), a training-free, plug-and-play method that uses two signals the model already computes to assign each reference token to one of three tiers, namely pruned, cached or kept. The first is a low-level redundancy r from VAE latents, since a token whose latent barely changes over time or across its neighbors adds little new content. The second is a high-level importance g from the vision-language-model (VLM) conditioner, where the attention that the prompt and any first or last frames pay to a token tells whether the output needs to read it. Tokens that are not redundant are kept, and redundant tokens are cached if important and pruned otherwise. Pruned tokens leave the sequence before the first step with all rotary position embedding positions unchanged. Cached tokens have their keys and values computed once and reused, and kept tokens are processed as usual. On MiniMax-H3, already distilled to 8 timesteps and quantized to int8, PCRG is 1.57x faster end to end on our V2V-Bench and 1.49x faster on the public VACE-Benchmark, while the quality of VBench drops by only 0.24% and 0.17%, respectively. PCRG shortens the sequence rather than changing how attention is computed or which steps are run, so it also stacks on step caching and sparse attention. On V2V-Bench it reaches 1.86x with TeaCache, 1.82x with Spark at nearly unchanged fidelity and 2.15x with Veda.
  </details>

- **[Train Short, Inference Long: Training-Free Horizon Extension for Autoregressive Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14027)**  `arXiv:2602.14027`  `cs.CV`  
  _Jia Li, Xiaomeng Fu, Xurui Peng, Weifeng Chen, Youwei Zheng, Tianyu Zhao, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Autoregressive (AR) video diffusion models such as CausVid and Self Forcing enable efficient long-video generation through chunk-wise rollout with local sliding-window attention, yet suffer from visual degradation, appearance drift, and motion collapse beyond their training horizons. From a long-context sequence modeling perspective, we identify two underexplored inference-time bottlenecks in AR video length extrapolation. First, scarce high-quality long-video data and high training costs restrict video models to short training horizons. Consequently, low-frequency dimensions of 3D temporal RoPE remain under-exposed during training, inducing stronger spectral bias than in LLMs and hindering direct transfer of LLM context-extension methods. Second, both standard independent noise initialization and consistency-oriented methods with positive inter-frame correlation lack high-frequency dynamic priors to counter motion decay over long rollouts. To address these issues, we propose FLEX (Frequency-aware Length EXtension), a training-free, plug-and-play inference framework comprising three complementary components. Frequency-aware 3D RoPE modulation interpolates under-exposed low frequencies while extrapolating well-exposed high frequencies. Antiphase Noise Sampling (ANS) introduces negative intra-chunk correlation to inject high-frequency motion priors at initialization. An inference-only attention sink works with RoPE modulation to anchor global consistency. Experiments on Self Forcing and CausVid show substantial gains in long-horizon synthesis, with Self Forcing + FLEX matching or surpassing both training-free and training-based baselines on VBench-Long at $6\times$ to $12\times$ length extrapolation. Additional evaluations on Krea-14B and minute-level generation with LongLive demonstrate strong generalization across model scales, inference pipelines, and native long-video generators.
  </details>
