- **[Seeing to Drive on a Single Board: Attention-Free Depth for Onboard Robot Navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32718)**  `arXiv:2611.32718`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Itai Mizlish, Boaz Ben-Moshe_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  A small ground robot can carry one camera and a few watts of compute, but the depth foundation models that could turn that camera into a range sensor are vision transformers sized for GPUs. We bring one to a Raspberry Pi 5 with the Hailo-8L, an accelerator that runs every layer in integer arithmetic, attention included. Quantised for it, Depth Anything V2 loses large parts of the scene, and we measure why: at 449 tokens, 91% of softmax weights fall below one int8 step while carrying 17-33% of the attention mass. Ranging obstacles by where they meet the floor needs only the camera height, so mapping, hybrid-A* planning and SLAM run on the Pi's own CPU. In real-time hardware-in-the-loop tests in a Gaussian-splat reconstruction of a real yard, the stack reaches the goal without contact in 7.9$\pm$1.1 of 10 scenes, against 9.2$\pm$0.4 with exact obstacles, and in 7.7$\pm$0.7 on its own pose with simulated wheel odometry (ten seeds each; 8.2$\pm$0.4 with the real Pi and Hailo-8L in the loop, five seeds). - **[FAVLA: A Force-Adaptive Multi-Rate VLA Model for Contact-Rich Robotic Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23648)**  `arXiv:2602.23648`  `cs.RO`  
  _Yao Li, Peiyuan Tang, Wuyang Zhang, Haojie Ren, Chengyang Zhu, Yifan Duan, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have shown strong potential for general robotic manipulation, but contact-rich tasks still require timely action refinement using force/torque feedback. Existing force-aware VLA models usually align all modalities to a single low operating frequency, which discards high-frequency contact cues that are important for reactive action correction. Additionally, they use static fusion, which cannot adaptively balance visual context and force feedback across manipulation processes. To mitigate these issues, we propose FAVLA, a force-adaptive multi-rate VLA model that explicitly separates low-frequency semantic reasoning from high-frequency force-conditioned action refinement. Specifically, FAVLA uses a low-rate VLM to encode visual-language-force context and predict near-future force statistics, while a high-rate action expert refines action chunks using the latest force observations. To decide when to update actions, we introduce a Force-Adaptive Multi-rate Inference (FAMI) mechanism, which schedules the action expert's inference rate from predicted future force variance, keeping low-rate reasoning in stable phases and increasing update frequency near contact transitions. To decide how to use force, we design a Force-Guided Dynamic Fusion (FGDF) module, which injects high-frequency force features into the action expert and dynamically balances visual-semantic and force cues across manipulation stages. Extensive real-robot experiments on high-precision and contact-rich tasks show that FAVLA outperforms force-aware VLA baselines, achieving an average success rate of 88.8% and lower peak contact forces. Zero-shot evaluations further demonstrate FAVLA's robustness to visual distribution shifts. Our project homepage is available at this website.

- **[Fix How It Acts, Not How It Thinks: Surgical Fine-Tuning of Frontier LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32064)**  `arXiv:2611.32064`  `cs.LG` `cs.CL`  
  _Sandeep Singh Sandha, Prann Bansal, Arvind Sundararajan_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Open-weight LLMs are competent software engineers, but they sometimes fail at tasks they are otherwise able to do. Often it is not a failure of reasoning. It is a few small missteps over a long trajectory (repeating a step that has already failed, or declaring the task done while a requirement is still unmet) that make the difference. Correcting this calls for a surgical weight update that steers the model toward the right actions without interfering with its reasoning. Such updates, typically learned with LoRA adapters, are often too small to survive merging into a released checkpoint. In our BF16 release, the median weight change is only 1.5% of a single rounding step, and merging loses roughly two thirds of the update's norm. When the same adapter is merged into the mixed FP8/NVFP4 release of the same model, 93% of the update disappears. Instead of increasing the size of the update, we directly train on the surviving part. We introduce merge-aware fine-tuning. Standard LoRA rounds the merged weights to the storage format only at export, so the loss never sees the rounded weights. We instead round the sum of base weights and adapter update to the release format at every training step, keeping the vendor's quantization scales, and compute the loss on these weights. The forward pass matches the serving engine numerics, and the gradient passes straight through the rounding, so the model we train is the model we ship. The loss optimizes only the model's actions and keeps its reasoning close to the base model. Applied to Nemotron-3.5, the recipe improves agentic coding in both the BF16 and the NVFP4 release, by up to 8.4 points on Terminal-Bench 2.1, and we publicly release both models.

  We give four large language models (LLMs) of the Claude family the complete weights of a 20k-parameter CIFAR-10 CNN as text and ask them to produce a 4-bit network without tools, data or the ability to run inference. Standard round-to-nearest (RTN) quantization to 4 bit lowers the test accuracy from 81.4% to 60.4%. We design four evaluation protocols that differ in the input and task description given to the LLMs and analyze their responses. - **[EPRO: Robust Policy Optimization Through an Imperfect Execution-Policy Predictor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32810)**  `arXiv:2611.32810`  `cs.LG`  
  _Yibo Wang, Changyi Yang, Yuanxin Liu, Zhaoran Wang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  RL for LLMs often separates rollout generation from policy training. Although the two numerical engines share model parameters, they can induce different policies. This creates a challenge beyond correcting a known training-inference mismatch: during learner updates, the execution policy corresponding to the current parameters cannot be directly observed. We view this as optimizing an unobserved execution policy through an imperfect predictor of its evolution. This perspective raises two fundamental questions: how can we estimate the execution policy's response to learner updates, and how much should we trust that estimate when optimizing the actual execution policy? We introduce Execution-Policy Robust Optimization (EPRO) to address these challenges. Starting from the observed rollout-time execution policy, we use training-policy changes to construct a differentiable predictor of the current execution policy, whose score yields Transported Score Centering (TSC). Our robust-improvement analysis reveals how prediction error limits the magnitude of reliably justified updates. For practical implementation, we introduce Policy Shift as an observable proxy for prediction risk and use Shift-Aware Soft Clipping (SSC) to attenuate risky updates. Across four Qwen3 model settings, EPRO achieves the highest average held-out accuracy among the compared methods on seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
