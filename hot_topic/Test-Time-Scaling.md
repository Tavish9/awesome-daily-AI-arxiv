- **[Compressing Onboard Language Models for Space Robots under a Behavioral-Fidelity Requirement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29302)**  `arXiv:2610.29302`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Jia Deng, Zizhe Wang, Xin Lu, Yi Huang, Zehao Jia_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Language models are being explored as the interface through which space robots interpret operator commands and select among verified skills. Running them onboard calls for low-bit compression, and flight use adds requirements that generic post-training quantization leaves implicit: a fixed memory budget and storage format, a static artifact under configuration control, and behavior close to the full-precision model validated on the ground. We treat compression as a ground-segment step and study QSelect, which composes one static low-bit model from compatible AWQ, GPTQ, and AutoRound checkpoints by assigning each linear module to one quantizer family and scoring every substitution at the final next-token distribution. The default fidelity mode needs no labels or tuned thresholds and accepts a substitution only when the forward KL divergence from the BF16 reference decreases; an optional target-adapted mode adds answer likelihood on a small labeled set. On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Llama-3.2-3B at 3 and 4 bits, composed models reach lower mean held-out perplexity than every single-family model in all six settings, and the fidelity mode lowers perplexity and KL further. In a matched scaffold, final-output scoring gives lower held-out KL than representation-similarity scoring in all 12 paired runs. With labels from training or development splits of the evaluated task families, the target-adapted mode raises 3-bit pooled accuracy on the two Llama models by 4.3 and 4.5 points over the best single-family model. At 3 bits, unquantized embedding and output layers make up 43-46% of the packed artifact. All evaluations use general-domain benchmarks on a ground GPU; space-domain tasks and flight hardware are left to future work.

- **[OMAR: Open-World Multi-Attribute Reasoning for Scene-Conditioned Shelf Organization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29761)**  `arXiv:2610.29761`  `cs.RO`  
  _Benedikt Kreis, Rohit Menon, Benno Wingender, Muhammad Ehsan Ullah, Nils Dengler, Maren Bennewitz_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  With the advent of foundation models, robots are increasingly capable of understanding and executing explicitly specified human tasks. Extending these capabilities to open-ended tasks, however, requires robots to infer appropriate actions from the observed scene. Household organization exemplifies this challenge, as robots must often act without detailed instructions, predefined object vocabularies, or known object-receptacle associations. While robots may follow instructions or learn organization preferences from demonstrations, inferring the latent organization of an occasionally changing scene and placing a novel object consistently with it remains challenging. We propose an open-world multi-attribute reasoning pipeline that grounds unknown objects across views and constructs an instance-level 3D scene graph for placement reasoning. First, a vision-language model proposes labels together with attributes such as content and packaging type, which are grounded through segmentation, depth, and multi-view association. Then, an LLM reasons over this representation to infer the latent shelf organization and rank scene-consistent object placements without explicitly user-provided preferences. The resulting placement rankings guide subsequent geometric and kinematic feasibility checks for execution. Our experimental evaluation shows that our approach leads to improved instance mapping and placement coherence over single-view language-grounding and geometry-only baselines, enabling autonomous placement of previously unseen objects without predefined object-receptacle mappings or class-specific retraining.

- **[CausGround: Causal Interaction Grounding for Adaptive Embodied Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29702)**  `arXiv:2610.29702`  `cs.RO`  
  _Yijun Shen, Diqi He, Jinbang Guo, Wanyue Zhang, Jiaming Mi, Huan Yang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) show promising reasoning capabilities, but reliable physical interaction requires planning that accounts for when actions are executable and how they change the world. These preconditions and effects are environment-dependent and often not captured by MLLMs, making adaptation challenging. Yet existing approaches either require costly environment-specific training or rely on unstructured interaction experience that is difficult to reuse. Therefore, we introduce CausGround, a non-parametric causal interaction grounding framework that distills interaction experience into reusable action-centered causal knowledge. CausGround identifies action preconditions and effects and organizes them in a factorized world-state representation capturing object attributes and inter-object relations. At planning time, task-relevant causal dependencies are grounded in current observations to support backward reasoning over preconditions and forward reasoning over effects for action selection and multi-step planning. We evaluate CausGround on simulated high-level planning, interaction reasoning, and real-world robot manipulation. On adapted VirtualHome and RoboCasa benchmarks, CausGround improves vision-based high-level planning across MLLM backbones, increasing average task success from 24.9% to 53.3% on VirtualHome and closed-loop planning success from 43.0% to 57.0% on RoboCasa. On SWITCH, average interaction reasoning accuracy increases from 25.8% to 31.9%, indicating improved action selection, recovery, and verification. Moreover, real-world experiments show that CausGround reasons backward from MLLM-interpreted goals to derive executable high-level action sequences for sequential manipulation. These results demonstrate that reusable action preconditions and effects support long-horizon embodied planning across diverse environments without parameter updates.

  Multi-Agent Debate (MAD) improves the reasoning performance of Large Language Models (LLMs) through multi-round interaction. However, LLMs in MAD are highly susceptible to blind conformity. - **[SPARE: Sparsity Allocation via Routing Entropy for Efficient MoEs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29662)**  `arXiv:2610.29662`  `cs.LG` `cs.CL`  
  _Yifan Yang, Hanling Yi, Dongquan Yang, Feng Lin, Rong Xiao, Aijun Li, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures scale model capacity efficiently, yet their substantial memory footprint poses a major barrier to deployment. Expert pruning reduces this footprint, but most methods focus on which experts to remove and prune every layer equally. We propose SPARE (Sparsity Allocation via Routing Entropy), a training-free, selector-agnostic method that decides how many experts to remove from each layer using routing entropy, the entropy of tokens' normalized Top-K gate weights on calibration data. When a token's gate weight is spread evenly, removing one active expert discards little of it, so SPARE prunes layers with more diffuse routing more aggressively. A perturbation bound under random pruning formalizes this intuition, and controlled interventions with a fixed selector link concentrated routing to larger output changes. With the same selector and budget, SPARE improves average accuracy over uniform allocation on Mixtral, Qwen3, and DeepSeek models, especially at high pruning ratios. At 50% expert pruning, it raises the eight-task average of Qwen3-30B-A3B from 57.2% to 63.8%, and the math, code, and science average of DeepSeek-R1 from 80.1 to 82.8.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), particularly through group-relative policy optimization (GRPO), has emerged as a central paradigm for the post-training of large language models (LLMs). While RLVR is highly effective at enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, it commonly induces \textit{distribution sharpening}, whereby the policy distribution becomes excessively peaked and rare-yet-correct trajectories are progressively suppressed, thus diminishing sample diversity.   _Chengxi Li, Hanlin Wu, Zhicheng Cai, Huan-ang Gao, Wei-Ying Ma, Jingjing Liu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Reinforcement learning (RL) based on PPO-style clipping has become an effective approach to improving the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs). However, standard token-level clipping measures policy change only through the conditional probability of the current token, while the prefix conditioning context is itself generated by the policy. This mismatch becomes especially consequential in long-response and off-policy training. We reformulate autoregressive generation as a finite-horizon Markov decision process and derive its performance-difference identity under behavior-policy sampling. The resulting change of measure identifies the cumulative likelihood ratio of the generated prefix as the exact importance weight for each stepwise policy-improvement term. This yields EPIC, a PPO-style sequence-clipping method that directly regulates prefix-level policy drift without relying on a local quadratic approximation or expert-routing replay. Experiments on off-policy reinforcement learning with a sparse mixture-of-experts language model show that EPIC consistently outperforms standard and sequence-level clipping baselines across mathematical-reasoning benchmarks, while remaining competitive with routing-replay-based alternatives. - **[LOADBench: A Large Open Analog Dataset and Benchmark for Machine Learning in IC Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21591)**  `arXiv:2610.21591`  `cs.AI`  
  _M. Leibl, F. Azevedo, S. Uhlich, A. Bonetti, A. Venkitaraman, C. Hsieh, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Machine learning (ML) for analog IC design is constrained by limited open data and the absence of standardized evaluation: results reported in different papers are typically obtained on bespoke, often closed, datasets and with ad-hoc metrics, leaving comparisons across methods unreliable. We address both gaps with a large opensource dataset and a suite of standardized benchmarks. The dataset contains more than 100 operational amplifier (opamp) topologies (1-, 2-, and 3-stage) in the SkyWater130 PDK, each shipped with structural and functional annotations, topology-specific sizing constraints (symmetry and sizing rules), an Ngspice testbench, and a multi-objective NSGA-II sizing run producing around 103 Pareto-optimal and 106 intermediate sizing-performance pairs per topology. On top of this data we define six benchmark tasks with precise protocols: inverse sizing, topology selection, sub-circuit classification, structural circuit generation, Pareto-membership prediction, and multi-objective optimization. Thus, the data presented here constitutes the most comprehensive dataset for ML development in over four decades of analog IC design automation. The dataset, ground-truth annotations, and reference implementations of the metric-defining routines are released under an open license, enabling reproducible and directly comparable evaluation across heterogeneous analog design automation methods.
