  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion-based vision-language-action (VLA) models cascade pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) with diffusion action experts, but typically restrict the VLM to providing static conditioning. This separation limits how much pretrained perception and reasoning can guide action generation, especially in out-of-distribution settings. We introduce JADE, a Joint Action DEnoiser that enables the VLM and action expert to jointly refine actions. Our key insight is that structured action latents allow the VLM to read the current noisy action together with visual observations and language instructions. Additionally, joint denoising enables JADE to generate actions with fewer sampling steps, helping offset the additional cost of VLM participation. An adaptive step-size schedule further balances task performance and inference latency. On RoboDojo, JADE achieves an average success rate of 12.67%, the highest among the evaluated $\pi_{0.5}$-based methods, and is competitive with recent VLA and WAM foundation models pretrained at scale. It also improves performance on RoboTwin 2.0 and raises the average success rate across four real-world bimanual tasks by 20.8 percentage points compared with $\pi_{0.5}$.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>

While recent Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM) agents show strong open-world reasoning capabilities, existing architectures that rely on direct tool calling struggle with geometric uncertainty and thus are limited to fully-observable tabletop environments. In contrast, Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) systems can solve long-horizon tasks under partial observability but incur high computational costs and require closed worlds. - **[Human Sensorimotor Model Adaptation to Account for Robot Accuracy and Speed Augmentation in Human-Robot Co-Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02181)**  `arXiv:2611.02181`  `cs.RO`  
  _Frits van Hall, Kevin Haninger, Luka Peternel_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  In human-robot co-manipulation, the robot's trajectory should accommodate the human's, where human sensorimotor behavior is affected by the task's spatial and temporal constraints, as well as personal factors. Several robot trajectory planning methods have been proposed to embed human sensorimotor models in the robot trajectory planning, but these methods do not consider temporal constraints that adapt to how the human behaves when interacting with the robot. To better align human and robot collaborative motion, this work adapts a stochastic optimal control problem with a final state constraint from the task constraints and a temporal constraint from Fitts' law.
