- **[Pretraining Knows What, Simulation Teaches How: Compositional Generalization for Agentic Robotics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35817)**  `arXiv:2610.35817`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Benjamin Pikus, Daniel Saunders, Mircea Mironenco, Salman Mohammadi, Mathew Mathew, Sirou Zhu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Frontier models such as GPT-6 Astra now directly control robots and can generalize better than vision-language-action models (VLAs). We argue that this ability could arise from two distinct, composable skills: semantic reasoning, to understand what a task asks for in a scene, and motion control, to move the robot to do it. - **[Asynchronous Robot Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05155)**  `arXiv:2611.05155`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Sombit Dey, Luc Van Gool, Danda Pani Paudel_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Scaling vision-language foundation models has significantly advanced generalist robot manipulation, but the increased model scale introduces computational latency that degrades real-time physical reactivity. Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architectures execute high-level semantic reasoning and action ex- ecution sequentially within the primary control loop, creating a fundamental tension between semantic-reasoning capability and robot agility. To address this, we propose Asynchronous Robot Policy (ARP), an architectural framework that decouples semantic reasoning from low-level action generation using asynchronous processes operating at independent frequencies. In ARP, a high-capacity Vision-Language Model (VLM) asynchronously updates a semantic memory at low fre- quency, while a lightweight Vision-Action Expert, conditioned on this semantic memory, generates robot actions at a much higher control frequency. Such a formulation, enables having a fast perception-action loop without compromising on the semantic capability. To overcome asynchronous memory staleness, ARP trains the vision-action expert on the semantic staleness and execution delay, with temporally mismatched observation across processes. On Franka manipulation tasks, ARP achieves an 18% higher success rate and 20% less time need for execution in quasi-static setups, while 3-5 times baseline performance in dynamic simulation environments. Furthermore, ARP enables operating the VLM at 10 times lower than its nominal frequency with no significant performance loss making it much more efficient than the baseline. These results highlight the architectural advantages of modeling robot policies as asynchronous processes, enabling real-world reactive deployment without sacrificing the scale of foundation models.

- **[LLM4Robot-Bench: How Far Can GPT-6 Astra Go in Robotic Manipulation? A Hierarchical, Paired Sim-Real Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34527)**  `arXiv:2610.34527`  `cs.RO`

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We introduce LLM4Robot-Bench, a hierarchical, paired Sim-Real benchmark for evaluating physical interaction capabilities in contact-rich manipulation. Its 16 household tasks span three cumulative levels: contact selection and geometric alignment (L1), force regulation within sustained contact (L2), and coordinated contact reconfiguration and force regulation (L3).   _Teng-Ruei Chen_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Routing benchmarks often judge a router against a hindsight oracle that counts a query as solved whenever any model's recorded answer is correct, but under stochastic decoding that oracle mixes two different objects: the clairvoyant ceiling of committing to one model per query and the premium of calling several models and keeping any correct answer. Only 31 responses reached the cap, 25 of them with exactly periodic tails, yet the capped responses account for up to 60.2% of a model's output-length-squared proxy, and long generations made a fourth planned model, Llama-3.1-8B, too slow to finish. The premium is far below the companion's 3.5 pp, but models, pool size and protocol changed together; an oracle gap should therefore be reported with the pool, the protocol, the samples per cell and the decoding budget that produced it.

- **[LoopVQ: Sub 1-bit KV Cache Quantization for Looped Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05304)**  `arXiv:2611.05304`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `cs.CL`  
  _Minsoo Cheong, Sungjoo Yoo_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Looped transformers are a parameter-efficient alternative to standard transformers for language modeling. By reapplying the same layers across multiple loop steps, recent looped language models match the performance of non-looped models several times their size. While this architecture effectively reduces parameter memory, each loop step still stores its own keys and values, so the key-value (KV) cache grows linearly with the number of loop steps and becomes the main memory bottleneck. We observe that the KV cache of looped models is highly redundant along the loop axis and that the loop steps differ substantially in their sensitivity to quantization. We also implement LoopVQ in SGLang, where it nearly halves the decode latency at a 32K-token context and raises the peak serving throughput by up to $4.3\times$ and $6.1\times$ on the 1.4B and 2.6B Ouro models compared to the BF16 baseline.

  Diffusion Large Language Models (dLLMs) have emerged as a compelling paradigm for language modeling, offering the unique potential for highly efficient inference via multi-token parallel decoding. However, unlocking this potential remains challenging. High-quality generation typically defaults to one-token-per-step static decoding, while existing parallel algorithms rely on heuristic confidence thresholds, introducing a fragile trade-off between decoding efficiency and generation quality.
