- **[ForeseeDrive: Future-Token Conditioning for Anticipatory Generative Driving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14286)**  `arXiv:2611.14286`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Shaokai Wu, Xunchu Zhou, Pinzhi Wang, Bo Dong, Yuting Hu, Yanbiao Ji, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Generative driving planners can represent diverse ego futures, but a downstream scorer can choose only among sampled candidates; it cannot recover an absent maneuver. We present ForeseeDrive, which turns future scene evolution into a deployable interface for shaping candidate generation. Rather than reconstructing dense future states or scoring a fixed menu, ForeseeDrive derives query-indexed tokens from logged actor motion, occupancy evolution, route interactions, and TTC margins, and predicts the same token space from present context at deployment. The tokens condition trajectory geometry before candidates materialize. A zero-token-gated residual path and candidate-preservation loss limit unnecessary drift, while a TTC-margin guard targets brittle safe-to-unsafe boundary crossings. Controlled DiffusionDrive/NAVSIM diagnostics show an exact zero-token no-op, a small aligned-over-shuffled selected-PDM advantage under preservation, mixed oracle-best quality, and localized TTC-boundary failures. Together, the benchmark results and bounded mechanism tests support future-conditioned generation as a practical interface for anticipation. Code is included in the supplementary materials.

- **[PARLEY: Evaluating LLM Collaboration Through Cooperative Asymmetric Puzzles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14242)**  `arXiv:2611.14242`  `cs.MA`  
  _Javier Hernandez, Andrew D. Wilson_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  As large language models power autonomous agents, understanding how they collaborate and where they fail becomes critical. We introduce PARLEY, an extensible platform for studying LLM agent collaboration through four asymmetric puzzle tasks grounded in cognitive psychology (referential communication, joint coordination, perspective-taking, and spatial reasoning). GPT-4o agent pairs succeeded in 60.5% of conversations (N=400, 100 per task), with task-level variation (34-80%) reflecting distinct collaborative demands. From these conversations, we derived the data-driven PAC taxonomy: six failure types organized into Perception (visual interpretation and spatial reasoning), Adaptation (progress tracking and feedback integration), and Coordination (verification and grounding). Failure to incorporate external feedback was a critical indicator of unsuccessful collaboration, with agents repeatedly applying unsuccessful strategies. We also show that a taxonomy-guided intervention (N=200) improved average task success to 70.5%. These results demonstrate how PARLEY can be used to derive meaningful, data-driven insights that can in turn lead to improved agentic collaboration.

- **[Free Lunch Uncertainty: Sequence-Level Rényi Entropy from Particle Decoding for Adaptive LLM Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13414)**  `arXiv:2611.13414`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `cs.CL`  
  _Erfan Baghaei Potraghloo, Milad Heidari Sorkhehdizaj, Shahab Rahimirad, Aliasghar Pourghani, Seyedarmin Azizi, Souvik Kundu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Quantifying uncertainty over an entire LLM completion is critical for selective prediction and adaptive compute. Zero-cost signals such as mean token entropy and log-probability are local to tokens or to one completion, and self-consistency needs many extra completions. We show that Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) decoding for power sampling already estimates the normalizing constant of the power distribution, which gives the sequence-level R'enyi entropy (SRE) of the model's completion distribution at zero marginal cost, with a guarantee at every particle budget. A R'enyi chain rule extends this readout to a family of stepwise entropies of any order. We prove that orders above one are tied to the most likely token of each step, which explains why they track log-probability, while the order-1/2 member accumulates the log of per-step search effort. This member is the best zero-cost selective-prediction signal in 10 of 13 settings. On MATH500 its AUROC is 0.022 to 0.044 above log-probability with all four models and within length quartiles, and the same order is selected when each setting is held out. An 8-particle probe identifies the problems that benefit from more particles, and a two-pass policy matches 64-particle accuracy on MATH500 with 34.2% fewer particles over three seeds.

- **[Auditing the Judge: Internal Signals for LLM Reasoning Assurance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14323)**  `arXiv:2611.14323`  `cs.LG`  
  _Mayank Gulati, Gerhard Wunder_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Language-model systems use self-judgment to decide which answers to release, review, or recompute. Because intervention is costly, an assurance score must rank errors well at the available budget, not merely over the full ranking. We compare the judge's continuous verdict-token score with a supervised correctness probe trained on attention-head activations from the same prompt. Exact identities link fixed-budget error recall to residual risk and show that AUROC averages recall over all budgets. Across three open 7-12B models on GSM8K and MATH, the probe finds 1.3-22.5 additional percentage points of errors at 5% review in all six model-dataset settings (median 7.4 points). Internal signals can improve supervised, in-domain budget allocation, but are not domain-general confidence estimators.

- **[Tree-Ising: Repairing Language Models with Bit-Level Ising Edits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14210)**  `arXiv:2611.14210`  `cs.LG`  
  _Hadi Salloum, Ammar Ali_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Weight-space programming, introduced in recent MIT work by Takehi et al., enables a frontier LLM agent to read a network's weights and write a repair. Their GPT-6 Astra agent corrected the reasoning loops of Qwen3.5-0.8B without gradients, using 158 agent turns, 89.7 million tokens, and 50 forward-pass requests. Our method, Tree-Ising, casts this repair as a sparse discrete optimization problem over the model's own BF16 weights. Forward statistics define an objective that weakens a loop direction, strengthens a concluding direction, and preserves the layer's output on productive tokens. Within a state-tree representation, where lossless BF16 storage acts as a special case, the admissible edits are sign flips and exponent shifts that preserve all shared group exponents and mantissas. We decompose this objective exactly into independent QUBO (Ising) problems, one per output row, which we solve on a GPU using simulated annealing or quantum-inspired simulated bifurcation. On the agent's two layers, Tree-Ising achieves 54.6% on MATH-500, outperforming the agent's edit (51.7%) under the same evaluation protocol, while the base model scores 7.9% as reported. The simulated bifurcation solver yields a statistically indistinguishable repair at 55.1%. Both solvers outperform the agent edit across all five math benchmarks, with statistically significant improvements on MATH-500 and GSM8K after correcting for multiple comparisons. Our approach modifies 13% to 17% fewer weights and reduces BF16 bit flips by a factor of 2.2 to 2.3. It requires only four forward-pass requests and zero LLM tokens, with the annealing recipe costing about $0.90 in GPU time. On Qwen3.5-2B, with layers and dose selected using only the base model, the same recipe increases greedy GSM8K accuracy from 21.6% to 69.4%. As a proof of concept, we execute an eight-variable repair subproblem using QAOA on an IBM Heron processor.
