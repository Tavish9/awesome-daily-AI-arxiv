- **[TinyAstral: Transferring Self-Evolving Harnesses to Tiny Generalist Manipulation Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41216)**  `arXiv:2610.41216`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Kehong Gong, Xiaoxu Zheng, Guanli Hou, Seunghun Lee, Mengqi Guo, Runjia Tan, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robot agents built on vision-language models depend on both the planner and its harness: the tools, feedback, memory, and recovery logic around it. Can improvements to a large teacher's harness be carried into a compact agent with lower inference compute cost? We present TinyAstral, a two-stage approach. We first freeze an open-weight teacher and improve its harness through recursive self-improvement (RSI): frontier LLM agents read the teacher's trajectories and propose harness edits, and each edit is accepted only if it passes a paired statistical test. We then freeze the final harness, collect teacher trajectories with reasoning and failure recovery, and distill a small student that replaces the teacher in the same harness. On RoboTwin, harness evolution raises the teacher's success from 33.4% to 63.6%; the final 4B student reaches 62.8%. Across four harness versions, distilled 4B students score close to their corresponding teachers, and later versions yield stronger students, showing that harness evolution gains carry into compact models. The harness gains also transfer to other frozen planners without retraining. On identical episodes, the final 4B student outperforms open 4B agents, the open 27B harness-RSI model SenseNova-RoboRSI, and our own teacher model placed in three competitors' harnesses. It stays close to its teacher on all three training benchmarks, and on three benchmarks never used for RSI or training it scores close to a 552B planner running the same harness. On the measured deployment episodes, it uses about one fifth of its teacher's accelerator time.

  _Jiaming Wang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Inserting an unseen part requires sub-millimetre precision and the correct orientation of keyed features. Current zero-shot agentic manipulation methods built on vision-language models (VLMs) produce spatial outputs that are far too coarse for this. We propose to let a VLM build a sketch model of the task, i.e., a few geometric primitives and two mate frames, which a VLM agent measures with geometric tools in multi-view depth and which a standard planner and compliant controller execute.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>

While control barrier functions (CBFs) provide a principled mechanism for collision avoidance, their behavior is typically determined from geometric state alone and therefore cannot distinguish between semantically different situations with similar physical configurations.   _Penghui Qi, Zichen Liu, Tianyu Pang, Chao Du, Wee Sun Lee, Min Lin_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Scaling test-time compute is crucial for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs). Existing approaches typically employ reinforcement learning (RL) to maximize a verifiable reward obtained at the end of reasoning traces. However, such methods optimize only the final performance under a large and fixed token budget, which hinders efficiency in both training and deployment. In this work, we present a novel framework, AnytimeReasoner, to optimize anytime reasoning performance, which aims to improve token efficiency and the flexibility of reasoning under varying token budget constraints. Empirical results in mathematical reasoning tasks demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms GRPO across all thinking budgets under various prior distributions, enhancing both training and token efficiency.

These findings indicate that prompting can steer models into a temporal frame, but does not endow them with the ability to verify whether a response is temporally admissible. We further argue that supervised fine-tuning is insufficient, since ex-ante correctness is not an intrinsic property of an answer, but a relation between the answer and the cutoff. To address this gap, we propose TCFT, a Temporal Critique Fine-Tuning framework that trains models to acquire cutoff-aware temporal verification. Given a query, a cutoff, and a candidate response, TCFT teaches the model to identify post-cutoff leakage, explain temporal boundary violations, and judge temporal admissibility. Experiments with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct show that TCFT consistently outperforms prompting and SFT baselines, reducing average leakage by 41.89 and 37.79 percentage points, respectively.

  Large reasoning models achieve strong performance through reinforcement learning but can suffer safety degradation. External teacher distillation can restore safety yet may compromise reasoning performance. We formalize safety realignment as a KL projection: the student's native filter-accepted distribution is uniquely KL-optimal, while teacher targets incur excess KL when they differ. Guided by this analysis, we propose ThinkSafe, which generates safety-alignment data from the frozen student without external teachers. Refusal steering elicits safety reasoning, and a safety guard filters the responses before offline fine-tuning. Our analysis bounds steering-induced target distortion and gives a sufficient condition for lower average target KL than teacher distillation. Experiments on DeepSeek-R1-Distill and Qwen3 show substantial safety improvements with competitive reasoning performance.
