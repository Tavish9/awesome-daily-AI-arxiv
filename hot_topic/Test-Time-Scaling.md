- **[UniLoop: Unified Loop Closure Detection from Retrieval to Geometric Verification and Relative Pose Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06378)**  `arXiv:2611.06378`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Jingwen Yu, Pengyu Wang, Jin Wu, Weinan Chen, Ping Tan, Hong Zhang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Loop closure detection conventionally cascades separate models for global retrieval, geometric verification, and relative pose estimation, leading to redundant computation and disconnected visual representations. We propose UniLoop, a unified framework that reuses the hierarchical representations of the pretrained geometric foundation model throughout this pipeline. A shared image encoder produces features for compact global descriptors; the same features are passed to the cross-view decoder for pairwise verification; and the original geometric predictions provide relative camera constraints for pose-graph optimization. - **[SCRUB: Contact-Based Surface-Frame Estimation for Force-Motion Control with an Armless Quadruped](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06649)**  `arXiv:2611.06649`  `cs.RO`  
  _Sourav Selvaraj, Juan Pablo Espinosa Chessal, Mahdi Agheli_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Wiping, sanding, and inspection robots press a sliding tool on a surface with controlled force. Controlling that force needs the surface normal, which is unknown on real-world, unmapped surfaces. Surface-normal estimators can help estimate the surface normal, but they either need a friction model or assume that the instantaneous tool velocity lies along the surface; however, walking violates this assumption. We propose SCRUB, a contact-based surface-frame estimator, which lets an armless quadruped slide a body-mounted tool along near-vertical surfaces in the absence of known, axis-aligned, or vision-predicted normals. SCRUB's predictive controller presses and slides the tool using a Kalman-filter estimate of the normal from two cues: 1) A force cue measures the estimate's tilt across the motion. If friction directly opposes the motion, the cue is zero exactly when that tilt is zero, for any friction coefficient. However, if the cue's sliding direction is wrong, friction appears as a false tilt. 2) A velocity cue measures tilt along the motion. Walking and pressing move the tool in and out. SCRUB therefore subtracts the commanded press rate from the tool velocity, averages the result over each gait cycle, and treats this mean as lying along the surface. On Unitree Go1 hardware at nominal speed and load, SCRUB completed every wall traverse and column orbit. No orbit completed with either cue removed, without gait averaging, or with a fixed normal.
  </details>

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Reinforcement learning (RL) has become central to improving large language models (LLMs) on complex reasoning tasks, yet RL post-training is largely studied in isolation from the pretraining that precedes it. As a result, two basic questions remain open: (1) how do pretraining choices (model size, data) shape the returns to RL compute, and (2) what does RL actually do to the model?
