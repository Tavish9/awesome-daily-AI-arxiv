- **[Decoding Geometry, Not Tokens: Efficient VLMs for Robot Affordance and Trajectory Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39416)**  `arXiv:2610.39416`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Akila Ayanthi, Darshana Priyasad, Tharindu Fernando, Dimity Miller, Sridha Sridharan, Clinton Fookes, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Predicting where to grasp and how to move is fundamentally a geometry problem. Vision-language models treat it as language generation instead. They tokenize spatial coordinates into text and decode them autoregressively. This mechanism ignores spatial structure and truncates continuous precision. Errors compound across coordinate tokens during decoding. In this paper, we show that compact off-the-shelf VLMs with 0.5-2B parameters already encode rich spatial information in their intermediate hidden states. Autoregressive decoding is the dominant bottleneck for trajectory prediction, while affordance prediction depends additionally on feature depth and decoder architecture. We expose the latent spatial information with a lightweight, geometry-compatible prediction head that attaches to a frozen backbone. It reads features from an intermediate hidden layer, conditions a learned query on the task instruction and refines it via cross-attention over the visual tokens to regress affordance boxes and trajectory waypoints directly as continuous values, without modifying the backbone. We evaluate on ShareRobot-Bench and AGD20K. Our 2B variant reduces trajectory error by over 60% and improves ShareRobot-Bench affordance mAP by 85% relative to the autoregressive baseline. It approaches RoboBrain on trajectory prediction (0.251 vs 0.191 DFD) at 3.8x higher throughput. These results demonstrate a favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off for instruction-conditioned 2D geometric prediction.

- **[Generalizable VLA Finetuning via Representation Anchoring and Language-Action Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13429)**  `arXiv:2607.13429`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Dwip Dalal, Shivansh Patel, Chahit Jain, Jeonghwan Kim, Utkarsh Mishra, Yuchen Song, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Finetuning a pretrained vision-language model (VLM) on robot demonstrations via behavior cloning (BC) has become the standard recipe for vision-language-action (VLA) policies. However, BC finetuning progressively overwrites the pretrained representations that support visual and semantic generalization. Co-training on web image-text data, a common remedy, applies language and action losses to separate observations, leaving VLAs with language-action misalignment that standard manipulation benchmarks do not expose. We propose Anchor-Align, which augments BC with two objectives: Vision-Language Anchoring distills layer-wise representations from a frozen VLM copy to prevent this drift, while Language-Action Alignment converts each action target into a discrete motion-direction label and jointly trains language and action prediction on the same robot observation. Across these settings, Anchor-Align consistently improves over BC on novel targets, layouts, and motion-sensitive bimanual tasks requiring coordinated control. At scale in simulation, we demonstrate consistent improvements on OOD perturbations, perceptual robustness, and long-horizon control across LIBERO-PRO, LIBERO-Plus, and CALVIN, respectively, suggesting that preserving pretrained representations and effective action learning are not fundamentally at odds. - **[SpaceAct: Benchmarking 3D Spatial and Temporal Reasoning via Closed-Loop Action and Active Perception](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38405)**  `arXiv:2610.38405`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Chenglong Shi, Zekai Yuan, Yue Song, Jon Pu, Will Wu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  To solve problems in 3D space rather than merely describe images, multimodal large language models (MLLMs) need 3D spatiotemporal reasoning: reconstructing 3D geometry from occluded 2D views, anticipating physical dynamics, and executing precise, closed-loop actions. Prior studies test passive VQA on static images or symbolic planning, requiring no execution or supplying privileged coordinates while ignoring collision, statics, and timing. - **[Evidence-Gated Task and Motion Planning with Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20084)**  `arXiv:2608.20084`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Tsunehiko Tanaka, Matthew Stephenson, Alistair Macvicar, Edgar Simo-Serra_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robots executing long-horizon manipulation tasks from natural-language instructions must reason about both semantic task structure and geometric feasibility. Hierarchical approaches combining Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) offer a promising solution, but VLM-generated subgoals may rely on prior knowledge without sufficient observational support. This can lead to the omission of task-relevant subgoals or attempts to execute instructions whose requirements cannot be met in the environment. We propose Evidence Acquisition and Feasibility Gating (EAFG), a framework for evidence-gated task and motion planning that acquires visual evidence to specify appropriate task plans and assess whether given instructions can be fulfilled. When proceeding, the VLM generates task subgoals conditioned on the acquired evidence, and TAMP computes low-level actions. We evaluate EAFG in three cooking scenarios involving explicitly specified required objects, underspecified instructions, and instructions requiring an absent object. EAFG improves task completion over a VLM-TAMP baseline in the explicit and underspecified scenarios and enables appropriate halting when a required object is absent.

- **[PhysVLMM: Interaction-Conditioned Physical Belief Maps for Articulated Objects in Robot Scene Maps](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40539)**  `arXiv:2610.40539`  `cs.RO`  
  _Dibyendu Ghosh_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Mobile robots must open doors and drawers, yet their scene maps record what each object is and whether it has moved, not how it will respond to a pull. This paper proposes PhysVLMM, a method that maintains a physical belief for every articulated part of a Vision-Language-Motion Map and answers a planner's queries about it, with two algorithms: a map update and a query. Watching a part move provably cannot identify its dynamics, and a measured pull identifies only the pulled part, up to one direction. PhysVLMM therefore (i) keeps that direction as a posterior instead of a point estimate, (ii) passes the evidence of each pull in closed form to the untouched look-alike parts of the map, and (iii) answers whether an action will succeed at a stated risk, or abstains. On real doors and drawers in 18 homes, it identifies the joint type in 86% of held-out pulls (48% for a learned single-view method) and predicts the response to a pull with 41% lower error than differentiable simulation (CRPS 0.323 vs. 0.544), four orders of magnitude faster (13 ms vs. 230 s per case). The posterior lowers the final-position error from 0.460 to 0.270, sharing lowers the force error of untouched parts from 56% to 42%, and at every stated risk from 0.02 to 0.30 the false-safe answers stay within it. Pooling all measured parts of a home does as well as the look-alike groups, and simpler variants predict where a pulled part stops at least as well.

- **[Surrogate Uncertainty for Black-box Visual Grounding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39798)**  `arXiv:2610.39798`  `cs.RO`  
  _Hanbo Zhang, Tianrun Hu, Cedar Site Bai, Anxing Xiao, David Hsu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Visual grounding is crucial for robots to understand language instructions with visual observations. This paper addresses the problem of visual grounding uncertainties, where a robot uses a black-box vision-language model (VLM) to ground free-form natural-language instructions to target objects. Prevailing methods derive uncertainty from the VLM itself. Yet, outputs of the VLM, e.g., generated uncertainties, are from the same parameters and structures producing the predictions, and inherit the bias. We give a formal Bayesian definition of the problem that treats the VLM prediction as an observation. An external surrogate Bayesian model then quantifies per-query uncertainty over a local neighborhood of a held-out calibration set, decomposed into aleatoric and epistemic components. A hierarchical retrieval across input modalities realizes a compact joint-space neighborhood, mitigating the curse of dimensionality. The estimator further integrates with conformal prediction or model-based planning for active visual grounding, where robots are allowed to take verbal or physical actions to ground targets. Across grounding benchmarks and state-of-the-art VLMs, it consistently improves both calibration and task performance. On a real Fetch robot under open-world instructions, it enables principled trade-offs between confident action and information gathering, substantially improving robustness.
