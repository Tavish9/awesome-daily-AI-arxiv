- **[I See What You Want: Decoding Hierarchical Operator Intent from Visual Teleoperation History](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16419)**  `arXiv:2611.16419`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Xiangyi Jia, Kaixian Qu, Shengzhi Wang, Chenyu Yang, Robert Katzschmann, Marco Hutter_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language models (VLMs) have become a powerful foundation for robotic scene understanding, task planning, and language-conditioned control. A natural question follows: can these models also decode what a human operator intends while teleoperating a robot, without any explicit instruction? We formulate this as hierarchical intent prediction, jointly inferring the ongoing atomic action and the broader mission it serves from a short visual teleoperation history. To study this, we introduce VI AMO (Vision Intention Action and Mission Ongoing), a dataset of 6,600 episodes from eleven robot sources with 13,653 temporally aligned action annotations and 9,702 mission annotations. Large proprietary models struggle with this problem: on held-out episodes, GPT-4o reaches Sentence-BERT similarities of only 0.563 and 0.470 for action and mission, and GPT-5.6 Luna achieves only 0.581 and 0.554. Fine-tuning a VLM on VI AMO yields our Vision-Intention Model (VIM), which raises these scores to 0.825 and 0.821 and still reaches 0.726 and 0.717 on the 35.4% of test actions with both labels absent from training. Finally, as a downstream application, VIM's predicted mission enables a vision-language-action (VLA) policy to take over from the operator mid-task in a real-world environment unseen during VIM fine-tuning, reducing human teleoperation time by 34.6% relative to GPT-4o and 39.8% relative to GPT-5.6 Luna, while cutting intent-prediction latency by roughly 70% on average.

- **[COMBS: Concealed Object Multi-modal Bayesian Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15964)**  `arXiv:2611.15964`  `cs.RO`  
  _Taylor Bergeron, Kevin Leahy_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  This work addresses the problem of finding a concealed object in an environment by unifying formalisms for partially observable environments with multi-modal priors drawn from a mix of ontologies, semantic networks, LLMs, and VLMs. We propose a concealment-adaptive mixed-observability MDP (CA-MOMDP), a Bayes-adaptive setting where uncertainty is over both object locations and which nodes can conceal them, held as a hypothesis with a bounded ontology prior and resolved only by interaction. We define a hierarchical transition system (HTS) to abstract the environment into room and object nodes that can be planned over via our CA-MOMDP. We further propose a COMBS-score: a multi-modal fusion of a human informed semantic network, LLM, and VLM, to initialize the belief over object locations, which grounds our abstractions and reduces reliance on a single model. Belief is maintained via Bayes' rule, which never lets a look rule out a closed container. We prove a present target is found in a bounded number of searches under exact perception and that its location is never ruled out given missed detection under noisy perception. We ablate each component to demonstrate their significance. Across five simulator configurations, from a gridworld to Matterport3D with a real detector, COMBS finds 92-100% of in-distribution and 92-100% of out-of-distribution targets, matches or beats the baselines in processing speed, significantly outperforms a per-step LLM policy at lower cost on the larger scenes, recovers more out-of-distribution placements than a TidyBot ranking, and matches an LLM-only prior at a fraction of its per-decision latency. COMBS is the only standalone method that can declare a target absent at a stated error level, and the per-step LLM policy offered a termination action abandons 12-22% of targets that were present, against 1.6-3.2% for COMBS on the same episodes.

- **[ActionCodec2: A Streaming Action Tokenizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15490)**  `arXiv:2611.15490`  `cs.RO`  
  _Zibin Dong, Yicheng Liu, Shiduo Zhang, Tianyuan Yuan, Baijun Ye, Jinqiu Wang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Most action tokenizers for autoregressive vision language action (VLA) models compress motion into chunks that must be decoded in full before their actions become executable. We introduce ActionCodec2, a non-neural streaming tokenizer that releases actions incrementally while keeping trajectory reconstruction error within a prescribed tolerance. Its encoder quantizes each step relative to the decoder's reconstructed state, so the selected motion primitive corrects residual error rather than allowing errors from independently quantized increments to accumulate. Finding the smallest such primitive set is a geometric covering problem; we derive the exact minimum for translation and constant-factor-tight bounds for rotation. Set-BPE then losslessly merges co-occurring primitive symbols across coordinates and neighboring steps, without a fixed coordinate ordering. A universal version fitted on 20k hours of data from 24 embodiments averages 8.8 tokens per second for 15 Hz end-effector motion on this corpus. With the same VLA backbone, ActionCodec2 outperforms the compared tokenizers on three simulation benchmarks and in zero-shot real-robot evaluation. Its streaming representation allows policies to incorporate state feedback within an action chunk, substantially improving performance over open-loop execution under randomized dynamics, external disturbances, and moving objects. The same tokens also can serve as a direct control interface for VLM agents, which compose successful action sequences into reusable macros. These agent-built macros reduce control decisions and thinking-token usage across attempts and transfer to a held-out task. More results are available atthis https URL

- **[Progress Probes $P^2$: Lightweight Runtime Monitoring for Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31613)**  `arXiv:2610.31613`  `cs.RO`  
  _Atiksh Bhardwaj, Edward W. Duan, Junwon Seo, Prithwish Dan, Wei-Chiu Ma, Andrea Bajscy, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can perform diverse manipulation tasks directly from visual observations and natural language instructions, but they remain prone to mistakes and unpredictable failures. Deploying these policies without continuous human supervision will require robust runtime monitors that detect when the policy has gone off-course. Existing monitors use external foundation models, such as vision-language models (VLMs) to supervise the policy, introducing significant complexity and computational cost for monitoring at runtime. In this work, we investigate an alternate approach: \textit{can a VLA's own internal activations provide the information needed to monitor its execution?} Our method trains linear probes on policy activations from demonstrations to estimate progress, then flags executions that deviate from expected progress. In experiments with the open-source VLA $\pi_{0.5}$ and across simulated and real-world manipulation tasks, this approach is competitive with or outperforms VLM-based monitors in task failure detection, as well as methods which require failure data for training. These findings suggest that simple probes of VLA internals can be used to develop reliable runtime monitors, opening a path toward building policies that can act reliably and monitor their execution without external foundation models.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Despite impressive advances in large language models (LLM), vision-language models (VLM) still exhibit limited spatial reasoning capabilities. - **[Unbundling Multimodal Vision Encoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17334)**  `arXiv:2611.17334`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Ellis Brown, Chris Hoang, Shengyi Qian, Shang-Wen Li, Mike Lewis, Marjan Ghazvininejad, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  How multimodal language models should encode visual input remains up for debate. Most models use a pretrained vision encoder, while some recent recipes train the encoder from scratch with the language model or remove it entirely. Vision encoders bundle together three design choices: a visual prior from upstream pretraining, dedicated vision capacity, and pre-fusion placement of that capacity (i.e., before the multimodal backbone). Because published models also differ in data, architecture, and compute, comparing them cannot isolate the effect of each choice. We disentangle these choices via a controlled framework where we pretrain all models from scratch on common data and compare them on visual question answering and language benchmarks at matched lifetime compute. We find that the visual prior is not necessary; an encoder trained jointly from scratch is on par with a pretrained one, without a separate training stage. What matters instead is dedicated vision capacity: an encoder-free model trails on visual understanding even when its backbone is enlarged by the encoder's parameter count. Yet that capacity need not precede multimodal fusion, as vision-only modules added in-fusion to early backbone layers perform comparably to a pre-fusion encoder and can lower time to first token. These results also hold across backbone sizes, when encoder pretraining is treated as free, and when starting from a pretrained language model. Overall, our findings favor dedicated vision capacity trained jointly with the backbone and placed early within it.

- **[MAST: Medoid-Anchored Subspace Steering for Visual Grounding in Multimodal LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15168)**  `arXiv:2611.15168`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Zhitao Chen, Zhenxin Cai, Jingyuan Li, Jin Sha_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Existing activation steering methods for multimodal large language models (MLLMs) use the global activation mean as the steering vector, which tends to cause cross-task interference and unstable performance. We trace this failure to the multi-centered geometry of task activations. The global mean lands in the empty region between clusters and is faithful to no single task. Moreover, the steering signal in each task-pure local cluster is low-rank, and different query tasks benefit from emphasizing different families of local-basis directions under the shared routing protocol. To address this, we propose MAST (Medoid-Anchored Subspace Steering), a training-free, input-adaptive activation steering framework. MAST groups samples by task, performs multi-center clustering within each group, and selects one representative medoid per cluster. At inference time, each query is routed to multiple representative medoids. For each matched cluster, a low-rank local steering direction is extracted and aggregated across clusters to produce the final steering vector. Building on MAST, MAST-PR (Probe-Reweighted) uses a lightweight task-aware probe to reweight task-specialized local-direction families, yielding a further average improvement of +8.09 points over MAST.

- **[UMER: Unifying Embedding and Ranking via Pair-Aware Discriminative Reasoning for Universal Multimodal Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18504)**  `arXiv:2608.18504`  `cs.AI`

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Modern Web information access increasingly involves heterogeneous multimodal content, including images, videos and visually rich documents. Users may seek not only globally similar content but also candidates that satisfy fine-grained attributes, relations or answer evidence under complex instructions. Supporting such access requires both efficient corpus-scale retrieval and fine-grained relevance reasoning. Multimodal large language model (MLLM)-based embedding methods derive representations from MLLM hidden states, and recent work further uses Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning to improve embedding capability by injecting intermediate semantic evidence into the representation. However, existing CoT methods typically use item-wise reasoning over queries and candidates in isolation, providing no explicit evidence to distinguish a positive from a semantically confusable hard negative. Moreover, contrastive embeddings capture global similarity but struggle with meta-tasks requiring answer verification, category judgment or fine-grained reasoning. In this paper, we propose UMER, a Unified Multimodal Embedding and Ranking framework for universal multimodal retrieval. UMER replaces item-wise reflection with PairAware Discriminative Reasoning, which compares query-candidate pairs to identify instruction-relevant matching and discrepancy evidence. UMER jointly learns contrastive embeddings for efficient global matching and discriminative ranking for explicit pairwise relevance judgment within a single MLLM. A complementary mutual distillation strategy further transfers reliable pairwise preferences between the embedding and ranking functions. On MMEB-V2, UMER-R scores 65.5/70.6 with Qwen2-VL-2B/7B, outperforming the strongest included matched-backbone baselines by 1.4/2.0 points. UMER also supports budget-adjustable inference from efficient embedding retrieval to optional pair-aware reranking.
