- **[Contact Harness: Enabling VLM Agents to Perform Contact-Rich Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29377)**  `arXiv:2611.29377`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Shilin Shan, Yuxuan Hu, Chuhao Zhou, Yimeng Dong, Bohan Hou, Jiaqi Bai, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Agents built on pretrained VLMs have demonstrated zero-shot control of real robots, but vision alone is often insufficient for reliable contact-state assessment in contact-rich manipulation. Force and tactile feedback provide complementary information, but their semantic ambiguity makes them difficult for agents to use directly. These signals must be interpreted according to their physical meaning, together with task phase and visual evidence. We propose Contact Harness, which uses reusable, optimized skills to guide a VLM agent in assessing contact states from multimodal feedback and selecting subsequent actions. A compliant execution mechanism regulates in-contact motions using real-time force feedback. The framework requires no model fine-tuning, task demonstrations, or simulation training. We conduct zero-shot real-robot evaluations using GPT-6 Astra on Rokae and Franka robots. With a single prompt skill, Contact Harness achieves a full-insertion rate of 90.7% across 15 connector types, with further evaluations on a real PC under variations in lighting, background, and position. Across four additional contact-rich tasks, including whiteboard wiping, writing, peeling, and pivoting, Contact Harness achieves an average success rate of 100%. On six simulated tasks, Contact Harness reaches 96.7% success, compared with at most 68.3% for pretrained VLA baselines fine-tuned on task demonstrations, showing the potential of agent-based manipulation for contact-rich real-world deployment.

- **[Tripwire: Robot Agents that Program Their Own Interrupts under Human Disturbance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27182)**  `arXiv:2611.27182`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Sunli Chen, Yun Zong, Qinhong Zhou, Xiaowen Qiu, Jiaben Chen, Zeyuan Yang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Agent harnesses are turn-based: the agent acts, waits for the result, then reasons. When the action invokes a pretrained robot policy, such as a vision-language-action (VLA) or world action model (WAM), the policy may react continuously to observations, but the agent cannot revisit its decision until the call returns. Meanwhile, a person may step into the robot's path or move the object it is about to grasp. Invoking the agent more often narrows this blind interval, but a reasoning model takes seconds per decision and most control steps need no new one. We propose TRIP, a training-free, event-driven harness in which the agent programs its own interrupts. With each call, the agent writes a tripwire: a lightweight, stateful Python program that runs alongside the policy, can query a compact VLM (for example, to locate a person and measure their distance), and decides whether the call continues or control returns to the agent. When it fires, the harness halts the policy and returns control with the reason; the agent then reassesses, adapts, and continues under a rewritten tripwire. We evaluate TRIP with three VLAs and one WAM on RoboHD, a benchmark of 168 RoboCasa365 scenes in which a simulated person disturbs the robot and any human-robot contact counts as failure. Compared with a turn-based harness using the same agent and tools, TRIP halves the rate of human contact with the strongest policy, raising safety from 61.3% to 81.3%, without sacrificing task success (38.7% vs. 31.0%). Real-robot experiments show that TRIP completes two manipulation tasks under human interference where the turn-based harness and the policy alone largely fail.

- **[3DVLA: Enhancing Vision-Language-Action Models via 3D Spatial and Instance Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29416)**  `arXiv:2605.29416`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Zhongyu Xia, Yousen Tang, Bingqing Wei, Yongtao Wang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action models have achieved remarkable progress in robotic manipulation, yet they suffer from a critical limitation: a lack of 3D scene understanding. This deficiency manifests as three intertwined challenges: weak extraction of 3D spatial positions without enforcing multi-view consistency, inadequate 3D instance understanding, and fragile reasoning under occlusion. Although mature 3D perception methods exist, their direct integration into VLA pipelines is hindered by architectural incompatibility and by heavy reliance on costly instance-level annotations. To address these challenges, we propose 3DVLA, a framework compatible with existing VLA models, which introduces 3D understanding without requiring extra manual labels or discarding VLM priors. Specifically, 3DVLA tackles the three challenges through: (1) pervasive 3D feature encoding with explicit multi-view consistency constraints across all modalities and a Spatially-Conditioned Geometry Aggregation method, (2) an object-centric 3D instance module with high-level instance tokens for 3D instance awareness, and (3) a masked self-supervised 3D encoding branch that retains its predictor for visual token completion to handle occlusions. We integrate 3DVLA with multiple VLA baselines and evaluate on LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin 2.0, and real-world settings. Results show consistent and significant gains in manipulation performance, validating both the effectiveness and plug-and-play compatibility of our approach.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA) policies can execute individual skills but fail to complete tasks that require combining several of them. In our audit of Xiaomi-Robotics-1, an estimated 43\% of failures involve choosing the wrong target or losing track of task progress. Wrapping a frozen VLA in a vision-language model (VLM) harness is a natural remedy. Existing harnesses can gain substantially from task- and policy-specific preparation, raising the question of how much of that gain transfers to a new task or policy. An ideal harness should (i) need minimal preparation for a new task, (ii) need no redesign for a new policy, and (iii) preserve standalone capabilities the policy already has.   _Benjamin Alt_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Frontier vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly used as high-level robot controllers, yet their safety is evaluated almost entirely in text. We introduce SayDo, a benchmark that assesses the propensity of frontier models to cause harm in embodied contexts. SayDo comprises eleven laboratory and household tasks that pair a benign instruction with a hazard latent in the scene. Tasks are implemented in six settings along an embodiment gradient, from chat questions about an imagined robot to closed-loop control of a simulated robot arm.
