- **[MESA: An Evaluation Framework for Compositional, Semantic, and Spatial Generalization in Robotics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05567)**  `arXiv:2611.05567`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Albert Wilcox, Frank Chang, Nhi Nguyen, Aishani Chakraborty, Jeremy A. Collins, Vaibhav Saxena, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Despite growing interest in foundation models for robotic manipulation, systematic evaluation of their capabilities remains elusive. Existing benchmarks are either narrow and saturated, offering little signal on SOTA methods, broadly scoped, providing diversity at the cost of controlled experimentation, or require training on large, messy open-source datasets. We introduce MESA, an evaluation framework designed for precise, reproducible measurement of language-conditioned policy generalization. MESA provides five evaluation suites that isolate in-distribution performance and four distinct axes of generalization: spatial configuration, object instance, object category, and subtask composition, enabling researchers to diagnose where and why policies fail, not just whether they fail. To populate these suites at scale, we develop MESA-Gen, a pipeline for task and demonstration generation built by improving MimicGen. We instantiate the framework on two platforms and release both: the single-arm MESA suite, and BiMESA, the first large-scale benchmark for bimanual generalization. We evaluate seven baseline methods spanning diffusion policies, VLM-based approaches, and pretrained VLAs, finding that the strongest model achieves only 52% on MESA and 49% on BiMESA, providing meaningful signal while leaving ample room for progress. MESA rankings are broadly consistent with RoboArena scores, suggesting that MESA captures method-level differences relevant beyond simulation. Project website:this https URL.

  _Shuangyu Xie, Kaiyuan Chen, Uksang Yoo, Xinyue Zhu, Meadow Shen, Jaimyn Drake, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  For robots to operate reliably in commercial and industrial applications, they must persistently perform specific tasks with known variations in object shape and pose. We call this Variational Automation (VA) to differentiate these settings from fixed automation. Inspired by recent papers on Agentic Robotics, where pretrained LLM and VLM coding agents combine interpretable robot programming with the open-world adaptability of model free policies, we propose a general set of agentic robotics benchmark design criteria that includes accessibility, repeatability, diversity, relevance to agentic robotics and practical applications, physical realizability, resistance to benchmark saturation, and computational efficiency. We introduce the Variational Automation Benchmark for Agentic Robotics (VABAR), an open, easily replicable, simulation-based manipulation benchmark to systematically evaluating agentic robotics systems. Built fully on open-source tools, VABAR features detailed implementations using the Newton physics engine, standardized task variations, difficulty tiers, success metrics, and a library of 33 generalizable skills with human-authored programmatic reference solutions. We provide initial baseline evaluations using an open agentic manipulation harness, Graph-as-Policy (GaP), and Isaac Lab Arena.

- **[$A^3$-VLA: Automatic Perception-Guided Attention Alignment for Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06447)**  `arXiv:2611.06447`  `cs.RO`  
  _Shengzhe Zhang, Qi Zhang, Dazhong Shen, Chao Wang, Shaopeng Zhai, Hui Xiong_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) enable robots to perform manipulation tasks by leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to process visual observations and language instructions. However, the action prediction objective itself lacks fine-grained task-specific perceptual supervision, making it prone to relying on task-irrelevant visual shortcuts, leading attention away from target objects and key interaction areas, thereby limiting its operational performance and generalization ability. To address this, we propose Automatic Perception-Guided Attention Alignment for Robot Manipulation ($A^3$-VLA), a framework that derives task-specific visual guidance from the manipulation process itself. Specifically, $A^3$-VLA first generates heuristic candidate regions for target objects and the robot from interaction-induced visual changes, and refines them with a visual segmentation model offline. These regions serve as explicit perceptual guidance through two complementary objectives: an attention allocation loss that encourages sufficient focus on task-critical regions when language or robot state tokens query the visual input, and a contrastive learning loss that aligns the representations of candidate target regions with language instructions while separating them from task-irrelevant regions. Extensive experiments on simulated and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate that $A^3$-VLA consistently improves multiple VLA backbones, with an average success rate gain of 6.5% - 25.2% across benchmarks.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Agents built on vision language models (VLMs) now control robots by writing code, but the strongest of them refine task-specific code over many rounds of trial and revision. We present HexaShell, a thin, task-agnostic harness designed to test how far general tools can go instead. The RoboCasa365 result is listed first on its leaderboard. On real dual-arm robots, HexaShell succeeds in 76 of 90 episodes across nine tasks.

- **[EPIC: Efficient Predicate-Guided Inference-Time Control for Compositional Text-to-Image Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11722)**  `arXiv:2605.11722`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Sunung Mun, Sunghyun Cho, Jungseul Ok_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent text-to-image (T2I) generators can synthesize realistic images, but still struggle with compositional prompts involving multiple objects, counts, attributes, and relations. We introduce EPIC (Efficient Predicate-Guided Inference-Time Control), a training-free inference-time refinement framework for compositional T2I generation. EPIC casts refinement as predicate-guided search: it parses the original prompt once into a visual program of object variables and typed predicates, then verifies each candidate image against this program using visual evidence extracted from that image. - **[Modality-Aware Expert Pruning for MoE-Based Multimodal Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05927)**  `arXiv:2611.05927`  `cs.CV` `cs.CL`  
  _Donggeun Kim_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) enables multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to scale capacity efficiently, but expert parameters dominate memory even though only a few experts are activated per token: they account for over 90% of model size in modern MoE-MLLMs (e.g., 54 GB out of about 58 GB in BF16 for InternVL3.5-30B-A3B and Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B). Expert pruning has emerged as an effective compression strategy, but existing methods aggregate importance uniformly across tokens, ignoring that the same expert may serve different roles for vision versus language processing. To address this, we propose MAEP (Modality-Aware Expert Pruning), a training-free framework that decomposes expert importance by modality in a single forward pass. MAEP introduces (1) Delta-H Output ($\Delta H$), a redistribution-aware metric measuring the MoE output change when an expert is removed, and (2) low-attention vision tokens as a vision-side pruning signal that is dataset-stable: its expert-importance ranking remains stable across different datasets. Text-side importance serves as a preservation signal while vision-side importance serves as a pruning signal, and experts are ranked globally across layers. Experiments on three MoE-based MLLMs across multimodal and text benchmarks show that MAEP outperforms modality-agnostic baselines on the majority of multimodal benchmarks while remaining competitive on text-only tasks, reducing the BF16 expert weight footprint by about 27 GB (about 47% of total memory) so that the full weight footprint of an approximately 58 GB model fits within a single 40 GB GPU.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Monocular 3D object detection offers a low-cost route to 3D perception, but it remains fundamentally ill-posed, since depth must be inferred from a single view. Recent transformer-based detectors learn geometry purely from image features and 3D box annotations, lacking the high-level semantic priors that vision-language models (VLMs) acquire from large-scale pretraining. VLMs offer such priors, but deploying one directly as a detector is impractical due to its heavy inference cost. - **[Reasoning-Aware Cross-Modal Association Unlearning in Multimodal Large Reasoning Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06168)**  `arXiv:2611.06168`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Xinghao Li, Chenchen Tan, Youyang Qu, Shujie Cui, Xingliang Yuan, Cunjian Chen, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Multimodal large reasoning models (MLRMs) integrate visual inputs with textual and parametric knowledge, allowing sensitive information to be accessed through cross-modal associations during reasoning. This raises privacy concerns when a visual input should no longer provide access to such information. Machine unlearning can remove this influence without retraining the model from scratch. However, multimodal unlearning methods mainly target samples, concepts, or knowledge content, without separating the knowledge from the cross-modal association through which it is accessed. In reasoning models, target information may disappear from the final answer while remaining in intermediate reasoning, or be replaced by incorrect substitute knowledge. We further introduce CLEAR-R and MLLMU-R to evaluate target leakage, hallucinated reassociation, reasoning preservation, and modality-specific utility. Experiments show that VKCR substantially reduces image-conditioned target leakage and hallucinated reassociation, including across heterogeneous task formulations, while preserving non-sensitive visual reasoning and permitted text-only knowledge.

- **[UniCompress: From Multi-LoRA Experts to One Compact Model for Edge Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06075)**  `arXiv:2611.06075`  `cs.AI`  
  _Mingkang Liu, Peipei Jiao, Weiming Meng, Tianyuan Liu, Lichen Pang, Junyan Li_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Embodied and edge systems increasingly rely on vision-language models (VLMs) to support heterogeneous skills, yet deploying a separate task adapter for each skill while retaining a high-precision backbone creates substantial memory and management costs. We present UniCompress, a deployment-oriented framework that converts multi-task expertise into one compact, adapter-free model. Starting from a consolidated BF16 Qwen3-VL-4B teacher, UniCompress constructs an asymmetric INT2 student using GPTQ initialization and teacher-guided quantization recovery. The approach jointly quantizes and recovers the projection weights in the vision encoder and language backbone, enabling aggressive compression of the complete vision-language model while preserving its multi-task behavior. We evaluate UniCompress on four heterogeneous embodied tasks: posture recognition, household-safety understanding, multimodal dialogue routing, and mobile-GUI action prediction. UniCompress reduces the approximate weight storage from 8.9 GB in BF16 to 2.1 GB in INT2, corresponding to a reduction of approximately 76%. It improves the INT2 macro task score by 34.3 percentage points over GPTQ-INT2 while remaining within 0.9 points of the consolidated BF16 teacher. These results demonstrate the potential of teacher-guided joint vision-language INT2 recovery for deploying a single multi-skill VLM on resource-constrained embodied platforms.
