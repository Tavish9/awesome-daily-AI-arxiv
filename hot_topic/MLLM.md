- **[UniMem: Unifying Visual and Textual Memory in VLAs for Non-Markovian Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22869)**  `arXiv:2608.22869`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Lars Osterberg, Maggie Wang, Mac Schwager_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  While Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have leveraged internet-scale pretraining and task-specific finetuning to achieve strong performance on long-horizon tasks, they often struggle with non-Markovian tasks that require memory. Existing approaches to memory typically involve additional Vision-Language-Models (VLMs) for long-term memory management, introducing a memory bottleneck and a fractured training pipeline. Conditioning on multiple historical frames can provide the VLA with access to more descriptive features of past scenes, but can degrade performance if frames are chosen at arbitrary, fixed intervals. To address these limitations, we present UniMem, a framework that unifies high-level visual and textual memory and low-level control under one backbone. UniMem employs an event classifier to initiate textual and visual memory updates, a keyframe encoder for dense visual memory, and a keyframe caching technique to minimize overhead during policy rollouts. We evaluate UniMem across five simulation and four hardware tasks targeting sequential and spatial memory, demonstrating that our unified, single-model system outperforms fixed-interval image sampling baselines (93.4% vs. 68.2%) in simulation and hierarchical VLM-VLA baselines (80.0% vs. 43.5%) in hardware, while offering faster inference and a simple training pipeline for easy adoption. - **[Externalise the Memory: A Pre-Registered Study of Where an Embodied Agent's Experience Should Live](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30478)**  `arXiv:2610.30478`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Cheng-Yin Tu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Should an embodied agent's experience be baked into its policy weights, or kept external and retrieved at inference? A memory-augmented teacher (a VLM that retrieves lessons, with their episode images, from its own past attempts) produces trajectories on a vision-conditioned reach task; from the same trajectories we distil LoRA student policies and evaluate them under matched protocols, including one that re-attaches the teacher's frozen retrieval buffer to a fixed set of student weights. We pre-registered the hypotheses, arms, tests, and analysis rules before training and before any p-value. Distilling the memory-augmented behaviour into weights beat an action-only control by +1 pp (n.s.); the registered test proves underpowered for its own minimum effect, so we withdraw - rather than refute - the memory-in-weights claim. Attaching retrieval to a fixed set of student weights added +34 pp (z=5.15), reaching the pooled memory-augmented teacher reference (49.3%); the pre-registered protocol contrast agrees (+23 pp, z=3.36); under a pre-registered auto-reset sensitivity count the two are +36 and +29 pp. An exploratory behavioural probe with pre-registered per-arm predictions is consistent with behavioural distillation transferring the marginal correction but not the conditional, situation-specific correction that retrieval supplies. The recipe that follows is to externalise the memory: in follow-up controls registered before they ran, the same frozen buffer lifts the base model with no adapter to 48% (sensitivity 48%) and the other three adapters from 19-26% to 53-55% (sensitivity 17-21% to 50-55%), and the adapter adds nothing detectable once the buffer is attached. On a second, harder task, the retrieval advantage points the same direction but falls short of significance (+6 pp, 95% CI [-4.5, +16.4]); exploratory diagnostics tie the attenuation to memory coverage.

- **[Progress Probes $P^2$: Lightweight Runtime Monitoring for Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31613)**  `arXiv:2610.31613`  `cs.RO`  
  _Atiksh Bhardwaj, Edward W. Duan, Junwon Seo, Prithwish Dan, Wei-Chiu Ma, Andrea Bajscy, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can perform diverse manipulation tasks directly from visual observations and natural language instructions, but they remain prone to mistakes and unpredictable failures. Deploying these policies without continuous human supervision will require robust runtime monitors that detect when the policy has gone off-course. Existing monitors use external foundation models, such as vision-language models (VLMs) to supervise the policy, introducing significant complexity and computational cost for monitoring at runtime. In this work, we investigate an alternate approach: \textit{can a VLA's own internal activations provide the information needed to monitor its execution?} Our method trains linear probes on policy activations from demonstrations to estimate progress, then flags executions that deviate from expected progress. In experiments with the open-source VLA $\pi_{0.5}$ and across simulated and real-world manipulation tasks, this approach is competitive with or outperforms VLM-based monitors in task failure detection, as well as methods which require failure data for training. These findings suggest that simple probes of VLA internals can be used to develop reliable runtime monitors, opening a path toward building policies that can act reliably and monitor their execution without external foundation models.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  This paper presents TRACE, a traversability-aware reactive navigation framework designed for the autonomous operation of ground robots in unstructured forest environments. Unlike traditional approaches, TRACE leverages VLM-based confidence maps from positive and negative natural-language prompt sets and combines them through evidential fusion to produce a dense traversability map that prioritizes the terrain based on signs of previously traversed paths.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>

Existing recovery methods either rely on inference-time mechanisms with fixed policies or learn from failure examples and reference corrections rather than verified action outcomes. To enable recovery learning through verifiable interaction, we introduce the Embodied Replanning for Recovery Environment (ERR-Env), which provides feedback on task progress and execution errors. - **[Edit, Don't Imitate: Minimal-Edit On-Policy Distillation for Multimodal Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30231)**  `arXiv:2610.30231`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI`  
  _Wen Ye, Ailin Deng, Zirui Wang, Shixing Yu, Han Zhang, Tian Yun, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Visual mathematical reasoning can fail before reasoning begins: when a vision-language model (VLM) misreads a diagram, subsequent reasoning proceeds from false premises. This perceptual bottleneck is especially pronounced in smaller models, while accurate textual descriptions can substantially narrow the gap to larger models. Because obtaining such descriptions at inference requires additional annotation or model calls, we propose minimal-edit corrective on-policy distillation. Using reference figure descriptions and verified reference solutions as training-only information, a teacher scores incorrect student rollouts under a minimal-edit objective, and only teacher-disfavored tokens contribute to the corrective loss. Because the minimal-edit teacher evaluates corrections rather than generating its own solution, the method remains effective with a frozen same-size teacher, although its gains are smaller than with a larger teacher on most metrics.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language models (VLMs) deliver strong multimodal reasoning capabilities, but their large computational cost and high parameter counts make deployment challenging on resource-constrained devices. Low-rank decomposition has emerged as a promising compression technique, yet existing methods often optimize local matrix reconstruction error, rely on uniform or heuristic rank allocation, and focus mainly on attention projections while leaving feed-forward networks underexplored. In this paper, we propose~\textit{LASER} (\textbf{L}oss-\textbf{A}ware \textbf{S}ingular-value d\textbf{E}composition and \textbf{R}ank allocation), a low-rank compression framework for efficient low-precision VLM inference.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We study how visual information is routed in vision-language models (VLMs). Using causal patching on controlled synthetic and natural datasets, we find that models rely on two distinct pathways to solve visual tasks: A direct pathway, where visual information is retained in image token representations and read out by the final token at later layers, and a text-mediated pathway, where visual information is first transferred to the query tokens and then read out by the final token.
