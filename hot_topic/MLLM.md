- **[Review the Episodes, Refine the Lessons: Simple Memory Evolution for Embodied Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11509)**  `arXiv:2611.11509`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.LG`  
  _Yifei Yao, Yude Zou, Xing Gao, Guanbo Jiao, Yukai Wang, Jianming Ma, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent studies of embodied agents based on frontier multimodal large language models (MLLMs) develop harness engineering around the MLLM, such as harnesses with post-trained policies and recursive self-improvement loops. Yet whether these additions exceed the native capability of the MLLM remains unexplored. We introduce SimplEvo to explore how far an MLLM can improve under direct control when it retains its execution experience in self-written text memories. It requires no weight updates, post-trained policies, or privileged information. The MLLM writes a coordinate-free task memory once from a single demonstration. Under a strict train-test protocol, it then creates a bounded working memory on training episodes only and refines it after each episode with lessons on procedure, completion, and recovery. Both memories stay fixed at test time. On EBench, SimplEvo lifts the success rate (SR) of GPT-6-Astra under direct control from 46.7% to 81.7%, and tops the RoboDojo leaderboard with 69.63% SR and a score of 75.32. Compared with memories that also store past visual frames, SimplEvo reaches a similar SR with text memory alone while using fewer tokens. With its memories fixed, SimplEvo also generalizes to unseen layouts and randomized scene configurations. Moreover, the working memory transfers across models: written by GPT-6-Astra, it can be reused as is by weaker MLLMs and improve their performance without any adaptation of their own.

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are well-suited for complex reasoning in dynamic and uncertain environments, making them a promising tool for this selective CP task. - **[DeltaForge: A Post-hoc Mixture-of-Experts VLA for Multi-Domain Autonomous Driving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11856)**  `arXiv:2611.11856`  `cs.LG` `cs.RO`  
  _Bingzhang Wang, Mo Poorsartep, Yasaman Esfandiari_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Fleets of driving vision-language-action (VLA) models are developed one domain at a time: each operator finetunes a shared base model on private data, producing specialized experts that are costly to consolidate into a single model. Weight merging loses accuracy as heterogeneous domains accumulate, and dense mixture-of-experts routing is prohibitively expensive to combine them. We present DeltaForge, to our knowledge the first post-hoc mixture-of-experts VLA for driving, which consolidates N independently finetuned per-domain models into a single multi-domain model from their weights and a small proxy set alone. Across six diverse domains spanning urban, off-road, and highway driving on cars, trucks, and all-terrain vehicles (ATVs), DeltaForge approaches the accuracy of the per-domain experts and surpasses every merging, routing, centralized, and federated baseline at close to single-model parameter and compute cost. Adding a new domain requires only projecting its weight update onto the shared basis with no end-to-end retraining needed. The resulting model also generalizes to domain combinations it was never explicitly trained on.

- **[Prompt Driven Exploration: Language as an Exploration Space for VLA Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08837)**  `arXiv:2607.08837`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI`  
  _Sunshine Jiang, John Marangola, David Zhang, Raghuram Kowdeed, Ruiyang Luo, Nitish Dashora, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Exploration is essential to reinforcement learning (RL) since a policy cannot improve by repeatedly sampling the behaviors it already prefers. Standard methods inject stochasticity in the action space, but such jitter only yields rollouts close to the original action distribution. Escaping a weak policy often requires global perturbations that action noise cannot produce. Large language models (LLMs) and vision-language-action (VLA) models offer a pathway: they condition the policy on a natural language prompt, and since the rollout follows from it, modifying the prompt induces global changes. The challenge is finding prompts that induce useful global changes. With a weak policy that rarely succeeds, reward is too sparse to select on. Sampling a prompt and holding it fixed throughout a rollout provides episode-level commitment, analogous to posterior-sampling exploration. We call this strategy Prompt Driven Exploration (PDE). Across manipulation and reasoning tasks, PDE enables RL to learn successful policies even from zero-reward starts, and improves sample efficiency more broadly.
