- **[Self-Refining Vision Language Model for Robotic Failure Detection and Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12405)**  `arXiv:2602.12405`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Carl Qi, Xiaojie Wang, Silong Yong, Stephen Sheng, Huitan Mao, Sriram Srinivasan, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Reasoning about failures is crucial for building reliable and trustworthy robotic systems. Prior approaches either treat failure reasoning as a closed-set classification problem or assume access to ample human annotations. Failures in the real world are typically subtle, combinatorial, and difficult to enumerate, whereas rich reasoning labels are expensive to acquire. We address this problem by introducing ARMOR: Adaptive Round-based Multi-task mOdel for Robotic failure detection and reasoning. We formulate detection and reasoning as a multi-task self-refinement process, where the model iteratively predicts detection outcomes and natural language reasoning conditioned on past outputs. During training, ARMOR learns from heterogeneous supervision - large-scale sparse binary labels and small-scale rich reasoning annotations - optimized via a combination of offline and online imitation learning. At inference time, ARMOR generates multiple refinement trajectories and selects the most confident prediction via a self-certainty metric. Experiments across diverse environments show that ARMOR achieves state-of-the-art performance by improving over the previous approaches by up to 30% on failure detection rate and up to 100% in reasoning measured through LLM fuzzy match score, demonstrating robustness to heterogeneous supervision and open-ended reasoning beyond predefined failure modes. We provide additional visualizations on our website:this https URL

- **[REKI: Registering Knowledge in VLAs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22178)**  `arXiv:2610.22178`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Juliette Marrie, Paul Pacaud, Adrien Ramanana Rahary, Shizhe Chen, Moritz Böhle_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We introduce REKI, a 2.4B vision-language-action model (VLA) pretrained exclusively on public data that sets a new Pareto frontier between performance and model size: on LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin Clean2Rand and RoboCasa365, it is outperformed only by models about twice its size. REKI consists of a vision-language model (VLM) followed by a flow-matching action expert, and is pretrained on a curated mixture of public robot, egocentric human and vision-language data. Additionally, we endow it with a memory that gives the policy access to its recent past at negligible cost: a few register tokens in the action expert summarise the output of the VLM at each frame and persist in the expert's cache. This memory improves success in simulation and on real robots, and makes oscillations between poses about four times rarer. REKI uses a simple model design and training paradigm, and is optimised to be efficient at training and inference. We release the full code across data curation, training, inference and evaluation, deployment, and all pretrained and finetuned model weights:this https URL.

- **[ProTracer: Proprioception-Guided Failure Diagnosis in Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21369)**  `arXiv:2609.21369`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Chang Dong, Mehdi Hosseinzadeh, King Hang Wong, Lingqiao Liu, Francois Fraysse, Feras Dayoub, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  This paper presents a comprehensive framework for robot manipulation failure analysis that includes binary failure detection, failure categorization, explanation generation, and the additional capability of failure onset localization, which aims to identify the earliest moment at which a robot execution deviates from a valid task-completion trajectory and is ultimately followed by task failure. To address these tasks, we propose ProTracer, a training-free framework that leverages existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) together with proprioceptive signals for failure analysis. Our method uses proprioceptive dynamics to identify temporally informative action boundaries and converts richer robot-state signals into structured natural-language descriptions that can be jointly analyzed together with visual observations by the VLM. We further introduce FailTime, a benchmark with synchronized visual and proprioceptive observations for evaluating conventional failure diagnosis tasks as well as failure onset localization. Experiments demonstrate that ProTracer achieves strong performance across both conventional failure diagnosis tasks and the newly introduced failure onset localization task, highlighting the importance of proprioceptive reasoning for fine-grained temporal failure analysis.

Two common strategies are supervised fine-tuning (SFT), which trains on teacher-generated demonstrations, and on-policy distillation (OPD), which provides teacher feedback on trajectories generated by the student itself.   _Tiantian Peng, Yuyang Liu, Shuo Yang, Qiuhe Hong, YongHong Tian_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  The modality gap is intrinsic to multimodal alignment, where embeddings from different modalities remain partially separated in a shared representation space. In continual learning, CLIP-based vision-language models (VLMs) not only suffer catastrophic forgetting but also experience modality gap drift that disrupts the shared embedding geometry. Such drift degrades cross-modal alignment and generalization. We propose FPCD, an efficient framework combining Fast Null-space Projection (FNP) and Contrastive Distillation (CD). These results highlight the importance of controlling modality gap drift during continual adaptation of VLMs.
