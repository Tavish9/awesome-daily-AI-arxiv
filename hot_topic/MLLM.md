- **[Do VLAs Know They Will Fail? Interpretable Success and Failure Patterns in Physical AI Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32208)**  `arXiv:2610.32208`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.LG`  
  _Christian A. Rogers, Qian Lin, Daniel S. Brown_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as powerful general-purpose robot policies, but their successes and failures remain difficult to diagnose and understand. In this work, we show that sparse autoencoder (SAE) representations of OpenVLA activations contain outcome-predictive information and can be used to predict task failure before the robot reaches an unrecoverable state. Moreover, to provide insights into what common behaviors may contribute to these outcomes, we identify individual SAE features whose strong activations are associated with specific stages of success or failure, and uncover recurring behavioral patterns by localizing temporal intervals where these features exhibit significant activation changes. We further develop a VLM-based interpretation pipeline to generate behavioral descriptions of these patterns and validate their correspondence to feature activations on held-out intervals.

- **[Predictive Long-Horizon Progress Modeling with Action-Aware Quantification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32776)**  `arXiv:2610.32776`  `cs.RO`  
  _Tianchi Xu, Yalin Zheng, Yangxin Zhu, Ruonan Ren, Zheng Fu, Yunlong Wang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Quantitative and dense task progress feedback is essential for robotic manipulation across data curation, policy evaluation, and reinforcement learning. Existing progress annotations often rely on sparse keyframe markings derived from subjective manual judgments, limiting their scalability and cross-task consistency, particularly in long-horizon manipulation. To overcome these challenges, we propose action-aware quantification, a unified progress specification that decomposes long-horizon trajectories into object-level subtasks via a vision-language model (VLM) and assigns weighted action credits to capture fine-grained execution dynamics. Using VLM auto-labeling and human calibration, we construct an 800+ hour training video dataset and LOOM, a long-horizon progress benchmark containing 19,618 human-calibrated evaluation frames. To model these dense progress dynamics, we introduce NimCritic, a predictive progress critic trained via Joint Video-Progress Learning. We further incorporate Historical Progress Perturbation to mitigate sequential drift, and design a contrastive Video-Progress Alignment objective that bridges asymmetric expert streams by mutually grounding intermediate visual dynamics and denoised progress trajectories. NimCritic matches or outperforms state-of-the-art process reward models (PRMs) on the established Video Progress Benchmark (VPB) and LOOM, surpassing GVL (GPT-6-Sol) on VPB by about 65% in terminal success accuracy (TSA). On the large-scale RoboArena policy evaluation benchmark, it also retrieves human-selected successful rollouts with about 97% overall recall. These results establish action-aware quantification as a scalable foundation for dense progress feedback in robotic manipulation.

- **[OWLS: Open-world Language-conditioned Speed Fields for Zero-Shot Rover Navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32596)**  `arXiv:2610.32596`  `cs.RO`  
  _Chahyon Ku, Erica Tevere, Georgios Georgakis, Rudra Mukherjee, Bernadette Bucher_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Semantics can provide a proxy for physical traversability. Terrain that looks like loose sand or a rock field is likely to have similar physical properties. Open-vocabulary vision-language models (VLMs) enable this physical reasoning over terrain outside any fixed label set. We present Open-world Language-conditioned Speed Fields (OWLS), which plans directly over the dense feature map of a VLM. Prompted with a description of traversable terrain, the VLM scores every pixel of a single camera image; OWLS projects these scores onto the ground, adapts them to the platform with two morphological operations, and uses the result as the speed field of a minimum-time planner, so terrain appearance and obstacle clearance shape one trajectory. OWLS needs no task-specific training, no terrain annotation, no persistent map, and no exteroceptive sensor beyond the camera. We evaluate it on a physical rover indoors and off-road, on simulated Mars and Moon rovers over 200 procedurally generated terrains, and on real planetary imagery.

  Segmenting robot demonstration data into disjoint temporal and semantic subsegments is valuable for learning, cleaning, retrieval, and analysis. Segmentation is often performed manually in a tedious and error prone process. Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have the potential for automating this process, but directly ingesting raw multi camera video streams into MLLMs can be costly and can quickly exhaust context windows. In this paper, we introduce KiRoSe (Kinematics for Robot Segmentation), a novel framework that focuses on the companion stream of robot kinematic data.   _Yiyuan Pan, Hanjiang Hu, Shangtao Li, Xusheng Luo, Changliu Liu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  A central challenge in deploying robot policies is inference-time policy steering: redirecting a policy to satisfy natural-language preferences unseen in training, without retraining. Existing methods fail in two modes: end-to-end policies such as vision-language-action models (VLAs) require fine-tuning or expert-level guidance, while neuro-symbolic methods rely on predefined symbols whose edits can yield logically valid but physically infeasible plans. We propose ReStruct, a zero-shot method built on a neural automaton policy that decomposes a visuomotor policy into a high-level state-machine skeleton and a low-level residual controller. - **[Feature-Space Smoothing: Certified Robustness of Deep Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16200)**  `arXiv:2601.16200`  `cs.LG` `cs.CV`  
  _Song Xia, Meiwen Ding, Chenqi Kong, Wenhan Yang, Xudong Jiang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Modern deep learning models exhibit strong capabilities across diverse applications, yet remain vulnerable to malicious inputs that induce erroneous predictions via feature-space distortion. To address that, we propose Feature-Space Smoothing (FS), a representation-level certification framework built upon Gaussian randomized smoothing. We show that FS converts a given feature encoder into a smoothed variant that is guaranteed to maintain a certified lower bound on the cosine similarity between clean and adversarial features under l_2-bounded perturbations. We then establish that this Feature Cosine Similarity Bound (FCSB) can be extended to the prediction-wise certification under the cosine similarity measure, and the value of FCSB is determined by the encoder intrinsic Gaussian robustness score. Building on those insights, we introduce the Gaussian Smoothness Booster (GSB), a plug-and-play module to improve the encoder Gaussian robustness score. Specifically, the GSB module is plugged to enhance the feature-space consistency and maintain the feature utility for downstream tasks under Gaussian perturbations. This design enables seamless integration of FS on the protected model, e.g., Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), without additional model retraining or alignment, improving its robustness while preserving the performance for downstream task-oriented decoding. Extensive experiments demonstrate that integrating FS consistently provides non-trivial certified robustness and significantly improves task-oriented performance under strong white-box adversarial attacks across diverse models and applications.
