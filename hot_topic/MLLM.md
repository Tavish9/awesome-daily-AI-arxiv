- **[E0: Expressive Fine-Grained Discrete Action Prediction for Vision-Language-Action Models via Tweedie Discrete Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21542)**  `arXiv:2511.21542`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Zhihao Zhan, Jiaying Zhou, Likui Zhang, Qinhan Lyu, Hao Liu, Weizheng Li, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models offer a unified framework for robotic manipulation by integrating visual perception, language understanding, and control generation. However, existing VLA systems still struggle to generalize across diverse tasks, scenes, and camera viewpoints, and often produce coarse or unstable actions. We argue that these limitations are closely tied to the structural properties of actions in VLA settings, including the inherent multi-peaked nature of action distributions, the token-based symbolic reasoning of pretrained VLM/VLA backbones, and the effective finite resolution imposed by real-world robotic control. Motivated by these properties, we introduce E0, a tweedie discrete diffusion framework that formulates action generation as iterative denoising over quantized action tokens. By operating in a discrete action space with a principled diffusion process, E0 naturally aligns with token-based reasoning, supports fine-grained yet executable action control, and avoids the distributional mismatch of masking-based discrete diffusion. Experiments on LIBERO, VLABench, ManiSkill, and a real-world Franka arm demonstrate that E0 achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse environments, outperforming strong baselines by 8.1% on average.

- **[Objectives as Policies: Vision-Language Model Cost Programs for Contact-Rich Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18298)**  `arXiv:2611.18298`  `cs.RO`  
  _Zihao Lu, Trong Gia Hung Nguyen, Fuquan Wang, Yifan Zhu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language models (VLMs) have shown strong semantic and spatial reasoning capabilities in 3D environments. Recently, connecting VLMs with model-predictive controllers (MPC) has emerged as a paradigm for combining strong semantic reasoning with physics-based planning. However, connecting these two for contact-rich robotic manipulation requires a task representation that bridges the two and captures how physical interactions can be reasoned by VLMs. In this work, we introduce Objectives as Policies (OaP), a framework that connects a pretrained VLM to a shared sampling-based MPC through an editable objective program. We evaluate our framework in both simulation and the real world on four tabletop manipulation tasks, and show that it outperforms strong baselines. - **[Grounding Object-to-Object Hazard Reasoning into 3D Risk Fields for Task-Conditioned Safety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17566)**  `arXiv:2611.17566`  `cs.RO`  
  _Mariana Sosa Guzmán, Yide Song, Amin Abyaneh, Gregory Dudek, Hsiu-Chin Lin_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Safety in robotics does not solely depend on geometric constraints, but also on semantics: the given task, environment properties, and interactions between objects in the scene. We present a framework based on vision-language models (VLMs) to infer task- and scene-dependent safety constraints for robot manipulation. We provide the VLM a structured safety tool based on an RGB-D observation and a natural-language task instruction that first decides whether the task should be rejected since its execution may result in a non-recoverable hazardous outcome. - **[The Alignment Illusion in Multimodal Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30210)**  `arXiv:2609.30210`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Hong-Han Wang, Yuntao Wang, Hu Ding_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Layer-wise visual-text similarity in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) is widely interpreted as evidence that the language model progressively integrates visual content into a shared representation space. This reading rests on the assumption that scalar alignment scores reflect content-level cross-modal interaction. To test this assumption, we apply controlled interventions to the visual stream. Across 13 MLLMs from five families spanning 0.5B to 72B parameters, replacing projector-output visual tokens with Gaussian noise sharply reduces task accuracy, yet four standard scalar measures (CKA, SVCCA, MIR, and the leading principal-angle cosine) fail to consistently separate the corrupted stream from the original. We call this failure the alignment illusion and trace it to the shared language-model pathway: anisotropic MLP down-projections pull visual and text tokens toward common output directions, producing weight-induced alignment. We introduce the principal-angle gap (PA gap), defined as the difference between the top two principal-angle cosines, to distinguish leading-direction dominance from the broader overlap observed with structured visual content. Under graded visual corruption, the PA gap tracks task accuracy more consistently than the scalar scores we consider; under a structured but irrelevant image, it further exposes regimes in which internal geometry and task accuracy come apart. Internal visual-text alignment in MLLMs is therefore best read as a geometric diagnostic of the visual stream inside the language model rather than a direct proxy for content-level cross-modal interaction, and is most informative when calibrated by controlled task evidence.

- **[AmbiSteer: Selectively Eliciting Ambiguity-Aware Responses in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17900)**  `arXiv:2611.17900`  `cs.CV` `cs.CL`  
  _Monica Riedler, Samuele Antonelli, Barbara Plank, Raffaella Bernardi_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language models (VLMs) often exhibit overconfidence, providing firm answers even when visual evidence is insufficient. A prominent case is referential ambiguity in Visual Question Answering (VQA), where multiple entities in an image are plausible referents of a question. Addressing such cases requires recognizing when ambiguity is present: ambiguous questions can be handled by asking for clarification or enumerating plausible referents, while unambiguous questions should receive a direct answer. Current VLMs struggle with this distinction, often defaulting to a single interpretation. To investigate whether activation steering can serve as an inference-time mechanism to elicit such behaviors, we introduce AmbiSteer, a lightweight framework to selectively elicit ambiguity-aware behavior. Across three VLMs, we find that steering alone has limited and model-dependent effects, while prompting is generally more effective. Combining prompting and steering substantially increases clarification and enumeration behaviors, but also elicits them on unambiguous cases. AmbiSteer addresses this using a linear classifier to predict input ambiguity and condition the steering direction accordingly, increasing the proportion of inputs handled appropriately from 59.0% to 77.0% on average across models. Our work sets the ground for lightweight approaches to reduce model overconfidence in uncertain multimodal settings.
  </details>

- **[Zoom Where the Answer Looks: Cross-Resolution Self-Distillation for MLLMs under Visual-Token Budgets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32664)**  `arXiv:2610.32664`  `cs.CV`  
  _Zhensheng Hu, Guanglong Xu, Guanqiang Hu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Under the small per-view visual-token budgets of efficient deployment, the fine text of documents, charts and infographics becomes illegible to multimodal large language models (MLLMs). Zooming in recovers it, but the region must be chosen before the model answers. Existing methods decode an answer or a box first, at the cost of generation, or read the attention available before answering, which localizes the evidence only in top layers whose depth varies with model size. Learned region proposers instead distill the attention of the model's answers, but on the same budgeted view. Yet this attention is precise only on a view the model can read: on Qwen3-VL-4B, a window around its peak covers 83% of the evidence on a high-resolution view but 54% on the budgeted view. Since answers and high-resolution views are available during training, we introduce LookZoom, a zooming method that trains a dedicated look token by cross-resolution self-distillation of the answer's attention: with the MLLM frozen, the token learns to attend on the budgeted view, before any answer, where the model's own correct answers attend on a high-resolution view. The region around its attention peak is the window to zoom into, and reinforcement learning then trains the MLLM to answer from both views. With 98 visual tokens per view on five text-rich benchmarks, LookZoom surpasses attention-guided zooming by 20-24 points and grounding-based zooming and learned crop routing by 12-15 points on Qwen3-VL at three scales, and it outperforms learned region proposers at every budget from 98 to 392 tokens. On Qwen3-VL, the look token alone, with the MLLM frozen, already surpasses every baseline that leaves the language model unchanged, and the recipe carries over to InternVL3.5 and Qwen2.5-VL.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Large vision-language models (LVLMs) achieve strong multimodal performance, yet they remain prone to object hallucination---generating answers that are linguistically fluent but visually ungrounded.   Human Label Variation (HLV) is increasingly recognised as an informative signal that reflects meaningful differences in how people interpret the same input.
