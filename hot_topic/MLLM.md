- **[What Would Removing This Corruption Change? Failure Attribution for Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27277)**  `arXiv:2610.27277`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Carson Sobolewski, Anjali Parashar, Fei Chen, Yingke Li, Chuchu Fan_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA) models are sensitive to visual corruptions, but knowing that a corruption is typically harmful does not establish whether removing it would benefit the current episode. Furthermore, when several corruptions co-occur, deciding what to correct requires understanding which ones contribute to failure risk and how much their removal would help. We define this problem as failure attribution. Detecting corruption presence alone cannot answer this question, as corruptions can be present but relatively harmless, while directly measuring removal benefits requires costly paired policy rollouts with and without the corruption present. To address this, we introduce a factorized risk model that learns separate representations of the scene and corruptions, allowing us to estimate removal benefits through latent intervention from the current image and task instruction, without paired clean images or additional counterfactual rollouts. We evaluate our approach on OpenVLA and $\pi_{0.5}$ in simulation and $\pi_{0.5}$-DROID on a Franka robot. Our method outperforms monolithic and vision-language-model (VLM) baselines, including achieving approximately 3x lower attribution error than frontier VLMs. We also demonstrate our method adapts to execution context and corruption intensity, and can be used for online failure monitoring on hardware.

- **[VLM-Guided Contact Retargeting and Generative Partner Modeling for Humanoid-Human Interaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28523)**  `arXiv:2610.28523`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Yuto Shibata, Kotaro Amaya, Kashu Yamazaki, Lalit Jayanti, Yoshimitsu Aoki, Mariko Isogawa, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Humanoid-human interaction requires robots to operate under severe partial observability: during close physical contact, the human partner is occluded, truncated, or outside the robot's field of view, causing pose estimation to fail and often necessitating external tracking or privileged human state. At the same time, heavy mutual occlusions introduce errors into the human-human motion-capture data used for learning. We present REACT, a framework for learning deployable humanoid-human interaction policies from human-human demonstrations. REACT treats semantic contact structure as the key supervisory signal connecting demonstration processing, interaction learning, and onboard control. First, a VLM-guided contact modeling and retargeting pipeline identifies semantically meaningful contacts and transfers them onto humanoid-human geometry while preserving interaction structure. Using these annotations, privileged interaction policies learn physically grounded behaviors through interaction-aware contact rewards and are then distilled into deployable egocentric depth policies trained entirely from onboard sensing using photorealistic human depth rendering. To address severe partial observability, we introduce generative partner-state modeling objectives that predict partner motion from occluded egocentric views. We evaluate REACT in simulation and on a real Unitree G1 humanoid across five contact-rich interactions. Semantic contact modeling substantially improves preservation of demonstrated interactions, while generative partner-state modeling improves contact recall on four of five interactions, most under heavy occlusion. On real hardware, REACT performs all five interactions using onboard sensing alone.

- **[A Theoretical Analysis of Environment-Aware Out-of-Distribution Detection for Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27211)**  `arXiv:2610.27211`  `cs.LG`  
  _Salimeh Sekeh, Mary Wisell_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Continual vision-language models (VLMs) encounter a stream of environments and must also reject inputs that do not belong to the environment at hand.Class information from earlier environments can aid out-of-distribution (OOD) detection, yet existing detectors score each environment in isolation, and when previous environments help or hurt is theoretically unclear. We develop a theory of environment-aware OOD detection built on contribution scores, pairwise cosine expectations measuring how the image and text features of one environment align with those of another. We propose a cross-environment maximum-concept-matching (MCM) score with two terms: a primary term matching the test image against the current class texts, and a secondary term matching it against the class texts of a previous environment, weighted by the intra-modal alignment between the two environments. We prove that, under a positive environment transition and a bounded class-matching margin for OOD inputs, this score attains a false-positive rate no larger than that of the standard MCM detector at an adjusted threshold. We then show that the score gap of an MCM detector between two environments equals the self-contribution of the in-distribution environment minus its cross-modal contribution into the OOD environment, which separates soft OOD environments, with explicit AUROC, true-positive, and false-positive guarantees, from hard ones, which no threshold that retains the in-distribution data can reliably reject. For cascaded detection, in which the text prototypes of all seen environments are pooled, the false-positive rate on every past environment is non-decreasing in time, and we characterize when detection provably fails. A contribution balance factor applied to the gradients of the classification loss and of a smoothed false-positive rate completes the framework.
