- **[VLM-guided Grounding Expert for Generalised Referring Expression Comprehension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22625)**  `arXiv:2611.22625`  `cs.RO`  
  _Mingcheng Zhu, Wentian Xu, Tao Lin, Shiying Duan, Ziyun Liang, Xuecheng Qi, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Generalised referring expression comprehension (GREC) aims to identify and localise the zero, one or multiple objects referred to by a language expression. In robotic manipulation, grounding also needs to remain accurate when requests for the same objects are paraphrased or embedded in additional context in robotics. However, grounding expert models are sensitive to instruction variation, while generative vision--language models (VLMs) struggle with complete target recovery and rejection of absent targets. These complementary limitations motivate combining the contextual representations of VLMs with the direct spatial prediction capabilities of grounding experts. We therefore introduce VLM-guided Grounding Expert (VGE), which combines a frozen VLM with a trainable grounding expert through layerwise joint attention and text-conditioned instance scoring. These interfaces leverage multimodal understanding of the VLM to guide object-query refinement and candidate selection while retaining direct bounding-box prediction. Across three datasets, VGE achieves the highest averaged F1 score among the evaluated methods on all five test splits, with differences of at most 1.2 percentage points across instruction conditions within each split. On the dataset with robotic scenes, VGE also achieves the highest F1 across instruction variants and no-target, single-target and multiple-target cases. VGE provides a $5.3$--$13.0\times$ inference speedup over the baseline with the same VLM base model. These results support combining VLM context with specialised spatial prediction to address both grounding challenges, with particularly strong performance in robotic scenes. Code will be released upon publication.
  </details>

- **[ReLAP: Latent Action Pretraining through Recurrent Video Prediction in JEPA Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22077)**  `arXiv:2611.22077`  `cs.RO`  
  _R. Gnana Praveen, Ali Rouzbayani, Zhiying Jiang, Bidhan Roy_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Videos provide a rich source of physical interaction for learning robot behavior without action annotations, yet effective latent actions must capture not only local transitions but also how those transitions evolve over time. We introduce ReLAP, a latent-action pretraining framework that couples local transition learning with recurrent multi-step prediction in a frozen JEPA representation space. A shared inverse dynamics model infers compact continuous latent actions from adjacent observations, while a latent world model recurrently predicts the future JEPA features. Because each predicted feature becomes the context for the next transition, errors at later steps backpropagate through the rollout to earlier latent actions, encouraging temporally coherent dynamics beyond pairwise reconstruction. We transfer this representation to a vision-language model using two complementary objectives: latent distillation from the frozen inverse teacher and recurrent future-feature prediction through the frozen latent world model. The resulting policy is then adapted to robot demonstrations with action flow matching. At deployment, the explicit latent-action and world-model branches are removed, and the fine-tuned VLM predicts physical actions directly, reducing inference complexity. With only 11M pretraining samples and 1.06B trainable parameters, ReLAP achieves 97.9% on standard LIBERO and 70.0% over zero-shot LIBERO-Plus perturbations. On the LARY latent-action benchmark, ReLAP ranks second in both mean regression error and mean classification accuracy among the compared methods. These results demonstrate that recurrent action-free latent action pretraining can learn compact, temporally structured, and transferable representations for downstream robot control.
  </details>

- **[Scaling Laws for Vision-Language Fusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22351)**  `arXiv:2611.22351`  `cs.LG` `cs.CV`  
  _Seng Pei Liew, Teppei Suzuki, Seiya Tokui_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language models (VLMs) have emerged as central foundation models combining multimodal perception with text comprehension. While VLMs are traditionally built via late fusion by continued pre-training an off-the-shelf language model on multimodal (image-text) data, mixing modalities from the start of pre-training (early fusion) has recently gained traction. However, the fundamental trade-offs between these paradigms remain poorly understood, resulting in expensive and heuristic-driven training recipes. In this paper, we systematically analyze the learning dynamics of these multimodal pre-training processes, showing that validation losses during vision-language fusion can be modeled by separating the base learning trajectory, where different data types contribute at distinct rates, from a distribution shift driven by the competition between modalities. We derive a scaling law that unifiedly describes these dynamics, enabling accurate loss predictions across mixture ratios, training steps, and model sizes. Crucially, our law characterizes the trade-off between early and late fusion: under a fixed data budget, early fusion preserves text performance while late fusion favors vision performance, yielding a trade-off that increasingly tilts toward early fusion as model size scales. Extensive experiments confirm that our scaling law reliably captures multimodal learning dynamics and governs fusion decisions.
  </details>

- **[What Does Weight-Space Programming Add to Diffusion Editing? Program Construction versus Score-Guided Refinement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22228)**  `arXiv:2611.22228`  `cs.LG` `cs.CV`  
  _Zitian Gao, Jingyi Xu, Ziqi Han, Chengkai Lei, Ye Liu, Liangliang Shi_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Weight-space programming (WSP) lets a language model construct executable weight updates for another network. We study diffusion concept editing through an interface in which programs define training contexts, preservation constraints and update directions, while a shared numerical fitter selects their coefficients. We compare generated programs with published editors, a shared-panel hand-written baseline and budget-matched search over conventional edits on 24 TIMED test edits for Stable Diffusion 1.4. The preregistered rewrite pipeline shows no advantage over the strong baseline. In an exploratory comparison, one-shot Astra programs improve fresh-scene VLM joint success, which requires subject presence together with whole-image target matching, by +10.0 points over the shared-panel baseline, while CLIP relative match shows no detectable change. These programs organise adjustable directions around protected text-state subspaces. Probing and rewriting the Astra programs then raises CLIP by +6.2 points while joint success changes by -9.7. On a fixed 144-image sample, five additional evaluators give point estimates in the same directions under whole-image target matching; checks of subject-local change give less decisive differences. Together, these results identify useful behaviour in complete generated programs and show that score-guided revision need not preserve it: program construction and revision should be evaluated as distinct stages of a WSP pipeline.

- **[VisHead-Spec: Head-Routed Visual Attention for Multimodal Speculative Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21839)**  `arXiv:2611.21839`  `cs.LG` `cs.CV`  
  _Xin Guo, Arushi Rai, Xiang Zhou, Qiang Zhang, Siyu Lin, Xiangjun Fan, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Speculative decoding accelerates vision-language models(VLMs) while preserving the target model's outputs, but its speedup diminishes under high concurrency serving as the system becomes more compute bounded as the batch size increases. This problem is pronounced for multimodal speculative decoding as the long range of the image tokens in the prompt can significantly increase the computational cost during draft token prediction. The block-diffusion drafters (e.g. DFlash, DSpark) can effectively speed up the draft model efficiency by predicting multiple draft tokens in one forward pass, but their speedup for VLMs under high concurrency is still modest (<9%). In order to understand this gap, we examine the information flow between different modalities from target model to draft model and identify the sparsity pattern of visual attention between the target and draft models: only small set of key-value heads in the draft model need direct visual feature attention in the context and only visual features from top level layers in the target model need to be attended in the context. We therefore propose VisHead-Spec, which selects a small, fixed set of key-value (KV) heads to attend to all image features from top level layers in the target model and restricts the remaining heads to text features. On Qwen3-VL-8B and MMSpec, VisHead-Spec-8 lets 8 of the drafter's 40 KV heads read the image while keeping accepted length within 0.3% of the block-diffusion draft model baseline (i.e. DSpark). At batch size 64, its throughput gain over autoregressive decoding increases to 16.0%, compared with 9.5% for DSpark; at batch size 1, its speedup remains comparable to DSpark.
  </details>

- **[The Illusion of Grounding: Do Vision-Language Models Weigh Retrieved Text and Images Like an Ideal Observer?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23127)**  `arXiv:2611.23127`  `cs.LG` `cs.CL` `cs.CV`  
  _Basavaraj R M, Harshal Chandel_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Retrieval-augmented vision-language models (VLMs) answer from two sources of evidence that can each fail: the retrieved text can be wrong or noisy, and the image can be degraded or contradicted. We introduce GIB-200K, 200,000 image-question items built from COCO and Visual Genome, each expanded into controlled stress conditions: hard-negative retrieval noise (1-20 documents), visual ambiguity (Gaussian and motion blur, low-resource crops) and cross-modal conflict with counterfactual flip pairs. Instead of only measuring degradation, we ask how models combine the two modalities. A training-free consistency check that compares the answer with and without retrieval trades coverage for accuracy under misleading documents, while falling back to the image fails when the image is heavily degraded. All per-instance outputs, code, and an audit that recomputes every reported open-model number are released.

- **[Shared Context, Separate Engines: Stateful Streaming Inference for Multimodal LLMs on Edge SoCs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35309)**  `arXiv:2610.35309`  `cs.LG`  
  _Yilong Li, Chengpo Yan, Yijing Zeng, Hanlei Wu, Mingjun Song, Suman Banerjee_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Streaming multimodal large language models (MLLMs) must take in new video frames while answering questions about what they have already seen. On an edge SoC, if one engine does both, ingestion and answering compete for its time while the other engines sit idle. To take over a query, another engine must first import or rebuild the accumulated history, because each runtime keeps it as KV state in its own format. \name lets one engine answer from history that another engine built. Each query reads the history as it stood when the query was asked, so one engine can keep ingesting while another answers. The CPU and GPU can also decode answers to different queries simultaneously. Phase-level state continuation lets an engine prefill a query, pass its KV state to another engine for decoding, and return to ingestion. State-Driven Execution Planning (SDEP) chooses where each query is prefilled and decoded, based on the state each engine holds and the work queued on it. Across M4 Max, RK3588, and QCS6490, \name outperforms single-engine execution. On a live M4 Max stream, SDEP cuts p95 time to first token by $76\%$, and every query receives its first token within one second. Under backlog, it raises request throughput by up to $35\%$. For two concurrent video streams on RK3588, SDEP delivers $19.6\%$ higher request throughput than single-engine execution and $27.2\%$ more than running both engines in parallel without shared state. \name delivers up to $2.48\times$ the throughput of the same engines run serially. Every answer matches serial execution token for token.

- **[Spool: Low-Latency Streaming Inference for Hybrid Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23228)**  `arXiv:2611.23228`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Rui Ning, Om Chabra, Hari Balakrishnan_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Applications such as visual accessibility agents and robotics require answering questions about recent video content promptly, over a bounded window of past frames (e.g., the last two minutes). Hybrid vision-language models (VLMs), which interleave attention with linear recurrent layers such as Gated DeltaNet (GDN), are attractive for this setting, but maintaining a sliding window is expensive. The reason is that recurrent layers combine all past frames into a shared state, so expiring old frames normally requires re-running the entire window through the model. We observe that the recurrent update is affine in the state with input-dependent coefficients, so the effect of each frame can be captured as a transformation that composes associatively. We present Spool, a training-free framework that caches per-frame state transformations and composes them efficiently as the window advances, removing expired frames from each recurrent layer's state without replaying frames through the backbone. Removal is exact within each layer; as with key-value eviction in attention, expired frames can retain residual influence through the cached inputs computed by earlier layers. We design a dedicated data structure and GPU kernels to achieve low caching and composition overhead. Across five open-source hybrid VLMs and four video reasoning benchmarks, Spool achieves the lowest p95 time-to-first-token in all 20 model-benchmark tests, reducing it by a geometric mean of $6.6\times$ over the faster of two recent vLLM baselines, while improving accuracy by 2.3 percentage points on average.

- **[Is model self-assessment enough for diverse LVLM ensembles?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22956)**  `arXiv:2611.22956`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Michael Smith, Frank P. Ferrie_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Ensembles of diverse Large Vision Language Models have demonstrated performance beyond that of single models across multiple datasets and tasks. However, the necessary aggregation process usually relies on training, parameter fitting, or complex task-specific pipelines. Furthermore, evaluation is limited, making a fair comparison difficult. We create a unified pipeline where we perform candidate response selection: we build an ensemble by selecting the best answer from a set provided by different models. We comprehensively evaluate across 34 different means of confidence estimation, 24 ensemble configurations, 20 datasets, and two prompt specifications. We show that simple training and parameter-free approaches can be sufficient, even for ensembles of different architectures. These simple self-assessment approaches rely solely on each constituent model's confidence, without explicit calibration, yet are able to perform better than said constituent models. They are also competitive with more involved alternatives on standard metrics such as accuracy, including on open-ended problems. However, we also discover that dataset, ensemble composition, and prompt wording are key confounding variables insufficiently explored by prior work that significantly affect ensemble performance, such that the ensemble can perform worse than its best constituent model. These must be optimized for to get the most out of ensembles, at the cost of limiting their generalizability. We recommend that practitioners consider training-free ensembles only if deployment conditions can be precisely characterized, with Best-of-N configurations of strong models being a good starting point.

- **[LoopZip: Visual Token Compression for Efficient Looped Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22728)**  `arXiv:2611.22728`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Weizhong Huang, Zhicheng Liu, Yan Zhang, Fei Chao, Xiawu Zheng, Liujuan Cao_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Looped vision-language models (VLMs) deepen computation by reusing shared layers, approaching larger models' capabilities with fewer parameters and easing the storage and deployment burden of stacking independent layers. However, repeatedly processing long visual sequences still incurs substantial compute and GPU memory costs. Visual token compression reduces these costs by shortening sequences. Existing methods mainly select original tokens directly or combine selection with aggregation. In looped models, the former may overlook changing token importance across calls and discard information needed later; the latter lack joint consideration of output changes across calls, limiting information preservation. We propose LoopZip, a training-free visual token compression algorithm addressing both issues. Visual aggregation builds a compact candidate pool before the looped backbone, and cross-invocation selection averages instruction attention from the first two existing calls of the same shared attention layer to retain tokens needed by both states. Weight-tied value correction then jointly fits shared visual-value adjustments to both pre-selection layer outputs, reducing output deviations across states. To validate LoopZip's effectiveness, we conduct extensive experiments covering two open-source looped VLMs, eleven benchmarks, and three pruning ratios. On LoopVL-1B, LoopZip retains 97.16% of the original model's performance at 50% pruning ratio and exceeds the strongest baseline by 5.36% at 87.5% pruning ratio. Across Hugging Face Transformers, vLLM, and SGLang, LoopVL-1B with LoopZip reduces peak allocated GPU memory by 58.36%-58.98% and achieves 2.01-2.47x end-to-end speedups over the original model.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) require visual encoders that extract fine-grained visual features while constructing semantically disentangled and discriminative representations. Conventional methodologies encounter a fundamental trade-off: contrastive learning enhances inter-class discrimination by suppressing intra-class variability, whereas generative reconstruction emphasizes perceptual fidelity but lacks explicit comparative supervision. The naive aggregation of these objectives via composite loss functions often induces optimization conflicts and instability due to gradient interference within the shared latent space. The proposed objectives are designed to be plug-and-play, requiring no architectural modifications to existing generative reconstruction frameworks. Theoretical analysis establishes rigorous connections to AUC/pAUC optimization and provides generalization guarantees. Extensive experiments validate that our method advances both discriminative accuracy and fine-grained perceptual representation.
