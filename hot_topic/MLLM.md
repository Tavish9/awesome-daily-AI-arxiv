- **[$μ_0$: A Scalable 3D Interaction-Trace World Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13769)**  `arXiv:2606.13769`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Seungjae Lee, Yoonkyo Jung, Jusuk Lee, Jonghun Shin, Amir Hossein Shahidzadeh, Yao-Chih Lee, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  World models that capture how actions induce physical change enable scalable robot learning without reliance on embodiment-specific action labels. Pixel-space video models provide broad visual priors but expend model capacity on dense appearance reconstruction, while direct action models require embodiment-specific labels that hinder scalability. We present $\mu_0$, a scalable world model based on 3D traces. Rather than predicting dense pixels or directly modeling actions, $\mu_0$ forecasts smooth 3D trajectories for salient interaction points such as objects, tools, hands, and contact regions, yielding a compact, embodiment-agnostic motion interface. To enable training from diverse video sources, our TraceExtract system automatically extracts 3D supervision by selecting keypoints, constructing globally aligned traces, and associating motion segments with hierarchical language captions. This TraceExtract supervision pretrains $\mu_0$ by combining a pretrained vision-language backbone with a modular trace expert, which represents each query via B-spline control points and predicts future traces. Experiments show that $\mu_0$ outperforms baselines in both 2D and 3D trace prediction, including trace prediction models and tokenized VLM methods. Because $\mu_0$ is frozen and reusable, it can be paired with action experts for downstream robot embodiments. Despite action-free pretraining, the resulting trace-conditioned policies achieve performance competitive with VLA models pretrained with action supervision, such as $\pi_0$. These results establish 3D traces as a scalable and transferable representation for cross-embodiment manipulation.

  _Yicheng Xu, Zhikai Xu, Ziyuan Chen, Yuhang Dong, Tao Zhang, Zhucun Xue, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robots must act under physical and social constraints while adapting to changes in their environment and human needs. Existing embodied-agent systems often focus on specified goals or abstract execution into symbolic actions, limiting evaluation in an evolving world. We introduce TARSphere, a robot-oriented full-stack framework for physically and socially grounded long-horizon embodied agents. TARSphere-Infra runs robot policies in a physics simulator with embodied NPCs and constrained dialogue. An event-driven finite-state machine (FSM) uses completed actions and delivered information to update task states and evaluate outcomes. TARSphere-Harness, its embodied brain harness, lets a frozen multimodal large language model (MLLM) act through multi-level executable skills, using observations, hierarchical memory, and execution feedback to revise its decisions. On this foundation, agent-in-the-loop curation aligns task semantics, causal dependencies, and scene geometry to construct TARSphere-Bench: 360 episodes across 30 indoor scenes. Five paired conditions assess perceptual robustness, disruption adaptation, task update, human coordination, and intent understanding for navigate-and-report and fetch-and-deliver tasks. Our evaluation of 8 MLLMs shows that general-purpose models can outperform embodied models, while task updates and human interaction remain difficult. Frequent collisions and falls further motivate evaluating execution safety alongside task success, even with a shared action interface and decision budget.

- **[CausGround: Causal Interaction Grounding for Adaptive Embodied Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29702)**  `arXiv:2610.29702`  `cs.RO`  
  _Yijun Shen, Diqi He, Jinbang Guo, Wanyue Zhang, Jiaming Mi, Huan Yang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) show promising reasoning capabilities, but reliable physical interaction requires planning that accounts for when actions are executable and how they change the world. These preconditions and effects are environment-dependent and often not captured by MLLMs, making adaptation challenging. Yet existing approaches either require costly environment-specific training or rely on unstructured interaction experience that is difficult to reuse. Therefore, we introduce CausGround, a non-parametric causal interaction grounding framework that distills interaction experience into reusable action-centered causal knowledge. CausGround identifies action preconditions and effects and organizes them in a factorized world-state representation capturing object attributes and inter-object relations. At planning time, task-relevant causal dependencies are grounded in current observations to support backward reasoning over preconditions and forward reasoning over effects for action selection and multi-step planning. We evaluate CausGround on simulated high-level planning, interaction reasoning, and real-world robot manipulation. On adapted VirtualHome and RoboCasa benchmarks, CausGround improves vision-based high-level planning across MLLM backbones, increasing average task success from 24.9% to 53.3% on VirtualHome and closed-loop planning success from 43.0% to 57.0% on RoboCasa. On SWITCH, average interaction reasoning accuracy increases from 25.8% to 31.9%, indicating improved action selection, recovery, and verification. Moreover, real-world experiments show that CausGround reasons backward from MLLM-interpreted goals to derive executable high-level action sequences for sequential manipulation. These results demonstrate that reusable action preconditions and effects support long-horizon embodied planning across diverse environments without parameter updates.

  _Qinghang Liu, Conglin Wang, Zongsheng Li, Boyong He, Han Gao, Pengcheng Wang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Long-horizon robot manipulation requires a robot to remember task-relevant information beyond its short observation window. Existing memory-augmented policies typically retrieve relevant information from history and reason over it at execution time, placing a heavy reasoning burden on the high-level model. To address this challenge, we propose PAL (\textbf{P}lan first, \textbf{A}ct \textbf{L}ater), a hierarchical memory framework for vision-language robot manipulation. It maintains a short-term visual buffer, a semantic-event memory, and an executable \texttt{subtask\_list}. We evaluate our approach on memory-dependent benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks. On RoboMME, PAL achieves a \textbf{56.05\%} success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline, FrameSamp-Modul, at 44.51\%. In real-world experiments across four manipulation tasks, \pal{} reaches \textbf{60.83\%} overall success, surpassing MemER (45.00\%) and FrameSamp-Modul (28.33\%). These results show that proactively writing future-relevant subtasks into hierarchical memory improves long-horizon visual reasoning and robot control. - **[The Price of Choice in Selective Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29067)**  `arXiv:2610.29067`  `cs.LG`  
  _Daniel Cieślak, Andrzej Czyżewski_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  A selective classifier may be claimed to satisfy a risk-coverage contract if some rule from a predeclared menu (a threshold or a union of $K$ score intervals) attains coverage at least $\kappa$ and error at most $\alpha$. Refuting this existential claim requires ruling out every permitted rule. The intersection-union test (IUT) achieves validity without a multiplicity correction, but its sample cost can increase with menu richness. We characterize this cost and compare it with the optimal audit. For a fixed interior risk target $\alpha$ and gap $\gamma$, the minimax sample size for K-interval menus at coverage floor $\kappa$ is $K(1-\kappa)/(\kappa\gamma^2)$, up to logarithmic factors, in the stated small-gap, large-menu regime; exact IUT attains this rate. The lower bound holds even with a known score distribution, while the upper bound extends to menus of VC dimension $d$. Procedures that refuse to reject whenever any rule appears feasible on the audit sample can pay a linear $K$ cost even when a square-root rate is possible. Reference-free testing reaches a smaller scale under constant-risk alternatives, whereas unknown heterogeneous risks can make the linear cost unavoidable. In simulations with $K=4$, the raw menu cost varies 7.7-fold between $\kappa=.5$ and $\kappa=.95$; after theorem-motivated normalization the endpoint values differ by approximately 5%. Held-out experiments with three VLM judges show positive exact-hiding windows in all 60 split-specific confidence-gate families, with 28/30 contracts chosen on audit-A remaining hidden on untouched audit-B. On a cleaner multi-annotator target, hiding is weaker and transfers in 3/10 resplits. Frozen image and multimodal classifiers show boundary behavior consistent with the IUT guarantee and strong power relative to practical certificates.
