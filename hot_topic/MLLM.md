- **[MessyNav: Zero-Shot Navigation via Manipulation in Messy Environments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07417)**  `arXiv:2611.07417`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Kenneth Llontop, Cherie Ho, Dylan Zhou, Anuva Banwasi, Priya Sundaresan, Carlota Parés-Morlans, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Real-world environments are messy, with paths often blocked by everyday objects like chairs and toys, requiring robots to perform interactive navigation by manipulating obstacles to clear a path. Existing methods rely on extensive simulation-based learning or per-scene instructions specifying which objects to avoid or manipulate. They struggle to generalize across novel objects, diverse environments, and varied contexts such as a cart of fragile glassware that should be avoided while an empty one can be pushed. We formalize this problem as open-world interactive navigation and present MessyNav, a zero-shot framework based on vision-language models (VLMs). At its core, MessyNav performs explicit VLM-based affordance reasoning before task planning to decide whether to swipe, pick up, or avoid an object and produce a high-level plan. Evaluated on a mobile manipulator across real-world indoor and outdoor environments, MessyNav improves success rate by 27 percentage points (pp) over the strongest baseline in real-robot trials and achieves statistically significant gains in a larger-scale offline evaluation, without task-specific training.

- **[Vision-Language Models Are Joint Denoisers in Robotic Action Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01215)**  `arXiv:2611.01215`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Xicheng Gong, Wenxuan Song, Chengze Liu, Qiwei Li, Qize Yu, Jiaolong Yang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion-based vision-language-action (VLA) models cascade pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) with diffusion action experts, but typically restrict the VLM to providing static conditioning. This separation limits how much pretrained perception and reasoning can guide action generation, especially in out-of-distribution settings. We introduce JADE, a Joint Action DEnoiser that enables the VLM and action expert to jointly refine actions. Our key insight is that structured action latents allow the VLM to read the current noisy action together with visual observations and language instructions. At every denoising step, the VLM contextualizes the noisy action and guides the action expert through gated cross-attention, so that its guidance evolves with the action being generated. We further supervise the VLM to recover clean action latents from noisy ones, which gives it a direct action-learning signal and couples perception with control. Additionally, joint denoising enables JADE to generate actions with fewer sampling steps, helping offset the additional cost of VLM participation. An adaptive step-size schedule further balances task performance and inference latency. On RoboDojo, JADE achieves an average success rate of 12.67%, the highest among the evaluated $\pi_{0.5}$-based methods, and is competitive with recent VLA and WAM foundation models pretrained at scale. It also improves performance on RoboTwin 2.0 and raises the average success rate across four real-world bimanual tasks by 20.8 percentage points compared with $\pi_{0.5}$.

- **[RoboCurate: Harnessing Diversity with Action-Verified Synthetic Data for Robot Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18742)**  `arXiv:2602.18742`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Seungku Kim, Suhyeok Jang, Byungjun Yoon, Dongyoung Kim, John Won, Jinwoo Shin_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Synthetic data generated by video generative models has shown promise for robot learning as a scalable pipeline, but it often suffers from noisy action labels due to imperfect video generation or inaccurate action prediction. Recently, vision-language models (VLMs) have been leveraged to validate video quality, but they cannot directly evaluate the generated actions themselves. To tackle this issue, we introduce RoboCurate, a novel framework for action-verified synthetic robot data that addresses inconsistencies between generated videos and annotated actions. Specifically, RoboCurate executes the actions predicted by an inverse dynamics model (IDM) in a simulator and verifies action consistency by measuring the motion agreement between the simulator rollout and the generated video. To better leverage such a framework, we further expand the scene diversity of synthetic data beyond the available dataset through off-the-shelf generative models with structured editing axes. We then use a VLM to pre-filter physically implausible generated videos before verifying their action labels. Across diverse manipulation scenarios, RoboCurate yields substantial relative improvements in success rates compared to using only real data, achieving +90.3% on GR-1 Tabletop (300 demos), +29.1% on DexMimicGen in the pre-training setup on simulation benchmarks, and +179.9% and +42.9% in the co-finetuning setup on challenging real-world ALLEX and OpenArm humanoid benchmarks, respectively.

- **[Event-Grounded Sparse Autoencoders for Vision-Language-Action Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17204)**  `arXiv:2605.17204`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Xinchen Jin, Aditya Chatterjee, Pranav Kumar, Rohan Paleja_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies translate language and visual inputs into robot actions, where their hidden representations directly shape closed-loop behavior. However, mechanistic interpretability tools from language and vision-language models do not transfer cleanly to VLAs: outputs are robot actions rather than human-readable tokens, and validating internal changes requires expensive closed-loop rollouts. We propose an event-grounded interpretability pipeline that anchors SAE feature analysis to behavioral events rather than text contexts. We extract keyframes from end-effector trajectories and cluster them within each task using visual, robot-state, and temporal cues. The resulting events guide SAE feature ranking, with optional VLM annotations providing descriptions of the events. To our knowledge, our pipeline is among the first to ground SAE-based VLA analysis in closed-loop behavioral events. Across two simulation architectures and a real-robot study, event-grounded ranking yields the strongest causal effects on OpenVLA and transfers to the continuous action chunks of $\pi_{0.5}$. SAE is a sparse but imperfect intervention basis: usability varies with architecture and intervention site, and aggressive intervention reveals safety and interpretability limits. Overall, event-grounded SAE analysis emerges as a practical starting point for behavior-anchored VLA interpretability, motivating future work on SAE analysis beyond end-effector motion, finer-grained closed-loop evaluation, and safe interventions for high-stakes VLA deployments.

- **[AffordJEV: Affordance Grounding by Confirming Candidate Regions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07896)**  `arXiv:2611.07896`  `cs.RO`  
  _Jincheng Li, Yutian Zhang, Jianyu Zhang, Xinshun Wang, Mengyuan Liu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Affordance grounding asks which visible part of an object performs a requested operation, a basic capability for embodied manipulation and human--robot interaction. Most existing methods follow a System Two paradigm: a multimodal language model first writes a chain of thought and then turns its conclusion into a box or points for a segmenter. This incurs considerable inference cost, gives the semantic decision little direct control over the extent of the final region, and makes mistakes hard to correct. Yet locating an affordance is essentially a perceptual judgment that need not be argued out in words. We therefore propose, in which frozen segmenters propose candidate regions and a System One model decides directly which of them perform the operation, generating no text and reading each decision from a single forward pass. Our contributions are twofold. (1) An inference framework that confirms candidate regions level by level, from object to part to sub-part, with evidence passed between the levels in both directions so that the levels correct one another. (2) A System One model trained for this framework: existing System One models are built for general-purpose decisions, judge one candidate per request and are ill-suited to the framework's region questions, so ours learns directly from the requests the framework itself produces and judges all candidates of a level at once. Trained only on InstructPart and evaluated with definition-aligned operation words, outperforms chain-of-thought methods in domain and in zero-shot transfer to UMD and IIT-AFF, is better overall than existing System One models run in the same framework, and needs fewer judge requests. To our knowledge, is the first approach to bring System One models to affordance grounding.

- **[Compositional Unalignment: Auditing Safety in Large Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07216)**  `arXiv:2611.07216`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `cs.CL`  
  _Hong kyu Lee, Kai Shu, Li Xiong_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Large vision-language models (LVLMs) are vulnerable to compositional harm: an image and a prompt that are benign in isolation become harmful through the model's cross-modal reasoning. Recent alignment methods report strong scores on compositional safety benchmarks, but an LLM judge only measures observed behavior and cannot reveal how robustly safety is integrated into the model. We argue that un-alignment provides a complementary audit of alignment robustness. We first show that minimal supervised fine-tuning reaches an LLM-judge score comparable to a multi-stage reinforcement-learning pipeline, so similar benchmark scores cannot establish robustness by themselves. - **[Visual-Redundancy-Controlled Parallel Decoding for Diffusion-Based Multimodal Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25820)**  `arXiv:2605.25820`  `cs.LG`  
  _Yulin Yuan, Hongshuo Zhao, Xiangming Meng_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion-based multimodal large language models (dMLLMs) decode by iteratively predicting tokens at multiple masked positions in parallel. This turns each decoding step into a position-selection problem: the model must choose not only which predictions are reliable in isolation, but also which positions should be committed together as context for later decoding steps. Existing confidence-based decoding ranks masked positions independently and commits the top-K positions, largely ignoring whether the committed tokens provide complementary visual grounding. Across diverse multimodal benchmarks, VRCD reduces visual redundancy and remaining-position entropy with modest runtime overhead.   _Jai Kumar Sharma, Peeyush Tapadiya_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language classifiers can learn new labels from text alone, so the label lists they serve keep growing. We show that this growth silently changes conformal decisions about old labels. Common softmax-based conformal scores compare each label with an average over all labels; adding labels changes that average, and with it the score of every old label, even when the model, the image, and the old calibration data stay the same. Recalibrating after the update keeps the coverage guarantee, yet across eleven settings it changes at least one old decision on 53-90% of new images.
