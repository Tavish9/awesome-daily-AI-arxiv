- **[Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18562)**  `arXiv:2611.18562`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Jiaming Tang, Zhuoyang Zhang, Junxian Guo, Fangzhou Zhao, Yilong Zhao, Zekai Li, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robot-use agents (RUAs) have emerged as a promising approach to general-purpose robot control. However, strong performance has so far been achieved only by frontier proprietary models such as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2, whereas open-weight models remain substantially less capable. We show that an open-weight 27B reasoning model can be trained to use real robots natively through reinforcement learning, entirely in simulation and without demonstrations. Like a computer-use agent (CUA), the model observes the environment, reasons in a chain of thought, and generates the next end-effector pose, without any harness. We build a Post-training Engine for Agentic Robotics (PEAR) and use it to train the model on simulated robot tasks at scale with robot-use reinforcement learning (RURL), mirroring how frontier models acquire computer use. We find that the skills acquired in simulation generalize well beyond the training tasks: they transfer to an unseen robot, to the real world, and to novel instructions. After training on LIBERO and RoboDojo in simulation, the model succeeds in 60% of our trials on a real dual-arm robot, whereas the base model succeeds in none. On LIBERO, training raises the success rate from 1.6% to 74.4%, approaching GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol at maximum reasoning effort. Compared with $\pi_{0.5}$, a vision-language-action model fine-tuned on demonstrations, our model is substantially more robust to changes in the task itself. On LIBERO-Pro, $\pi_{0.5}$ succeeds in only 0.75% of episodes when assigned a new task in a familiar scene whereas our model succeeds in 57.4%. Finally, the model retains its general capabilities, with no measurable degradation on nine reasoning, coding and vision benchmarks. These results highlight robot-use reinforcement learning as a promising component of foundation-model post-training.

- **[La-JEPA: Compact Robot Policies with Latent World Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08156)**  `arXiv:2611.08156`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Mateo Clémente, George Z. Xue, Amin Abyaneh, Liheng Ma, Rui Heng Yang, Tongtong Cao, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  World-action models adapt pretrained video generators, and vision-language-action models build on vision-language backbones, tying policy architecture and inference cost to large transformers designed for generation rather than control. While recent world-action models mostly scale the video backbone and its data, we argue that the choice of latent space is just as critical, and that the policy should be trained natively in that space. We introduce Latent Action JEPA (La-JEPA), a compact world-action model whose policy transformer is trained from scratch in the feature space of a pretrained V-JEPA 2.1 encoder; it predicts future features with actions during training and generates only actions at inference. Among policies without embodied pretraining, La-JEPA achieves state-of-the-art average RoboTwin 2.0 success under both clean-only and full training, and state-of-the-art LIBERO-Plus success with corrected instructions, while using far fewer parameters than the strongest competitors; with full training, its success scales with model size up to 2.1B. Its S and B policies run two to five times faster than Fast-WAM and Faster-WAM while outperforming both on LIBERO-Plus and RoboTwin 2.0, and La-JEPA outperforms the closest JEPA-based methods, LeapBot-WA and JEPA-WAM, without their pretrained policy backbones. On four real-robot tasks, its average success is comparable to $\pi_{0.5}$. A design study shows that JEPA features outperform video VAE latents, most clearly out of distribution, and that training-time future prediction improves the policy even when skipped at inference. Tailoring the policy's design, including its latent space, to control can thus matter more than the knowledge transferred from a pretrained generative backbone, suggesting that robot foundation models may benefit from architectures designed for control rather than inherited from generation.

- **[SiMDex: Mining Similar Egocentric Videos for Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04196)**  `arXiv:2608.04196`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Nie Lin, Takehiko Ohkawa, Sijin Chen, Ruoshi Wen, Zhuohang Li, Liqun Huang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent years have witnessed an explosive trend of scaling ego-centric human videos for robot manipulation, yet it remains unclear which data actually benefits dexterous manipulation. We present SiMDex, a similarity-based data mining framework that casts human data selection for VLA post-training in dexterous manipulation as a recommendation problem. For each robot demonstration, SiMDex runs a recall-ranking-re-ranking cascade over ~32M egocentric human samples: two stages, recall and ranking, select a task-relevant subset in a morphology-agnostic action space, and re-ranking verifies it on pixels, with no change to VLA architecture or training. Against a strong baseline trained with an equal amount of randomly sampled human data, SiMDex uses only ~1.49M mined samples (<5% of the pool) yet improves the overall success rate from 47.7% to 61.7%, showing that selective curation outperforms indiscriminate data mixing.

- **[Tripwire: Robot Agents that Program Their Own Interrupts under Human Disturbance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27182)**  `arXiv:2611.27182`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Sunli Chen, Yun Zong, Qinhong Zhou, Xiaowen Qiu, Jiaben Chen, Zeyuan Yang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Agent harnesses are turn-based: the agent acts, waits for the result, then reasons. When the action invokes a pretrained robot policy, such as a vision-language-action (VLA) or world action model (WAM), the policy may react continuously to observations, but the agent cannot revisit its decision until the call returns. Meanwhile, a person may step into the robot's path or move the object it is about to grasp. Invoking the agent more often narrows this blind interval, but a reasoning model takes seconds per decision and most control steps need no new one. We propose TRIP, a training-free, event-driven harness in which the agent programs its own interrupts. With each call, the agent writes a tripwire: a lightweight, stateful Python program that runs alongside the policy, can query a compact VLM (for example, to locate a person and measure their distance), and decides whether the call continues or control returns to the agent. When it fires, the harness halts the policy and returns control with the reason; the agent then reassesses, adapts, and continues under a rewritten tripwire. We evaluate TRIP with three VLAs and one WAM on RoboHD, a benchmark of 168 RoboCasa365 scenes in which a simulated person disturbs the robot and any human-robot contact counts as failure. Compared with a turn-based harness using the same agent and tools, TRIP halves the rate of human contact with the strongest policy, raising safety from 61.3% to 81.3%, without sacrificing task success (38.7% vs. 31.0%). Real-robot experiments show that TRIP completes two manipulation tasks under human interference where the turn-based harness and the policy alone largely fail.
  </details>

- **[MAWAM: A Multi-Agent World-Action Model for Coordinated Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25105)**  `arXiv:2611.25105`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Xianzhe Fan, Chi-Pin Huang, Fu-En Yang, Jing Wang, Yecheng Wu, Yu-Cheng Chou, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Every move in a robot team changes the world in which its teammates must act. Coordinated manipulation therefore calls for predicting the team's actions together with the physical future they jointly create. We introduce MAWAM, a multi-agent world-action model that jointly generates each robot's future views and executable action chunks in a single sample. Built from a pretrained single-robot world-action backbone, MAWAM jointly denoises all robots' action and future-view tokens through cross-agent attention, while preserving a separate control interface for each robot. MAWAM achieves the highest success on all three real-robot tasks (laundry-bag collection, bath-towel folding, and umbrella handover), with 67.8% mean success, 18.9 percentage points above MA-VLA trained on the same demonstrations and cameras. In simulation, MAWAM reaches 90.3% mean success on eight tasks with teams of up to four arms. Ablations show that cross-agent attention and team-stage future-video supervision both improve coordinated control: without attention, a four-arm relay falls from 95.0% to 39.0%, and without this supervision, Place Food falls from 78.0% to 47.0%.

- **[FoRe: A Force-Guided Framework for Autonomous Grasp Recovery in Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27011)**  `arXiv:2611.27011`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Shuai Peng, Yongkai Liao, Yuxin Cheng, Haoyuan Wang, Jian Huang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA) models have demonstrated strong performance in grasp-based manipulation, yet reliable recovery from local grasping failures remains challenging. Specifically, in pick-and-place tasks, policies may continue transport after a failed grasp instead of retrying, potentially reflecting overreliance on proprioceptive cues for stage progression and limited retry supervision. Existing recovery approaches typically rely on deployment-time guidance or demonstration augmentation. We introduce Force-guided Recovery (FoRe), a framework for autonomous recovery from local grasping failures. Using force feedback, FoRe partitions demonstrations into approach-grasp and transport-placement stages to train separate policies. To supplement limited retry supervision, FoRe then initializes a dedicated retry policy from the approach-grasp policy and refines it through local reinforcement learning (RL) from post-failure states. To avoid inappropriate stage progression, a controller combines force feedback with grasp-command status at inference to coordinate the stage policies, detect grasp failures, and invoke the retry policy. We also construct LIBERO-SUCTION, a force-augmented suction manipulation benchmark adapted from LIBERO, with complete demonstration trajectories for policy training. FoRe consistently improves task success rates for VLA-Adapter and $\pi_{0.5}$ on LIBERO-SUCTION, LIBERO, and real-world tasks. On LIBERO-SUCTION, average absolute gains are 18.4% and 6.5%, respectively. The corresponding real-world absolute gains are 25.0% and 21.7%. FoRe also remains effective with only one demonstration per task on both simulation benchmarks.
  </details>

- **[GenPA: Image Generators as Efficient Robot Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27446)**  `arXiv:2611.27446`  `cs.RO`  
  _Zian Deng, Tianyi Zhang, Zilong Zheng, Ziyu Liu, Tao Zhang, Yao Lyu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Pretrained visual generation models have shown great promise in robotic manipulation. However, a representative line of work still treats future visual prediction as the central link between visual generative pretraining and action learning, retaining it as a training objective even when future scenes are no longer generated at inference time. We argue that the instruction-following, spatial understanding, and visual imagination capabilities acquired through image generation pretraining can support control without future visual prediction: image generation models themselves can be directly adapted into powerful action learners. Building on this perspective, we propose GenPA, which fine-tunes an image generation backbone with a lightweight 19M-parameter action regression adapter and reuses its native generative objective for perception co-training. The native image-editing interface accommodates different visual targets without task-specific perception heads, enabling comparisons of current-scene perception, future prediction, and their combinations within a common policy architecture. At deployment, GenPA requires only a single backbone forward pass and action regression, without visual generation or iterative action denoising. Without additional large-scale robot-data pretraining, GenPA demonstrates strong control performance and robustness to perturbations across simulation benchmarks (99.10% success on LIBERO and 83.79% on LIBERO-Plus) and real-world manipulation tasks. Under compiled inference, GenPA takes 20.9 ms per action chunk on a single H100 GPU, achieving 1.7-3.4x speedups over strong VLA and WAM baselines. Our latency analysis further highlights architectural advantages of visual generation backbones for efficient robot control. Ablations across visual tasks and their combinations identify current-depth supervision as a simple, effective training recipe.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Visual labeling of egocentric demonstrations often leaves contiguous gaps in action supervision. Reliable poses around a gap still provide its net translation and rotation. We use this information to train vision-language-action policies while preserving ordinary flow matching on reliable local actions. A composition objective constrains the aggregate motion of missing-action estimates, combining multiple fixed windows when a gap exceeds the policy horizon. A shared interval branch learns from reliable endpoints and supplies the composition target. On LIBERO with 20% of demonstrations and 70.5% missing actions, the main method achieves 87.9% mean success over three training runs. In one paired comparison with matched observation exposure and training cost, its advantage over masking is 10.5%. On Ego-informed RoboTwin gaps, success is 45.4%, versus 40.3% for masking. On five real-robot tasks, mean success is 59.6%, versus 54.8% for interval flow matching alone and 48.8% for masking.

Global and detail codebooks capture shared interaction structure and fine-grained execution variation respectively, preserving both reusable patterns and execution details. - **[Efficient VLA Reinforcement Learning via Subchunk Rollouts in Latent World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27645)**  `arXiv:2611.27645`  `cs.LG`  
  _Nan Tang, Han-Tao Zhang, Jing-Cheng Pang, Hao-Nan Wang, Hao-Xiang Ren, Yi-Fei Sheng, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) can improve pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) policies through imagined experiences. However, existing VLA-MBRL methods primarily rely on large-scale image or video generation world models, incurring large computational overhead. Latent world models offer a more efficient alternative but face two challenges: compounding errors during recursive prediction and incompatibility with VLAs' visual input interfaces. In this work, we propose LS-MBPO, a lightweight residual MBRL framework that leverages latent world models to improve upon pretrained VLAs. LS-MBPO introduces two key techniques: (1) Hierarchical Forcing, which combines dense causal supervision with autoregressive feedback to mitigate compounding prediction errors; and (2) Latent Subchunk Rollouts, which leverage action chunks from a frozen VLA policy and a latent residual policy for successive subchunk refinement, bridging the gap between latent imagination and VLA policy execution. We further introduce a Progress-Conditioned Branch-Start Curriculum that progressively shifts rollout starting points from later to earlier task stages, enabling effective policy learning from short imagined rollouts. Extensive experiments on LIBERO, RoboTwin, and a real-world ALOHA robot show that LS-MBPO achieves superior task performance and training efficiency compared to existing methods.

- **[SCAP: Stochastic Context for Action Expert Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27187)**  `arXiv:2611.27187`  `cs.CV` `cs.RO`  
  _Xiao Lin, Yaoxu Lyu, Ronghao Dang, Zhili Liu, Kaijing Ma, Yang Zhou, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Single-modality action-expert pretraining controls competition from visual shortcuts before vision--language--action (VLA) adaptation. Yet single-modality pretraining can impair the vision--language interactions needed for subsequent joint prediction. We construct a diagnostic model in which VA or LA pretraining can disable an initially active pathway for joint vision--language modeling. In this model, subsequent training with complete visual and language inputs cannot restore the pathway, even with unlimited adaptation. We therefore introduce Stochastic Context for Action Expert Pretraining (SCAP), a new action-expert pretraining stage that precedes full-input training. During this stage, visual and text tokens are randomly masked before entering the VLM. In this unified diagnostic model, SCAP can preserve the joint vision--language modeling pathway during pretraining and subsequent full-input training. Compared with direct full-input training, it can also reduce reliance on visual shortcuts and increase reliance on language instructions. SCAP consistently improves performance across four different VLA architectures. In real-robot experiments, it achieves 76.67% average success across three instruction-conditioned manipulation tasks, outperforming full-input pretraining by 25 percentage points.

- **[Beyond a Single Human Demonstration: Learning from Scored Trajectory Alternatives for End-to-End Driving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27515)**  `arXiv:2611.27515`  `cs.CV`  
  _Xinyao Rao, Zecheng Tang, Liuxuan Sun, Zheyuan Lai, Minjun Yu, Xun Cao, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Human driving logs record only one trajectory per scene, leaving other feasible driving choices without explicit trajectory targets or quality labels. This limits supervision for end-to-end planners that generate and refine multiple candidate trajectories. Meanwhile, conditioning refinement on a large vision-language model adds substantial online inference cost. We propose DriveSTA, which extends autoregressive proposal generation and proposal-conditioned residual flow refinement with scored trajectory alternatives. For each training scene, we generate alternatives offline through smooth deformations of the recorded trajectory and evaluate their simulated execution against logged traffic. Diverse, high-quality alternatives supervise distinct proposals and define residual targets for quality-guided denoising, while candidates spanning the full score range train a scorer to assess driving quality. A shared joint-embedding predictive architecture (JEPA), adapted through masked video-feature prediction, encodes observed frames once and provides temporal context for both refinement and scoring. On NAVSIM v1 Navtest, DriveSTA ranks first among the compared planners trained on NAVSIM data alone. With expanded training data, DriveSTA-Scale achieves state-of-the-art results among the compared methods on Navtest and closed-loop HUGSIM, while reducing mean planner forward-pass latency by 73% relative to ChainFlow-VLA in matched evaluations.
  </details>
