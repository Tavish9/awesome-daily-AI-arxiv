- **[Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18562)**  `arXiv:2611.18562`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Jiaming Tang, Zhuoyang Zhang, Junxian Guo, Fangzhou Zhao, Yilong Zhao, Zekai Li, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robot-use agents (RUAs) have emerged as a promising approach to general-purpose robot control. However, strong performance has so far been achieved only by frontier proprietary models such as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2, whereas open-weight models remain substantially less capable. We show that an open-weight 27B reasoning model can be trained to use real robots natively through reinforcement learning, entirely in simulation and without demonstrations. Like a computer-use agent (CUA), the model observes the environment, reasons in a chain of thought, and generates the next end-effector pose, without any harness. We build a Post-training Engine for Agentic Robotics (PEAR) and use it to train the model on simulated robot tasks at scale with robot-use reinforcement learning (RURL), mirroring how frontier models acquire computer use. We find that the skills acquired in simulation generalize well beyond the training tasks: they transfer to an unseen robot, to the real world, and to novel instructions. After training on LIBERO and RoboDojo in simulation, the model succeeds in 60% of our trials on a real dual-arm robot, whereas the base model succeeds in none. On LIBERO, training raises the success rate from 1.6% to 74.4%, approaching GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol at maximum reasoning effort. Compared with $\pi_{0.5}$, a vision-language-action model fine-tuned on demonstrations, our model is substantially more robust to changes in the task itself. On LIBERO-Pro, $\pi_{0.5}$ succeeds in only 0.75% of episodes when assigned a new task in a familiar scene whereas our model succeeds in 57.4%. Finally, the model retains its general capabilities, with no measurable degradation on nine reasoning, coding and vision benchmarks. These results highlight robot-use reinforcement learning as a promising component of foundation-model post-training.
  </details>

- **[E0: Expressive Fine-Grained Discrete Action Prediction for Vision-Language-Action Models via Tweedie Discrete Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21542)**  `arXiv:2511.21542`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Zhihao Zhan, Jiaying Zhou, Likui Zhang, Qinhan Lyu, Hao Liu, Weizheng Li, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models offer a unified framework for robotic manipulation by integrating visual perception, language understanding, and control generation. However, existing VLA systems still struggle to generalize across diverse tasks, scenes, and camera viewpoints, and often produce coarse or unstable actions. We argue that these limitations are closely tied to the structural properties of actions in VLA settings, including the inherent multi-peaked nature of action distributions, the token-based symbolic reasoning of pretrained VLM/VLA backbones, and the effective finite resolution imposed by real-world robotic control. Motivated by these properties, we introduce E0, a tweedie discrete diffusion framework that formulates action generation as iterative denoising over quantized action tokens. By operating in a discrete action space with a principled diffusion process, E0 naturally aligns with token-based reasoning, supports fine-grained yet executable action control, and avoids the distributional mismatch of masking-based discrete diffusion. Experiments on LIBERO, VLABench, ManiSkill, and a real-world Franka arm demonstrate that E0 achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse environments, outperforming strong baselines by 8.1% on average.
  </details>

- **[ESI: An Embodied Self-Improvement Flywheel for LLM-VLA Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18176)**  `arXiv:2611.18176`  `cs.RO`  
  _Yundi Cai, Zihan Wang, Chenxi Han, Yi Cheng, Houde Liu, Zilei Wang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Independently pretrained language models and vision-language-action (VLA) policies provide complementary building blocks for embodied agents, but connecting them does not automatically produce coordinated behavior. The VLA may generate actions that diverge from the LLM's intended subtask and, upon regaining control after a correction, undo the promising state restored by the LLM. We introduce ESI (Embodied Self-Improvement), an automated flywheel that uses environmental interaction to align reasoning and action. ESI treats the modular agent as the unit of post-training, repeatedly refining the prompts that guide the LLM, the shared skill interface that expresses its intent, and the VLA parameters that realize that intent, then redeploying these components together. Our key insight is that collaboration itself can supply supervision for improving this interface. The LLM's subtask decisions provide semantic supervision for what the policy should execute, while its effective corrections and subsequent student continuations provide action-level supervision for recovery and handoff. The student also learns from its own effective execution, allowing locally useful experience to contribute even when the overall episode fails. On ten selected RoboDojo tasks, ESI achieves a mean Score of 62.03 and a mean success rate of 49.41% using an LLM-VLA agent without generating task-specific control code. On both metrics, it outperforms the published reference results of all hybrid embodied agents, including the Agent+WAM system GO-3 Nano Preview (47.27 Score and 36.60% success), as well as all compared code-based methods.
  </details>

- **[Agentic-Teacher: An Agentic Data Engine for Failure-Driven VLA Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17394)**  `arXiv:2611.17394`  `cs.RO`  
  _Gongyue Teng, Yuqing Xie, Yixian Zhang, Feng Gao, Shu'ang Yu, Jiaji Rao, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Improving a pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) model requires demonstrations that address its weaknesses and provide effective supervision for learning. Coding agents can autonomously generate demonstrations through environment interaction, yet successful execution alone does not ensure effective supervision for VLA learning. We introduce Agentic-Teacher, an autonomous data engine for failure-driven VLA post-training. Given a VLA checkpoint, a task specification, and configured environment tools, the engine analyzes failed rollouts, selects intervention states, and iteratively develops closed-loop code policies through execution feedback. Validated policies become reusable demonstration generators that collect successful continuations from states where the VLA struggles. To make these demonstrations suitable for learning, Agentic-Teacher introduces temporally correlated action noise to broaden state-action coverage, removes idle frames while preserving gripper transitions, and filters trajectories using the frozen base model's flow-matching loss. The resulting demonstrations are combined with successful VLA rollouts for post-training, yielding an updated policy that operates without coding-agent assistance. Agentic-Teacher thus connects the weaknesses of a target VLA to both the generation and curation of its training data. In simulation, it improves $\pi_0$ success rates from 79.4% to 87.8% on LIBERO-10, from 80.4% to 90.6% on MetaWorld-10, and from 0% to 30.0% across three RoboCasa tasks.
  </details>

- **[CARE-Drive: Coupled Alternating Refinement for Ego-trajectories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15360)**  `arXiv:2611.15360`  `cs.CV` `cs.AI`  
  _Xiao Ma, Yijun Chen, Zhiquan Hu, Jicheng Zhao, Ran Ji, Yuming Li, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA) models bring multimodal understanding and world knowledge to end-to-end autonomous driving. To further improve the quality of predicted trajectories, existing studies introduce candidate refinement, residual denoising, and token correction. However, these methods typically focus on unified refinement of the entire trajectory, paying limited attention to the distinct physical quantities and their interactions. We further investigate physical specialization and coordination in trajectory refinement, centering on position and heading for simpler and more intuitive correction modeling. To this end, we propose CARE-Drive (Coupled Alternating Refinement for Ego-trajectories), which decomposes trajectory refinement into waypoint residual correction and position-conditioned heading generation. Through imitation learning and a future-token auxiliary task, the backbone provides predictive representations and a base trajectory. The position expert predicts bounded metric waypoint residuals, while the heading expert reconstructs vehicle orientation from adjacent angular increments conditioned on the corrected path. With the backbone frozen, the two experts are optimized alternately through supervised initialization and reinforcement fine-tuning. This design simplifies refinement through clear physical specialization while preserving conditional coupling during prediction and optimization. On NAVSIM v1, CARE-Drive achieves 88.8 PDMS, improving by 2.4 points over the unrefined base trajectories generated by the same backbone. Ablation studies further support the effectiveness of specialized experts and staged training.
  </details>

- **[Gamer: A Generalist Vision-Language-Action Foundation Model for Long-Horizon Real-Time Game Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18397)**  `arXiv:2611.18397`  `cs.CL` `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Run Luo, Kuan Zhang, Qi Deng, Yukun Chen, Zhihao Yang, Yufei Zhang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We introduce Gamer, a generalist agent framework built for long-horizon tasks in complex interactive environments. Its end-to-end architecture combines perception, memory management, and multi-step reasoning. A shared training and deployment pipeline supports interaction with both conventional 2D graphical interfaces and complex 3D simulated worlds. We first continue pre-training on a dataset constructed from tens of thousands of hours of device-level keyboard and mouse interaction. These trajectories span simulated games and embodied environments, allowing the model to learn environmental dynamics across both physical and virtual domains. Sparse-thinking instruction tuning then substantially reduces redundant computation while preserving the reasoning needed for complex decisions. We train the policy for long-horizon interaction with a stable multi-turn reinforcement learning method that addresses finite context windows, accumulated rollout errors, and sparse rewards. For real-time interaction, a cloud-edge inference engine serves numerous simulated game environments concurrently, supports unbounded-context execution, and keeps online inference latency low. Across multiple challenging 3D open-world games, Gamer demonstrates solid generalization and operational efficiency comparable to skilled players. It also achieves competitive results on a range of conventional 2D game benchmarks. These results suggest that even repetitive keyboard and mouse trajectories can support substantial gains from scaling data and learning algorithms. Training on these trajectories helps Gamer learn complex skills more steadily in environments with both physical and digital properties. This approach offers a path toward generalist agents that can interact across digital and physical worlds.

- **[D-VLA: A High-Concurrency Distributed Asynchronous Reinforcement Learning Framework for Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13276)**  `arXiv:2605.13276`  `cs.AI` `cs.RO`  
  _Yucheng Guo, Yongjian Guo, Zhong Guan, Wen Huang, Haodong Yue, Shuai Di, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  The rapid evolution of Embodied AI has enabled Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to excel in multimodal perception and task execution. However, applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to these massive models in large-scale distributed environments faces severe systemic bottlenecks, primarily due to the resource conflict between high-fidelity physical simulation and the intensive VRAM/bandwidth demands of deep learning. This conflict often leaves overall throughput constrained by execution-phase inefficiencies. To address these challenges, we propose D-VLA, a high-concurrency, low-latency distributed RL framework for large-scale embodied foundation models. D-VLA introduces "Plane Decoupling," physically isolating high-frequency training data from low-frequency weight control to eliminate interference between simulation and optimization. We further design a four-thread asynchronous "Swimlane" pipeline, enabling full parallel overlap of sampling, inference, gradient computation, and parameter distribution. Additionally, a dual-pool VRAM management model and topology-aware replication resolve memory fragmentation and optimize communication efficiency. Experiments on benchmarks like LIBERO show that D-VLA significantly outperforms mainstream RL frameworks in throughput and sampling efficiency for billion-parameter VLA models. In trillion-parameter scalability tests, our framework maintains exceptional stability and linear speedup, providing a robust system for high-performance general-purpose embodied agents.
  </details>

- **[VLAN: Vision-Language Accessible Navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18346)**  `arXiv:2611.18346`  `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Jie Hu, Yu Zheng, Wenzhi Wu, Weixing Feng, Cunlei Cheng, Jiaming Zhang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Accessible mobility is user-conditioned rather than scene-intrinsic, as walkable paths for one embodiment are inaccessible to another. Yet, existing benchmarks evaluate traversability under a fixed and limited embodiment. We present Vision-Language Accessible Navigation (VLAN), containing 1,000 scenes, 20K navigation trajectories, and 160K keyframes across diverse lighting, environmental conditions, scene semantics, and path geometries. For each trajectory, VLAN provides embodiment-specific actions and accessibility features for legged and wheeled agents, including humanoid and quadruped robots and people with visual or mobility impairments, while everyday obstacles and accessibility labels are categorized as Passable, Bypass, HighRisk, and Blocked. Unlike goal-only evaluation, our benchmark measures physical safety and alignment with user-specific constraints via goal-reaching, collision-aware, and accessibility-aligned metrics. We benchmark modern vision-language models and find they often fail to consistently adapt obstacle relevance, path selection, and safety criteria across user profiles, suggesting accessibility-aware navigation requires dedicated datasets, tasks, and metrics beyond conventional embodied navigation evaluation. All data and code open-source atthis https URL.

- **[FlashRT-PAI: Fast Kernels Are Not Fast Pipelines for Physical AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24349)**  `arXiv:2610.24349`  `cs.AI`  
  _Liang Su, David Chen, Jason Li, Kevin Guo, Teresa Lv_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA), video-action (VA), and world action models (WAMs) form the backbone of modern physical AI systems, yet their real-time single-batch inference remains a defining bottleneck. Latency is dominated not by individual kernel computation, but by system-level overhead: thousands of short kernel launches, intermediate data movement across operator boundaries, and redundant recomputation across consecutive decisions. General-purpose graph compilers cannot address these costs, as they optimize individual calls in isolation and lack visibility into cross-call state semantics and global pipeline structure. We present FLASHRT-PAI, a low-latency inference engine built around model-hardware pipeline co-design rather than isolated kernel optimization. FLASHRT-PAI combines three core mechanisms: deep kernel fusion with launch overlap eliminates inter-operator boundaries and launch gaps; an execution contract formalizes the pipeline's stage DAG, update rates, and named buffers to enable cross-decision state reuse and selective refresh; and execution-state capsules enable fast snapshot, restore, and fork of persistent pipeline state. The system also supports drop-in acceleration of unmodified PyTorch models via parity- and latency-gated kernel replacement. Evaluation covers six policies on RTX 5090 and four on Jetson AGX Thor. At matched 16-bit precision, FLASHRT-PAI is 1.3-3.7x faster than official compiled paths. Combining pipeline co-design with reduced precision delivers 3.38-12.66x speedup over official eager implementations. In LIBERO-10, four-bit pi0.5 achieves 94.04% success on Thor and 93.32% on RTX 5090, compared with 92.4% for the measured official FP16 reference. FLASHRT-PAI is open source, distributed via Hugging Face Kernel Hub, and its opt-in pi0.5 kernels have been adopted into the LeRobot framework.
  </details>

- **[Recursive Belief Vision Language Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20659)**  `arXiv:2602.20659`  `cs.AI`  
  _Vaidehi Bagaria, Bijo Sebastian, Nirav Kumar Patel_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action models must enable agents to execute long-horizon tasks under partial observability. However, most existing approaches remain observation-driven, relying on short context windows or repeated queries to vision-language models (VLMs). This leads to loss of task progress, action repetition under perceptual aliasing, and high inference latency. While semantic grounding is important, long-horizon manipulation fundamentally requires persistent, action-conditioned state representations. Current VLAs lack such representations and exhibit limited temporal and physical reasoning, making them ill-suited for multi-stage control. This paper introduces RB-VLA, a belief-centric architecture trained with self-supervised world-model objectives that maintains a compact latent state encoding task-relevant history, dynamics, and object interactions. Queried once per task, the VLM provides high-level intent, while the belief tracks task progress and enables phase-aware, causally grounded control under partial observability without storing raw observations or scaling memory with time. The belief and intent jointly condition a diffusion policy for robust closed-loop execution. RB-VLA outperforms prior VLAs on long-horizon benchmarks, achieving 52.5 percent and 37.5 percent higher success rates on multi-stage pick-and-place and stacking tasks, respectively, compared to pi_0. It also reduces inference latency by up to five times relative to baselines and eliminates memory growth across timesteps observed in existing VLAs. Ablations show the belief module is the primary driver of performance, increasing success rates from 32.5 percent without belief to 77.5 percent with belief.
  </details>
