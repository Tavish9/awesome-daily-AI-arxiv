- **[Same Path, Less Time: Training-Free Fast Execution of Vision-Language-Action Models under Inference Latency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01390)**  `arXiv:2611.01390`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Fuyi Chen_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA) models predict chunks of future actions, yet deployed robots waste time in two ways that are independent of model capability: they halt while the next chunk is inferred, and they move at the speed of slow, cautious demonstrations. Existing remedies remove one loss each, and stacking them fails, because asynchrony and speed-up each change the correspondence between planned progress and executed motion. We present Path-preserving Time Scaling (PTS), a training-free execution layer for frozen action-chunking policies built on one principle: the robot's motion is a time reparameterization of a reference path, and the path changes only in response to new observations. A path layer decodes every replan deterministically in one step and enters it at the progress consumed during inference. A time layer sums the planned increments, keeps the arm at 1x while the gripper moves, and caps the rate by the actuator's linear range and by chunk feasibility under latency. We show that misaligned entry and stochastic sampling add a bias and a variance to the continuation error, and derive a break-even success rate that states when speeding up pays off. On LIBERO with two frozen VLAs under injected inference latency, PTS raises task throughput over the strongest deployable baseline by 33% for pi0.5 and 104% for SmolVLA while matching or improving the success rate, is best on all six model-suite combinations, and exceeds zero-latency 1x execution. Against asynchronous 1x execution with the same one-step decoder, the time layer alone adds 28% and 38%. On LIBERO-Dynamic it improves on stop-and-wait execution by 38% and is comparable to asynchronous 1x execution.

- **[Behavioral Steering of Vision-Language-Action Models in the Low-Parameter Regime](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00522)**  `arXiv:2611.00522`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _James Tcheng, Feiyu Gavin Zhu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Reinforcement learning (RL) is increasingly used to adapt pretrained vision-language-action models (VLAs). We investigate the problem of behavioral steering: adapting a VLA's execution style to preferences such as carrying objects higher. To investigate how little trainable capacity such steering requires and how much pretrained capability can be retained, we freeze an OpenVLA-OFT 7B policy and optimize TinyLoRA adapters with 8 to 384 trainable parameters using either policy-gradient RL (GRPO) or evolutionary search (CMA-ES). With just 256 trainable parameters, we raise carry height by 120 mm on trained tasks at 92% success and by 74 mm on a held-out task at 80% success. By rescaling the learned update, we can tune the trade-off between style gains and capability retention without retraining. Increasing adapter capacity generally improves this trade-off, retaining more pretrained capability at comparable style gains. The choice of optimizer also depends on capacity, with CMA-ES being competitive at low parameter counts, while GRPO offers better trade-offs as capacity grows. These findings show that extremely low-dimensional updates can steer pretrained VLA behavior, with adapter capacity, optimizer choice, and update scaling offering practical control over adaptation and retention.

- **[When Human Corrections Travel: Safety in Federated VLA Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32888)**  `arXiv:2610.32888`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Vigneshwaran Subbaraju, Obaidullah Zaland, Monowar Bhuyan_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA) policies can adapt to local human intervention, while federated learning can combine distributed robot experience without pooling raw records. We examine their prospective intersection: sharing adaptations derived from diverse human corrections across tasks, environments, people, and robot embodiments. Corrections differ in modality, safety semantics(urgency, behavioral scope, etc.), and privacy sensitivity. A shared update that improves mean task success may suppress a rare safety intervention or produce an unsafe response on another robot. We argue for three requirements: preserve the context and uncertainty of multimodal corrections when deciding what to share; assess candidate updates in each receiving robot's closed loop before deployment; and evaluate leakage alongside retention of safety-critical evidence. Immediate, legible defensive behavior remains necessary during human interaction. We offer counterexamples and a falsifiable evaluation agenda, not experimental results.

- **[A Phenomenology of Neural Geometry in Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20840)**  `arXiv:2610.20840`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Mozes Jacobs, Aiden Swann, Mathilde Papillon, Siddharth Boppana, Fenil R. Doshi, Usha Bhalla, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robotic manipulation is inherently geometric, but what shape does the world take inside the policy that performs it? Prior work on vision-language-action (VLA) policies establishes that task-relevant variables are linearly readable from activations, yet readability certifies only that a variable is present and says nothing about the shape its values trace, which may be curved and multi-dimensional. Using controlled sweeps, natural rollouts, linear probes, and activation interventions, we build a taxonomy of seven task-relevant concepts in VLA models. We find that realized geometry is predominantly multi-dimensional and is shaped by the constraints under which the policy acts. Physical joint limits, for example, leave wrist orientation an open arc rather than a circle, and distance to the target, a single scalar, is realized in two orthogonal subspaces, a coarse ruler for the approach and a fine ruler for the grasp. The same geometries reappear in two further VLA models on real-world robot data, indicating that they are neither model-specific nor artifacts of simulation. We demonstrate how the findings are actionable by steering along the geometry a concept realizes. Overall, this work lays out an initial phenomenology of realized geometry in VLA policies and demonstrates that a concept's shape carries as much importance as its readability.

- **[PREM: Prediction-Regulated Memory for Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00634)**  `arXiv:2611.00634`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Xiao Huang, Ya Zhang, Weidi Xie_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  A robot's next action can depend on recent motion or earlier visual evidence absent from its current observation. A recurrent visual-memory architecture can retain this history in a visual state, but its usefulness depends on what the state represents and how it is updated with new observations. We introduce Prediction-Regulated Memory (PREM), a framework that uses action-conditioned future prediction to guide both memory learning and updating in vision-language-action policies. During training, predicting future visual features provides temporal supervision for memory learning. During execution, PREM compares current visual features with the prediction made at the preceding decision and uses the spatially normalized prediction discrepancy to regulate updates to the visual state at each spatial location. The updated visual state supplies temporal context for action generation alongside current observations. Built on $\pi_{0.5}$, PREM improves average success rate from 9.63% to 20.74% on DOMINO dynamic manipulation tasks and from 14.4% to 83.8% on the RMBench M(1) memory-dependent setting. Further evaluations on LIBERO and LIBERO-Pro show improvements in general manipulation and robustness to spatial perturbations. PREM incurs a mean inference overhead of 7.7 ms on a single NVIDIA H800 GPU.
