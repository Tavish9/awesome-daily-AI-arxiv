- **[XS-VLA: Teaching Tiny Vision-Language-Action Models with Spatial Supervision and Demonstration Conditioning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04171)**  `arXiv:2607.04171`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Iok Tong Lei, Ying Jie Yap, Wei Huang, Qingchen Xie, Qianzhi Li, Yujie Zhang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  How can richer training supervision improve robot control while keeping the deployed policy compact? We present XS-VLA, a staged training framework using teacher-derived spatial labels and demonstration-conditioned action learning. Coarse-Grained Spatial Distillation (CSD) initializes the backbone through an auxiliary region-label task. Latent Flow Matching (LFM) then conditions an action-space velocity field on a demonstration latent, using KL regularization while jointly optimizing the backbone and action modules. The deployed policy contains 243.99M parameters and operates without the teacher or posterior encoder. XS-VLA achieves 90.25% average LIBERO success in each of two training seeds, compared with 86.00% for a SmolVLA-0.25B base trained under our settings. Ablations examine both training stages through matched image pretraining and Huber/MSE controls. On three Mobile ALOHA tasks, average strict success increases from 21.7% to 65.0%. These results demonstrate the control utility of auxiliary representation initialization and regularized demonstration-conditioned flow learning for compact VLA policies

- **[MAPLE: Latent Multi-Agent Play for End-to-End Autonomous Driving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14201)**  `arXiv:2605.14201`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Rajeev Yasarla, Deepti Hegde, Hsin-Pai Cheng, Shizhong Han, Yunxiao Shi, Meysam Sadeghigooghari, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA) models are effective as end-to-end motion planners, but can be brittle when evaluated in closed-loop settings due to being trained under traditional imitation learning framework. Existing closed-loop supervision approaches lack scalability and fail to completely model a reactive environment. We propose MAPLE, a novel framework for reactive, multi-agent rollout of a dynamic driving scenario in the latent space of the VLA model. The ego vehicle and nearby traffic agents are independently controlled over multi-step horizons, while being reactive to other agents in the scene, enabling closed-loop training. MAPLE consists of two training stages: (1) supervised fine-tuning on the latent rollouts based on ground-truth trajectories, followed by (2) reinforcement learning with global and agent -specific rewards that encourage safety, progress, and interaction realism. We further propose diversity rewards that encourage the model to generate planning behaviors that may not be present in logged driving data. Notably, our closed-loop training framework is scalable and does not require external simulators, which can be computationally expensive to run and have limited visual fidelity to the real-world. MAPLE achieves state-of-the-art driving performance on Bench2Drive and demonstrates scalable, closed-loop multi-agent play for robust E2E autonomous driving systems.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We describe our solution for the 2026 BEHAVIOR Challenge: 100 long-horizon household tasks in the OmniGibson simulator. We placed 9th on the final leaderboard, the only independent team in the top 10. The core of the solution is a single vision-language-action (VLA) model, one checkpoint for all 100 tasks, built on our 1st-place 2025 solution, which is based on $\pi_{0.5}$. On the 50 tasks shared with 2025, its Q rises from 0.260 to 0.371; across all 100 tasks it scores 0.392. On top of the learned policy, 14 tasks are routed to a GPT-6 Astra coding agent that controls the robot through observation and motion tools, starting every episode from a frozen copy of its own practice session on training instances. Across these 14 tasks, Astra's mean Q is 0.639, against 0.108 for the learned policy. The public submission included only part of the Astra results and scored 0.446 Q with 27.3% full-task success.

- **[Is Your Robot's Memory Just a Clock? Temporal Shortcuts in Memory-Augmented VLA Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14488)**  `arXiv:2611.14488`  `cs.RO`  
  _Chubin Zhang, Bo An_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Memory-augmented vision-language-action (VLA) policies are meant to remember what happened. Many of them, however, fill memory by time: they sample frames uniformly across the episode and stamp each frame with its time step. Because time passes even when nothing happens, such a memory may not tell waiting from working. We show that it does not. On the counting tasks of RoboMME, we make the robot briefly pause or fidget its wrist. Such a null event changes nothing the task depends on, yet a released memory VLA counts the interval as work done and stops early. We call this failure phantom progress. It cuts success from 47 to 16 of 50 on one task and from 44 to 10 on the other. When the same frames are withheld from memory, success returns, so the cause lies in memory rather than in the interruption itself. We trace the failure to a temporal shortcut: because demonstrations proceed at a steady pace, elapsed time predicts progress. Indeed, rescaling the time stamps alone makes the policy stop early when the clock runs fast and repeat when it runs slow. Surprisingly, removing the time encoding does not cure the failure, because the frames of a null event still occupy slots of the time-sampled memory. Across RoboMME's released designs, vulnerability follows how memory is indexed. Of the fixes the diagnosis suggests, training with injected pauses does not remove the failure and impairs counting, whereas indexing memory by events recovers half or more of the loss without retraining. A memory meant to remember what happened should count events rather than clock ticks.
  </details>

  _Huaijin Pi, Zehong Shen, Yong Wang, Kechun Xu, Changpeng Wang, Dingkun Wei, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We study how to generate diverse, physically executable robot demonstrations for dexterous vision-language-action (VLA) learning. Existing demonstration-generation methods typically rely on costly teleoperated examples. Meanwhile, human hand--object datasets provide a reusable source, but existing transfer methods mainly reproduce given interactions. Turning these interactions into VLA training data further requires coverage of different objects and manipulation locations, together with executable arm and finger actions, and visual observations. To achieve this, we present DexSeed, a framework for low-cost generation of dexterous robot data from human hand--object interactions. Experiments show improved contact fidelity in retargeting and more reliable whole-arm execution than the evaluated baselines. Simulation evaluation and real-world deployment further demonstrate that a VLA fine-tuned solely on the generated demonstrations can execute language-specified grasps. Project page:this https URL.

- **[Camera equalisation of an 11.5x drop in environmental illuminance: four imitation-learning policies on an SO-101 arm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13810)**  `arXiv:2611.13810`  `cs.RO`  
  _Simon Cowling_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Real-robot benchmarks of learned visuomotor policies often record room illuminance as the lighting condition. We show that, on a typical low-cost rig, room light and image light can be almost unrelated. Four imitation-learning policies (ACT, SmolVLA, Diffusion Policy and pi0.5) were trained on the same 50 demonstrations of a pick-and-place task on an SO-101 arm. Each was run for ten rollouts at about 520 lux and again at about 45 lux, an 11.5x reduction. Success was 20/40 in normal light and 19/40 in low light: a change of -2.5 percentage points (95% CI -23 to +19), with no evidence that the policies differed in sensitivity (interaction p = 0.86). The recorded video shows why: mean image brightness was only 3% lower in low light on the front camera and 19% lower on the wrist camera. Both cameras were set to automatic exposure and white balance, which most likely absorbed the difference; exposure was not locked in this experiment. An accidental repeat of one 10-rollout block reproduced its success count (7/10) but matched the first block at only 6 of 10 placements, consistent with chance. We conclude that lux alone does not describe what a policy sees. Camera settings, and image statistics compared with the training recordings, are the quantities to report and control. Data and code are public.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robot agents can improve reusable skills, planning procedures, and recovery strategies from execution feedback, yet a fixed practice source leaves the search for new weaknesses unchanged. - **[ODEAction: From Predicting Chunks to Evolving Actions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09672)**  `arXiv:2611.09672`  `cs.RO`  
  _Qishen Wang, Pengxiang Ding, Shifeng Bao, Mingyang Sun, Hengtao Li, Yi Gao, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA) models typically generate actions in chunks, dividing continuous robot execution into separately predicted action chunks, which can introduce discontinuities at chunk boundaries. We propose ODEAction, a chunk-free action generation paradigm that replaces action chunk prediction with Continuous Action Evolution. We use an ordinary differential equation (ODE) to describe the continuous evolution of actions, and define the vector field of this ODE as the Action Transition Field to characterize how actions evolve with respect to evolution time. During inference, the model takes the current action as the initial condition and numerically solves the ODE defined by the learned Action Transition Field, thereby recursively generating subsequent actions with explicit dependencies. ODEAction is implemented as a modular action head that can be integrated with different vision-language backbones. Experiments on LIBERO, EBench, RoboTwin, and real-robot tasks show that, compared with action-chunk-based baselines, ODEAction improves both task performance and trajectory quality, with particularly pronounced gains on fine manipulation tasks.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) enable robots to perform manipulation tasks by leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to process visual observations and language instructions. However, the action prediction objective itself lacks fine-grained task-specific perceptual supervision, making it prone to relying on task-irrelevant visual shortcuts, leading attention away from target objects and key interaction areas, thereby limiting its operational performance and generalization ability. To address this, we propose Automatic Perception-Guided Attention Alignment for Robot Manipulation ($A^3$-VLA), a framework that derives task-specific visual guidance from the manipulation process itself. - **[APS-VLA: Action-Guided and Future-Aware Token Pruning for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13030)**  `arXiv:2611.13030`  `cs.AI`  
  _Xiaoyang Xing, Chun-Mei Feng, Anthony Cohn, Xiaoliang Gong, Asoke Nandi_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA) policies have demonstrated strong generalization in robotic manipulation, yet processing dense visual tokens often incurs high inference latency. Visual token pruning offers a practical way to reduce this cost, but identifying a compact set of tokens that preserves reliable action generation remains challenging. Existing methods largely rely on static or immediate heuristic cues to estimate token importance. We introduce APS-VLA, a framework that learns visual token selection from robot demonstrations while keeping the pretrained VLA backbone frozen. A lightweight Decision Influence Estimator scores tokens conditioned on current visual observations, language instructions, and robot proprioception. Its training combines two complementary signals: action supervision matches both the full-input policy predictions and demonstrated actions, while an auxiliary visual transition objective uses demonstrated actions to predict subsequent visual features from the selected representation. Across five real-world tasks on two dual-arm platforms, APS-VLA reduces mean policy inference latency from 137.25 to 93.25 ms, achieving a $1.47\times$ speedup with an average success rate of 61%, compared with 60% for the unpruned baseline.   </details>
