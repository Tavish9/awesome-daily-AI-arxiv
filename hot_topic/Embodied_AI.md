  _Yuankai Luo, Woping Chen, Tong Liang, Zhenguo Li_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often bundle architectural changes with different pretraining data, backbone scales, and optimization recipes, obscuring what actually drives progress. - **[TacInno: Teaching Vision-Language-Action Models the Contact Dynamics Beyond Vision and Action](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26405)**  `arXiv:2611.26405`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Weile Li, Yunfan Lou, Muqing Shi, Zihao Zhang, Zhenbiao Huang, Boxian Yao, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have recently been equipped with tactile sensing to perceive visually subtle contact events in contact-rich manipulation. However, adding tactile input yields inconsistent gains, and some visuo-tactile VLA models rely little on tactile input, because much of the tactile change, such as the gel deformation during gripper closure, is predictable from visual observations and executed actions. To address this problem, we introduce TacInno, which isolates the contact dynamics that vision and executed actions cannot explain, termed tactile innovation, and uses tactile innovation only as a training target. Specifically, the TacInno tokenizer, conditioned on visual observations and executed actions, compresses the future tactile change into a discrete code, and the policy, TacInno-VLA, learns to predict the code, adding no module for the code at deployment. TacInno-VLA achieves average success rates of 87.5% across six simulation tasks and 70.5% across four real-world tasks, outperforming the strongest visuo-tactile baseline by 10.7 and 9.0 percentage points, respectively. Ablation studies validate the contributions of visual and action conditioning, multi-frame encoding, and auxiliary code prediction. Together, these results demonstrate the effectiveness of tactile innovation as a supervision signal for contact-rich manipulation.

- **[UniVEP: Unifying Velocity and Endpoint Prediction for Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26103)**  `arXiv:2611.26103`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Zhengtu Liang, Qiwei Liang, Tao Lin, Shihang Weng, Boyang Cai, Xiangyu Chen, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Flow-matching robot policies usually use one prediction target throughout action generation, even though recovering actions from noise and polishing nearly clean actions bring different challenges. We systematically compare velocity prediction (V), endpoint prediction (X), and velocity-weighted endpoint prediction (X--V) across multiple robot policies, benchmarks, and inference budgets. On StarVLA and LiLa-WAM, X--V works best for few-step generation, while V gains more from iterative refinement. We further uncover their complementary strengths across noise levels: clean actions have concentrated covariance spectra, endpoint predictions stay more stable under high noise, and velocity predictions offer more accurate corrections near clean actions. Inspired by these findings, we introduce UniVEP, which learns both prediction targets together with consistency supervision and switches from endpoint recovery to velocity refinement as noise drops. Each step only needs one network run, and joint training also boosts single-step endpoint generation. Tests on simulated and real-world manipulation show better success rates and efficiency. On RoboTwin, UniVEP hits 61.36% success with one StarVLA run, beating the best single-target baseline at sixteen runs (59.96%). With LiLa-WAM, two runs outperform the best sixteen-step single-target baseline and cut inference latency by 77.1%.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies must react while visual inference is still running. Overlapping computation and execution reduces waiting, yet leaves perceptual memory stale and successive action chunks disconnected. - **[Mind2Act World: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Embodied Mind, Physical Action, and Mind-Action Composition in Robotic Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26444)**  `arXiv:2611.26444`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Yuzhi Huang, Jie Wu, Fanding Huang, Junchao Lin, Weichen Zhang, Ziyi Xiong, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Embodied intelligence aims to let robots perform complex physical tasks with human-like adaptability and reliability, which requires three complementary capabilities: history-based cognitive reasoning, precise and timely physical execution, and the composition of reasoning and execution through environmental feedback. Yet existing benchmarks either evaluate reasoning and execution within the same tasks or assess them separately without their composition, so they reveal little about whether a failure comes from reasoning, execution, or their composition. We introduce Mind2Act World, a diagnostic benchmark of 30 simulated manipulation tasks from five real-world application domains. Each task has three difficulty levels and two scene settings (Clean and Random), yielding 180 configurations. The tasks form three views: Mind tests decisions based on past observations, Act tests precise and timely execution, and Mind2Act combines both in closed-loop tasks where action outcomes guide later decisions. Even the strongest agent succeeds in 52.7% of Mind and 39.0% of Act episodes but in only 12.0% of Mind2Act episodes. The strongest agent retains task state but struggles with sustained contact, whereas end-to-end policies execute practiced motions but retain little task state. These findings point to composing reasoning and execution through environmental feedback as the main open problem.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Off-road vision-language-action (VLA) models generate scene-conditioned plans, but frequent trajectory updates can repeatedly invoke expensive multimodal inference. Reusing a prediction reduces this demand while leaving its commands exposed to changes in vehicle motion. FastRoverVLA addresses this mismatch by decoupling visual plan generation from motion-conditioned execution. Its training-free interface maintains decoded plans between visual predictions. A speed-curvature action buffer is aligned with measured motion at each update, integrated into a local trajectory, and advanced by consuming the current action.
