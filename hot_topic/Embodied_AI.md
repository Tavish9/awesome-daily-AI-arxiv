- **[What Can a VLA Learn from the Agent That Calls It? Task-Level Experience Distillation for Embodied Navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16246)**  `arXiv:2611.16246`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Jinze Tao, Yuxiao Han, Kangkang Dong, Yancong Wei, Xiaojun Zhu, Bokui Chen, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA) models drive the robot directly from camera views, yet a VLA alone shows three typical failures. It walks past the goal, stops before reaching it, and rarely recovers after going off track. Pairing a VLA with a large language model (LLM) planner fixes these failures, but queries the LLM throughout every episode, adding token costs and latency. We introduce Harness-to-Executor (H2E), a task-level distillation method that pairs an LLM planner and a frozen VLA as a heterogeneous training agent, keeps only sessions that reach the goal, and then trains a VLA alone on them so no LLM is used at test time. In these sessions, the planner gives sub-instructions and moves the robot with its own tools, detours are common, and the planner decides when to stop. H2E therefore labels every motion as a VLA action for the original task, whoever made it. It masks the loss on steps moving away from the goal and assigns end labels to states near it, so the VLA learns where the task ends. H2E sets a new state of the art on REVERIE-CE and NavRAG-CE. It raises an 8B VLA's success rate by 15.6% and 11.1% respectively, surpassing a method trained on 10M samples. The distilled VLA exceeds its agent on unseen episodes with zero LLM tokens and runs 3.7 times faster.

- **[Data Pyramid for Embodied Manipulation: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24744)**  `arXiv:2607.24744`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Yifan Ye, Yankai Fu, Yaoxu Lv, Bohan Hou, Jun Cen, Lingdong Kong, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Multimodal foundation models learned to see and to speak by consuming the whole internet. Embodied agents admit no such shortcut, since they require data that couple observations with physical states and actions. These signals can be provided, to varying degrees, by multiple data sources. In this work, we organize the embodied data ecosystem as a "pyramid'' spanning five complementary sources: real-robot data, UMI-style data, egocentric and exocentric data, simulation data, and general vision-language data. We organize the pyramid around the tension between scalability and robot alignment, and further characterize each source in terms of data quality, diversity, reusability, and physical fidelity. We then analyze recent embodied foundation models through the lens of data recipes, examining how different sources are selected, aligned, and mixed during pretraining. For embodied brain models, vision-language-action models, and world-action models alike, we relate data composition to capabilities in perception, reasoning, planning, action generation, and world prediction. To facilitate community use, we additionally consolidate over 10,000 hours of trajectories from widely used robot datasets into a unified LeRobot format, with transparent frame-level quality annotations for filtering. We close by discussing six open challenges: building large-scale tactile datasets, collecting failure and recovery data, developing scalable data-collection pipelines, aligning actions across embodiments, leveraging egocentric data for dexterous manipulation, and designing principled data recipes for robot learning. We hope this work lays the foundation for the design of next-generation embodied systems.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Existing agentic manipulation systems treat pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) policies as opaque, language-only primitives: when a policy call fails under unfamiliar instructions or scene perturbations, the agent cannot steer its behavior and is forced into futile retries or costly manual takeover. - **[VSpark: Speculative Decoding for Autoregressive Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22666)**  `arXiv:2611.22666`  `cs.CV` `cs.RO`  
  _Suho Yoo, Youngjoon Jang, Hyebin Cho, Taeyeop Lee, Jaewook Jeong, Joon Son Chung_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Our aim is to accelerate autoregressive vision-language-action models (VLAs), which generate robot actions one token at a time. Speculative decoding makes this possible: a lightweight draft model proposes several tokens at once, and the VLA verifies them in a single pass, accepting only those that match its own predictions, so decoding remains lossless. The speedup, however, is limited in robot control, where many different tokens encode nearly the same motion and a draft close to the correct action is still rejected. On four VLAs with different action tokenizers and four manipulation benchmarks, VSpark achieves 1.28x to 2.10x speedup while keeping the VLA's decoding lossless.

We evaluate the framework on simulated manipulation benchmarks and additionally study a component combination in a Real2Sim2Real task, examining task performance, optimization behavior, and flow consistency.
