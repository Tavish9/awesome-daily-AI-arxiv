- **[PDRVLA: A Vision-Language-Action Model with Physical Dynamics Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37381)**  `arXiv:2610.37381`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Hanqing Wang, Zhaotian Cai, Ruichuan An, Bohan Zeng, Tao Zhang, Yilin Chen, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision--Language--Action (VLA) models generate robot actions from visual observations and language instructions, but their understanding of physical dynamics remains limited. Video world models can capture scene dynamics, yet generating future videos introduces high latency into the control loop. We introduce PDRVLA, whose lightweight Physical Dynamics Module extracts physical tokens from vision--language features to condition action generation without online video generation. The module learns through direct prediction of future RGB, depth, and optical-flow features, with auxiliary supervision through a frozen pretrained video world model. Training relies entirely on publicly available data, expanded through human-to-robot synthesis, subtask annotation, and embodied chain-of-thought (ECoT) annotation. Across four simulation benchmarks, PDRVLA achieves success rates of 99.3% on LIBERO, 89.9% on LIBERO-Plus, 93.6% on RoboTwin~2.0, and 81.5% on SimplerEnv. Ablations show improved future prediction with world-model supervision and evaluate how subtask conditioning, future object-pose supervision, and ECoT training affect action success.
  </details>

- **[SimForcing: Distilling Simulation Motion Priors into Real-Domain Robot World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06598)**  `arXiv:2610.06598`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Xiaodong Wang, Tianle Li, Chuanxin Song, Junliang Xie, Zhanmi Zhong, Suiying Wu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Action-conditioned robot world models must respond precisely to robot trajectories while preserving realistic visual dynamics, yet learning both from heterogeneous robot videos remains challenging. Simulation offers structured motion supervision, but appearance differences hinder direct transfer, and inaccurate simulation predictions can misguide real-video generation. We present SimForcing, a simulation-guided framework that uses simulation both as a source of transferable motion knowledge and as a controllable reference for prediction. First, we transfer motion knowledge from a simulation teacher through latent-motion distillation, aligning temporal changes in latent space to internalize motion priors while mitigating the influence of appearance differences. Second, we introduce multi-block simulation conditioning with condition dropout to exploit predicted simulation trajectories without relying excessively on their accuracy. Our simulation-conditioning classifier-free guidance scheme unifies these two ideas by balancing predictions based on internalized motion knowledge with those additionally guided by simulation latents. The jointly trained student generates both simulation conditions and real-domain videos, requiring no additional world model at inference. On Bridge, SimForcing achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FVD among the compared methods without external embodied pretraining. Evaluation on InternData-A1 further supports its applicability across robot datasets. Moreover, using our trained world model to initialize a vision-language-action model improves LIBERO success, suggesting its utility for downstream policy learning.this https URL

- **[MESA: An Evaluation Framework for Compositional, Semantic, and Spatial Generalization in Robotics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05567)**  `arXiv:2611.05567`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Albert Wilcox, Frank Chang, Nhi Nguyen, Aishani Chakraborty, Jeremy A. Collins, Vaibhav Saxena, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Despite growing interest in foundation models for robotic manipulation, systematic evaluation of their capabilities remains elusive. Existing benchmarks are either narrow and saturated, offering little signal on SOTA methods, broadly scoped, providing diversity at the cost of controlled experimentation, or require training on large, messy open-source datasets. We introduce MESA, an evaluation framework designed for precise, reproducible measurement of language-conditioned policy generalization. MESA provides five evaluation suites that isolate in-distribution performance and four distinct axes of generalization: spatial configuration, object instance, object category, and subtask composition, enabling researchers to diagnose where and why policies fail, not just whether they fail. To populate these suites at scale, we develop MESA-Gen, a pipeline for task and demonstration generation built by improving MimicGen. We instantiate the framework on two platforms and release both: the single-arm MESA suite, and BiMESA, the first large-scale benchmark for bimanual generalization. We evaluate seven baseline methods spanning diffusion policies, VLM-based approaches, and pretrained VLAs, finding that the strongest model achieves only 52% on MESA and 49% on BiMESA, providing meaningful signal while leaving ample room for progress. MESA rankings are broadly consistent with RoboArena scores, suggesting that MESA captures method-level differences relevant beyond simulation. Project website:this https URL.

- **[Bridging the Morphology Gap: Adapting VLA Models to Dexterous Manipulation via Intent-Conditioned Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12109)**  `arXiv:2606.12109`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Chuanke Pang, Junyi Huang, Jianye Fang, Zhijun Zhao, Yaobing Wang, Kun Xu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) models offer semantic and spatial priors for robot manipulation, but their parallel-gripper interfaces leave multi-finger contact coordination implicit. Adapting these models requires both timely grasp transitions and embodiment-specific hand trajectories. We introduce InDex, an intent-conditioned framework that connects these two requirements. A normalized aperture signal, derived from retargeted demonstrations, supervises a scalar grasp intent while preserving full hand-action supervision. Stage 1 learns synchronized end-effector-intent chunks; Stage 2 conditions a diffusion hand decoder on these predictions, visual-language context, and proprioception. Sequential optimization establishes the upstream interface before learning dexterous control with low-rate co-adaptation. Across four simulated tasks, InDex with $\pi_{0.5}$ achieves 85.8% mean task success, improving the native policy by 35.5 percentage points, and reaches 69.0% on held-out objects with similar geometry without fine-tuning or recalibration. Improvements also hold across three VLA backbones and separately trained real-world policies, where mean success increases from 71.0% to 83.0%. Component ablations and timing interventions support the value of explicit grasp-intent conditioning and expressive hand generation. These results identify arm-hand coordination as an actionable bottleneck in adapting parallel-gripper priors to dexterous manipulation.

- **[Where Is VLA Inference Time Spent? Bottlenecks and Optimization Interactions Across GPUs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06710)**  `arXiv:2611.06710`  `cs.RO`  
  _Muhammad Danish Waseem, Ahmed Ali-Eldin Hassan_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA) models map camera observations and language instructions to robot actions. However, their large backbones and repeated action generation can make inference take longer than the robot's real-time requirements for its next action. To reduce the load of VLA computations, researchers have proposed multiple optimization approaches, including quantization and pruning. These optimizations can be combined with other general optimizations such as better kernel compilation or using CUDA graphs instead of the eager mode. However, most of these optimization results are usually reported on a single GPU type, typically for a few VLA models, with no analysis of what the real bottlenecks are for a model on a particular hardware or how these optimizations would generalize beyond the reported results. In this paper, we provide a comprehensive measurement study of seven families of inference optimization methods, from CUDA graphs and compilation to token pruning and quantization, on seven state-of-the-art VLAs evaluated on four GPU families ranging from a datacenter H100 to an embedded Jetson Thor. We analyse how CPU launch overhead, memory bandwidth, and kernel efficiency jointly shape optimization gains, and how applying one method changes the benefit of another. From this analysis, we derive VLA-Triage, guidelines for stacking optimizations under latency budgets. The guidelines identify which stages can provide the required savings and which combinations need testing in the deployed execution mode.
  </details>

- **[Robocasa-Mem: Benchmarking Environment and Process Memory in Long-Horizon Mobile Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06475)**  `arXiv:2611.06475`  `cs.RO`  
  _Sihang Chen, Chen Ye, Wei Chen, Xiaohan Lei, Zhipeng Tang, Sha Zhang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  In the real world, mobile manipulation robots must perform complex object interactions and make multi-step decisions, often relying on task-relevant information that is no longer observable. However, existing benchmarks provide limited evaluation of such memory-dependent decision-making in mobile manipulation. To address this gap, we introduce RoboCasa-Mem, a new benchmark comprising nine mobile manipulation tasks that require agents to use interaction history at key decision points. Specifically, the tasks are grouped into two categories of memory: One is environment memory, which captures spatial locations, object attributes, and object relationships. The other is process memory, which captures single-task progress and multi-task scheduling. Particularly, we introduce Behavior-Conditioned Evaluation. For tasks with multiple valid execution paths, instead of fixing a single target or trajectory, the evaluator derives later task constraints from the robot's earlier actions. This allows us to evaluate whether later behavior is consistent with the robot's own interaction history while reducing shortcuts based on fixed target assignments. We provide 1,800 successful demonstrations for policy training. We also develop a baseline on our benchmark, namely Hierarchical Memory-Augmented Policy(HMAP). Its high-level planner uses structured entity and process memories to generate subtasks. Its low-level VLA controller uses subgoal-conditioned queries to incorporate VGGT-Omega geometry into action generation. Together, RoboCasa-Mem and HMAP baseline support systematic evaluation of memory-dependent behavior and the development of memory-aware mobile manipulation policies.

- **[$A^3$-VLA: Automatic Perception-Guided Attention Alignment for Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06447)**  `arXiv:2611.06447`  `cs.RO`  
  _Shengzhe Zhang, Qi Zhang, Dazhong Shen, Chao Wang, Shaopeng Zhai, Hui Xiong_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) enable robots to perform manipulation tasks by leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to process visual observations and language instructions. However, the action prediction objective itself lacks fine-grained task-specific perceptual supervision, making it prone to relying on task-irrelevant visual shortcuts, leading attention away from target objects and key interaction areas, thereby limiting its operational performance and generalization ability. To address this, we propose Automatic Perception-Guided Attention Alignment for Robot Manipulation ($A^3$-VLA), a framework that derives task-specific visual guidance from the manipulation process itself. Specifically, $A^3$-VLA first generates heuristic candidate regions for target objects and the robot from interaction-induced visual changes, and refines them with a visual segmentation model offline. These regions serve as explicit perceptual guidance through two complementary objectives: an attention allocation loss that encourages sufficient focus on task-critical regions when language or robot state tokens query the visual input, and a contrastive learning loss that aligns the representations of candidate target regions with language instructions while separating them from task-irrelevant regions. Extensive experiments on simulated and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate that $A^3$-VLA consistently improves multiple VLA backbones, with an average success rate gain of 6.5% - 25.2% across benchmarks.
  </details>

- **[Can Astra Solve RoboMME without Breaking the Bank?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06184)**  `arXiv:2611.06184`  `cs.RO`  
  _Bingao Chen, Haoquan Fang, C. Karen Liu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robotic manipulation often requires remembering information that is no longer visible, such as an object's location before occlusion or the order of a demonstrated action sequence. A large vision-language planner can read this history, but calling it before every action chunk is slow and costly. We present a three-tier system for the RoboMME benchmark. GPT-6 Astra (System 2) selects a grounded subtask from stored visual history, a $\pi_{0.5}$ vision-language-action policy (System 1) executes it, and a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-4B monitor (System 1.5) checks after each action chunk whether the subtask is finished. The instruction is kept until the monitor reports completion or a task-specific event occurs, while the policy keeps acting on new observations. On RoboMME, the system succeeds in 79.13% of episodes with an average of 3.63 Astra calls and 80.84 seconds per episode. This is 34.62 points above the best published non-oracle baseline and 4.95 points below GroundSG+Oracle, which gives the low-level policy ground-truth subgoals.

- **[Future Representation Distillation for Imagination-free World Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04527)**  `arXiv:2611.04527`  `cs.RO`  
  _Jaehyeong Jo, Sangwon Jang, Sihyun Yu, Seonghyeon Ye, Yekyung Nah, Hojung Jung, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  World Action Models (WAMs) use the predictive knowledge of pretrained video diffusion models for robot control through future video prediction. However, iterative video denoising is costly, and inaccurate visual predictions can compromise action prediction. Our key idea is to make this predictive knowledge available through visual representations computed from the current observation, enabling action prediction without generating future videos. We introduce DreamFree, a WAM that repurposes a pretrained video diffusion model as a predictive visual encoder through representation distillation. The visual encoder learns to predict a frozen video diffusion model's representation using only the current observation. An action module is then trained to predict actions by extracting action-relevant information from the visual encoder. Across real-world and simulated tasks, DreamFree achieves higher success rates than state-of-the-art VLAs and WAMs, while performing real-time closed-loop control at 11Hz with a 14B video diffusion backbone. Experiments show that DreamFree can translate stronger video priors and additional video data into better robot control and enable learning new robot tasks from video demonstrations, including videos from a different embodiment, without action annotation for the new tasks.

- **[Test-Time Improvement of VLA Policies via Selected Diffusion Noise for Spurious-Robust Action Smoothing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14084)**  `arXiv:2606.14084`  `cs.RO`  
  _Duc Minh Nguyen, Bao-Ngoc Dao, Tung M. Luu, Binh Gia Nguyen, Vinh Tong, Anji Liu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies have demonstrated strong generalization in robot manipulation, but their deployment can remain unreliable due to spurious visual reliance and stochastic action generation, leading to grounding failures and kinematically unstable behaviors. We introduce Selected Diffusion Noise (SDN), a training-free method that treats diffusion noise as a controllable degree of freedom for improving test-time reliability. SDN samples multiple candidate action chunks and applies two-stage selection: it first filters candidates based on task-relevant visual grounding using a counterfactual negative set, then selects the kinematically most stable chunk based on JerkRMS. Experiments on three VLA policies across both simulation and real-world environments show improvements of 8-10% and 18.33%, respectively, demonstrating SDN's effectiveness for test-time improvement.

- **[Back to the Familiar Future: Failure Recovery for VLA Policies via Pre-Imagined Milestone Selection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09258)**  `arXiv:2606.09258`  `cs.RO`  
  _Suyeon Shin, Juwon Kim, Hyeonbin Park, Hyunseo Kim, Hyundo Lee, Hyung-Sin Kim, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA) policies can deviate from nominal trajectories during manipulation, even when tasks remain physically feasible. Recovering from these deviations is challenging, as they push the policy into unfamiliar state spaces where direct re-planning frequently destabilizes action sequences. We propose Back to the Familiar Future (B2FF), a recovery framework for foresight-driven VLAs that leverages future visual conditioning as a recovery interface. Before execution, the VLA generates a milestone bank of familiar future states conditioned on the clean initial observation. At recovery time, a recoverability-aware selector selects a recovery milestone from this bank and enforces it as a fixed visual goal. This enables the VLA to robustly map off-trajectory observations back to a familiar future. On failure-injected LIBERO, under controlled recovery timing aligned with the injected failure, B2FF increases the average success rate of a baseline VLA from 56.3% to 74.0%, demonstrating that pre-imagined milestones can guide recovery without fine-tuning the low-level action generator.

- **[From Pixels to Actions - Towards Native Vision-Language-Action Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06918)**  `arXiv:2611.06918`  `cs.CV`  
  _Weichen Fan, Haiwen Diao, Penghao Wu, Siyuan Hu, Ziqi Huang, Tianqi Liu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action models are typically built on a simple premise: perception is first compressed by visual encoders, and action reasoning begins only afterwards. Yet manipulation often hinges on precisely the visual signals most vulnerable to this abstraction, e.g., local geometry, boundaries, depth, and subtle state changes. This raises a fundamental question: should action policies inherit a fixed visual endpoint, or directly access evolving visual computation? We introduce NEO-VLA, a structurally encoder-free vision-language-action framework. Images are tokenized by lightweight patch convolutional layers and evolve jointly with language throughout the backbone, allowing a flow-matching action expert to draw from instruction-conditioned visual representations at different depths. This simple change reveals a consistent pattern. Native architectures outperform encoder-based counterparts under controlled comparisons; full fine-tuning reduces but does not erase the gap, and additional scale alone cannot recover it. More strikingly, layer-wise probing shows that the action-critical signals often peak well before the final visual representation. Across diverse manipulation settings and distribution shifts, NEO-VLA consequently achieves stronger and more robust control. Together, these results suggest that embodied control benefits from access to visual representations across the computational hierarchy, rather than a fixed encoder output.
  </details>

- **[MotoVerse: A Benchmark for Predictive Perception of Motorized Two-Wheelers in Dense Unstructured Traffic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06007)**  `arXiv:2611.06007`  `cs.CV`  
  _Varun A. Paturkar, Shankar Gangisetty, C.V. Jawahar_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Motorized Two-Wheelers (MTWs) dominate personal transport across much of the Global South, yet remain largely overlooked in predictive perception research for autonomous driving and ADAS. We introduce MotoVerse, a benchmark for predictive perception from an MTW's ego-centric perspective. MotoVerse comprises 949 maneuver clips collected in dense, unstructured traffic and trajectory annotations for over 31,000 surrounding road agents, including over 16,000 MTWs, enabling two complementary tasks: Ego-Rider Maneuver Anticipation (ERMA) and Traffic Agent Trajectory Prediction (TATP). Each clip is further annotated with an MTW-specific challenge taxonomy comprising dynamic ego-motion, weak traffic structure and contextual occlusion, enabling challenge-aware evaluation. We benchmark established conventional prediction models, general-purpose Vision-Language Models (VLMs), driving-specific VLMs and vision-language-action models. Our experiments reveal three key insights: (i) video backbones reach most of their full-observation accuracy from the first fifth of a maneuver, whereas zero-shot VLMs need most of the maneuver to be observed; (ii) zero-shot VLMs, whether general-purpose or driving-specific, largely remain close to simple baselines on both tasks; a preliminary LoRA study shows that adaptation lifts anticipation sharply but leaves trajectory prediction behind conventional models; and (iii) the rider's own motion drives forecasting difficulty: for every model, including a static baseline, errors are highest during left turns and rise under dynamic ego-motion. We believe MotoVerse will serve as a challenging benchmark for safer and more robust predictive AI for intelligent two-wheelers. - **[Gnomon4D: Learning Where and When to Focus for Geometric Motion-Grounded World-Action Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02474)**  `arXiv:2611.02474`  `cs.CV`  
  _Lingwei Dang, Yan Zhou, Juntong Li, Zhenhao Zhang, Yiteng Chen, Xiaoqiang Liu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  World-action models (WAMs) predict scene changes to guide robot actions, yet their RGB futures entangle state changes with appearance and lack 3D geometry. Recent WAMs add geometry as a training signal or a control interface, or build native 3D models, but decide what geometry to model rather than how the action should read it. Intuitively, control-relevant cues lie at embodiment--object interactions and their transitions, yet probing existing WAMs reveals that their action attention underweights these interactions in favor of the rest of the embodiment. To focus the action on these interactions, we present Gnomon4D, a WAM that jointly generates future video, robot actions, and GeoDelta, a video-aligned 4D geometric motion field recording the image-plane displacement and depth change of embodiment and object points. With the embodiment and objects in one field, their relative motion, which marks an interaction, can be read off. Guided by this motion and the task, Interaction-Salient Attention (InSAttn) selects where the action reads, and Transition-Anchored Aggregation (TransAgg) selects when, around grasp and release. As GeoDelta encodes surface motion rather than embodiment-specific controls, it serves as a human--robot quasi-action that links action-free pretraining on EgoManip-5000H, which we curate, to robot mid-training on Robot-30H. Gnomon4D leads on RoboTwin~2.0, VLABench, and RoboDojo, and outperforms real-robot baselines, especially under visual shifts. Project page:this https URL.
