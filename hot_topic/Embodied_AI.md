- **[What Does It Take For A Generalist Robot Policy To Adapt Using Minimal Data?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11363)**  `arXiv:2608.11363`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Shreyas Kowshik, Sreyas Venkataraman, Leo Wang, Niharika Pant, Max Simchowitz, Aviral Kumar_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  A central goal in robot learning is to move beyond task-specific human data collection toward robots that improve through autonomous interaction. Yet fully autonomous learning remains difficult with current policies: sparse rewards and weak zero-shot exploration often prevent online learning from discovering successful behavior. As a first step towards fully autonomous learning, we propose the minimal-data adaptation regime, in which a pretrained robot policy must learn a new task from as little as one demonstration followed by autonomous online interaction. We establish that, although pretrained policies fail zero-shot on many new tasks, a single demonstration suffices to direct their behavioral prior to coarse task-following behavior, albeit with unreliable performance. From this insight, we isolate the key design principles required to convert coarse task-following into reliable task success. These include (1) the surprising efficacy of pretrained VLA representations to support sample-efficient value learning from online interaction (2) the sufficiency of residual policy learning to learn corrective behavior and (3) the need for careful data-balancing to prevent exploration collapse. As a representative of these principles, we propose MiDAS, a simple offline-to-online RL recipe that combines few-demonstration behavior cloning with value-based residual learning over frozen VLA representations. Across LIBERO and RoboCasa tasks, MiDAS substantially improves performance from single-demonstration initializations, including policies that succeed only rarely, validating the principles highlighted above. On a bimanual YAM platform, starting from a brittle initial policy, MiDAS improves task success after approximately six hours of online interaction.

- **[Memory before Goal: Role of Pre-History Memory for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31166)**  `arXiv:2610.31166`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Chenyuan Chen, Ran Ding, Lechen Zhang, Zewen He, Yoshihiko Nakamura_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Humanoid loco-manipulation in dynamic scenes is non-Markovian: cues vanish as objects change or are hidden, or leave the view as the robot turns and walks, yet whole-body vision-language-action (VLA) policies see only the present. We present WholeBodyMem, an object-guided perceptual memory that keeps the pre-history of object events seen before the robot acts on its goal. SAM3 tracks the task objects, and their events causally select key frames within an adaptive budget, which the action expert reads by cross-attention together with object tokens encoding appearance, geometry and time. Keeping the right frames rather than more frames, WholeBodyMem surpasses memoryless and uniform-memory baselines, raising its base policy's success from 29.1% to 72.6% in simulation and from 36.7% to 88.3% on the real robot, while also improving the standard SIMPLE tasks (70.2% to 86.9%).

- **[Progress Probes $P^2$: Lightweight Runtime Monitoring for Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31613)**  `arXiv:2610.31613`  `cs.RO`

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can perform diverse manipulation tasks directly from visual observations and natural language instructions, but they remain prone to mistakes and unpredictable failures. Deploying these policies without continuous human supervision will require robust runtime monitors that detect when the policy has gone off-course. Existing monitors use external foundation models, such as vision-language models (VLMs) to supervise the policy, introducing significant complexity and computational cost for monitoring at runtime. In this work, we investigate an alternate approach: \textit{can a VLA's own internal activations provide the information needed to monitor its execution?} Our method trains linear probes on policy activations from demonstrations to estimate progress, then flags executions that deviate from expected progress. - **[Echo: Coupled Action and Task Status Signal Denoising for Vision Language Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31569)**  `arXiv:2610.31569`  `cs.RO`  
  _Yufeng Liu, Bohao Fan, Juntu Zhao, Xinyi Wang, Hang Yu, Wenzhuan Luo, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recognizing task completion and failure enables robots to stop upon success and initiate recovery or replanning after failure. Existing methods augment Vision Language Action (VLA) policies with task status signal prediction to support these execution decisions. However, learning and integrating these signals can require fine-grained supervision, external monitoring models, or complex architectural designs. Moreover, the potential of task status signals to directly guide action generation remains underexplored. We present Echo, a framework that combines task status signal learning without frame-level progress annotations with a compact design for status prediction and action generation within a single VLA. Specifically, Echo represents task status with stop and failure signals, added as two dimensions of each action chunk and denoised together with robot actions. Their combination distinguishes ongoing execution, successful completion, and failure. To enable status signal-guided action generation, Echo controls interactions between the signals and actions and denoises the signals faster than the actions. These allow later action denoising steps to condition on cleaner status estimates while preserving accurate signal prediction. Extensive real-world manipulation experiments show that Echo accurately predicts task status and consistently improves policy performance.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA) models adapted through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) inherit a structural asymmetry: expert demonstrations teach the policy where success behavior lies, but provide no signal about where it ceases to be reliable. - **[Measuring the Reasoning-Action Coupling in Driving Vision-Language-Action Models through Counterfactuals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30953)**  `arXiv:2610.30953`  `cs.AI`

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Driving vision-language-action (VLA) models bring an appealing form of explainability to a safety-critical domain, since a system that can state the reasons for its maneuvers offers the transparency that deployment and public trust in autonomous driving demand. These models read the world into language before they act on it, describing the scene and reasoning over it in text that ranges from chain-of-thought to multi-stage question answering on the way to a trajectory. That framing quietly assumes two separable properties, namely that the trajectory follows the generated reasoning and that the generated reasoning tracks the causes that actually produce the trajectory. Overall, our findings indicate that the reasoning these models present to account for their resulting driving behavior is weakly coupled to that behavior itself, pointing to limits on the explainability these systems are intended to provide.
