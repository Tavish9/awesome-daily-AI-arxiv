- **[PDRVLA: A Vision-Language-Action Model with Physical Dynamics Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37381)**  `arXiv:2610.37381`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Hanqing Wang, Zhaotian Cai, Ruichuan An, Bohan Zeng, Tao Zhang, Yilin Chen, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision--Language--Action (VLA) models generate robot actions from visual observations and language instructions, but their understanding of physical dynamics remains limited. Video world models can capture scene dynamics, yet generating future videos introduces high latency into the control loop. We introduce PDRVLA, whose lightweight Physical Dynamics Module extracts physical tokens from vision--language features to condition action generation without online video generation. The module learns through direct prediction of future RGB, depth, and optical-flow features, with auxiliary supervision through a frozen pretrained video world model. Training relies entirely on publicly available data, expanded through human-to-robot synthesis, subtask annotation, and embodied chain-of-thought (ECoT) annotation. Across four simulation benchmarks, PDRVLA achieves success rates of 99.3% on LIBERO, 89.9% on LIBERO-Plus, 93.6% on RoboTwin~2.0, and 81.5% on SimplerEnv. Ablations show improved future prediction with world-model supervision and evaluate how subtask conditioning, future object-pose supervision, and ECoT training affect action success.
  </details>

- **[REA: A Routed Embodied Agent Runtime for Asynchronous Brain-Cerebellum Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42053)**  `arXiv:2610.42053`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Pengfei Zhou, Yixing Ma, Zhengfeiyang Zhang, Haoyuan Chen, Zhenglin Wan, Hexin Wang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Foundation models are becoming remarkably reliable at high-level reasoning, yet they remain too slow and costly to serve as fine-grained controllers. Conversely, fast policies like VLAs and whole-body controllers offer more efficient reflexes, but struggle to generalize. While pairing a deliberative brain (foundation models) with a reactive cerebellum (fast policies) seems natural for embodied systems, two obstacles remain: methods are built and scored on incompatible stacks, and their asynchronous coordination is difficult. For example, an agent that calls the VLA in the agentic loop can stall the fast execution, while a VLA that calls the agent on errors tends to re-trigger fallbacks repeatedly or completely miss silent failures. To address this, we propose REA (Routed Embodied Agent), a unified runtime where a deliberative System 2, an embodied router System 1 and reactive System 0 policies cooperate under a single golden rule - no running policy waits on the brain. Instead of querying the brain at each failure, REA employs a route memory that stores each policy's measured success ahead of time, a boundary-triggered judge gate that consults the brain on policy execution, and a shared-budget escalation ladder for bounded recovery. Evaluated on 3,000 test episodes across 60 LIBERO-Pro tasks, REA reaches 95.9% success and beats the best single-agent baseline in its own policy pool (94.7%) while reducing control steps by 25% and control time by 17%. In a separate 100-episode RoboTwin 2.0 study, REA achieves 84% success with VLA policies and 86% with VLA+code, compared with 80% for the strongest single VLA baseline MEDex. These results demonstrate learned and programmatic policies operating within the same runtime. REA also transfers to BEHAVIOR-1K and a real Unitree G1 humanoid, achieving the highest observed success among the compared methods.
  </details>

- **[$π$-StepNFT: Wider Space Needs Finer Steps in Online RL for Flow-based VLAs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02083)**  `arXiv:2603.02083`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Siting Wang, Xiaofeng Wang, Zheng Zhu, Minnan Pei, Xinyu Cui, Cheng Deng, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Flow-based vision-language-action (VLA) models have emerged as a powerful paradigm for embodied control, but multi-step denoising makes policy-gradient online reinforcement learning (RL) difficult because the final-action likelihood induced by the sampler is not directly tractable. We identify an exploration--supervision mismatch within the denoising trajectory, where stochastic differential equation (SDE) sampling explores a wider local stochastic transition space while supervision derived from the final denoised output does not directly use the adjacent transitions observed during sampling. We propose $\pi$-StepNFT (Step-wise Negative-aware Fine-Tuning), a flow-native online RL framework that couples wider stochastic exploration with finer step-wise supervision without computing the final-action likelihood. $\pi$-StepNFT treats observed SDE transitions as supervision units, using variance-normalized transition errors to rank mirrored positive and negative velocity branches and map rollout feedback to local velocity updates. On LIBERO, starting from shared few-shot SFT initializations, $\pi$-StepNFT raises average success from 57.6\% to 92.0\% for $\pi_0$ and from 77.1\% to 94.0\% for $\pi_{0.5}$. On ManiSkill, it achieves the highest reported mean success among the evaluated RL baselines on five test-only visual perturbations. Our project homepage is available atthis https URL.
  </details>

- **[VLA Policies Do Not Know When Not to Act: Unintended Task Execution and Start Suppression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41609)**  `arXiv:2610.41609`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Minsu Jang, Jae-Woo Choi, Youngwoo Yoon, Dohyung Kim_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  A robot should act only on an instruction from a person or on an intent that the robot explicitly represents and generates. Unintended actions taken without such an instruction can cause risk or inconvenience to people and the environment. We examine how recent vision-language-action (VLA) policies respond when the instruction is missing or meaningless. We apply a paired protocol with empty, neutral, nonsense, infeasible, and wrong-task instructions to six model checkpoints in LIBERO-Goal simulation. Holding the initial state fixed and varying only the instruction, we ask three questions: whether the robot starts to move, which task it completes, and what the instruction determines. Even when the instruction was empty, the robot started moving in every episode with all six checkpoints, and with five of them it often completed a task that the instruction had not specified. On two GR00T checkpoints, the instruction determined which task was executed but not whether the robot started moving; the robot moved even on infeasible instructions. We then tested three methods for suppressing the start: prepending "if no task is given do not move" to the instruction; a gate that holds the robot when the first action chunk for the given instruction nearly equals that for an empty instruction; and fine-tuning the policy to hold still on non-task inputs. The prefix stopped the robot in none of the six checkpoints, and the gate held it on non-task inputs in five of the six checkpoints. The fine-tuned policy never completed a task on non-task inputs, but it did not reliably keep the robot still, and it failed to suppress the start on infeasible instructions.

- **[STAR: Steering Action Representations during Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41009)**  `arXiv:2610.41009`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Guangyu Zhao, Yongchang Zhang, Haoxuan Ru, Kaichen He, Haoran Wen, Kun Zhan, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action models (VLAs) map language instructions and visual observations to robot actions, showing strong capabilities across robotic manipulation tasks. However, in our probing experiment, we find that action representations near the action output do not fully preserve task-relevant visual grounding. Motivated by this late-layer grounding gap, we introduce STAR, for STeering Action Representations, a training-free inference method for pre-trained VLAs. STAR applies an exponential moving average over late-layer residual updates, allowing later action representations to inherit object-aware update directions from earlier layers without model updates or architectural changes. It further uses recent proprioceptive states to adapt the mixing strength, using conservative mixing when execution dynamics change rapidly. Experiments on robot manipulation benchmarks show that STAR improves late-layer object awareness and increases average task success rates by 4.0 percentage points. Our code is available atthis https URL.
  </details>

- **[Detect and Suppress: A Mechanistic Defense against Adversarial Patches in VLA Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03498)**  `arXiv:2610.03498`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Yukiya Horiba, Koshiro Aoki, Shunsuke Yasuki, Bum Jun Kim, Taiki Miyanishi_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Adversarial patches can disrupt Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by manipulating visual observations, leading to failures in robot control. However, it remains poorly understood which internal mechanisms underlie these failures and how targeted interventions can mitigate them. In this work, we mechanistically analyze VLA representations using a sparse autoencoder (SAE) and identify a feature whose activation strongly correlates with the presence of an adversarial patch. Based on this analysis, we suppress the identified feature at inference time only when a linear probe detects an attack. This intervention improves robustness without the cost of fine-tuning the VLA. We evaluate our method against VLA adversarial patch attacks on LIBERO-10. Conditional intervention improves success rate under intermittent attacks, whereas continuously applying the same intervention substantially degrades policy performance. These results show that attack-related internal representations can provide useful targets for VLA adversarial defense and that controlling when to intervene is important for limiting disruption to nominal policy behavior.
  </details>

- **[Nexus-VLA: Bridging Human and Robot Experiences via a Shared Hierarchical Semantic Latent Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42021)**  `arXiv:2610.42021`  `cs.RO`  
  _Biaoliang Guan, Tingyu Yuan, Wen Ye, Ziming Ji, Xu Yang, Yan Huang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Large-scale egocentric human videos provide rich and scalable manipulation experience for robot policy learning, yet substantial embodiment gaps in visual appearance, kinematic structure, and action spaces hinder effective knowledge transfer. Existing methods often decouple cross-embodiment knowledge alignment from robot policy learning, making it difficult for human manipulation knowledge to directly and fully contribute to robot action learning. To address this issue, we propose Nexus-VLA, a hybrid policy learning framework that enables cross-embodiment knowledge transfer through end-to-end human-robot joint training, allowing human manipulation experience to directly contribute to robot policy learning. We first introduce a hierarchical cross-embodiment semantic alignment paradigm that progressively establishes shared manipulation semantics from task intent to fine-grained interactions, enabling more complete use of the manipulation knowledge contained in human videos. Building on this hierarchy, we design a shared manipulation latent representation that jointly encodes multi-level shared knowledge into an internal representation directly used for continuous action generation, improving policy learning efficiency while effectively leveraging human knowledge. Across RoboCasa-GR1, OOD settings, and real-world robot experiments, Nexus-VLA achieves an 80.5% task success rate on RoboCasa-GR1 and demonstrates strong advantages in human-data utilization, learning efficiency, and cross-scenario generalization.

- **[TRACE: Target-State Risk-Aligned Conditional Execution for Embodied Tracking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41275)**  `arXiv:2610.41275`  `cs.RO`  
  _Mingyang Lyu, Gongce Cao, Yiyang Jia, Lu Jia, Erliang Lin, Yuhao Zhang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Embodied visual tracking requires a robot to follow a language-specified person under occlusion, look-alike distractors, and ambiguous instructions. We show that improving tracking persistence can simultaneously increase safety violations. Using a compact 0.6B vision-language-action (VLA) tracker with a frozen backbone, we update only the planner head with proximal policy optimization (PPO). PPO improves success and following rates across all three EVT-Bench settings, but increases the collision rate on distracted tracking because episodes that previously ended in target loss can instead end in contact. We introduce TRACE, an execution-time target-state risk gate that preserves the learned policy and intervenes selectively. A lightweight risk head processes 84 cues, including candidate boxes, identity margins, a learned range-and-bearing probe, and the policy's commands. When the calibrated risk exceeds a threshold, TRACE rescales the translational component of the next waypoint while leaving yaw unchanged. The executed action is identical to PPO's on more than 98% of steps. In paired evaluations on identical episodes, TRACE reduces the distracted-tracking collision rate by 3.96 percentage points, while further improving success and following rates. Matched slowdown controls without target-state timing do not reproduce this improvement. Concurrent evaluation reproduces a collision reduction of 3.77 points, compared with 0.29 points for identical-arm null pairs, indicating that the effect exceeds evaluation variability. These results show that target-state-aware execution can recover the safety cost of policy post-training with minimal intervention.
  </details>

- **[CaPtain: A Pipeline for Post-Training Code-as-Policy Robot Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40799)**  `arXiv:2610.40799`  `cs.RO`  
  _Yijia Fan, Xilin He, Yilei Jiang, Dongming Wu, Jiaxu Wang, Xiangyu Yue_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Across coding, web, and tool-use tasks, language-model agents are now routinely trained with the same recipe: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to learn the interface, then multi-turn reinforcement learning (RL) to master it. Code-as-policy (CaP) casts robot manipulation in the same form, as the policy writes code against robot APIs and reads back the results, so the same recipe should apply. Yet existing CaP agents sidestep training: they either prompt closed frontier models or delegate manipulation to pre-trained vision-language-action models (VLAs) and high-level skills, while small open vision-language models (VLMs) fail outright. We present CaPtain, an open post-training pipeline that turns a small open VLM into a capable CaP agent, with no VLA, frontier model, or privileged state at test time. CaPtain trains and deploys the policy inside CaPtain-Harness, which exposes low-level perception, geometry, and guarded-motion primitives across simulation and real hardware. We first align the base model via SFT on synthetic trajectories generated with GPT-6 Astra, then apply multi-turn RL. Multi-turn RL must credit tens of agent requests, whose physical effects often surface turns later, from a single end-of-episode outcome. We therefore introduce a physical critic that reads privileged simulator state, never seen by the policy, and credits each request by how much it changes the predicted chance of success. Starting from Qwen3.5-4B (0% zero-shot), CaPtain-4B reaches 55.4% held-out LIBERO success after SFT and 73.8% after RL. On LIBERO-PRO, it reaches 78.5%, surpassing several frontier-model agents, and its RL gains persist across three perturbation suites (+17.6 to +21.2 points). Without target-domain training, it attains 83.3% on SimplerEnv WidowX, exceeds frontier models on CaP-Bench, and runs on a real robot after simulation-only adaptation. We release all code, data, and model weights.
  </details>

- **[LOGIV: Graph-Verified Execution with Causal Local Repair for Long-Horizon Robot Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40016)**  `arXiv:2610.40016`  `cs.RO`  
  _Enqiao Lu, Yuxiang Xie, Xingrui Yu, Yuyang Wang, Yipeng Gu, Zhirui Fang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Agentic robots must turn a high-level goal into actions while keeping the physical conditions needed for future steps valid. A completed action does not ensure that its effects will persist: a later disturbance can invalidate support that the remaining plan still assumes. We present LOGIV, a graph engineering framework for agentic robot execution that uses an explicit task graph to connect symbolic planning, observation-based verification, and embodied action. LOGIV compiles a validated plan into condition-support and resource-ordering edges, checks their physical validity during execution, and uses dependency tracing to determine what must be restored after a failure. Repair is constrained by retained unfinished actions, and the combined continuation is symbolically validated before execution resumes. The framework operates above existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and World Action Model (WAM) policies without adding trainable components. Across 20 dual-arm tasks from RoboTwin V2 and BiCoord with three execution backbones, LOGIV improves mean task success without injected disturbances by 10.67 and 15.33 percentage points, respectively. Under predefined perturbations, it achieves higher task completion and condition restoration than three adapted recovery baselines for every backbone on both benchmarks. On three real-robot tasks, successful trials increase from 12/30 to 17/30. These results support explicit, continuously checked task dependencies as a practical interface for reliable agentic robot execution.
  </details>

- **[OpenFlyV2: An onboard vision-language-action model for aerial perception and navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39287)**  `arXiv:2610.39287`  `cs.RO`  
  _Junli Liu, Zhongrui You, Chenhui Li, Zhen Li, Yuyang Tian, Qizhen Chen, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Human cognition flexibly draws on a shared perceptual foundation to understand environments, attend to referenced entities, reason about spatial relationships, and act. Endowing aerial robots with an analogous functional organization remains challenging, as existing systems are often specialized for individual capabilities, dependent on offboard computation, or unable to sustain closed-loop operation on resource-constrained platforms. We present OpenFlyV2, a unified onboard vision-language-action architecture that uses a shared vision-language representation to support semantic understanding, language-grounded perception, spatial reasoning, and continuous aerial control within a single model. A compact action expert converts the shared multimodal context into short-horizon flight waypoints, while motion-aware temporal memory and action priors reduce the computational cost of closed-loop execution. We construct \dataset{}, a simulation-based dataset comprising approximately 70,000 navigation trajectories and 60,000 vision-language instruction--response pairs. OpenFlyV2 generates action chunks, each comprising 12 waypoints, entirely onboard at 10 Hz using an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX. It transfers zero-shot from simulation to physical quadrotor flight, achieving a 48\% navigation success rate across four real-world environments without real-world aerial training data. Extensive results demonstrate that broad vision-language competence and continuous closed-loop flight can coexist within a single model on a resource-constrained aerial platform. - **[VEGA: Learning Navigation VLAs from In-the-Wild Egocentric Video with Geometric Trajectory Supervision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18426)**  `arXiv:2606.18426`  `cs.RO`  
  _Gershom Seneviratne, Yohan Abeysinghe, Jianyu An, Vaibhav Shende, Rahul Kumar, Dinesh Manocha_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We introduce VEGA, an approach for training navigation VisionLanguage-Action (VLA) models from unlabeled egocentric navigation videos. Internet-scale egocentric videos provide a scalable source of navigation-relevant visual observations, capturing cluttered scenes, close-range obstacles, and natural human motion through real-world spaces. However, these videos are not directly usable for policy learning because they do not provide obstacle-aware trajectories conditioned on explicit navigation goals in the robot's coordinate frame. VEGA addresses this gap by reconstructing local scene geometry from monocular video, sampling navigation goals (represented as text, image, or spatial waypoints) and generating obstacle-aware trajectories using the constructed geometry. The resulting trajectory distribution is then used to train a flow-matching VLA navigation policy. By using geometry exclusively during training, VEGA distills obstacle-aware planning directly into a vision-based policy. Furthermore, we introduce VEGA-Bench, a benchmark containing 250k scenes and approximately 5 million navigation goals paired with scene geometry, designed to evaluate goal progress, collision avoidance, and obstacle clearance of VLAs. Our evaluation shows that VEGA achieves competitive goal progress while reducing collisions by 33.0% and improving obstacle clearance by 17.9% over the strongest baseline on VEGABench, while improving success by at least 150.0%, reducing collisions by at least 66.7%, and improving obstacle clearance by at least 60.0% in real-world trials. Ultimately, we demonstrate that video-derived geometric supervision provides a scalable and effective signal for training obstacle-aware navigation VLAs. The code and benchmark will be released at the time of publication.

- **[SACQ: Structure-Aware Codebook Quantization for Ultra-Low-Bit Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41408)**  `arXiv:2610.41408`  `cs.CV` `cs.LG` `cs.RO`  
  _Haesung Lee, Insu Han_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models enable capable robot behavior, but their large memory footprint limits deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms. Weight quantization is a natural remedy, yet post-training quantization (PTQ) recipes inherited from large language models degrade when applied to VLAs at ultra-low precision. Existing low-bit PTQ methods often fail to explicitly account for the structure of downstream operations and allocate precision without considering its effect on the policy actions. We introduce SACQ, an ultra- low-bit weight-only vector-codebook quantization framework for VLAs. SACQ combines (1) structure-aware codebook fitting with role-specific quadratic metrics that reflect input statistics and downstream sensitivity; (2) action-aware mixed-bit allocation based on the increase in final-action error as individual layers are compressed; and (3) two-stage post-quantization refinement that reassigns codeword indices with second-order error compensation and calibrates codebooks and row-group scales against the decoded action. Using GR00T N1.7, SACQ achieves 93.85% average success across four LIBERO suites at 1.5 bits per language/action weight while reducing model memory by 4.83x. With INT8 activation quantization and low-precision attention, it retains 93.55% success while running 1.51x faster than BF16. On SimplER, SACQ further achieves 63.76% and 52.27% success under Visual Matching and Variant Aggregation at 2 bits per language/action weight, compared to 58.90% and 53.45% for BF16, respectively.
  </details>
