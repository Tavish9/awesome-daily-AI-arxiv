- **[DUET: Decoupled Upper- and Lower-Body Execution for Task-Agnostic Humanoid Loco-Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23872)**  `arXiv:2610.23872`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Kapalik Khanal, Biswash Khatiwada, Stephen Afrifa, Fan Wu, Ramesh Bahadur Bist_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Changing a humanoid robot's manipulation policy should not require retraining the controller that maintains the robot's balance. This study introduces DUET, a decoupled execution framework that enables locomotion and manipulation controllers to operate simultaneously by combining their commands only at the robot interface. A reinforcement-learning locomotion policy controls the 12 leg joints and a waist yaw joint, tracking planar velocity, turning rate, and pelvis height from deep crouching to upright standing on a 23-degree-of-freedom Unitree G1. The 10 arm joints are excluded from the action space, while their positions and velocities are included only as observations, so that any compatible manipulation policy that generates arm-joint targets can control the upper body, and the locomotion policy keeps the lower body stable without requiring shared training or direct coordination. This study demonstrates the modular approach on hardware by pairing the same locomotion checkpoint with two different vision-language-action models, GR00T N1.7 and pi0.5. In simulation evaluation, the locomotion controller kept the robot's lower body stable with no falls while tracking the commanded planar velocity, yaw rate, and pelvis height with errors of 0.186 m/s, 0.142 rad/s, and 0.016 m, respectively. Ablation studies identify the upper-body pose curriculum as the dominant component, and removing this training step reduces episode survival from 20.0 to 7.4 seconds and increases the fall rate to 6.06 per environment minute. Robustness tests show no falls under pushes of up to 1.0 m/s within the trained payload range, and no falls at twice the payload limit for pushes below 0.7 m/s. These results show that a manipulation policy can be exchanged without retraining, restarting, or otherwise touching the layer that keeps the robot upright.

- **[A Phenomenology of Neural Geometry in Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20840)**  `arXiv:2610.20840`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Mozes Jacobs, Aiden Swann, Mathilde Papillon, Siddharth Boppana, Fenil R. Doshi, Usha Bhalla, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robotic manipulation is inherently geometric, but what shape does the world take inside the policy that performs it? Prior work on vision-language-action (VLA) policies establishes that task-relevant variables are linearly readable from activations, yet readability certifies only that a variable is present and says nothing about the shape its values trace, which may be curved and multi-dimensional. Using controlled sweeps, natural rollouts, linear probes, and activation interventions, we build a taxonomy of seven task-relevant concepts in VLA models. We find that realized geometry is predominantly multi-dimensional and is shaped by the constraints under which the policy acts. Physical joint limits, for example, leave wrist orientation an open arc rather than a circle, and distance to the target, a single scalar, is realized in two orthogonal subspaces, a coarse ruler for the approach and a fine ruler for the grasp. The same geometries reappear in two further VLA models on real-world robot data, indicating that they are neither model-specific nor artifacts of simulation. We demonstrate how the findings are actionable by steering along the geometry a concept realizes. Overall, this work lays out an initial phenomenology of realized geometry in VLA policies and demonstrates that a concept's shape carries as much importance as its readability.

Yet fully autonomous learning remains difficult with current policies: sparse rewards and weak zero-shot exploration often prevent online learning from discovering successful behavior. As a first step towards fully autonomous learning, we propose the minimal-data adaptation regime, in which a pretrained robot policy must learn a new task from as little as one demonstration followed by autonomous online interaction.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robotic manipulation is not merely observation-to-action generation, but the realization of task-specified state changes in the physical world. Existing vision-language-action (VLA) methods directly learn observation-to-action mappings, imposing a substantial learning burden due to the gap between semantic understanding and action execution. Although world-action models (WAMs) improve control through future prediction, modeling complete future states wastes capacity on static appearance and action-irrelevant changes. We propose Vision-Language-Delta-Action (VLDA), a paradigm that reformulates full future prediction as compact actionable transition modeling. LT-Filter captures task-relevant manipulation-centric changes while suppressing static reconstruction biases. We implement VLDA within LoongForge, an open-source training framework for large-scale foundation and embodied models, using its modular and distributed infrastructure for unified two-stage optimization. Experiments show that VLDA improves long-horizon consistency, robustness, and generalization, validating actionable Delta as an alternative to direct action prediction and full future-state modeling.

  _Yupei Lin, Zhongzhan Huang, Junxin Li, Guoming Ling, Shanshan Zhong, Hefeng Wu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Benchmark compression reduces evaluation cost by selecting a small set of samples. Existing methods typically guide selection using a single metric, although benchmarks often assess models along multiple dimensions. We find that model rankings are often weakly correlated across metrics, indicating that different metrics capture distinct aspects of model performance. Samples selected for one metric do not reliably preserve scores and rankings on others when used for compressed evaluation. At 80\% compression, each of the five single-metric baselines yields higher reconstruction error than equal-size random sampling in over 70\% of cross-metric comparisons. In addition, HoloCosm remains competitive with existing baselines in single-metric evaluation. We will release our code and experimental results.

We show that this discarded tail retains transferable denoising progress: action-tail initialization can match or outperform a native 10-step Gaussian baseline using fewer denoising steps. However, existing VLA interfaces offer limited flexibility in representation access: VLM information is exposed through fixed layer assignments for each action layer, while intermediate action states are only propagated implicitly through residual streams without explicit reuse. - **[FlashRT-PAI: Fast Kernels Are Not Fast Pipelines for Physical AI Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24349)**  `arXiv:2610.24349`  `cs.AI`  
  _Liang Su, David Chen, Jason Li, Kevin Guo, Teresa Lv_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA), video-action (VA), and world action models (WAMs) form the backbone of modern physical AI systems, yet their real-time single-batch inference remains a defining bottleneck. Latency is dominated not by individual kernel computation, but by system-level overhead: thousands of short kernel launches, intermediate data movement across operator boundaries, and redundant recomputation across consecutive decisions. General-purpose graph compilers cannot address these costs, as they optimize individual calls in isolation and lack visibility into cross-call state semantics and global pipeline structure. We present FLASHRT-PAI, a low-latency inference engine built around model-hardware pipeline co-design rather than isolated kernel optimization. FLASHRT-PAI combines three core mechanisms: deep kernel fusion with launch overlap eliminates inter-operator boundaries and launch gaps; an execution contract formalizes the pipeline's stage DAG, update rates, and named buffers to enable cross-decision state reuse and selective refresh; and execution-state capsules enable fast snapshot, restore, and fork of persistent pipeline state. At matched 16-bit precision, FLASHRT-PAI is 1.3-3.7x faster than official compiled paths. Combining pipeline co-design with reduced precision delivers 3.38-12.66x speedup over official eager implementations. In LIBERO-10, four-bit pi0.5 achieves 94.04% success on Thor and 93.32% on RTX 5090, compared with 92.4% for the measured official FP16 reference. FLASHRT-PAI is open source, distributed via Hugging Face Kernel Hub, and its opt-in pi0.5 kernels have been adopted into the LeRobot framework.
