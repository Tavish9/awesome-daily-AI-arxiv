  _Xiaogang Wang, Debasish Mishra, Jia Hao Toh, Wangsheng Liu, Yash Shah_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-model-based robotic navigation has gained increasing popularity, yet existing approaches still struggle to deliver effective and efficient navigation performance, \textit{e.g.}, diffusion-based policies that require costly iterative denoising. In this work, we propose three improvements to bridge these gaps. First, we adopt a rectified flow framework to reduce the required inference steps to one while achieving comparable or even superior performance. Second, we leverage a bidirectional Mamba-based visual encoder that employs selective state-space modeling to more effectively capture bidirectional temporal dependencies across observation frames, even with longer input sequences. To address this, we propose a feature-wise timestep conditioning design that explicitly conditions the visual embedding on the flow timestep, enabling noise-level-aware trajectory generation, thereby restoring a meaningful gradient pathway and enabling coherent interaction between the rectified flow and the temporal encoder. Extensive quantitative and qualitative experiments across multiple datasets demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms both baseline and competitive methods.

- **[Coherent Moment Propagation: Epistemic Uncertainty for Pre-trained Robot Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26855)**  `arXiv:2611.26855`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Miguel Saavedra-Ruiz, Anqi Li, Claudia D'Arpino, Alperen Degirmenci, Tucker Hermans, Fabio Ramos_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Flow matching policies underpin the state-of-the-art in robot learning, with impressive results ranging from long-horizon planning to dexterous manipulation. However, they provide no measure of whether their predicted commands can be trusted, particularly in novel situations outside their training set. Epistemic uncertainty reflects uncertainty about the model's weights and quantifies this ignorance, but calculating it at inference time from a flow matching policy is not trivial. Actions are produced by an ODE solver, requiring uncertainty to be propagated through the entire integration process. BayesDiff addresses this problem for diffusion models by propagating the mean and variance of the state through the denoising chain under a last-layer Laplace posterior approximation, but it severely underestimates the epistemic uncertainty. In this paper we: i) prove that, for flow matching and diffusion ODEs, BayesDiff's total variance decays at rate $O(1/T)$ as the number of Euler steps $T$ used by the solver increases; ii) introduce Coherent Moment Propagation (CoMP), two recursions inspired by the extended and unscented Kalman filters that fix this problem by carrying the cross-covariance between state and weights; and iii) widen the last-layer posterior with observation embeddings the policy already computes, improving uncertainty estimates without retraining for CoMP and several test-time estimators alike. We demonstrate these benefits on three manipulation benchmarks with applications in failure detection and demonstration selection for fine-tuning, where CoMP corrects BayesDiff's variance collapse while matching or outperforming several uncertainty estimators.

- **[NormFlowSDE: Normalizing Flows for Amortized Bayesian State Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27362)**  `arXiv:2611.27362`  `cs.RO`  
  _Yi Zhang, Lars Mikelsons_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Bayesian state estimators, such as Kalman filters, estimate states from noisy observations based on dynamical models. However, obtaining accurate models can be challenging when the system involves effects that are difficult to model faithfully, such as friction, impacts, and deformation in contact-rich manipulation. Uncertainty in system parameters, inputs, and disturbances, together with simplified noise models, can further introduce simulation-to-reality mismatch and degrade estimation accuracy. These limitations motivate learning a distribution over state trajectories conditioned on observations and controls without explicitly specifying the underlying physical models. We introduce NormFlowSDE, an amortized Bayesian state estimator that learns a distribution over state trajectories by combining neural stochastic differential equations with conditional normalizing flows. Its key design choice is to utilize the capability of normalizing flows to learn stochastic behavior of variables, while leveraging the continuous-time dynamics of SDEs to model the evolution of states over time. Once trained, the model draws state paths for new measurement sequences without per-sequence optimization. In addition, we connect NormFlowSDE to stochastic continuous-time dynamics by proving weak convergence of a mean-centered, drift-corrected inference scheme to an Itô diffusion under stated assumptions. Finally, we benchmark NormFlowSDE against direct estimators and learned particle filters based on normalizing flows on partially observed dynamical systems, including simulated systems and real-world robot datasets. The results show that NormFlowSDE attains the lowest estimation error with calibrated uncertainty intervals on both robot datasets and filters four times faster than the fastest particle filter on every dataset.

They are often partly observed, and learning their distribution enables imputing missing values and forecasting future ones. We introduce graph-aware diffusion (GAD), whose forward process is a heat diffusion on the graph driven by Gaussian noise.
