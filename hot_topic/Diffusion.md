- **[Model-Aware Generation of Safety-Critical Scenes for Robust End-to-End Driving via Distilled Planner Surrogates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35448)**  `arXiv:2610.35448`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Khaled M. Seyam, Julian Wiederer, Samar Fathalah, Markus Braun, Bin Yang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  End-to-end driving models perform well on standard benchmarks but collide frequently in safety-critical scenarios. We present a failure-mode analysis on NeuroNCAP which shows that 95.9% of DiffusionDrive's collisions are planning failures despite reliable detection, pinpointing the bottleneck in planning rather than perception. We attribute this to a fundamental data imbalance, as safety-critical interactions are rare in real driving logs, so planners are hardly ever trained on them. We close this gap with a novel model-aware framework that generates safety-critical training scenes against a specific planner. Each planner is attacked at the bird's-eye-view (BEV) level through a differentiable surrogate distilled from its own planning head. This keeps the expensive video renderer out of the attack loop. We extract 50 scenes per planner, each pairing an attack with a safe escape trajectory that serves as the fine-tuning label. A fully automated quality-diversity search generates both the attacks and the escapes, so that their variety is not specified by hand. Only these scenes are rendered to fine-tune the planner, which reduces the computational cost significantly. Fine-tuning on these scenes roughly halves the closed-loop collision rate of two planners on NeuroNCAP (SparseDrive 28.0% to 12.2%, DiffusionDrive 37.5% to 19.2%) while largely preserving normal driving. A cross-planner transfer experiment shows that the gain comes from targeting the specific planner. Each planner improves most on the scenes generated against itself, and DiffusionDrive fine-tuned on SparseDrive's scenes retains only 6.7 of its 18.3-point collision-rate reduction.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent vision-language-action (VLA) models can generate plausible end-effector motions, yet they often fail in long-horizon, contact-rich tasks because the underlying hand-object interaction (HOI) structure is not explicitly represented. We propose FlowHOI, a two-stage flow-matching framework that generates semantically grounded, temporally coherent HOI sequences, comprising hand poses, object poses, and hand-object contact states, conditioned on an egocentric observation, a language instruction, and a 3D Gaussian splatting (3DGS) scene reconstruction.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Attention over tens of thousands of visual tokens dominates the inference of video diffusion transformers (DiTs). Existing methods reduce this cost through sparsity, low precision, or both, but use a single precision for all kept tiles. Many tiles can be skipped with little error, others must be computed but gain little beyond low precision, and a few retain substantial error even at low precision. The crucial final step of the single-pass discrete denoising process involves using the logit distribution at each position to sample conditionally independent tokens. The resulting draft is thus a set of per-position marginals, rather than a joint distribution: no draft token is guaranteed to depend on its predecessors. Such independently sampled marginals tend to produce sequences with tokens that are individually likely, but jointly improbable under the target model's distribution, which verifies each token conditionally. This can cause early rejection and limits acceptance length. To address this, we propose xPress as a means to restore the missing causality in diffusion drafters. xPress is a lightweight causal refiner that reconciles the whole diffusion block at once through parallel refinement, restoring and propagating causal dependencies across the draft without a token-by-token loop. On Qwen3-8B, across seven math, code, and chat benchmarks, xPress raises acceptance length by about 30% on average (up to +56%) and its end-to-end decoding throughput by about 1.3 on average (up to 1.7) compared to the original dFlash diffusion drafter.
