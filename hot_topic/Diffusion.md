- **[Self-Distilling Future Video Representations for World Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30320)**  `arXiv:2610.30320`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Huiwon Jang, Suhyeok Jang, Hyeonmo Kang, Seungku Kim, Jinwoo Shin, Sihyun Yu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  World Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as a promising approach to learning generalist robot policies using rich spatiotemporal priors from pre-trained video diffusion models. A recent line of WAMs improves inference efficiency by conditioning a separate action decoder on intermediate future video representations extracted from a single denoising step, avoiding costly iterative denoising required for video generation. This design makes action prediction depend directly on the quality of these future representations. In this paper, we introduce Future REpresentation learning via self-Distillation with temporal context (FRED), a framework that trains WAMs to improve the quality of future video representations from noise in a single denoising step. Specifically, FRED uses self-distillation to predict future representations from noise, using representations extracted from ground-truth future observations as targets. We further condition the video model on historical observations, providing temporal context for predicting future representations better. Across diverse real-world manipulation scenarios, FRED consistently outperforms competing WAMs. It achieves particularly large gains on long-horizon and out-of-domain tasks under zero-shot environment generalization, and substantially improves human-to-robot skill transfer, reaching 50.0% task success on tasks absent from robot training data and demonstrated only in human videos, compared with 19.4% for the best competing WAM.

- **[PhysibleMDM: Enforcing Inference-Time Physical Feasibility Constraints on Motion Diffusion Models via Noise Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30500)**  `arXiv:2610.30500`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Maximilian Stölzle, David Müller, Agon Serifi, Ruben Grandia, Espen Knoop, Moritz Bächer_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Motion Diffusion Models (MDMs) can generate diverse and visually appealing human motion; yet, the resulting trajectories frequently violate physical constraints, limiting their downstream use in simulation and control. We present PhysibleMDM, a framework that promotes physical feasibility of MDM output by optimizing at inference time the diffusion noise without retraining the generative model. We introduce a set of surrogate feasibility constraints that can be evaluated without full trajectory simulation. These constraints combine kinematic checks (e.g., ground penetration, slip, and self-collision) with dynamic feasibility tests computed via an inverse-dynamics inner loop formulated as a cone program. We solve the resulting black-box noise optimization problem using the Separable Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (Sep-CMA-ES), thereby avoiding reliance on computationally expensive and discontinuous gradients. An autoregressive scheme then chains optimized segments to synthesize long motion sequences. Across 100 motion styles on an SMPL humanoid, PhysibleMDM reduces mean ground penetration, self-collision penetration, and ground slip by 75%, 92%, and 74%, respectively, relative to unoptimized diffusion output. Across 11 sequences for a custom bipedal robot, it achieves median reductions of 89.5% in per-sequence mean acceleration-reconstruction error and 79.5% in the mean inverse-dynamics residual.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion trajectory planners with domain-specific experts face a practical question in unseen regions: can they rely on selecting among existing experts, or must the planner itself adapt? We study this question using a planner with a shared adapter and per-domain expert adapters. Across three real-world held-out maps, none of eight test-time routing schemes improves the fleet-mean lateral offset over the static default, which uses the same expert for every scene. Even an oracle that selects the best expert for each scene reduces the mean offset by only 0.9 m. On nuPlan, fine-tuning across training cities also improves open-loop offsets, although closed-loop performance deteriorates. Further diagnostics reveal that two test-time adaptation baselines achieve low offsets by producing degenerate trajectories. These results show the limited headroom for selecting among existing experts and the greater open-loop gains available from adapting the planner.
