- **[Stochastic Diversity Windows: Diagnosing Noise Influence in Robotic Diffusion Sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22743)**  `arXiv:2611.22743`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Tina Lakhani, Jing Wu, Yu-Kun Lai, Ze Ji_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion models are increasingly adopted in robotics and control due to their capability to express multiple feasible solutions to a given task. However, the role of stochasticity in the diffusion model-sampler across denoising remains poorly understood. We introduce a per-timestep counterfactual diagnostic that tests how changing the sampled noise affects the final output. Across five model-sampler configurations spanning three robotics benchmarks, diverse architectures, domains, and conditioning mechanisms, we reveal highly varied temporal sensitivity profiles, where noise-induced deviations can amplify, partially contract, or undergo near-total attenuation downstream. From these profiles, we define stochastic diversity windows (SDWs): compact intervals that identify where sampler stochasticity has the strongest measured influence on the final output. Guided by these windows, we apply two heuristic interventions: targeted stochastic amplification, which increases sample diversity exploration of the solution space, and an SDW-hybrid sampler, which replaces DDPM steps with DDIM skips in low-impact regions, resulting in network evaluation reductions of up to 40-60% and wall-clock time by up to 85%, with no consistent changes detected in feasibility, cost, or diversity across held-out tasks.
  </details>

  Diffusion-based end-to-end autonomous driving models have shown strong potential in generating multimodal planning trajectories. However, their performance still depends heavily on trajectory initialization, mode evaluation, and supervision quality. Weakly constrained noisy initialization may introduce infeasible proposals and increase the burden of denoising. Moreover, selecting the optimal mode remains challenging when candidate trajectories are geometrically similar but differ in safety, interaction risk, and progress. To address these issues, we propose FIRE-Drive, a feasibility-informed risk evaluation framework for end-to-end autonomous driving. Experiments on NAVSIM v1 show that FIRE-Drive achieves a Predictive Driver Model Score of 91.5 with a ResNet34 backbone. Component ablations and a scorer comparison under matched candidate inputs support the contributions of feasible initialization, auxiliary supervision, and risk-informed selection.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>

We introduce Body Schema Policy (BSP), an imitation learning policy that builds such a schema into the vision-action interface. - **[RiDEx: Reward-Informed Directional Exploration for Diffusion Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22052)**  `arXiv:2611.22052`  `cs.LG` `cs.CV`  
  _Song Yan, Chenfeng Wang, Wei Zhai, Xingchen Wang, Tao Zhang, Yawen Shao, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Reward-based diffusion post-training updates model parameters from task rewards. While existing approaches improve sampling and policy optimization, they still rely largely on isotropic random perturbations in a high-dimensional latent space for exploration. Such variation often spends the perturbation budget on changes with little influence on the target reward, making exploration inefficient. We study where diffusion models should explore and how to learn from that exploration. We introduce Reward-Informed Directional Exploration (RiDEx), which concentrates local exploration in reward-relevant subspaces of the denoising velocity field. Conditional residuals or differentiable reward gradients propose the subspace, and equal-and-opposite perturbations at the same generation state measure its terminal reward response. The reward difference supplies signed local supervision in the explored coordinates, while all generated images contribute to global DiffusionNFT learning. RiDEx thereby couples exploration-space selection with feedback about the changes it produces. On Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium, RiDEx attains the highest peak rewards among the evaluated methods on four alignment and preference tasks. Its gradient-direction instantiation achieves 15x and 29x target-attainment speedups over DiffusionNFT on HPSv2 and PickScore, measured in H200 GPU hours. Controlled experiments confirm that reward-aligned directions elicit stronger responses at equal perturbation magnitude, and that applying their measured credit improves curve-average reward across all four tasks.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Speculative decoding accelerates vision-language models(VLMs) while preserving the target model's outputs, but its speedup diminishes under high concurrency serving as the system becomes more compute bounded as the batch size increases. On Qwen3-VL-8B and MMSpec, VisHead-Spec-8 lets 8 of the drafter's 40 KV heads read the image while keeping accepted length within 0.3% of the block-diffusion draft model baseline (i.e. DSpark). At batch size 64, its throughput gain over autoregressive decoding increases to 16.0%, compared with 9.5% for DSpark; at batch size 1, its speedup remains comparable to DSpark.

- **[The Hidden Cost of MoE Diffusion Decoding: Profiling and Fixing the Sampler](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21852)**  `arXiv:2611.21852`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `cs.CL`  
  _Harshit Samrat, Ramakrishna Paramahamsa Kompella_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Masked diffusion language models (dLLMs) decode by iteratively denoising a full canvas of tokens, so every forward pass runs a per-token sampling and stopping pipeline over the entire canvas-by-vocabulary logits tensor. We profile single-user decoding of a 26B-parameter diffusion Mixture-of-Experts model on a bandwidth-bound edge accelerator (NVIDIA GB10) and report a result that is easy to miss: after weight quantisation, this logits pipeline, not the expert GEMMs, is the single largest consumer of decode time.
