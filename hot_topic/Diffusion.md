- **[Feedback World Model Enables Precise Guidance of Diffusion Policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15705)**  `arXiv:2605.15705`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Tuo An, Jindou Jia, Gen Li, Jingliang Li, Chuhao Zhou, Pengfei Liu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  World models aim to improve robotic decision making by predicting the consequences of actions. However, in practice, their predictions often become unreliable once the robot encounters states outside the training distribution, limiting their effectiveness at deployment. We observe that execution itself provides a natural but underutilized signal: after each action, the robot directly observes the true next state, revealing the mismatch between predicted and actual outcomes. Building on this insight, we propose feedback world model, an inference-time mechanism that closes the loop between prediction and observation at inference time. Instead of treating the world model as a static open-loop predictor, our method maintains a lightweight feedback state that is updated online to iteratively correct future predictions, compensating for model errors using real-time observations without additional training data or parameter updates. We show that this process can be interpreted as a latent-space observer and admits convergence guarantees under bounded, asymptotically convergent dynamics residuals and a stable feedback gain. We further introduce action-aware guidance to better translate corrected predictions into control by emphasizing action-controllable components while suppressing irrelevant variations. Experiments on LIBERO-Plus, Robomimic, and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate that our method substantially improves both prediction accuracy and policy performance under distribution shift, reducing world model prediction error by up to 76.4%. The same feedback mechanism improves the predictions of the pretrained foundation model Fast-WAM-Joint without updating its parameters, demonstrating its plug-and-play applicability. These results show that incorporating real-time feedback at inference time provides a simple yet powerful alternative to static world modeling.

- **[Test-Time Motion Steering for Perceptive Humanoid Fall Recovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30061)**  `arXiv:2610.30061`  `cs.RO`  
  _Osher Azulay, Cheng-Lin Hsieh, Stella X. Yu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Humanoid fall recovery requires selecting a motion that suits both the surrounding scene and the task. The same fallen pose can admit several ways to stand, with different routes, support transitions, and final headings. Yet reliable recovery alone does not provide control over which of these alternatives the robot executes. Steering this choice requires anticipating the physical consequences of a requested movement. We present RISE, an approach to perceptive humanoid recovery that lets task objectives steer how the robot returns to standing at inference time. It builds on two insights: diverse recoveries can be composed from shared movements, and anticipating how these movements unfold allows recovery to be steered toward a task objective. We learn a recovery vocabulary that connects intended movements to motor actions through sensory feedback. A visual diffusion planner learns from executions of this vocabulary to predict motion intent as future body states. Task costs guide this prediction toward a desired heading, rise speed, or path. The planner then generates motion latents conditioned on the steered intent, allowing the requested movement to shape its execution. Simulation experiments demonstrate diverse recoveries, terrain adaptation, and test-time steering without retraining, complemented by real-world demonstrations on a Unitree G1. - **[Action-Conditioned World Model for Goal Plane Guidance in Robotic Neck Ultrasound](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21918)**  `arXiv:2607.21918`  `cs.RO`  
  _Siqi Fan, Ran Liu, Zixuan Yang, Xiaoyu Fu, Xiaoqing Gao, Yingqi Xing, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Ultrasound examinations typically follow specific scanning protocols that require navigating through an ordered sequence of anatomical landmarks and target imaging planes. We present a local action-conditioned world model framework for goal plane probe guidance in robotic ultrasound. Autonomous ultrasound tasks often require large numbers of probe-motion trajectories for training, but collecting high-quality demonstrations is labor-intensive and explicit simulators are difficult to build because ultrasound appearance depends on contact, tissue deformation, and view-dependent acoustic artifacts. We address this problem with a two-stage model-based learning pipeline. First, a latent conditional diffusion world model predicts future ultrasound observations from recent context frames, probe motions and temporal offset. Second, a goal-conditioned temporal transformer predicts ordered probe motions and is fine-tuned using rewards from the frozen world model. Experiments on the self-collected dataset show that the world model preserves action-dependent anatomical structure on target-directed scans. In real-world closed loop experiments, the framework achieves success rates of 70.0\% for carotid guidance and 65.0\% for thyroid guidance. These results demonstrate the potential of learned ultrasound dynamics for training goal-directed robotic probe navigation within protocol-constrained organ specific tasks.

- **[MCLR: Improving Conditional Modeling via Inter-Class Likelihood-Ratio Maximization and Unifying Classifier-Free Guidance with Alignment Objectives](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22364)**  `arXiv:2603.22364`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Xiang Li, Yixuan Jia, Xiao Li, Jeffrey A. Fessler, Rongrong Wang, Qing Qu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion models achieve strong generative performance but often rely on classifier-free guidance (CFG), an inference-time intervention that modifies the sampling trajectory. Since standard denoising score matching (DSM) should recover the target distribution when the score is learned accurately, the practical necessity of CFG suggests deficiencies in learned conditional scores. We identify weak inter-class separation as one such limitation: samples generated from the same noise under different labels can retain similar class-agnostic structure. To address this deficiency, we introduce MCLR, a principled fine-tuning objective that explicitly maximizes inter-class likelihood-ratios, thereby strengthening class-specific structures under standard sampling. Across diverse models and datasets, MCLR substantially improves guidance-free conditional generation and narrows the gap to CFG. Theoretically, we prove that the CFG-guided score is the unique minimizer of a sample-adaptively weighted, ELBO-approximated MCLR objective, interpreting CFG as an implicit inference-time contrastive alignment procedure. We further compare MCLR with related contrastive objectives, including DPO, CCA, and CFM, clarifying their connections and key differences in an otherwise fragmented literature.

  _Wang Ru_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Latent diffusion models almost universally place a Gaussian prior on the VAE latent space. For base generation this default is unproblematic; under reinforcement-learning fine-tuning, however, it carries a structural limitation. The standard VAE's KL term does not merely ignore mode structure - it actively taxes separation and tightness. When data are multimodal, the ELBO optimum compresses distinct modes into an overlapping latent. RL fine-tuning amplifies this overlap: the score network is under-resolved on the saddle region between modes, dynamic-to-variational amplification rises sharply, and the collapse boundary beta_c is pushed far above its variational scale. A three-source decomposition localises the amplification to the learned policy (zero ODE-solver contribution, bounded anchor mismatch); the coverage plateau is an unconditional benefit of mode-separating priors that no amount of RL capacity recovers. The remedy is conditional: a non-Gaussian prior is necessary precisely when the latent geometry would fall into overlap and the task is coverage-sensitive. A machine-checked theory skeleton supports the KL pricing structure - a divergent compactness tax under a Gaussian prior versus a finite assignment fee under a mixture - and the position-versus-plateau distinction delimits what is finite-resource from what requires changing the prior.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion and flow models provide expressive policy classes for robotic and sequential decision-making systems. However, their action likelihoods are generally intractable, limiting the direct use of widely adopted policy optimization methods such as PPO and GRPO.
