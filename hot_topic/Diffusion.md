- **[ForeseeDrive: Future-Token Conditioning for Anticipatory Generative Driving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14286)**  `arXiv:2611.14286`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Shaokai Wu, Xunchu Zhou, Pinzhi Wang, Bo Dong, Yuting Hu, Yanbiao Ji, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Generative driving planners can represent diverse ego futures, but a downstream scorer can choose only among sampled candidates; it cannot recover an absent maneuver. We present ForeseeDrive, which turns future scene evolution into a deployable interface for shaping candidate generation. Rather than reconstructing dense future states or scoring a fixed menu, ForeseeDrive derives query-indexed tokens from logged actor motion, occupancy evolution, route interactions, and TTC margins, and predicts the same token space from present context at deployment. The tokens condition trajectory geometry before candidates materialize. A zero-token-gated residual path and candidate-preservation loss limit unnecessary drift, while a TTC-margin guard targets brittle safe-to-unsafe boundary crossings. Controlled DiffusionDrive/NAVSIM diagnostics show an exact zero-token no-op, a small aligned-over-shuffled selected-PDM advantage under preservation, mixed oracle-best quality, and localized TTC-boundary failures. Together, the benchmark results and bounded mechanism tests support future-conditioned generation as a practical interface for anticipation. Code is included in the supplementary materials.

- **[From Prediction to Action: Learning Physical Context for Deformable Linear Object Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14274)**  `arXiv:2611.14274`  `cs.RO`  
  _Chengxiao Dong, Alessio Caporali, Hongyu Lan, Gianluca Palli_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  A representation that accurately predicts a deformable object's response is not necessarily the one most useful for choosing manipulation actions. We study this distinction between predictive utility and decision utility in planar single-grasp shape control of Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs). As a common decision backbone, we introduce Geometry-Pointer-Flow (GPF), a geometry-grounded policy that separates grasp-location selection from grasp-conditioned motion generation. Its geometry-only variant reduces one-step executed shape error by 32.8% relative to a DLO-specific diffusion policy, while using 18.7 times fewer parameters and achieving approximately 193 times faster inference. On this backbone, we compare physical context represented by ground-truth parameters, estimated parameters, and learned interaction-history representations, for both forward prediction and executed control. For prediction, ground-truth physical parameters reduce next-shape error by 15.8% over geometry alone, and estimated parameters are slightly more predictive than interaction history. This ordering does not carry over to control: estimated parameters provide no detectable one-step control gain, whereas history-conditioned GPF reduces executed error by 9.9% relative to geometry-only GPF across 470 paired simulation trials. In closed loop, interaction history improves strict-threshold attainment in simulation and yields the strongest zero-shot performance on hardware. All policies are trained exclusively in simulation and transferred to the physical robot without real-world training or fine-tuning. These results show that physical context should be evaluated by the decisions it supports rather than by predictive accuracy alone. Code will be released upon acceptance.

- **[Camera equalisation of an 11.5x drop in environmental illuminance: four imitation-learning policies on an SO-101 arm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13810)**  `arXiv:2611.13810`  `cs.RO`  
  _Simon Cowling_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Real-robot benchmarks of learned visuomotor policies often record room illuminance as the lighting condition. We show that, on a typical low-cost rig, room light and image light can be almost unrelated. Four imitation-learning policies (ACT, SmolVLA, Diffusion Policy and pi0.5) were trained on the same 50 demonstrations of a pick-and-place task on an SO-101 arm. Each was run for ten rollouts at about 520 lux and again at about 45 lux, an 11.5x reduction. Success was 20/40 in normal light and 19/40 in low light: a change of -2.5 percentage points (95% CI -23 to +19), with no evidence that the policies differed in sensitivity (interaction p = 0.86). The recorded video shows why: mean image brightness was only 3% lower in low light on the front camera and 19% lower on the wrist camera. Both cameras were set to automatic exposure and white balance, which most likely absorbed the difference; exposure was not locked in this experiment. An accidental repeat of one 10-rollout block reproduced its success count (7/10) but matched the first block at only 6 of 10 placements, consistent with chance. We conclude that lux alone does not describe what a policy sees. Camera settings, and image statistics compared with the training recordings, are the quantities to report and control. Data and code are public.

- **[RB-LDC: Redundancy-Balanced Latent Coding for Noise-Robust Diffusion Latents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13955)**  `arXiv:2611.13955`  `cs.LG` `cs.CV`  
  _Hyunseok Jeong, Jaeho Jeon, Young-Sik Kim_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Latent diffusion models decode approximately correct latents, making decoder sensitivity to perturbations relevant to image quality. After whitening, forward diffusion corrupts every direction at the same signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), despite unequal decoder sensitivities. We study an invertible linear layer between the encoder and diffusion process that shifts each direction's log-SNR. Its linear minimum mean-square-error (LMMSE) recovery risk has an exact spectral expression for any whitened latent distribution. Under an energy budget and a floor ensuring invertibility, minimizing decoder-sensitivity-weighted LMMSE risk integrated over the diffusion schedule yields a unique optimal allocation. The layer aligns with the decoder's expected pullback metric and equalizes marginal risk reduction across directions, computed by a deterministic spectral solver. At large budgets, the allocation follows a power law in sensitivity whose exponent is determined by the low-SNR tail of the loss weighting: one half for noise prediction and two thirds for data prediction under the cosine schedule, with a crossover on truncated schedules. We instantiate this principle as RB-LDC using Jacobian-based sensitivity estimates. On ImageNet at 256 x 256 resolution with SD-VAE and VA-VAE, trained RB-LDC improves reconstruction from noisy latents over budget-matched isotropic coding in all four metrics and five coding configurations, averaged over 15 SNRs and three pipeline seeds. It reduces average perturbed FID by 17.1 for VA-VAE block-local coding while preserving clean reconstruction. We also report intermediate SNRs where perturbed FID worsens. With a frozen diffusion model, RB-LDC yields lower FID point estimates in all 12 tested combinations of sampling progress and channel SNR under corruption during sampling.

- **[AdjointFlow: Accelerating Scientific Inverse Problems with Physics-Based Transport](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13389)**  `arXiv:2611.13389`  `cs.LG`  
  _Ethan Hanold, Nicholas M. Boffi, Qin Li, Leonardo Zepeda-Núñez_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Inverse problems are ubiquitous across scientific applications ranging from seismic imaging to astrophysics to nondestructive testing, yet classical solvers often struggle with nonconvexity and repeated evaluations of expensive physical models. Diffusion models can amortize this computation offline, but largely data-driven conditional models underutilize known physics and suffer from ill-conditioned transport maps when the target distribution is concentrated. Plug-and-play (PnP) methods explicitly leverage the physical model to guide a learned prior, improving data consistency but reintroducing costly iterative computations. We instead propose AdjointFlow, a framework built on stochastic interpolants that injects the known physics into the generative transport itself. We design a data-dependent, adjoint-based initialization to reduce transport distance to the target, modify the transport velocity to remain regular as the endpoint distributions concentrate, and parameterize the network to build the initialization directly into the learned velocity. Together, these enable accurate reconstruction at substantially lower cost than generative models starting from Gaussian noise. Experiments on inverse scattering, seismic imaging, and black hole imaging show that AdjointFlow consistently produces more accurate reconstructions than conditional diffusion models while being up to five orders of magnitude faster at inference than PnP methods. Optional inference-time refinement that queries the physical model can further improve data consistency, matching or exceeding the accuracy of PnP methods without retraining.

- **[FaST: Factored Synthesis of Transitions for Offline Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13013)**  `arXiv:2611.13013`  `cs.LG`

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
