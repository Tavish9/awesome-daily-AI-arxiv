- **[GSBR: Geometric Support Behavior Regularization for Online RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05295)**  `arXiv:2611.05295`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Charles Xu, Jacob Berg, Liyiming Ke, Sergey Levine_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Regularizing online reinforcement learning (RL) toward a pretrained base policy can improve sample efficiency and learning stability, but regularization that is too restrictive limits how much the policy can improve. Base policies -- typically diffusion or flow-matching models trained by imitation learning on large, diverse datasets -- capture many behaviors that are reasonable but suboptimal. Existing regularizers using such base policies are too restrictive in one of two ways: behavioral cloning regularizers pull the actor toward the full base-policy distribution, penalizing it for discarding suboptimal modes, while hard support constraints forbid improvements that lie just outside the base policy's support. We propose Geometric Support Behavior Regularization (GSBR), which penalizes actions by their distance from the base policy's support. Actions within the support incur no penalty, so the actor can concentrate on high-value modes; outside the support, where value estimates are less reliable, the penalty grows smoothly with distance while still permitting improvement when the value gained outweighs the penalty. GSBR estimates this distance from a finite set of base-policy samples, so it requires only sample access to the base policy. Across 19 tasks on four robotic benchmarks, GSBR raises the average success rate from 33% for the base policy to 95%, achieving the highest average success rate and throughput among all compared methods, with about 23% higher normalized throughput than the next-best RL method.   _Xinyue Xu, Jieqiang Sun, Jing, Siyuan Chen, Lanjie Ma, Ke Sun, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  A dexterous hand succeeds or fails through contact, yet every scalable record of human manipulation (video, vision-based hand tracking, motion capture) observes how the hand moves and misses how it pushes. We present DexCanvas, a hybrid real-synthetic dataset of 7,000 hours of dexterous hand-object interaction with per-frame contact points and contact forces, grown from 70 hours of optical motion capture that spans 21 manipulation types, from power and precision grasps to in-hand manipulation. The forces come from a real-to-sim pipeline: one reinforcement learning (RL) policy, conditioned on object geometry and manipulation type, drives an actuated MANO hand in physics simulation to reproduce each captured object trajectory, and the simulator measures the contacts that produce the reproduced motion. On six YCB objects absent from the capture set it succeeds 72.5% of the time. The contact channels make better policies: a Diffusion Policy that observes DexCanvas contact features succeeds 82.2% of the time, against 67.5% without them, over 18,522 trials. Contact-driven retargeting carries the same demonstrations to two dexterous robot hands, where the RL stage again produces stable rollouts. A first release of 111,857 force-annotated trajectories is publicly available on Hugging Face.

- **[TriTile: Three-Tier Attention for Video Diffusion Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35706)**  `arXiv:2610.35706`  `cs.LG` `cs.CV`  
  _Yeonjae Kim, Omin Kwon, Seong Hoon Seo, Jae W. Lee_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Attention over tens of thousands of visual tokens dominates the inference of video diffusion transformers (DiTs). Existing methods reduce this cost through sparsity, low precision, or both, but use a single precision for all kept tiles. We observe that the output error introduced by approximation varies widely across tiles. Many tiles can be skipped with little error, others must be computed but gain little beyond low precision, and a few retain substantial error even at low precision. We present TriTile, a training-free attention method that assigns each tile one of three approximation tiers: skipping it by replacing it with its block means, skimming it in NVFP4, or refining it with NVFP4 residual correction. A fused kernel executes all three tiers in a single pass on the FP4 tensor cores. Over dense FlashAttention, TriTile speeds up generation by 1.38-1.83x on Wan2.2 at 23.0-27.6 dB PSNR, 2.28-2.32x on HunyuanVideo at 34.5-36.0 dB, and 1.84x on LTX-2.5 at 34.1 dB, and in every setting it Pareto-dominates every baseline in speed and PSNR.
