- **[DPGS-SLAM: Diffusion-Prior and Provenance-Aware Monocular Gaussian Splatting SLAM for Dynamic Visual Sensing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12038)**  `arXiv:2611.12038`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Yifan Luo, Takafumi Matsumaru, Vibekananda Dutta, Xin He_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  This paper presents DPGS-SLAM (Diffusion-Prior and Provenance-Aware Monocular Gaussian Splatting SLAM) for dense mapping in environments containing dynamic objects. Objects in dynamic motion remain a significant challenge for dense visual SLAM since dynamic observations can contaminate camera tracking and scene reconstruction. Although 3DGS (3D Gaussian splatting) enables efficient dense mapping, existing 3DGS-SLAM systems may exhibit instability when dynamic objects violate their underlying static-scene assumption. Excluding dynamic observations can reduce their influence on tracking and mapping, but may leave occluded static backgrounds missing or weakly constrained. Our proposed DPGS-SLAM is a monocular RGB SLAM framework that supports provisional representations of insufficiently observed static backgrounds through asynchronous diffusion-based background completion and provenance-aware Gaussian mapping. Dynamic regions are identified through optical-flow-based motion discovery and temporally consistent mask propagation by SAM2. For selected keyframes, a fine-tuned diffusion inpainting model generates plausible background appearance in occluded regions, while metric depth predicted from completed images initializes provisional Gaussian primitives. Provenance labels distinguish generated primitives from observation-supported primitives, excluding generated content from camera-pose estimation and observed-map optimization. When previously occluded backgrounds become visible, eligible generated primitives are removed, and observation-supported primitives are independently initialized from real RGB observations and predicted depth. Experimental results demonstrated competitive camera tracking, with average ATE RMSE values of 1.2 cm on TUM RGB-D and 2.7 cm on BONN RGB-D datasets. On StaDy4D, DPGS-SLAM improved average PSNR by 4.60 dB over WildGS-SLAM using only monocular RGB input.

- **[Inner Product Regularization for Imitation Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17159)**  `arXiv:2611.17159`  `cs.RO`  
  _Benjamin A. Christie, Tianyu Qiu, Amy H. Choi, David Fridovich-Keil, Dylan P. Losey_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robots that learn from demonstrations should extrapolate their policy despite state perturbations such as sensor noise, scene variation, and compounding errors. One approach to this robustness is regularization. Prior regularizers consider each state on its own and limit how sensitive the policy is to any perturbation at that state. By contrast, we explore how the policy's output shifts when given the same perturbation at different states. Our insight is that, locally, the robot should respond to state perturbations in a consistent manner: e.g., if changing an irrelevant object position does not impact the policy at one state, that same change should not shift the policy output at a nearby state. Applying this insight, we develop inner product regularization (InneR). We show that the inner product on action space induces an inner product on state space through the policy Jacobian (the Fisher information for stochastic policies), and that the null space of this inner product governs which state perturbations have a first-order effect on the policy. By clustering states whose inner products are aligned, averaging the inner products within each cluster, and minimizing the smallest eigenvalues of that average, InneR shapes the learned policy so that nearby states share a common null space. InneR enforces consistent responses to perturbations without limiting policy expressiveness, and can be appended to existing imitation learning pipelines for deterministic or stochastic policies. In simulated experiments, InneR makes learned policies more robust to irrelevant state dimensions and shifts between training and test distributions, whereas norm penalties often perform worse than behavior cloning. On a real robot arm performing three manipulation tasks, InneR raises the average success rate of a diffusion policy from 18% to 57% without additional data.
