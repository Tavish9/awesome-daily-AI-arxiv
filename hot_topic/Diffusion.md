  _Jiawei Geng, Zhenyang Liu, Yuzheng Wu, Yanwei Fu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Rapid progress in embodied intelligence has enabled increasingly capable robots to follow language instructions and manipulate diverse objects. - **[Gossip Agents: Reputation through Opinion Dynamics over Evolving Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22674)**  `arXiv:2610.22674`  `cs.MA`  
  _Yutong Wu, Arlei Silva_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Sustained cooperation among self-interested learning agents is essential in many shared environments. Reputation systems are among the best-studied mechanisms for promoting such cooperation. Existing multi-agent reinforcement learning approaches, however, typically assume that reputation is either globally available to all agents or derived solely from an agent's own observations, leaving largely unexplored how reputational information propagates between agents. We study gossip, the exchange of information about third parties, as a mechanism for reputation diffusion over dynamic communication networks. Agents form private opinions from limited observations and exchange them with their neighbors following DeGroot opinion dynamics, while the communication network changes as agents interact and move. - **[Bernoulli Flow Models: Self-Consistent Generative Modeling for Binary Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11362)**  `arXiv:2610.11362`  `cs.LG` `cs.CV`  
  _Hao Mo, Liying Yang, Shumin Yao, Xinxing Yu, Ajian Liu, Xudong Mao, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Binary diffusion models typically require many function evaluations (NFEs) to generate high-quality samples, making inference costly. Reducing NFEs without sacrificing sample quality or relying on distillation or additional training remains challenging. Existing binary diffusion models define discrete one-step forward paths and derive reverse posteriors accordingly. In low-NFE cross-step sampling, they incorrectly approximate a true multi-step likelihood using a single-step transition, causing severe quality degradation. We propose Bernoulli Flow Models (BFM) to decouple generative dynamics from fixed discrete time steps. Instead of sequential one-step Markov diffusion chains, BFM defines a unified continuous global Bernoulli probability flow path between data and pure noise and derives analytical closed-form posterior transitions over arbitrary time intervals. Reducing the inference NFE therefore only requires re-evaluating this posterior on the new intervals rather than approximating skipped steps. This removes the structural training-inference mismatch of discrete chains and enables strictly self-consistent low-NFE sampling. Experiments show strong robustness under aggressive NFE reduction: on LSUN Churches 256x256, a 256-step-trained BFM achieves an FID of 9.22 with only 16 sampling steps, while the state-of-the-art discrete baseline degrades to 204.10. BFM also remains competitive with continuous and discrete generative baselines under standard full-step inference. These results establish BFM as a theoretically rigorous, self-consistent, and practical framework for fast binary data generation. Code is available atthis https URL.

- **[Large Diffusion Models Need More Than EMA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22945)**  `arXiv:2610.22945`  `cs.LG`  
  _Chuyu Hu, Huanran Chen, Shitong Shao, Yinpeng Dong_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Large-scale diffusion models are commonly trained with a constant learning rate (LR) and exponential moving average (EMA), despite the strong performance of LR decay and Schedule-Free optimization in training large language models. We study whether EMA is uniquely suited to diffusion training, or whether stronger alternatives have been underexplored. Our theory shows that averaging a constant-step trajectory has an inherent optimization limitation, whereas LR decay and Schedule-Free optimization admit vanishing stationarity guarantees under standard nonconvex assumptions. Empirically, LR decay and EMA provide complementary benefits: combining decay with EMA further improves the standard baseline at most tested starting points, although the gain remains sensitive to when decay begins. Overall, our results suggest that large diffusion models need more than parameter averaging alone: effective optimization requires balancing variance reduction with continued optimization.

- **[CoTA++: Understanding the Repeat Curse in dMLLMs from an Information Flow Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23290)**  `arXiv:2610.23290`  `cs.AI`  
  _Qiyan Zhao, Xiaofeng Zhang, Xu-Yao Zhang, Shijie Han, Zhepeng Li, Shunzhi Zhu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion-based multimodal large language models (dMLLMs) decode by iterative denoising with bidirectional attention, which precludes the standard autoregressive key--value cache. Practical deployments therefore rely on approximate caching that reuses intermediate states across denoising steps. Such caching, however, often makes a response repeat one token at adjacent positions in a run that breaks the sentence, a failure we term the \emph{Repeat Curse}. Tracing the context-token information flow reveals three disruptions. Within a step, caching disperses the attention that anchors a position on nearby context tokens. Across steps, similarity-based refresh is not staleness-aware, so some positions are decoded against stale key--value states. Across layers, the deep-layer entropy of the context fails to converge where repetition arises. We accordingly propose CoTA++, a training-free, plug-and-play framework that counteracts each disruption where it originates: Context-Token Attention Re-anchoring re-forms the attention routing against the current context, Decode-Aware Refresh reserves part of the cache's refresh budget for the positions about to be committed, and Context-Token Entropy-Guided Voting discounts the confidence of a position by the deep-layer entropy of its context, which reorders the unmasking. The three components act at non-overlapping stages of a decoding step and compose freely with existing dMLLMs and caches. Across three dMLLMs and two caching backends, CoTA++ reduces the sample repetition rate of long responses from up to 99.6\% to at most 17.0\%, recovers most of the accuracy lost to caching on nine benchmarks, and keeps the speedup of caching at under 15\% overhead, making approximate caching reliable for dMLLMs.
