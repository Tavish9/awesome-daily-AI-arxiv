- **[When Does Cross-View Consistency Matter in Multi-View Appearance Augmentation? A Controlled Study for Visuomotor Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17751)**  `arXiv:2611.17751`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Zixuan Jiang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Multi-view visual augmentation often seeks consistency across cameras, but it is unclear which forms of consistency matter for downstream control. We use simulation-state replay to manipulate cross-view coupling while preserving the per-camera perturbation distributions, physical trajectories, and action labels. Across seven LIBERO manipulation tasks, marginal-matched changes in global appearance, ghost injection, and target color have small average costs for a late-fusion Diffusion Policy: point estimates are near zero or negative, and the largest upper endpoint of the 90% posterior credible intervals is 2.55 percentage points. Within the tested tasks and policy configurations, these findings motivate selective consistency constraints: appearance coupling can sometimes be relaxed, whereas task-relevant geometry and cross-view spatial correspondence warrant protection.

  _Yefim Nudelman, Dor Atzmon, Tom Tirer_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>

Diffusion-based planners often address obstacle avoidance through sampling-time guidance, while some conditioned approaches aggregate map geometry into a global representation. The resulting multi-robot system achieves higher team success than the compared diffusion baselines, with experiments covering teams of up to 15 robots.

- **[Chameleon: Dynamic Format Adapter for Efficient Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33496)**  `arXiv:2609.33496`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Arnab Sanyal, Sandeep Chinchali_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Post-training quantization (PTQ) is the standard way to run modern diffusion models on memory-constrained accelerators, yet every existing diffusion PTQ scheme fixes the $\mathit{number\ format}$ in advance and only tunes the scale, zero point, or per-layer bit-width. At a fixed bit-width the best format depends on the distribution being encoded, and that distribution differs across weight channels, across layers, and along the diffusion timestep, where activation distributions slide from heavy-tailed and noise-dominated to tightly clustered and structured. We propose Chameleon, a PTQ framework that holds the bit-width fixed and treats the format itself as a discrete variable, chosen per weight channel and per activation layer, optionally refined per timestep bucket. An architectural fork adapts the same selection layer to multi-step UNets, single-step distilled models, and Diffusion Transformers. Most of the gain comes from the per-layer choice; the timestep refinement helps mainly at 6-bit activations. Across SDXL, SDXL-Turbo, and PixArt-$\alpha$ on COCO-2014, Chameleon achieves the best FID in all six backbone $\times$ bit-width settings, with CLIP within 0.24 of the FP16 reference and the best of all quantized methods at $W_{4}A_{8}$. On PixArt-$\alpha$, a real mixed-format kernel runs 1.09$\times$ faster than FP16 end-to-end with 3$\times$ smaller weights.

  We study probabilistic forecasting of dynamical systems such as fluids and waves from coarse observations. Since the unseen fine scales influence the dynamics, the same coarse initial state can evolve into a whole distribution of futures. We release our datasets as a first benchmark for probabilistic forecasting with ground-truth conditional ensembles.
