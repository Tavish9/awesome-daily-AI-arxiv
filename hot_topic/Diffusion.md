  _Chuanke Pang, Junyi Huang, Jianye Fang, Zhijun Zhao, Yaobing Wang, Kun Xu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) models offer semantic and spatial priors for robot manipulation, but their parallel-gripper interfaces leave multi-finger contact coordination implicit. Adapting these models requires both timely grasp transitions and embodiment-specific hand trajectories. We introduce InDex, an intent-conditioned framework that connects these two requirements. A normalized aperture signal, derived from retargeted demonstrations, supervises a scalar grasp intent while preserving full hand-action supervision. Component ablations and timing interventions support the value of explicit grasp-intent conditioning and expressive hand generation. - **[Closed-Loop Adaptive Sampling on a Generative Ocean Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05776)**  `arXiv:2611.05776`  `cs.RO`  
  _Shayesteh Hafezi, Alan Papalia_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Adaptive sampling can substantially improve the value of sparse ocean observations by directing mobile robots toward measurements that are most informative for an evolving environment. Recent learned generative ocean models can produce high-resolution forecasts and ensembles at substantially lower computational cost than traditional numerical models, creating an opportunity to use rich predictive forecasts directly in closed-loop robotic sampling. However, these models do not natively provide the explicit uncertainty representations assumed by classical informative-planning methods, and their forecasts often evolve in learned latent spaces rather than directly in physical ocean variables. We develop a multi-robot closed-loop adaptive sampling framework that uses a single learned generative model for forecasting, uncertainty estimation, planning, and measurement assimilation. The model forecasts an ensemble in latent space, decodes it to physical ocean states, and uses ensemble statistics to construct A- or D-optimal sampling objectives. We select sampling locations subject to robot reachability under predicted currents. The collected measurements are assimilated back into the latent state using observation-conditioned diffusion sampling. Across 120 test scenarios A- and D-optimal sampling generally outperforms static, random, and systematic-coverage baselines while adding little planning overhead. Our experiments uncover several scenarios in which model uncertainty diverges from forecast error, motivating future work in uncertainty quantification.

- **[Continuous Adversarial MeanFlow Transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19540)**  `arXiv:2608.19540`  `cs.LG` `cs.CV`  
  _Yara Bahram, Zahra Dehghani, Mélodie Desbos, Eric Granger, Pablo Piantanida, Mohammadhadi Shateri_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Training fast generators on new domains with limited data remains challenging for two reasons. First, adapting a pretrained diffusion or flow model leaves its costly multi-step sampling intact, and existing acceleration methods are tied to the source parameterization (x, epsilon, v, or u), so heterogeneous pretrained models share no common acceleration target. Second, while adversarial post-training sharpens generation quality, existing criteria each cover only one end of the step range. Discrete criteria compare jump endpoints and lose their signal as the jump shrinks, while continuous criteria score the instantaneous velocity and misjudge the large jumps of few-step sampling. Neither fits MeanFlow (MF) models, which predict average velocities over intervals of any length. We propose MeanFlow-Transfer (MF-T), which adapts a source domain model of any parameterization and compresses it into a few-step MF generator through a common velocity map. We then introduce Continuous Adversarial MeanFlow (CAMF), which scores the change of a learned potential along each predicted interval jump of MF. CAMF needs no gradient penalty or Jacobian-vector product (JVP). We also prove it stays well-conditioned for every interval length and recovers the instantaneous criterion as the interval vanishes. Adapting four ImageNet-based source models (DiT, SiT, JiT, iMF) to five target domains, MF-T + CAMF approaches or exceeds the fine-tuned many-step teacher in FID and FDD at up to 125x fewer Neural Function Evaluations (NFEs). Further, CAMF improves MF-T's few-step FID by about 30% on average, outperforming prior adversarial post-training on iMF at every NFE.

- **[Learning Pseudo-Riemannian Manifolds for Heterophilic Graphs via Graph Signature](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06265)**  `arXiv:2611.06265`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI`  
  _Yun Young Choi, Asung Kil, Sun Woo Park, Minho Lee, Seokhwan Kim_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Several widely used families of Graph Neural Networks carry a positive-definite geometric prior. Graph convolution read as heat diffusion needs a positive semi-definite operator, hyperbolic models place features on positive-definite Riemannian manifolds, and dot-product and GAT-style attention scores use positive-definite inner products. On heterophilic graphs, where edges often signify dissimilarity or structural repulsion, that prior is a mismatch: no positive-definite form can assign a negative squared distance to a direction the labels separate. To measure the mismatch, we introduce the Graph Signature Index ($s_{\mathcal{G}}$), the inertia of a class-compatibility matrix built from label topology alone, and we prove that no value of the edge or node homophily index in $(0,1)$ rules out a negative eigenvalue. We then propose the Pseudo-Riemannian Attention Network (PRAT), whose attention logit is a signed squared pseudo-distance under a learnable diagonal metric initialized from $s_{\mathcal{G}}$. The design is diagnose-then-learn: the signature supplies a sign structure that unconstrained training keeps outside near-zero directions, and the projection learns the rest of the metric around it. Across fifteen node-classification benchmarks PRAT outperforms or matches the strongest heterophily-adapted models. A controlled ablation ties that performance to the sign structure itself: with architecture and protocol fixed, forbidding the metric to be indefinite costs accuracy on the graphs $s_{\mathcal{G}}$ marks as indefinite and leaves the others unchanged.

- **[Revisiting the 16x Pretraining Compute Gap between Diffusion and Autoregressive Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17590)**  `arXiv:2610.17590`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI`  
  _Guanghan Wang, Bing He, Zhan Shi, Benoit Dumoulin, Hanqing Lu, Volodymyr Kuleshov_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion language models (DLMs) have attracted wide attention for their fast, parallel decoding, but they are believed to be far less efficient to train than autoregressive language models (ARMs): a widely cited study reports that a DLM needs roughly 16x the training compute of an ARM to match its likelihood, a figure that has made pretraining DLMs from scratch look prohibitive at scale. We find that the methodology behind this figure has fallen out of step with current DLM practice: it scores the model with its evidence lower bound (ELBO) and trains a full-sequence, decoder-only model. We therefore revisit the scaling laws of DLMs, adopting the exact likelihood under the model's own decoding order, block diffusion training, an encoder-decoder architecture, and a sweep over the block sizes used in practice. We complement these scaling analyses with an empirical rule for the optimal decoder depth of encoder-decoder block diffusion, a held-out compute budget beyond the fitting range at which the gap between variants persists, and downstream benchmark results where likelihood improvements carry over to accuracy. Overall, the 16x figure substantially overstates the pretraining cost gap between DLMs and ARMs, and pretraining DLMs from scratch is far more viable than previously assumed.
