- **[PlanDiv: Test-Time Scaling with Latent Plans for Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20010)**  `arXiv:2611.20010`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Hanbo Zhang, Lianlei Shan, Liuhaichen Yang, Zezhi Tang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Test-time scaling improves vision-language-action (VLA) policies by drawing several action candidates and executing the best one. We find that its returns diminish quickly when the candidates are resampled from one decoder: they differ by noise rather than by plan, and for each of three resampling sources, going from four to eight candidates adds only 0.06-0.07 to the average number of completed tasks on CALVIN. We introduce PlanDiv, an action head that makes the candidates differ by plan: a lightweight planner proposes K latent plan hypotheses, a shared masked-diffusion executor decodes one action chunk per plan from a single backbone pass, and the policy executes the chunk that its own likelihood ranks highest. The plans are learned from demonstrations alone, without plan labels or rewards. With the backbone, data and training recipe held fixed, eight plan-conditioned candidates raise five-task chain success on CALVIN ABC$\rightarrow$D from 56.9% to 69.9%, while eight candidates from autoregressive, plan-free or same-plan resampling reach at most 64.9%. The same-plan baseline is PlanDiv's own checkpoint with the plan held fixed, so this gap comes from the plans, not from drawing more samples. On LIBERO-Long, four plans remove 40% of single-candidate failures, against at most 21% for matched resampling. On a real Franka arm the gain appears where a task admits more than one solution: PlanDiv is on par with a single-output OpenVLA-OFT+ baseline on a one-solution task and succeeds in 51 of 54 trials against 40 of 54 on two tasks with two solutions.

- **[From Uncertainty to Determinism: Coarse-to-Fine Visual Floorplan Localization without Ray Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26817)**  `arXiv:2607.26817`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Shiyong Meng, Bolei Chen, Ping Zhong, Yang Wan, Rongzhi Wang, Jiazhi Xia, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Visual Floorplan Localization (FLoc) has emerged as a promising solution for indoor localization by matching egocentric images against minimalist structural maps. However, due to cross-modal information asymmetry and repetitive indoor layouts, visual FLoc is fundamentally challenged by multimodal pose distributions, where visually identical observations map to distinct, spatially separated locations. Existing ray-matching-based methods tackle this by explicitly predicting sparse geometric or semantic rays, which inherently incur information loss and demand resource-intensive preprocessing alongside exhaustive matching during inference. In this paper, we bypass the intermediate ray-matching paradigm and propose a coarse-to-fine visual FLoc framework that progresses from uncertainty to determinism. In the refinement stage, we propose a localized refiner that predicts bounded sub-meter pose residuals from candidate-centered floorplan crops, where structural ambiguities are largely eliminated. Our method effectively balances global multi-hypothesis tracking and local sub-meter refinement without requiring any offline map preprocessing or test-time lookup tables. Comprehensive results on the S3D (full) and ZInD benchmarks demonstrate that our approach achieves state-of-the-art accuracy and robustness.

- **[Simulation-Informed Diffusion for Decentralized Multi-robot Motion Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27697)**  `arXiv:2605.27697`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.LG`  
  _Jinhao Liang, Sven Koenig, Ferdinando Fioretto_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Decentralized multi-robot motion planning requires each robot to generate collision-free trajectories from local observations, without global sensing or reliable communication. However, most existing planners, whether classical or learning-based, generate trajectories from a static snapshot of the local observation, which limits their ability to anticipate the future behavior of neighboring robots. This limitation is critical as the number of robots increases and the environment becomes more cluttered. Motivated by world models that use future state estimation to improve planning decisions, this paper introduces Simulation-Informed Diffusion (SID), a decentralized framework built on constraint-aware diffusion models (CADM) that simulate neighboring robots' future trajectories to guide each robot's own planning. SID first uses CADM to simulate the future trajectories of neighboring robots from their currently observed states, and then uses the same CADM to plan each robot's own trajectory under safety constraints informed by these simulations. Crucially, the accurate simulation of neighbors enables a minimal communication scheme that triggers coordination only when necessary in highly congested scenarios. Experiments across diverse environments show that SID consistently outperforms baseline methods in terms of planning effectiveness and constraint satisfaction, and scales to scenarios with 108 robots and 160 obstacles.
