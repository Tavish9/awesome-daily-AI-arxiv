- **[Beyond the Benchmark: Localizing Released Autonomous Driving Models to Real Vehicles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31814)**  `arXiv:2611.31814`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Morui Zhu, Deyuan Qu, Qi Chen, Qing Yang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Released autonomous-driving models achieve strong benchmark performance, yet deploying them on a new real vehicle remains challenging. We study this deployment gap and find that released models can remain coupled to assumptions from their source sensing, data, and execution processes even after conventional adaptation. We introduce Released-to-Real (R2R), a deployment-localization methodology that identifies such source-system coupling and corrects only the affected components while preserving the released model.   _Zirui Li, Zhiqi Mao, Yangang Ren, Heye Huang, Haohan Yang, Qihang Huang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Learned driving planners keep being updated after deployment as new logs arrive, yet continual learning evaluations rarely ask whether an update improves on the deployed model, and their verdicts rest on unstated evaluation choices. We present ContinualDrive, a deployment-anchored benchmark for post-deployment continual learning of driving planners. It updates a deployed planner on real-world nuPlan logs one scenario-type group at a time, evaluates the same checkpoints in open and closed loop, makes four measurement axes explicit (evaluation loop, reference checkpoint, temporal aggregation and stream protocol), and anchors a three-valued release verdict to the deployed checkpoint. In the core suite of full and adapter updates, no regime measurably improved on the deployed planner in closed loop; on a panel more than ten times larger every evaluated final checkpoint trails it, and joint training on all groups recovers much of the loss without exceeding its nuPlan closed-loop score. We distill these findings into a reporting contract and will release the code and all checkpoints.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Subliminal learning refers to a surprising phenomenon in language models wherein a prompt given to one model (e.g., preference for cats) can be subliminally distilled to another model via training on data that is ostensibly meaningless (e.g., number sequences). This paper shows that text-based subliminal learning transfers cross-modally, in particular, a topic/preference (e.g., animals) transferred through number sequences across vision-language models also changes how images are generated and represented. - **[Denoising Diffusion with Progressive Posterior Sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31558)**  `arXiv:2611.31558`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Zeyu Luo, Shuai Huang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion models provide effective generative priors for solving noisy linear inverse problems. However, existing posterior sampling methods may require singular value decomposition (SVD) of the measurement operator or prior knowledge of the measurement noise variance, while guidance based on local gradients provides only a limited view of posterior geometry. We propose progressive posterior sampling (PPS) with manifold projection, which automatically estimates the measurement noise variance and avoids SVD.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Iterative refinement is a key capability of diffusion-based generation and has motivated growing efforts to bring similar behavior to discrete diffusion and flow models. A growing line of work enables such refinement by allowing previously generated tokens to be revised during sampling, thereby shaping the coupling between successive sampling states. Despite its growing role in sampler design, this coupling freedom remains insufficiently characterized. We introduce Activity Geometry to study these finite-step couplings through expected transition cost, which we call activity.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion models train one network across all noise levels, although their training objective is pointwise in the noise level. If training on each pair of adjacent levels were an independent problem, separate networks with the same total parameter count would be expected to match one shared network. But in practice, on images, sharing weights across levels gives better results, which hints at an implicit dependence on their order. An order fixes both which levels are adjacent and the direction in which they run. In this paper, we show that, of the two, the benefit of sharing rests on adjacency in the noise-level embedding.
