  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion-based vision-language-action (VLA) models cascade pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) with diffusion action experts, but typically restrict the VLM to providing static conditioning. We introduce JADE, a Joint Action DEnoiser that enables the VLM and action expert to jointly refine actions. Our key insight is that structured action latents allow the VLM to read the current noisy action together with visual observations and language instructions.   _Jiyue Tao, Shunheng Xin, Tongsheng Shen, Dexin Zhao, Feitian Zhang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Decentralized multi-robot coordination under partial observability remains challenging, especially when agents act solely on local sensing without communication. Privileged policy distillation provides a promising approach by transferring knowledge from a globally informed oracle to sensor-constrained students. However, in multi-agent systems, the same local observation may correspond to multiple global configurations requiring qualitatively different cooperative actions, making the conditional action distribution inherently multi-modal. Standard deterministic distillation collapses these modes to their mean, often yielding invalid or hesitant actions. - **[FewStepRL: Fine-tuning Few-step Diffusion Models with Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08240)**  `arXiv:2611.08240`  `cs.LG` `cs.CV`  
  _Haitao Lin, Minsi Ren, Yang Li, Xingtong Ge, Zheyuan Hu, Peiyan Hu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Reinforcement learning (RL) has become a standard post-training method for diffusion models, but most RL objectives roll out the current policy at every iteration, and with a many-step sampler these rollouts typically dominate the cost of training. Few-step models cut this cost, yet they model an average velocity over an interval, whereas existing RL objectives are built for an instantaneous velocity with a many-step sampler. We adapt RL objectives to few-step models systematically in three phases, namely a transformation, a decomposition and an adaptation. The transformation to $x$-space expresses each objective we consider through a common policy prediction, at the clean end by default and at an explicitly marked level for transition or flow-map objectives, so that objectives stated over densities, velocities or rewards become comparable. The decomposition writes the gradient of each of these objectives as the sum of a self-regression toward a reference and a reward augmentation, so that a method reduces, at the gradient level, to a choice of anchors and weights. The adaptation computes each anchor from a few-step model at the end of its transport or along its tangent, with the instantaneous velocity recovered from the network itself or from an auxiliary network. This yields few-step versions of FlowGRPO, AWM, DiffusionNFT, DiffusionOPSD and ReFL in one modular codebase. In our experiments, reward-gradient-based methods are the most effective when the reward is differentiable and its gradient is affordable; otherwise DiffusionNFT-style methods generally reach high rewards in relatively few iterations despite a sometimes higher per-iteration cost. Extra inference steps show no systematic aggregate benefit in our experiments, although selected outputs visibly improve with two to four times as many sampling steps as in training.

- **[FILO: A Unified Fisher-Geometric Framework for Low-Rank Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08734)**  `arXiv:2605.08734`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `cs.CL`  
  _Ziyun Liu, Fengmiao Bian, Jian-Feng Cai_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) reparameterizes a weight matrix $W$ as a product of two low-rank factors, but the Jacobian $J_G$ of the generator mapping the factors to the weight matrix is rank-deficient, so the factor-space preconditioner $J_G^* F_t J_G$ induced by any $W$-space preconditioner $F_t$ is singular, and consequently the standard chain rule cannot be uniquely inverted to map a preconditioned $W$-space direction back to a factor-space update. Therefore, we introduce FILO, a unified Fisher-geometric framework for low-rank adaptation that enables factor updates without directly inverting this singular operator and casts existing LoRA optimizers by their factor-space inversion strategy and $W$-space preconditioner $F_t$. Existing methods occupy four families along these axes: factor-space adaptive updates, block-diagonal surrogates for $J_G^* J_G$, Frobenius-residual pseudoinverse methods, and Riemannian manifold methods. Within this design space, a gradient-statistics-aware $F_t$ paired with a closed-form factor-space solve at $O((m+n)r)$ memory remains underexplored. We instantiate FILO as FILO-Adafactor by adopting the Adafactor diagonal Kronecker preconditioner $H_t$ on $W$ and selecting from the resulting factor-space solution family the element minimizing an $H_t$-weighted imbalance between the two factor contributions. By construction, the induced weight update is the closest LoRA approximation to the preconditioned $W$-space direction under the $H_t$-weighted norm. Across GPT-2 on E2E, Mistral-7B and Qwen2-7B on GLUE, ARC, and GSM8K, and diffusion-model personalization, FILO-Adafactor and its momentum variant are competitive with or improve over a representative set of LoRA optimizers while keeping peak GPU memory at the LoRA optimizer level.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>

To realize the block-level formulation, we construct a prefix-block state and use a single model evaluation to define a factorized one-step surrogate for the block likelihood.
