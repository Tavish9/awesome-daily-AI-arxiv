- **[TanCRAFT: Context-Reasoned Anticipatory Feedback for Assembly Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14470)**  `arXiv:2611.14470`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Sassan Mokhtar, Lars Doorenbos, Marius Bock, Sina Mokhtar, Katharina Beckh, Dominik Bach, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Preventing human error in physical assembly tasks requires assistance systems that can intervene before mistakes are made. This is especially challenging in settings where users are free to choose the construction order and correctness depends on the evolving assembly rather than a fixed next step. We present TanCRAFT, a four-stage real-time predictive assistance system that tracks the scene state, reasons about the current partial assembly, forecasts the near-future motion of manipulated pieces, and intervenes when the predicted placement is incompatible with the target. We instantiate our framework and confirm its effectiveness in a controlled tangram assembly task through offline benchmarks and a within-subject user study. Our findings show that the predictive feedback significantly reduces mistakes relative to unguided and reactive sessions, while yielding the lowest mean perceived workload and shortest mean completion time. Overall, these results demonstrate the value of predictive feedback for error prevention and provide a step toward cost-aware assistance for automation and human-robot workcells.

- **[ForeseeDrive: Future-Token Conditioning for Anticipatory Generative Driving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14286)**  `arXiv:2611.14286`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Shaokai Wu, Xunchu Zhou, Pinzhi Wang, Bo Dong, Yuting Hu, Yanbiao Ji, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Generative driving planners can represent diverse ego futures, but a downstream scorer can choose only among sampled candidates; it cannot recover an absent maneuver. We present ForeseeDrive, which turns future scene evolution into a deployable interface for shaping candidate generation. Rather than reconstructing dense future states or scoring a fixed menu, ForeseeDrive derives query-indexed tokens from logged actor motion, occupancy evolution, route interactions, and TTC margins, and predicts the same token space from present context at deployment. The tokens condition trajectory geometry before candidates materialize. A zero-token-gated residual path and candidate-preservation loss limit unnecessary drift, while a TTC-margin guard targets brittle safe-to-unsafe boundary crossings. Under their respective benchmark protocols, ForeseeDrive reaches 89.2 PDMS on NAVSIM navtest, 0.41\,m average L2 and 0.05\% collision rate on nuScenes validation, and a 76.50 driving score on Bench2Drive. Controlled DiffusionDrive/NAVSIM diagnostics show an exact zero-token no-op, a small aligned-over-shuffled selected-PDM advantage under preservation, mixed oracle-best quality, and localized TTC-boundary failures. Together, the benchmark results and bounded mechanism tests support future-conditioned generation as a practical interface for anticipation. Code is included in the supplementary materials.

- **[Elastic-WAM: Adaptive Spatiotemporal Imagination for Efficient Robot Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14257)**  `arXiv:2611.14257`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Haitian Li, Tianfu Li, Xuxi Zhao, Zizheng Liu, Yangcheng Yu, Yiwen Hou, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  World-Action Models improve robot control by predicting future visual dynamics, but iterative video denoising incurs substantial inference overhead. Recent approaches reduce this cost through partial future denoising, yet typically rely on fixed refinement budgets. Our empirical observations reveal that preferred refinement depths vary across tasks and states, while different future regions exhibit varying relevance to action generation. Motivated by these findings, we propose Elastic-WAM, which adaptively allocates world refinement computation across temporal denoising time and spatial future space. - **[From Depth to Safe Depth: Uncertainty-Aware Monocular Perception for Mobile Robot Navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13769)**  `arXiv:2611.13769`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Saeid Firouzi Daghigh, Saeed Ayat_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Monocular depth foundation models make it tempting to replace depth sensors on mobile robots with a single RGB camera. We show that, used off the shelf, this is unsafe: in a simulated indoor navigation benchmark, a standard Nav2 stack driven by Depth Anything V2 (metric) collides in 63\% of 120 episodes, because the model systematically over-estimates the distance to nearby obstacles. We propose a lightweight plug-in module that turns predicted depth into \emph{safe depth}. A 0.58\,M-parameter head on top of the \emph{frozen} depth model predicts, per pixel, a log-depth correction $\mu$ and an uncertainty $\sigma$, trained with a $\beta$-weighted Gaussian negative log-likelihood. The navigation stack consumes the one-sided bound $\hat d_{\text{safe}}=d\,e^{\mu-k\sigma}$, where $k$ is set by conformal calibration on a held-out world, and uncertainty also decides which pixels may be treated as floor. The correction halves the depth error (AbsRel $0.262\rightarrow0.134$) and reduces near-range over-estimation from 59.8\% to 12.0\%. In navigation, safe depth raises the success rate from 37\% to 70\% and lowers collisions from 63\% to 3\%, close to the 2\% of ground-truth depth. A constant safety margin calibrated to the same pixel coverage reaches only 30\% success because it freezes the robot; uncertainty-aware safety wins 52 of 56 discordant paired episodes against it. Gains hold on glass, texture-less walls and dark regions.

Our main contribution though is deriving and implementing a novel discrete variational inference framework, achieving our highest prediction accuracy in low-data settings and outperforming all classical IRT and matrix factorisation baselines.
