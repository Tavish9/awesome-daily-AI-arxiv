- **[SCALE: Real-Time Latent Communication for Cyber-Physical Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08390)**  `arXiv:2611.08390`  `cs.RO` `cs.MA`  
  _Shanghao Shi, Jian Xiao, Ching-Hsiang Chan, Sanjoy Baruah, Sastry Kompella, Yevgeniy Vorobeychik, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Latent communication enables agents to collaborate efficiently by directly exchanging intermediate model representations rather than raw sensor data or text messages. Existing approaches, however, are primarily designed for LLM agents and do not consider the bandwidth constraints and heterogeneous task deadlines faced by cyber-physical agents. In this work, we propose SCALE, a real-time latent communication method tailored for cyber-physical agents. Motivated by the spatio-temporal continuity of the physical world, SCALE matches tokens across consecutive latent messages using similarity scores to identify reusable information and avoid redundant transmission. It then profiles the semantic gain and communication cost of transmitting different amounts of latent information. Based on these profiles, SCALE employs a real-time scheduler that jointly determines which pending messages to transmit and how many latent tokens to send, subject to available bandwidth and task deadlines. We evaluate SCALE across three cyber-physical benchmarks and a physical spider robot case study under diverse network conditions and heterogeneous task deadlines. Our results show that SCALE significantly improves both task performance and on-time delivery in bandwidth-constrained networks. Our code is available at:this https URL.

- **[H-TRIP: Exact Many-to-Many Distance Index for Multi-Robot Exploration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08139)**  `arXiv:2611.08139`  `cs.RO`  
  _Elif A. Özaslan, Tolga Özaslan_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Frontier-based exploration systems repeatedly evaluate geodesic travel costs between robot poses and candidate viewpoints, and tour-based planners consume the full pairwise distance matrix built from them. Recomputing these matrices with one graph search per source scales with the explored map size, whereas distance caches that refresh only locally affected or newly created frontiers can retain stale costs between surviving pairs when newly discovered free space creates a global shortcut. We present H-TRIP (Hierarchical Tropical Rank-one Incremental Propagation), an exact distance index grounded in Kleene algebra over the min-plus semiring for monotonically growing occupancy grids. H-TRIP partitions the known map into a spatial hierarchy with fixed boundary ports. We prove that sequential insertions preserve exact shortest-path distances on the grid. Benchmark experiments confirm exact agreement with reference searches on fully built maps and show that dense many-to-many queries at a few dozen viewpoints are up to two orders of magnitude faster than sequential k x BFS. On exploration runs with sixteen robots, in-loop queries on the growing occupancy graph are 2.21x-8.59x faster than co-scheduled k x BFS on maps with side 256, 512, and 1024 cells.
