- **[Adaptive Coverage Algorithms for Mobile Ad Hoc Networks in Dynamic Worlds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41936)**  `arXiv:2610.41936`  `cs.RO` `cs.MA`  
  _Hugo Gandhi, Edwin Meriaux, Antonio Loría_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  This paper extends an existing framework for the coordination of mobile agents in unknown worlds, by considering the possibility of dynamic instability. In the original problem, known as the Partially Observable Cooperative Guard Art Gallery Problem (POCGAGP) (arXiv:2512.09495), the multi-agent system must establish a communication network which completely covers an initially unknown constrained space. However, the POCGAGP fails to account for the unpredictability often seen in real-world deployment. To address this, the dynamic case (DPOCGAGP) introduces two settings which can influence the mobile network during deployment: Agent Loss and World Change, which represent dynamic changes to the world. We then compare their performance against another state-of-the-art benchmark algorithm, and show that the adapted algorithms outperform across our benchmark test set, which comprises over 4500 test cases of varied size and structure.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision--Language--Action (VLA) models generate robot actions from visual observations and language instructions, but their understanding of physical dynamics remains limited. Video world models can capture scene dynamics, yet generating future videos introduces high latency into the control loop. We introduce PDRVLA, whose lightweight Physical Dynamics Module extracts physical tokens from vision--language features to condition action generation without online video generation. - **[When the Simulator Fails, Not the Policy: Auditing Contact Artifacts in Simulated Robot Benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41857)**  `arXiv:2610.41857`  `cs.RO`  
  _Alex Zhou, Junhao Wu, Xueyang Zhou, Yixiao Huo, Hongcheng Gao, Di Zhu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Simulation benchmarks score robot policies by success rate, and every failed episode counts against the policy. Many of these failures are the simulator's fault, not the policy's; we find the cause and fix it. Testing a vision language action model and a world action model in simulation, we find that the policy often places the object and the simulator then ejects it, and that the benchmark's own scripted demonstrations fail the same way. We present SimuGuard, an auditing plugin for LIBERO, RoboTwin, RoboCasa and RoboDojo. It leaves the evaluation unchanged. At every physics step, it records the state and flags an object that starts moving at a contact faster than the robot's contact or gravity account for. It replays a recorded episode bit for bit, and it reruns the episode with one simulation parameter changed. With it we trace these episodes on RoboTwin to the collision detection of the physics engine: for geometry one to two centimeters apart, the engine reports a penetration of about two centimeters, and the solver throws the object out at several meters per second. The cause is one constant: the distance algorithm discards triangles below a fixed area threshold, which valid triangles fall below at the centimeter scale. With that constant changed and nothing else, episodes with an artifact fall from 12 in 100 to none, and the success rate on five container tasks rises from 89% to 98%. Success rates in simulation thus count failures that the simulator causes. Benchmarks on PhysX 5.3.1 or earlier should rerun in a repaired engine. Where the engine cannot change, they should report the success rate together with the range it spans when every flagged episode fails or passes: 75.8% to 98.5% around a reported 83.1% for LingBot-VA.
