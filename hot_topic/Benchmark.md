- **[Compressing Onboard Language Models for Space Robots under a Behavioral-Fidelity Requirement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29302)**  `arXiv:2610.29302`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Jia Deng, Zizhe Wang, Xin Lu, Yi Huang, Zehao Jia_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Language models are being explored as the interface through which space robots interpret operator commands and select among verified skills. Running them onboard calls for low-bit compression, and flight use adds requirements that generic post-training quantization leaves implicit: a fixed memory budget and storage format, a static artifact under configuration control, and behavior close to the full-precision model validated on the ground. We treat compression as a ground-segment step and study QSelect, which composes one static low-bit model from compatible AWQ, GPTQ, and AutoRound checkpoints by assigning each linear module to one quantizer family and scoring every substitution at the final next-token distribution. The default fidelity mode needs no labels or tuned thresholds and accepts a substitution only when the forward KL divergence from the BF16 reference decreases; an optional target-adapted mode adds answer likelihood on a small labeled set. On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Llama-3.2-3B at 3 and 4 bits, composed models reach lower mean held-out perplexity than every single-family model in all six settings, and the fidelity mode lowers perplexity and KL further. In a matched scaffold, final-output scoring gives lower held-out KL than representation-similarity scoring in all 12 paired runs. With labels from training or development splits of the evaluated task families, the target-adapted mode raises 3-bit pooled accuracy on the two Llama models by 4.3 and 4.5 points over the best single-family model. At 3 bits, unquantized embedding and output layers make up 43-46% of the packed artifact. All evaluations use general-domain benchmarks on a ground GPU; space-domain tasks and flight hardware are left to future work.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We introduce WorldOracle, a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate action-conditioned world models (ACWMs) against paired real-world executions. Given the same initial observation and low-level action sequence, WorldOracle asks whether a predicted rollout reproduces what happens in the real world, including physical consequences and task-state transitions. We evaluate models along four complementary dimensions: physical state correctness, interaction spatial alignment, key interaction area fidelity, and multi-view action coherence. To support this evaluation, we curate real-world training and evaluation suites that cover different interaction dynamics and embodiments. Experiments on seven representative ACWMs reveal distinct model strengths and failure modes. WorldOracle provides a systematic foundation for measuring and advancing action-conditioned world modeling for manipulation. We release the datasets, evaluation code, and leaderboard:this https URL.

- **[ASPIRE: Agentic /Skills Discovery for Robotics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00272)**  `arXiv:2607.00272`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.MA`  
  _Runyu Lu, Yubo Wu, Ethan Kou, Haotian Lin, Letian Fu, Wenli Xiao, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Traditional robot programming is notoriously challenging: it requires orchestrating multimodal perception, managing physical contact dynamics, and handling diverse environment configurations and execution failures. We introduce ASPIRE (Agentic Skill Programming through Iterative Robot Exploration), a continual learning system that autonomously writes and refines robot control programs in a code-as-policy paradigm while compounding experience into a reusable skill library. ASPIRE operates in an open-ended learning loop, consisting of three components: (1) a closed-loop robot execution engine that exposes fine-grained multimodal traces, enabling the agent to autonomously diagnose failures, synthesize repairs, and validate outcomes; (2) a continually expanding skill library that distills validated fixes into reusable, transferable robotic knowledge; and (3) an evolutionary search procedure that generates diverse programs, systematically debugging them to explore beyond single-trajectory refinement. The accumulated skill library further enables zero-shot generalization: on representative unseen long-horizon tasks, ASPIRE achieves 31% success, outperforming the 4% success rate of prior methods. Performance scales with the capabilities of frontier models. With Claude Opus 5.5, ASPIRE nearly saturates these benchmarks, achieving 99% average success across all 80 LIBERO-Pro tasks and 100% success on representative Robosuite and BEHAVIOR-1K tasks. Beyond simulation, skills discovered in simulation reduce real-world robot programming effort across embodiments and robot APIs, while skills acquired through online interaction with physical robots enable ASPIRE to solve a Rubik's Cube with progressively improving efficiency.

- **[Asymmetric On-Policy Distillation Learns In-Context Exploration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30105)**  `arXiv:2610.30105`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.LG`  
  _Sriyash Poddar, Yanda Bao, Jacob Krantz, Matthew Chang, Xavier Puig, Roozbeh Mottaghi, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Sequential decision-making under partial observability is difficult because agents must explore to infer hidden, task-relevant information before acting effectively. Learning such information-gathering behaviors tabula rasa via online reinforcement learning is sample-inefficient, whereas offline imitation averages expert actions across hidden states and fails to learn exploration. In this work, we propose ASTEROID, a simple and effective student--teacher supervised learning framework that can learn test-time exploration. Although a privileged expert never needs to explore, we demonstrate that a history-conditioned student can learn to do so when trained with on-policy contexts: the student first collects on-policy context under partial observability, and the privileged expert provides action labels conditioned on that context. By distilling expert actions directly conditioned on the student's on-policy history, the student learns to systematically resolve partial observability, i.e., search while uncertain and exploit once the hidden state is revealed. This enables efficient test-time exploration (similar to Bayesian posterior sampling) without incurring the impractical training-data costs of reinforcement learning. Across a diverse range of eleven different environments, including discrete exploration benchmarks, robotic manipulation, navigation, and Procgen environments, ASTEROID achieves ~2x higher returns under fixed exploration budgets, uses ~100x fewer interactions than RL baselines, and outperforms frontier models at exploration. Finally, our method transfers policies trained in simulation to vision-denied real-world robot manipulation, exhibiting efficient exploration and increasing success by +40% over standard distillation. Overall, it provides a simple yet surprisingly effective framework to learn efficient test-time exploration.
  </details>

This paper proposes Linear-Network (Li-Net), a novel architecture designed for multi-channel time series forecasting that captures the linear and non-linear dependencies among channels. Li-Net dynamically compresses representations across sequence and channel dimensions, processes the information through a configurable non-linear module and subsequently reconstructs the forecasts. Moreover, Li-Net integrates a sparse Top-K Softmax attention mechanism within a multi-scale projection framework to address these challenges. A core innovation is its ability to seamlessly incorporate and fuse multi-modal embeddings, guiding the sparse attention process to focus on the most informative time steps and feature channels. Through the experiment results on multiple real-world benchmark datasets demonstrate that Li-Net achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art baseline methods. Furthermore, Li-Net provides a superior balance between prediction accuracy and computational burden, exhibiting significantly lower memory usage and faster inference times.
