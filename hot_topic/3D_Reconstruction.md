- **[A Probabilistic, Rotation-Preserving Kernel Operator Surrogate for Continuum-Robot Design Spaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26605)**  `arXiv:2610.26605`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Branden Frieden, Alan Kuntz, Varun Shankar_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We develop an uncertainty-aware, operator-learning-based surrogate for tendon-driven continuum robots (TDCRs) that generalizes across robot design and actuation while predicting the complete equilibrium configuration. Our central idea is a nonstationary Gaussian-process (GP) kernel whose metric is learned by a shallow neural network from mechanics-aware design coordinates. The network learns how similarity changes across design space, while the resulting kernel defines the design-to-pose operator, its posterior uncertainty, and a criterion for sequential simulator acquisition. We represent backbone position and rotational strain with spatial kernels and reconstruct orientation directly on SO(3), preserving valid material frames and tendon geometry. We use ASkotch with Nystrom preconditioning for scalable kernel operator solves and a separate Nystrom approximation for posterior covariance. Experiments show that kernel-coverage acquisition reduces mean trajectory error by approximately 15% relative to IID sampling, while posterior variance increases for increasingly unsupported designs. Compared with prior neural-operator TDCR models, the method is 30-50% more accurate using approximately 5x fewer training designs while additionally providing posterior uncertainty estimates.

- **[Vision-Language-Delta-Action: Learning Compact Deltas as Actionable Transition Interfaces for Robotic Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25874)**  `arXiv:2610.25874`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Lingsen Zhang, Wei Li, Jie He, Henghua Zhang, Kai Mo, Shiyong Li, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robotic manipulation is not merely observation-to-action generation, but the realization of task-specified state changes in the physical world. Existing vision-language-action (VLA) methods directly learn observation-to-action mappings, imposing a substantial learning burden due to the gap between semantic understanding and action execution. Although world-action models (WAMs) improve control through future prediction, modeling complete future states wastes capacity on static appearance and action-irrelevant changes. We propose Vision-Language-Delta-Action (VLDA), a paradigm that reformulates full future prediction as compact actionable transition modeling. Its core is a compact Delta variable that captures latent world-state changes, serving as an interface between vision-language understanding and action generation. VLDA learns Delta through a unified objective composed of the Local Transition Filter (LT-Filter) and Global Transition Anchor (GT-Anchor). LT-Filter captures task-relevant manipulation-centric changes while suppressing static reconstruction biases. GT-Anchor provides coarse future-level regularization to preserve the overall direction of state changes. Delta-Driven Action Refinement (DDAR) further grounds Delta in the action manifold and injects it as a structured seed to guide action denoising. We implement VLDA within LoongForge, an open-source training framework for large-scale foundation and embodied models, using its modular and distributed infrastructure for unified two-stage optimization. Experiments show that VLDA improves long-horizon consistency, robustness, and generalization, validating actionable Delta as an alternative to direct action prediction and full future-state modeling.

- **[SpaRV: Sparse Radiance Voxels for Scalable Geometrically Consistent LiDAR-Visual Mapping and Rendering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25665)**  `arXiv:2610.25665`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Jianheng Liu, Yunfei Wan, Fangming Cheng, Xiangyu Hong, Zuhao Zou, Haotian Li, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robotic simulation, navigation, and inspection need photorealistic and geometrically accurate maps. Radiance fields achieve photorealism, but building them from robot data raises three challenges. A robot's trajectory serves its task, not reconstruction, so images from few viewpoints leave the geometry under-constrained. Residual errors in camera poses and intrinsics blur the map. The map's GPU memory grows with scene size, which limits its level of detail in large scenes. We present \textbf{SpaRV}, a \textbf{Spa}rse \textbf{R}adiance \textbf{V}oxel map rendered by differentiable ray marching, which lets irregular LiDAR scans supervise it alongside images. LiDAR depth and occupancy anchor the under-constrained geometry, and normal and smoothness regularizers keep it smooth between LiDAR points. To correct camera errors, SpaRV alternates map training with photometric optimization of the camera poses and focal lengths. To bound GPU memory in training and rendering, SpaRV holds one submap at a time at full resolution and the rest at coarser octree levels. Experiments show that SpaRV keeps the geometry consistent away from the captured trajectory and sharpens textures by optimizing the camera parameters. On one 24\,GB GPU, SpaRV reconstructs a university campus captured in 63{,}582 images and simulates other cameras and LiDARs. Our code will be released at \url{this https URL}.

- **[Localization and Neural Splatting on the Edge: An FPGA Scene Memory for Robots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25068)**  `arXiv:2610.25068`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Que Haohua, Mingkai Liu, Handong Yao_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>

Scene-coordinate regression (SCR) and 3D Gaussian splatting (3DGS) fit because they avoid an SfM stage, but ACEsplat-style pipelines are GPU-centered, and moving them to an edge FPGA is hard because SCR is pose-sensitive and 3DGS rendering is irregular and capacity-sensitive. LENS wins performance per watt over a Jetson Orin NX on every deployment task, by 1.9x to 5.7x at about a quarter of the power, and is faster in absolute latency on localization and Wayspots rendering. On-device training reaches ACEsplat-level quality but not the GPU's time or energy, marking the remaining boundary for a future training accelerator.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
