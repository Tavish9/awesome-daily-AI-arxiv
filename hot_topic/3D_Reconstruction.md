- **[RangeNav: Task-Aligned LiDAR Range-Image Pretraining for Robot Navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29845)**  `arXiv:2610.29845`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Samuel Foo Enze, Manthan Patel, Fan Yang, Linus Kramer, Pascal Roth, Marco Hutter, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Autonomous mobile robots operating in dense crowds and cluttered sites require $360^\circ$ perception to react to surrounding hazards. While panoramic LiDAR offers full-surround coverage, learning representations within reinforcement learning (RL) is sample-inefficient under sparse rewards, and existing 3D pretrained point-cloud encoders are too memory-heavy for massively parallel RL simulation. - **[Reconstruction-Augmented Visuomotor Pre-Training of 3D-Grounded Bimanual Dexterity from RGB-Only Human Videos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29716)**  `arXiv:2610.29716`  `cs.RO`  
  _Xingyi He, Zhiwen Qiu, Adhitya Polavaram, Yunhao Cao, Cory Fan, Julius Samwer, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We present SpatialDex, a framework that learns 3D-grounded bimanual dexterity from RGB-only human videos through reconstruction-augmented visuomotor pre-training. The central idea is to learn human behavior together with its spatial context. From each video, we reconstruct the scene geometry and estimate the hand motion, align them at a shared video-level scale using the hand-size prior of 3D hand estimates.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  In implicit neural representations (INRs), 3D signals are encoded directly in network weights, making stable weight-space operations essential. However, permutation symmetry causes independently trained yet functionally equivalent models to appear distant in weight space, often inducing large loss barriers under linear interpolation. Existing neuron alignment methods either commit early to discrete matches and become trapped in inconsistent local optima or rely on computationally expensive gradient optimization over continuous relaxations, both of which are unreliable in challenging cases such as NeRF. We adopt progressive alignment, a layer-wise strategy that follows inter-layer dependencies from input to output.
