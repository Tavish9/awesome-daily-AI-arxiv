- **[Feasibility Ceilings Before Learning: Sizing and Timing Bounds for Drone-Mesh Interception](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20823)**  `arXiv:2610.20823`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG` `cs.MA`  
  _Dmytro Babarytskyi_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  A mesh of interceptors defending a fixed site must spread over the predicted arrival point of an approaching object. This is a recurring task in multi-agent robotics: N agents, each covering a disc of radius r, spread over a probability density so that a point drawn from it falls within some agent's disc. We give closed forms for the quantities that bound performance before any policy is chosen, and validate each by Monte Carlo. The uncertainty of a point predicted by extrapolating a noisy linear track follows from least-squares variance propagation plus a delta-method term for the uncertain arrival time, agreeing with simulation to within 1.8%. The coverage probability of a packed mesh admits a closed form accurate to 0.007 absolute, with a volumetric analogue. A naive area ratio misses a ceiling: while agents stay at least 2r apart, no mesh exceeds 90.69% coverage in the plane or 74.05% in a volume. This ceiling belongs to the separation constraint, not to the problem - permitting overlap substitutes Kershner's thinnest-covering density and removes it, the better regime above a crossover at p=0.80. Hexagonal packing is optimal among five arrangements; the centroidal-Voronoi configuration coverage control supplies reaches only half its coverage at small budgets. The Gaussian assumption is conservative under heavy tails and anisotropy but fragile under bias: a one-sigma aim-point offset costs 14 points of coverage. A repositioning team must also choose a commit time, and the admissible window can be empty. Against the applicable ceiling, analytic placement attains 85% of what the configuration permits while centralised PPO averages 15% over three trainer seeds, with a paired bootstrap against a null policy containing zero at every seed. We argue such ceilings should be computed before any learning experiment and quoted alongside every result.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Distilling vision-language features into 3D Gaussian Splatting yields a language map that can be queried with arbitrary text. In deployment, however, a small set of user-specified targets must be reliably located, which current distilled maps often miss, while open-vocabulary queryability for other words must remain intact. We propose a training-free, target-aware refinement of an already-distilled language map. Guided by a text-promptable segmenter, the visual embedding inside each detected target region is blended with the target text embedding, a convex combination whose coefficient sets the amount of injected prior, while non-target regions are left untouched. Generative policies such as diffusion and flow matching naturally capture such multimodal behaviors, yet which mode is executed is left to random sampling and cannot be actively controlled. We first reveal, both empirically and theoretically, that deterministic flow transport induces mode-dependent structure in the source space. - **[CF-Splat: Closed-Form Triangle Splatting with Mesh Priors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22108)**  `arXiv:2610.22108`  `cs.RO`  
  _Yixin Fang, Liang Zhao_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Splatting-based radiance-field representations have recently gained popularity in robotic perception, enabling high-quality novel-view synthesis (NVS) through explicit scene primitives. However, existing splatting methods typically rely on iterative nonlinear optimization that jointly optimizes millions of coupled primitive parameters, resulting in substantial computation time, peak GPU memory consumption, and susceptibility to local minima. In this paper, we propose CF-Splat, a novel closed-form triangle-splatting framework for efficient scene appearance estimation over a known mesh either pre-existing or provided by upstream mapping or reconstruction pipelines. Our key insight is that, by properly formulating the problem over the given mesh geometry and calibrated cameras, the rendered pixel colors can be expressed linearly with respect to the per-vertex spherical harmonics (SH) coefficients, allowing multi-view scene appearance estimation to be formulated as a global linear least-squares (LLS) problem without the need of iterative nonlinear optimization. To ensure the scalability to large-scale scenes, we develop a triangle-structured matrix-free preconditioned conjugate-gradient solver that avoids explicitly constructing the large sparse normal system while producing the same LLS solution. Experiments on diverse real-world datasets demonstrate that CF-Splat achieves competitive or superior NVS quality compared with state-of-the-art splatting methods. While, CF-Splat requires approximately 27.5-378.0 times less computation time and 1.8 times lower peak GPU memory on average than 3DGS across scenes of different scales on a consumer-grade laptop.

- **[Language models recognize dropout and Gaussian noise applied to their activations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17465)**  `arXiv:2604.17465`  `cs.AI`  
  _Damiano Fornasiere, Mirko Bronzi, Spencer Kitts, Alessandro Palmas, Yoshua Bengio, Oliver Richardson_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We provide evidence that language models can detect, localize and, to a degree, verbalize the difference between a-semantic perturbations applied to their activations. More precisely, we either (a) apply dropout or (b) add Gaussian noise to them, at a target sentence. We then ask a multiple-choice question such as "Which of the previous sentences was perturbed?" or "Which perturbation was applied?". Interestingly, Qwen3-32B and Qwen3.8-27B learn worse when supervised with wrong labels, again suggesting a prior towards the correct ones. Because dropout is used as a training-regularization technique, while Gaussian noise is sometimes added during inference, we discuss the possibility of a data-agnostic "training awareness" signal and the implications for AI safety.
