  _Can Wang, Qiyuan Qiao, Lei Liu, Ge Yuan, Xinhui Liu, Dong Xu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Learning robust manipulation policies for diverse everyday objects remains challenging because objects with different materials exhibit distinct deformation and contact dynamics. While diffusion policies provide a powerful framework for modeling multimodal manipulation behaviors, they typically lack explicit physical reasoning and may produce averaged actions when facing heterogeneous objects. We present a physics-aware diffusion policy that augments visual imitation learning with object physical understanding. These results show that incorporating physical knowledge is critical for scalable robot manipulation in diverse environments.

- **[The Smallest Singular Directions of the Unembedding Matter Disproportionately for Next-Token Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17084)**  `arXiv:2605.17084`  `cs.LG` `cs.CL`  
  _Weilun Xu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Interpreting, editing and compressing language models often involve assessing which parts of their internal representations matter for prediction. Many of these assessments rely on distance, such as reconstruction error or the length of an intervention. For next-token prediction, however, the logit differences that matter most are those among the few most plausible tokens. These differences can be small. We therefore test whether changes to the final hidden state that move the logits less also change the prediction less. Specifically, changes along the smallest singular directions of the unembedding, which maps this state to the logits, move the logits less than random changes of the same length. In open-weight language models across several families, their prediction change and their change in the next-token loss are nevertheless on average several times those of random changes. Across all singular directions, the prediction change is out of proportion to the logit change only at the smallest. These directions carry a large share of the difference between the model's leading candidate tokens. Consequently, distance-based methods undervalue these directions, and rankings by singular value place them last, although they matter disproportionately for prediction. More broadly, our study highlights that comparing internal representations by distance can miss differences that change what the model predicts.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Explicit reasoning in large language models (LLMs) is often associated with reliability, yet it can still pose risks when a model infers harmful goals under a benign context. We identify a distinct challenge: ensuring the safety of the \emph{multi-step reasoning process} itself, where locally coherent steps may still lead to globally harmful outcomes. To explore this vulnerability, we propose PDRA (Paradox-driven Reasoning-based Attack), a novel attack method that programs a harmful reasoning trajectory. PDRA first constructs a paradoxical narrative core by pairing a malicious actor (e.g., a terrorist) with a benign task (e.g., writing a safety bulletin). It then embeds this core into a structured analytical framework that forces the model through a mandatory three-stage analysis -- surface purpose, latent intent, and operational reconstruction -- guiding it to produce detailed harmful content while maintaining narrative coherence. Mechanistically, we show PDRA steers the model's internal representations away from refusal-related patterns, suppressing safety-triggering lexicons while activating planning-oriented terms. Empirically, PDRA achieves state-of-the-art attack success rates across diverse LLMs with high efficiency. \textcolor{red}{\textbf{Warning:} This paper may contain sensitive content.}

- **[Qrita: High-performance Top-k and Top-p Algorithm for GPUs using Pivot-based Truncation and Selection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01518)**  `arXiv:2602.01518`  `cs.AI`  
  _Jongseok Park, Sunga Kim, Alvin Cheung, Ion Stoica_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Despite their importance in model sampling, efficient implementation of Top-k and Top-p algorithms poses a significant challenge as model vocabularies grow with every new model release. Existing approaches often rely on sorting, which incurs significant computation and memory overhead on GPUs, or on stochastic approaches that alter the algorithm's output. In this work, we propose Qrita, an efficient Top-k and Top-p algorithm based on a pivot-based truncation and selection. Qrita leverages pivot-based search for both Top-k and Top-p with two key techniques: 1. Gaussian-based $\sigma$-truncation, which greatly reduces the search space of the vocabulary, and 2. Quaternary pivot search with duplication handling, which halves the number of pivot search iterations and guarantees deterministic output. We implement Qrita using Triton and evaluate its performance against the Top-k and Top-p kernels of high-performance LLM execution engines such as SGLang and FlashInfer, improving end-to-end serving throughput up to 1.4$\times$ with half the memory usage, while providing the same output of stable sort-based Top-k and Top-p algorithms. Qrita is now the default Triton Top-k and Top-p sampler of vLLM, and its implementation is available atthis https URL.
