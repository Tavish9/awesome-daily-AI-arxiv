- **[Online Learning of Heterogeneous Locomotion Dynamics under Reinforcement Learning Policies for Precise Loco-Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41892)**  `arXiv:2610.41892`  `cs.RO`  
  _Tianlin Zhang, Weipeng Xia, Lingwei Zhang, Yiyang Shao, Zhongyu Li, Yun-Hui Liu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  How to reuse a pretrained locomotion policy for precise loco-manipulation is still an open problem. We propose an online method to learn the closed-loop dynamics under a frozen locomotion policy. The learned model predicts floating-base motion and improves loco-manipulation accuracy. However, unobserved latent conditions of the policy introduce heterogeneous responses, meaning that the same observed state will lead to different motions. Direct learning without accounting for this heterogeneity introduces bias and fluctuations into dynamics estimates, degrading floating-base prediction and end-effector tracking. To address this problem, a contaminated Gaussian model is proposed to approximate the effects of latent conditions. The model uses two Gaussian components to approximate predictable and unpredictable responses under the same observed state. Then, the contaminated Gaussian model is combined with a Gaussian process to construct an online learner using random Fourier features (RFFs). The learner estimates each response's probability of belonging to the predictable component and uses it to weight online updates, capturing predictable responses while reducing bias and fluctuations caused by heterogeneous observations. With consistent and unbiased dynamics estimates, model predictive control (MPC) predicts floating-base motion accurately and enables precise loco-manipulation. A finite-sample error bound is established to ensure uniform convergence in online learning. Simulation and hardware experiments demonstrate consistent end-effector tracking across different manipulator configurations and tasks, with errors of 0.01 m.

- **[FlapSim: A Composable MuJoCo Simulation Platform and Reduced-Order Aerodynamic Model Hierarchy for Flapping-Wing Robotics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40902)**  `arXiv:2610.40902`  `cs.RO`  
  _Mingshan He_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Flapping-wing simulation must reconcile high-frequency wingbeat aerodynamics with the slower dynamics relevant to flight control and reinforcement learning. We present FlapSim, a modular open-source MuJoCo simulation platform that combines robot descriptions, wing geometry, and composable aerodynamic models in a deterministic simulation pipeline. Across $48$ held-out scenarios spanning frequency, gimbal, wind, frequency-step, gust, and cross-robot flat-plate regimes, we compare five reduced-order configurations operating at $1\,\text{ms}$ with a $50\,\mu\text{s}$ quasi-steady blade-element reference. Controlled ablations show that stroke harmonics are particularly important during frequency transitions, improving held-out frequency-step fidelity by $+0.3176$. Transfer-function analysis explains this effect: fixed-window cycle averaging retains harmonic contributions when the wingbeat frequency changes, so within-cycle reconstruction also improves the filtered wrench. Once phase alignment and harmonic synthesis are included, explicit transient lag states provide no additional benefit in this benchmark. These results characterize the accuracy--computation tradeoff of the aerodynamic hierarchy and identify timing, inflow, geometry, and harmonic representation as central considerations for reduced-order flapping-wing simulation.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Long-tailed recognition often learns a shared representation before correcting the classifier for class imbalance. Standard imbalance benchmarks obscure this question because they jointly vary the number of distinct examples available to rare classes and the frequency with which those examples shape the representation. We introduce a controlled evaluation framework that separates unique support from representation allocation and estimates the incremental value of encoder updates through matched classifier-only and full-network continuations from a common corrected model.   _Dravyansh Sharma_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Membership queries let a learner label arbitrary points and give near-optimal $\tilde O(n\log(1/\eps))$ label complexity for halfspaces under every marginal. A pool learner may label only sampled points. We ask when unlabeled data can replace membership queries. For homogeneous halfspaces the answer depends only on the angular law of $X/\|X\|$. If this law assigns zero mass to every hyperplane through the origin and positive mass to every nonempty open set of directions together with its reflection, a pool learner uses $O(n\log^3n\log(n/(\eps\delta)))$ labels, uniformly over all such marginals and targets, without density, moment, concavity or isotropy assumptions. For isotropic $s$-concave marginals in dimension $n\ge3$, unlimited pools keep logarithmic label rates for every finite $s$. With an unlabeled budget fixed in advance, class-uniform polylogarithmic rates hold exactly for $s>-1/(n+1)$, or $s>-1/n$ under central symmetry, and in the general class the optimal rate between these thresholds is $\tilde\Theta(\eps^{-(n-2)/n})$. This extends the range $s\ge-1/(2n+3)$ of previous sample-based guarantees, in the realizable setting.
