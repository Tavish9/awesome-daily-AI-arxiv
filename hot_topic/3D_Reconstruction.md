- **[What Does a Skill Vocabulary Cost? A Closed-Loop Decomposition over Demonstrated Motions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32858)**  `arXiv:2611.32858`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Maximilian Xiling Li, Nils Blank, Paul Mattes, Pankhuri Vanjani, Rudolf Lioutikov_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Discrete skill policies execute motions drawn from a finite vocabulary. In most current methods, this vocabulary is a latent codebook learned jointly with a selector and a decoder. When such a policy fails in closed loop, the failure cannot be traced to the vocabulary, to the choice of skill or to its execution. We present Movement Prototype Quantization (MPQ), which places a library of demonstrated motions after a trained policy instead of inside it. MPQ replaces each predicted action chunk with its nearest library element, corrects that element toward the prediction within a bound, and trains no parameters. Across six architectures, including a 7B vision-language-action model, the wrapped policy is statistically equivalent to the original. A reference policy executed through the library is therefore a baseline for any selector over the same library. Substituting a selector's components one at a time attributes its shortfall to them. On LIBERO-90, with selectors on three backbones, quantization costs nothing and the choice of skill costs at most 2 points under the reference's placement and correction. The selectors' own placement and correction account for nearly all of the remaining gap, up to 12 points. Offline reconstruction predicts none of these differences and misjudges library size, design and transfer across benchmark suites. Project page:this https URL.

  _Itai Mizlish, Boaz Ben-Moshe_

- **[BendGS: Observation-Conditioned Gaussian Self-Modeling for Continuum Robots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31844)**  `arXiv:2611.31844`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Jeong Woon Lee, Doungjun Kim, Namho Kim, Seungheon Chae, Jongwoo Kim, Hyoseok Hwang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Visual self-models learn from images how a robot moves under motor commands. Continuum robots need them even more, as they bend continuously without measured joint angles or fixed link geometry. Existing continuum self-models predict point clouds, image-space curves, or future images, but not the next 3D shape, appearance, and tip pose together from an observed body. 3D Gaussians offer a renderable representation of shape and appearance, yet articulated Gaussian self-models rely on joint angles to drive deformation. In this work, we propose BendGS, which uses a virtual backbone estimated from observation to drive command-conditioned Gaussian deformation, enabling next-state prediction for continuum robots without measured joint angles. In Gaussian modeling, a feed-forward network reconstructs the robot as 3D Gaussians from multi-view images, and the backbone, a chain of coordinate frames, is fitted to back-projected depth points to estimate the bending state. In transition, a network predicts its change under a relative motor command to deform the bound Gaussians. The deformed Gaussians render the next images and the 3D shape, and the distal frame gives the tip pose. Training losses use RGB-D images and masks, without bending or tip labels. On simulated and real robots, BendGS achieves the lowest Chamfer distance, best image quality, and lowest tip errors among the compared methods.
