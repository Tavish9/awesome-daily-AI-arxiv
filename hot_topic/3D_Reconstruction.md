- **[Skill-JEPA: Learning Planner-Aligned Action Spaces for World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26825)**  `arXiv:2611.26825`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Tomás Couso Coddou, Christ Devia, Rex G. Liu, Rodrigo Toro Icarte_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  World models based on Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) learn from pixels to predict the outcome of actions in a latent space. To reach a goal, a planner samples many candidate action sequences, uses the model to predict where each one leads, and executes the best one. However, standard planners draw their candidates from a Gaussian, while the model has only seen the structured, state-dependent actions of its training data. Its predictions for such candidates are unreliable, and planning fails more often as goals move farther away. We propose Skill-JEPA, which learns an action space aligned with the planner. A variational autoencoder conditioned on the current state encodes short action sequences into latent skills with a standard Gaussian prior, and decodes them back into actions. The world model predicts the effect of skills rather than raw actions, and the planner searches over skills starting from that same Gaussian, so its candidates resemble the model's training data. Skill-JEPA adds a single module, trained end-to-end with the world model, and leaves the planner untouched, with no subgoals, hierarchy, or hand-designed skills. Across three JEPA world models and four benchmarks, average success on goals 80 steps away rises from 42% with raw actions to 62%; the gains grow with goal distance and hold across four planners. On AntMaze, a quadruped locomotion task, success on goals two maze cells away rises from 0.4% to 38%. This suggests that part of the difficulty of reaching distant goals lies in the action space, and can be removed without changing the planner.

- **[TacHOI: Contact-Aware Object Reconstruction and Tracking for Egocentric Videos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26764)**  `arXiv:2611.26764`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Jackson Codd, Haoyu Li, Xianyi Cheng_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Egocentric videos offer a scalable source of human demonstrations for robot learning. However, recovering metric object geometry and motion from monocular video is hindered by scale-depth ambiguity and hand-induced occlusion, while image-derived contact and shape priors alone cannot uniquely determine metric scale. We introduce TacHOI, a training-free framework that integrates tactile contact with monocular vision for joint hand-object reconstruction and tracking, without requiring object templates or depth sensors. Starting from pretrained visual estimates, TacHOI uses tactile contact to constrain object depth relative to the estimated hand, while spatially separated contacts on a metrically calibrated hand provide a physical ruler for object extent. It incorporates these constraints into a joint optimization of hand poses, object trajectories, and anisotropic object scale alongside visual alignment. Across 100 ARCTIC and HO3D sequences with synthesized tactile observations, TacHOI achieves the lowest median ADD-S* among the evaluated baselines, reducing error from 15.6 to 3.6 cm on ARCTIC and achieving 2.8 cm on HO3D. Additional experiments on OpenTouch demonstrate its applicability to measured tactile observations.

- **[Learning Interaction Dynamics via Motion-Region Guidance for Robotic Video World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27344)**  `arXiv:2611.27344`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Yongkang He, Xiaofen Xing, Xiangmin Xu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Action-conditioned video world models imagine future observations under candidate robot actions, providing a promising visual tool for robot policy evaluation and interaction simulation. However, existing video world models often optimize global reconstruction errors instead of emphasizing interaction-relevant regions. As a result, they may preserve overall scene appearance but overlook local interaction details, leading to physically implausible outcomes such as hallucinated contact effects and inconsistent robot-object interactions. We introduce MoReWorld, a unified motion-region-guided framework that uses a shared motion region, the part of the environment that moves in response to the robot's actions, to focus dynamics learning and video generation on interaction-relevant regions. These results indicate that motion-region guidance helps the model preserve scene-level consistency while more faithfully predicting action-conditioned interaction dynamics.

  _Kangrui Zhang, Mu Huang, Xing Shen, Tiantian Liu, Bo Ren_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  While existing methods for reconstructing deformable object manipulation have made impressive progress, they typically represent a thin object with a single particle set that must serve two purposes at once: modeling bending, and resolving contact with the robot. To bridge this gap, we present HermiteTwin, a framework that reconstructs a physical digital twin of a thin deformable object from multiview video of a robot manipulating it.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Planning with learned world models amounts to finding action sequences whose predicted rollouts reach a goal. Sampling-based planners often struggle at longer horizons, while gradient-based methods can become trapped in local minima. Meanwhile, model predictive path integral control (MPPI), a complementary approach, alternates between estimating an expectation under a cost-weighted distribution from samples and updating the plan from this estimate. Its samples, however, are drawn afresh at every iteration from a Gaussian proposal and reach the target distribution only through importance weights. On navigation and manipulation tasks with visual and proprioceptive world models, our formulation outperforms gradient-based planners by $29$ and $50$, sampling-based planners by $35$ and MPPI by $7$ percentage points in average success rate, while succeeding in fewer iterations than gradient-based planners and within 4\% of MPPI.
