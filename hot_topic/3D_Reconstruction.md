- **[SomaAlign: Reliability-Aware Multimodal Representation Learning for Soft Robotic Grasping](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02794)**  `arXiv:2611.02794`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Haoyuan Wang, Shuai Peng, Yongkai Liao, Yuxiao Sun, Wei Luo, Xinxing Chen, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robust multimodal perception for soft robotic grasping is challenged by modality-dependent noise, semantic ambiguity, and limited coordinated multisensory data. We introduce Soma, a dataset containing 100 objects, 1,000 continuous grasping trajectories, and 74,914 multimodal samples grouped by software acquisition cycle, spanning remote RGB, hand RGB, depth, tactile, IMU, and structured text observations. We also collect Soma-Success, an independent grasp-outcome dataset with 1,000 trials (998 retained after quality control), to evaluate frozen-encoder transfer for post-hoc grasp outcome classification and early grasp success prediction. Built on Soma, SomaAlign learns multimodal representations in three stages: masked reconstruction estimates relative modality reliability; reliability-aware contrastive alignment uses pair-specific adaptive temperatures to adjust alignment sharpness; and conflict-aware semantic constraints improve intra-sample cross-modal consistency while preserving global feature geometry. SomaAlign achieves an average Recall@10 of 90.66% across 30 directed cross-modal retrieval tasks. The learned representations also support physical attribute recognition, multimodal question answering, cross-modal generation, and cross-dataset transfer to grasp outcome prediction. On Soma-Success, frozen SomaAlign encoders with lightweight probes achieve 94.59% post-hoc classification accuracy and 84.07% early prediction accuracy. Together, Soma, Soma-Success, and SomaAlign provide a data and representation-learning foundation for robust and transferable multimodal perception in soft robotic manipulation.

- **[Right Ranking, Wrong Scale: Reproducing the Robotic World Model's Dynamics Model and Testing the Uncertainty of Its Follow-up](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02699)**  `arXiv:2611.02699`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Joyjeet Singh_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We reimplement the dynamics model of the Robotic World Model (RWM) and test the uncertainty estimate that its follow-up, RWM-U, uses to penalize reward in offline policy optimization. Our implementation matches the released model's gradients. On held-out data, training on the model's own rollouts beats one-step training at the preregistered long horizon for all ten episodes, but loses on short one-step windows. RWM-U's ensemble disagreement ranks rollout errors better than forecast step (Spearman rho = 0.90 at 100 steps), yet its spread substantially understates error: one-sigma coverage on the released checkpoint is 4.6% at 100 steps, versus 68% for a calibrated Gaussian. Rolling ensemble members on their own predictions raises coverage to 33%. The implemented squared-error loss on sampled predictions drives each member's variance toward zero. A horizon-specific rescaling restores one-sigma coverage under leave-one-episode-out evaluation, but not two-sigma coverage. We also identify two defects in the released evaluation pipeline. The study covers one robot, one gait and one dataset; it trains no policy.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Contact transitions allow humanoid robots to redistribute object support across the whole body, enabling manipulation beyond hand-only grasps. Human videos provide a scalable source of such transitions for robots to learn from. However, body occlusion disrupts object pose tracking and corrupts retargeted motion.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Pretrained models are typically viewed as generalists, requiring bespoke post-training to achieve task-specific expertise. Gan & Isola (2026) recently proposed a striking alternative: that pretrained models lie in a dense neural thicket of task specialists, which can be surfaced with simple Gaussian weight perturbations. We examine this neural thicket of task-specific experts and identify several key findings that question this perspective.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Search-based planning remains a powerful policy improvement paradigm in reinforcement learning, driving milestone successes in discrete domains. However, extending this paradigm to continuous control exposes a structural mismatch: search evaluates a finite set of action candidates, whereas continuous RL typically relies on Gaussian policies. We show that winner-action distillation into a continuous Gaussian induces a \textit{projection error} and a stepwise variance-expanding gradient tendency when the selected action is sufficiently far from the current mean. Furthermore, it produces more stable action sequences with lower temporal variance, demonstrating its practical potential for real-world robotic deployment.
