  _Dixiang Wang, Minghao Zha, Arifur Rahman, Yinhong Qin, Yitao Jiang, Shiyang Lu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Animals exhibit diverse shape-changing behaviors, from the undulatory swimming of eels to the arching movements of inchworms. Reproducing these biological shapes with modular soft robots offers a promising approach to developing versatile bioinspired robotic systems. However, translating a desired shape into a feasible robotic configuration remains challenging. Existing cable-driven modular soft robots rely on computationally expensive physics-based modeling and combinatorial search for inverse design, making it difficult to efficiently reproduce diverse target shapes. To address this challenge, we present a learning-based real-to-sim-to-real framework that translates biological curves into robotic configurations through surrogate-assisted inverse design and uncertainty-guided refinement. At the same time, the proposed inverse design method reduces design time from $1.99 \times 10^6$ ms to 4.18 ms. On 5,000 test samples generated from previously unseen threading patterns, the method achieves a 93% shape reconstruction success rate under surrogate-based evaluation. Physical demonstrations with four bioinspired robots further illustrate the translation of biological curves into realizable robotic shapes.

- **[Continuous-Action World Models: Learning Cross-Embodiment Spatiotemporal Dynamics Priors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40401)**  `arXiv:2610.40401`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV` `cs.LG`  
  _Liangyu Li, Qingwen Liu_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We introduce Continuous-Action World Models (CoAWM), a framework for learning cross-embodiment spatiotemporal dynamics priors that support robot action generation across embodiments and execution frequencies. At its core is a shared latent function space that captures the spatial and temporal structure of robot actions. We learn this space through two stages. First, function pretraining brings trajectories from different embodiments and sampling frequencies into a common representation, learning shared dynamics priors through action reconstruction and consistency across temporal sampling views. By accommodating heterogeneous robot trajectories, this stage broadens the range of data sources available for pretraining and facilitates scaling to larger and more diverse datasets. Second, we connect the pretrained function space to a world-model backbone, enabling observations, language instructions, and the current robot state to condition the prediction of a latent action function. A single decoder takes any query time and returns the corresponding end-effector action. By separating shared dynamics priors from task-conditioned action selection and temporal sampling, our approach provides a unified foundation for transferring robot experience across embodiments and control frequencies.

We propose CALM, a conflict-aware lifecycle memory governance framework that decouples persistent memory preservation from query-time admission. CALM converts heterogeneous records into evidence-grounded memory cells, updates validity and lifecycle states using support, conflict, and dependency signals, and admits query-appropriate evidence through training-free or learned gating informed by temporal applicability. Across LongMemEval-S and LoCoMo with Qwen and GPT backbones, CALM outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, achieving the best overall accuracy. Compared with simple filtering and reranking, query-conditioned admission further improves evidence selection. Conflict-blocking and robustness analyses show CALM suppresses erroneous retrieved memories while retaining useful evidence. Historical-reconstruction experiments demonstrate that preserving superseded records supports accurate earlier-state reasoning without compromising current-state answers.
