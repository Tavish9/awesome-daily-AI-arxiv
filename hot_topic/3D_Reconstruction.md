- **[Stereo Visual Odometry with In-the-Loop Geometric Feed-Forward Models Towards Dense Metric Mapping](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25445)**  `arXiv:2611.25445`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Fahira Afzal Maken, Sagun Man Singh Shrestha, Edward Vanderfeen, Lucas Carvalho de Lima, Rongxin Li, Changming Sun, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We present a stereo visual odometry (SVO) framework that integrates geometric feed-forward models (GFMs) for dense depth prediction with factor-graph-based visual odometry to achieve accurate localisation and dense metric mapping. Our key idea is to exploit sparse but reliable metric geometry estimated from stereo and temporal multi-view observations to calibrate dense GFM predictions, while retaining the robustness and efficiency of model-based SVO. Rather than directly conditioning the GFM on potentially noisy stereo depth, optimised 3D landmarks are used to jointly estimate a scale-shift transformation over all keyframes within each local submap, yielding metrically consistent dense depth predictions. To balance accuracy and computational efficiency, we introduce a hierarchical factor-graph architecture. At the local level, sliding-window optimisation estimates high-frequency camera poses and multi-view-refined 3D landmarks. Dense predictions are generated only for selected keyframes and registered within each submap using SE(3) Generalised ICP. Consecutive submaps are further aligned in Sim(3) using their shared geometry, allowing residual scale inconsistencies to be corrected. These intra- and inter-submap constraints are incorporated into a global factor graph to produce a consistent trajectory and dense metric reconstruction. Extensive experiments on public and in-house datasets demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves competitive odometry accuracy against geometric and learning-based baselines while providing accurate dense metric reconstruction and improved robustness to failures in learned depth prediction.

- **[SpecFlow: Flow Matching in the Acceleration Spectrum for Multi-Agent Trajectory Forecasting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26390)**  `arXiv:2611.26390`  `cs.RO`  
  _Ziyang Xu, Peng Han, Hai Zhou_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Multimodal trajectory forecasting must cover diverse future behaviors without sacrificing dynamical coherence. Most generative forecasters evolve frame-wise positions directly, leaving the relationships among position, velocity, and acceleration implicit. We present SpecFlow, a conditional flow-matching framework that treats the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) coefficients of future accelerations as its generative state. SpecFlow learns a transport from Gaussian noise to acceleration spectra and reconstructs velocity and position through fixed analytical integration initialized by the last observed state. Together, these results demonstrate that acceleration-spectrum flow matching provides an effective structured generative state across smooth pedestrian motion, coordinated multi-agent sports dynamics, and heterogeneous outdoor trajectories.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) decompose language model activations into sparse features, yet these models traditionally encode each token independently, failing to expose information that persists across a sequence. - **[Chameleon: Dynamic Format Adapter for Efficient Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33496)**  `arXiv:2609.33496`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `cs.CV`  
  _Arnab Sanyal, Sandeep Chinchali_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Post-training quantization (PTQ) is the standard way to run modern diffusion models on memory-constrained accelerators, yet every existing diffusion PTQ scheme fixes the $\mathit{number\ format}$ in advance and only tunes the scale, zero point, or per-layer bit-width. At a fixed bit-width the best format depends on the distribution being encoded, and that distribution differs across weight channels, across layers, and along the diffusion timestep, where activation distributions slide from heavy-tailed and noise-dominated to tightly clustered and structured. We propose Chameleon, a PTQ framework that holds the bit-width fixed and treats the format itself as a discrete variable, chosen per weight channel and per activation layer, optionally refined per timestep bucket. An architectural fork adapts the same selection layer to multi-step UNets, single-step distilled models, and Diffusion Transformers. Most of the gain comes from the per-layer choice; the timestep refinement helps mainly at 6-bit activations. Across SDXL, SDXL-Turbo, and PixArt-$\alpha$ on COCO-2014, Chameleon achieves the best FID in all six backbone $\times$ bit-width settings, with CLIP within 0.24 of the FP16 reference and the best of all quantized methods at $W_{4}A_{8}$. On PixArt-$\alpha$, a real mixed-format kernel runs 1.09$\times$ faster than FP16 end-to-end with 3$\times$ smaller weights.
