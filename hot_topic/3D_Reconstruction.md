- **[Rethinking Actions: Continuity-Aware Priors for World-Model Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16645)**  `arXiv:2611.16645`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Shaobo Yang, Zichen Ai, Ziyang Ma, Qian Yang, Bin Fang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Actions in expert demonstrations exhibit temporal structure. An ordered-versus-shuffled analysis across five environments confirms this dependence. We ask whether action proposals that better reflect expert continuity can improve visual world-model planning. Rather than imposing generic smoothness, we learn continuations from the temporal structure of demonstrated actions. Our locality-gated deep-kernel Gaussian-process prior, LG-DKGP, combines recent and longer-range executed action history, predicts a neural continuation, and learns a temporal Gaussian-process (GP) residual. A training-derived exploration scale initializes search around the corrected mean; a frozen world model evaluates progress toward the goal. Training first fits the neural mean, then jointly optimizes the prior with the GP residual using expert future actions. The resulting planner achieves 87.07\% macro success across five simulated tasks spanning native and fragment-level goal attainment, compared with 71.03\% for INTACT, 61.66\% for PRISM, and 58.66\% for LeWM. Component interventions and expert-relative action-structure comparisons support demonstration-informed continuations and reveal task-dependent effects of visual conditioning, history length, and planning horizon.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  This paper presents DPGS-SLAM (Diffusion-Prior and Provenance-Aware Monocular Gaussian Splatting SLAM) for dense mapping in environments containing dynamic objects. Objects in dynamic motion remain a significant challenge for dense visual SLAM since dynamic observations can contaminate camera tracking and scene reconstruction. Although 3DGS (3D Gaussian splatting) enables efficient dense mapping, existing 3DGS-SLAM systems may exhibit instability when dynamic objects violate their underlying static-scene assumption. Egocentric human videos offer a complementary source of experience, and latent action models (LAMs) provide a popular means of leveraging these videos for policy supervision.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>

We introduce ActionCodec2, a non-neural streaming tokenizer that releases actions incrementally while keeping trajectory reconstruction error within a prescribed tolerance. Its encoder quantizes each step relative to the decoder's reconstructed state, so the selected motion primitive corrects residual error rather than allowing errors from independently quantized increments to accumulate. Its streaming representation allows policies to incorporate state feedback within an action chunk, substantially improving performance over open-loop execution under randomized dynamics, external disturbances, and moving objects. The same tokens also can serve as a direct control interface for VLM agents, which compose successful action sequences into reusable macros. Each RSP vector is sampled from an isotropic Gaussian fitted to the entrywise mean and variance of the pretrained embedding table; the sequence carries no learned content, and yet reaches accuracy comparable to optimized soft prompts on math reasoning benchmarks in several settings. The mechanism unfolds in two stages: because attention has to absorb a never-seen-before random position, the distribution over the first few generated tokens flattens and reasoning trajectories branch, and as generation continues this influence dilutes naturally so the response commits to a single completion. We show that during inference RSPs lift early-stage token diversity and, combined with temperature sampling, widen Pass@N, the probability that at least one out of N attempts is correct. Beyond inference, we carry the same effect into DAPO training and demonstrate practical gains. Our contributions are: (i) RSP isolates the simplest form of soft prompt -- training-free, freshly resampled -- providing a unified lens for the structural effect of injection that variants otherwise differing in training and form all share; (ii) a theoretical and empirical validation of the underlying mechanism; and (iii) an extension from inference to training.
