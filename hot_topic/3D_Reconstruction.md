- **[Skill-Adaptive Noise Scheduling for Diffusion Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30264)**  `arXiv:2611.30264`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Woo Kyung Kim, Gwangpyo Yoo, Eunyoung Park, Honguk Woo_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent advances in diffusion policies have demonstrated strong effectiveness when integrated into hierarchical skill-based learning frameworks, leveraging offline data to capture multimodal and temporally abstracted behaviors. However, prior approaches have rarely considered the diffusion process as a learnable component, adopting fixed noise schedules applied uniformly across skills, regardless of their distinct behavioral contexts. Such a design fundamentally limits the achievable performance, particularly for contact-rich and dexterous manipulation tasks where different skills exhibit distinct couplings between action dimensions. In this paper, we present a skill-adaptive noise diffusion framework (SaND) which introduces a learnable, skill-conditioned noise schedule that modulates the diffusion process per action dimension, adapting the noising dynamics to each skill. To prevent the learned noise schedule from collapsing into trivial solutions, we employ an auxiliary inverse objective that reconstructs the skill embedding from the Legendre coefficients of the derivative of the noise schedule, encouraging the schedules to remain skill-discriminative. This formulation naturally enables adaptive control over the denoising steps for each skill, where noise schedules are clustered in the Legendre coefficient space, and the appropriate number of denoising steps is determined at the point where reconstruction error abruptly increases. At inference, we identify each skill embedding's cluster from its noise schedule and apply the corresponding number of denoising steps, dedicating more compute to skills requiring fine-grained control and less to simpler ones. In simulation and on real robot hardware, SaND outperforms existing skill learning and planning baselines on contact-rich and dexterous manipulation tasks, achieving higher success rates while requiring fewer denoising steps.

- **[Rethinking Actions: Continuity-Aware Priors for World-Model Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16645)**  `arXiv:2611.16645`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Shaobo Yang, Zichen Ai, Ziyang Ma, Qian Yang, Bin Fang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Actions in expert demonstrations exhibit temporal structure. An ordered-versus-shuffled analysis across five environments confirms this dependence. We ask whether action proposals that better reflect expert continuity can improve visual world-model planning. Rather than imposing generic smoothness, we learn continuations from the temporal structure of demonstrated actions. Our locality-gated deep-kernel Gaussian-process prior, LG-DKGP, combines recent and longer-range executed action history, predicts a neural continuation, and learns a temporal Gaussian-process (GP) residual. A training-derived exploration scale initializes search around the corrected mean; a frozen world model evaluates progress toward the goal. Training first fits the neural mean, then jointly optimizes the prior with the GP residual using expert future actions. The resulting planner achieves 87.07\% macro success across five simulated tasks spanning native and fragment-level goal attainment, compared with 71.03\% for INTACT, 61.66\% for PRISM, and 58.66\% for LeWM. Component interventions and expert-relative action-structure comparisons support demonstration-informed continuations and reveal task-dependent effects of visual conditioning, history length, and planning horizon.

- **[Connectivity-Aware Exploration of Robotic Grasp Spaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22983)**  `arXiv:2609.22983`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Maksim A Kazanskii_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robotic grasping is typically formulated as the problem of identifying successful actions from a space of candidate grasp poses. However, the organization of successful actions within this space has received less attention. We study the multiscale structure of viable robotic grasps in SE(3) and investigate whether this structure can be exploited for more efficient exploration. Using a large-scale grasp dataset, we show that successful grasp sets exhibit heterogeneous and reproducible connectivity structure across objects. We then introduce a connectivity-aware sampling strategy that incrementally explores the currently observed grasp space by prioritizing potential bridges between components, structural frontiers, boundary extensions, and geometric novelty. In controlled reconstruction experiments, the method recovers the connectivity structure of successful grasp sets substantially more efficiently than random sampling and farthest-point sampling. We further evaluate whether connectivity acquired under hidden grasp viability can improve subsequent grasp discovery, and whether structural experience from previously explored objects can be retrieved and transferred to unseen objects. These results suggest that the spatial organization of viable actions provides information relevant to grasp-space exploration beyond the viability of individual candidate actions. More broadly, they motivate structure-aware exploration as a means of exploiting the geometry of viable action spaces in robotic manipulation.

- **[Occupancy Planar Patch: Incremental Probabilistic Bounded-Plane Reconstruction from LiDAR Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28403)**  `arXiv:2611.28403`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Jiahao Wang, Chaehyeon Song, Dongjiao He, Jan Quenzel, Lintong Zhang, Ayoung Kim, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Online surface reconstruction from LiDAR scans requires observation models that respect the sensor's physical measurement geometry. Unlike unordered point sets, LiDAR returns carry anisotropic range noise along known rays, which also implicitly identify free space. We introduce the Occupancy Planar Patch (OPP), an incremental bounded planar patch representation that evaluates surface likelihoods and free space directly in range-space, allowing sensor noise, plane uncertainty, and ray clearance to be explicitly modelled during reconstruction. A single ray-to-patch association weight jointly updates plane geometry, refines surface boundaries, and blends camera imagery. Geometric reconstruction operates in real time, requiring 81 ms per frame on a representative sequence, with texture projection adding 134 ms.
