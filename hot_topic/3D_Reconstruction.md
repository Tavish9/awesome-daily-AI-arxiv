- **[Fade to... Red? Scheduling Coloured Noise Exploration Supports Efficient Robot Learning with Reduced Movement Jerk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11868)**  `arXiv:2611.11868`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Francisco M. López, Francisco Cruz_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Exploration in deep reinforcement learning (RL) for continuous control is commonly driven by temporally uncorrelated Gaussian (white) action noise, which leads to jerky motions with locally constrained exploration. In contrast, temporally correlated coloured noise, particularly pink noise, has been shown to support more efficient exploration in RL. Motivated by these findings, we inquire whether scheduling changes to the noise colour over the course of training can outperform white noise for continuous control robotics. We perform a systematic study of 49 linear schedules between white and red noise, together with generalised state-dependent exploration (gSDE), on four sparse-reward manipulation tasks with a Fetch arm and a Shadow Dexterous Hand. We find that the noise colour at the end of training does not affect learning efficiency or end performance, whereas fading towards red noise halves the jerk of exploration in every task. Balancing learning efficiency, end performance and smoothness, red noise emerges as the preferred strategy: it learns as efficiently as pink noise with almost 2-fold less movement jerk. We therefore recommend exploration schedulers that fade towards red noise as the default for off-policy robot learning.

- **[From Depth to Safe Depth: Uncertainty-Aware Monocular Perception for Mobile Robot Navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13769)**  `arXiv:2611.13769`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Saeid Firouzi Daghigh, Saeed Ayat_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Monocular depth foundation models make it tempting to replace depth sensors on mobile robots with a single RGB camera. We show that, used off the shelf, this is unsafe: in a simulated indoor navigation benchmark, a standard Nav2 stack driven by Depth Anything V2 (metric) collides in 63\% of 120 episodes, because the model systematically over-estimates the distance to nearby obstacles. We propose a lightweight plug-in module that turns predicted depth into \emph{safe depth}. A constant safety margin calibrated to the same pixel coverage reaches only 30\% success because it freezes the robot; uncertainty-aware safety wins 52 of 56 discordant paired episodes against it. Gains hold on glass, texture-less walls and dark regions.

After whitening, forward diffusion corrupts every direction at the same signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), despite unequal decoder sensitivities. We study an invertible linear layer between the encoder and diffusion process that shifts each direction's log-SNR. Its linear minimum mean-square-error (LMMSE) recovery risk has an exact spectral expression for any whitened latent distribution.   <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  The most widely used RANSAC variants score a candidate model by summing per-point scores that fall to zero beyond a threshold. These scores derive from the negative log-likelihood of a Gaussian-uniform mixture. The threshold separates inliers, which follow the noise model, from outliers, and it is a function of the observation noise level, also known as the inlier scale. We remove this dependence on the threshold by reversing the usual order of RANSAC inference: rather than estimating the scale and then scoring against it, we marginalize the scale in closed form for a fixed inlier partition and then optimize over partitions. - **[Kierston & KierstonBench: Simulation of Allentown, PA and Evaluation of Language Models in Crime Investigation and Surveillance Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04785)**  `arXiv:2611.04785`  `cs.AI`  
  _Andrew Semenov, Svyatoslav Dorofeev_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Large language models are candidates for collecting and analyzing records in criminal investigations and mass surveillance. Their capability and accuracy in this work cannot currently be measured. Real deployments lack the ground truth needed to score a model's conclusions, and no benchmark evaluates these capabilities in a simulated environment. We present Kierston, a city-scale simulation engine, and KierstonBench, a benchmark that tests these capabilities against known simulated events. Kierston models approximately 125,000 residents of Allentown, Pennsylvania, generating their daily activities and the telecommunications, financial, administrative and license-plate records that surveillance and investigative systems collect about them. These records derive from a common behavioral timeline and are distributed across sources representing separate providers. Models investigate through a restricted records interface, linking observations across sources without access to the underlying simulation. The benchmark comprises 32 task families covering record linkage, routine reconstruction, incident investigation and assessment of deceptive accounts. In an evaluation of sixteen language models, most could not perform records investigation and submitted identifications the records did not support. A small number of frontier models solved most of the tasks and abstained when evidence was insufficient. In some cases these models still fell for common deceptions and assumptions, and none replaced an investigator.
