- **[RoboCode: Learning Reusable Action Primitives for Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32225)**  `arXiv:2610.32225`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Xianyue Peng, Devi Amarsaikhan, Iman Soltani_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Recent robot manipulation policies commonly predict continuous action chunks directly from observations, without explicitly modeling reusable structure in the action space. However, manipulation demonstrations often contain recurring short-horizon motion patterns that are shared across tasks. We introduce \textsc{RoboCode}, which learns these patterns through a shared soft-gated primitive action interface. Rather than assigning each action chunk to a single discrete code, \textsc{RoboCode} represents it as a soft composition of multiple learned primitives, providing a flexible intermediate representation of short-horizon actions. The primitive interface is learned from action chunks pooled across multiple tasks and then frozen while task-specific state encoders are trained to map current state inputs into the shared latent space.Experiments on five ManiSkill manipulation tasks address three questions. Third, \textsc{RoboCode} achieves the highest or tied closed-loop success rate on all evaluated tasks, with an average success rate of 75.6%, compared with 68.8% for ACT and 41.6% for the larger Diffusion Policy baseline. These results suggest that soft-gated primitive codebooks provide an effective and interpretable intermediate action representation for robot manipulation.

- **[Input Bias Correction in Gaussian Process Regression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31920)**  `arXiv:2610.31920`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Muzaffar Qureshi, Tochukwu E. Ogri, Trivikram Satharasi, Kyle Volle, Rushikesh Kamalapurkar_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  In traditional Gaussian process (GP) regression, the inputs are typically assumed to be known, and uncertainty enters only through the measurements. When scalar fields are mapped using GP regression with measurements obtained from a mobile sensor, the inputs correspond to the estimated pose of the mobile sensor. The goal in this letter is to understand how a recorded GP, trained on erroneous inputs (e.g., pose estimates from dead-reckoning), can be corrected in a numerically efficient way when better estimates of the inputs become available. In the developed bias-aware GP (Ba-GP) framework, the recorded GP posterior-mean gradient is used to propagate estimated input bias and noise into mean and covariance corrections. The resulting additive covariance structure is then exploited to develop numerically efficient first-order correction algorithms. A simulation study and an indoor light-intensity mapping experiment show that Ba-GP improves predictive accuracy and uncertainty calibration relative to noisy-input GP baselines, with an order-of-magnitude reduction in computational cost relative to full GP retraining.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Active 3D Gaussian reconstruction fundamentally relies on selecting informative next-best views under limited sensing budgets. Existing active 3DGS methods primarily plan viewpoints according to geometric information gain, treating object-induced hidden regions in the same manner as general unexplored space. Under tight frame budgets, such geometry-driven strategies may prioritize global scene coverage while leaving partially observed objects incompletely reconstructed. To address this limitation, we propose OccamView, an object-conditioned view-selection framework for frame-budgeted active 3D Gaussian reconstruction. Rather than predicting unseen object geometry or performing shape completion, OccamView maintains an online object memory from open-vocabulary detections grounded in measured RGB-D observations and represents unresolved local occupancy around detected objects as conservative hidden-region proxies. Candidate viewpoints are then evaluated using an occlusion-aware proxy-coverage score. Furthermore, we introduce a Geo-Floor mechanism that restricts object-conditioned re-ranking to geometrically competitive candidates, allowing object-conditioned cues to guide complementary observations while preserving the geometry-driven exploration behavior of the underlying planner. Experiments on Replica and Matterport3D under a unified frame-budgeted protocol show that OccamView consistently reduces Completion and improves Completion Ratio across five frame budgets, with particularly pronounced gains under limited frame budgets. These results demonstrate that lightweight object-conditioned cues effectively complement geometry-driven active view planning.
