- **[Value-Preserving Action Quantization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17252)**  `arXiv:2610.17252`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Max Muchen Sun, Jueun Kwon, Benjamin Riviere, Todd Murphey_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Search-based policy learning, such as Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS), offers the flexibility to trade runtime computation for data efficiency and adaptation to novel environments. For robot learning, however, applying search requires discretizing the continuous action space and associated dynamics, typically through action quantization. While reconstruction-based quantization is sufficient for representing the continuous action space alone, policy learning additionally requires the quantized actions to preserve their effects on future states and values. We show that such requirement is not necessarily captured by action reconstruction error. Instead, we prove that the error in the value function resulting from action quantization is tightly bounded by the reconstruction error of state reachability under the quantized actions. Based on these results, we propose value-preserving action quantization (VAQ), which quantizes actions by preserving state reachability. We further combine VAQ with MCTS (VAQ-MCTS) as a value-preserving, search-based policy learning framework for behavioral cloning. Using a wide range of object pushing tasks as a controlled benchmark, we evaluate VAQ-MCTS against state-of-the-art behavioral cloning methods. VAQ-MCTS matches or exceeds their performance under standard settings, while substantially improving data efficiency and robustness to heterogeneous demonstrations, while enabling zero-shot adaptation to novel environments. Hardware experiments further demonstrate the effectiveness of VAQ-MCTS, completing all 30 real-world object pushing trials with only 10 minutes of demonstration data.

- **[From Pixels to Executable Physics: Behavior-Grounded World Models for Embodied Action Consequence Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17630)**  `arXiv:2610.17630`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Ruo Chen, Jiahua Pang, Yingjie Niu, Hongyi Lin, Jun Tian, Yang Zhu, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  In outdoor environments, varying terrain and contact conditions can cause identical action commands to produce different motion outcomes for quadruped robots. Predicting these outcomes is essential for reliable navigation and informed path selection. Existing video world models can generate visually plausible futures, yet often struggle to capture robot dynamics and terrain interactions, leading to overly optimistic predictions of action outcomes. We present DR-WM, a physically consistent world model that incorporates robot-terrain contact dynamics to predict the consequences of quadruped actions. Starting from an initial RGB image, our method reconstructs scene geometry and integrates dense, dimensionless priors over terrain contact properties, calibrated using real-world action-response data. The resulting terrain model supports the simulation of physics with spatially varying contact properties such as friction. Given the initial state of the robot and candidate action sequences, we simulate its future motion and use the resulting camera trajectories and rendered geometry to guide the training of a video world model. The generated future observations are then used to update the terrain reconstruction, enabling iterative model rollouts without additional real-world images. Extensive experiments demonstrate that DR-WM substantially outperforms geometry-only baselines and baselines with uniform physical parameters on key physical metrics characterizing action outcomes, while achieving the best performance among the evaluated baselines. These physically grounded predictions support more informed action selection: across 90 challenging scenarios, an 8B vision-language model augmented with DR-WM outperforms an unassisted 32B vision-language model on five of six evaluation metrics.

- **[GNFormer: Gaussian Neural Field Transformer for Efficient Camera-based Occupancy Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15522)**  `arXiv:2610.15522`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Guangcheng Chen, Li He, Hong Zhang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Camera-based occupancy prediction provides geometric and semantic scene understanding for robot perception and has been adopted in autonomous vehicle and UAV perception systems. Gaussian primitives offer a promising alternative by potentially representing only occupied regions, thereby concentrating computation and representational capacity where needed and reducing wasted computation and memory in empty space. However, existing Gaussian-based methods suffer from poor primitive utilization, as ineffective training objectives leave some Gaussians in empty space, while the limited expressiveness of Gaussian functions restricts their ability to model complex shapes. To address these limitations, we propose a novel 3D representation, Gaussian Neural Field (GNF), which can accurately map voxel positions to semantic occupancies. In GNF, Gaussians and a neural network jointly parameterize the field, where the Gaussians define its spatial support and provide features for neural decoding, while the neural network enables more expressive occupancy modeling than Gaussian functions alone. We further develop the Gaussian Neural Field Transformer (GNFormer), a streamlined and flexible method that predicts GNFs for camera-based occupancy prediction. Across Occ-ScanNet, SSCBench-KITTI-360, and nuScenes-SurroundOcc benchmarks, GNFormer achieves the highest IoU and mIoU and the fastest inference among the compared single-frame methods. In the experiments, GNFormer can represent indoor and outdoor scenes with only 256 and 1024 Gaussians, respectively, substantially fewer primitives than prior Gaussian-based methods. The code will be released.

- **[ToF-Wheel Odometry: Navigating Degeneration of Localization With Limited Sensing Capabilities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14590)**  `arXiv:2610.14590`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Xu Yang, Wenhao Li, Zhengyu Wei, Zening Li, Longxiang Huang, Bo Wang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Wheel odometry provides an efficient motion prior for ground robots but is prone to transient violations such as wheel slip or terrain-induced tilt. In filtering-based odometry, these violations frequently trigger divergence when they coincide with constrained geometric observability (e.g., featureless corridors). We address this challenge by proposing ToF-Wheel Odometry (TWO), a tightly-coupled filtering framework that integrates wheel kinematics with geometric constraints from Time-of-Flight (ToF) cameras. The primary contribution of this work is a degeneracy-aware, directional covariance-shaping strategy that replaces heuristic online covariance tuning with geometry-conditioned inflation. The core idea is to infer process noise from geometric corrections with a strict reliability check. We decompose the posterior-to-prior correction into a wheel-explainable subspace and an orthogonal remainder. The process covariance is anisotropically adjusted according to the observability of the ToF update: if the geometry is degenerate in non-kinematic directions, the remainder is suppressed as sensor noise; if the geometry is distinct, it is admitted as a valid kinematic violation. Additionally, we introduce an intra-frame feedback loop that retrospectively repairs the process covariance within the current frame to reduce structural lag during bursty disturbances. Extensive experiments on challenging scenarios demonstrate that TWO improves trajectory accuracy and robustness compared to static and adaptive Gaussian filtering, robustly navigating the trade-off between kinematic reliability and limited sensing capabilities.
  </details>

- **[Flow-Support-Weighted Proposals for Test-Time Verified VLA Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14588)**  `arXiv:2610.14588`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Chen Zhao, Zipeng Ye, Haitao Shen, Guanlin Li, Yuchen Xie, Jing Zhang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Test-time sampling promises to turn one action prediction into a set of distinct executable candidates that a verifier can choose among. Downstream fine-tuning works against that promise. A vision-language-action model trained to solve one task distribution concentrates its flow-matching decoder around the dominant demonstrated action, so repeated samples differ only marginally and the verifier receives many copies of one decision. We call this proposal contraction and identify it as a key constraint on test-time scaling: repeated samples can remain highly redundant as the candidate budget grows. Action Dynamic Sampling (ADS) alleviates the constraint on the proposal side, during training. At each training step, ADS integrates $K$ additional trajectories from the current policy under fresh base noise, scores each by the flow support along its path with a Hutchinson trace estimator, and corrects the policy's local action distribution toward this weighted set through a detached finite-set KL term. Training cost rises; deployment cost is unchanged. Across simulation and four physical-robot tasks, ADS raises proposal spread without the success penalty that Gaussian perturbation incurs, lifts oracle best-of-8 success on RoboCasa from 57.6% to 63.0%, improves fixed verifiers by 2.4 to 4.9 points in simulation, and raises TokenPB by up to 13.0 points on real-world manipulation.

- **[Refining Compositional Diffusion for Reliable Long-Horizon Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03075)**  `arXiv:2605.03075`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI` `cs.LG`  
  _Kyowoon Lee, Yunhao Luo, Anh Tong, Jaesik Choi_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Compositional diffusion planning generates long-horizon trajectories by stitching together overlapping short-horizon segments through score composition. However, when local plan distributions are multimodal, existing compositional methods suffer from mode-averaging, where averaging incompatible local modes leads to plans that are neither locally feasible nor globally coherent. We propose Refining Compositional Diffusion (RCD), a training-free guidance method that steers compositional sampling toward high-density, globally coherent plans. RCD leverages the self-reconstruction error of a pretrained diffusion model as a proxy for the log-density of composed plans, combined with an overlap consistency term that enforces consistency at segment boundaries. We show that the combined guidance concentrates sampling on high-density plans that mitigate mode-averaging. Experiments on challenging long-horizon tasks from OGBench, including locomotion, object manipulation, and pixel-based observations, demonstrate that RCD consistently outperforms existing methods.

- **[D-World: An Explicit World-Language-Action Model with Recursive Self-Improvement under Dual Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17114)**  `arXiv:2610.17114`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Xuan Wen, Baichao Wang_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Frontier large language models (LLMs) now reason about space well enough to program robots, yet the world models behind robot policies are implicit, held in network weights or predicted video, and cannot be inspected or corrected directly. We present D-World, an explicit world-language-action model built on the Sense, Plan and Act decomposition. A perception agent writes the scene as an executable digital twin using generated vision and geometry code. A planning agent searches for actions in simulation and, after each executed action chunk, revises the twin's object states and named physical parameters against what the robot measured. A parameter revision is accepted only if replaying the latest chunk does not increase any measured residual beyond its tolerance, and every chunk is checked in the updated twin before it runs. Structural mismatches return to perception for reconstruction. We call this measurement-grounded cycle of correction and revision recursive self-improvement (RSI), in which the LLM stays frozen while the world it acts in improves. We model the choice of actions that both advance the task and expose model error as a dual control problem and show that the cost of incorporating evidence into the twin sets the threshold at which probing pays, so cheap local revision counteracts the turn-off of cautious controllers. Each episode runs a real-to-sim-to-real loop, and its recorded twins can be refitted and re-rendered offline into higher-fidelity environments for post-training. Because perception and planning communicate only through the twin and language, either can be replaced by a learned model, and since inference dominates the loop, the loop speeds up with faster models. On a dual-arm robot, towel and toaster tasks show local revisions, such as one reducing the bread's rendered-depth residual from 22.4 to 1.5 mm, that let execution continue without reconstruction.
