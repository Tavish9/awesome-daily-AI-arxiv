- **[Feasibility Ceilings Before Learning: Sizing and Timing Bounds for Drone-Mesh Interception](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20823)**  `arXiv:2610.20823`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG` `cs.MA`  
  _Dmytro Babarytskyi_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  A mesh of interceptors defending a fixed site must spread over the predicted arrival point of an approaching object. This is a recurring task in multi-agent robotics: N agents, each covering a disc of radius r, spread over a probability density so that a point drawn from it falls within some agent's disc. We give closed forms for the quantities that bound performance before any policy is chosen, and validate each by Monte Carlo. The uncertainty of a point predicted by extrapolating a noisy linear track follows from least-squares variance propagation plus a delta-method term for the uncertain arrival time, agreeing with simulation to within 1.8%. The coverage probability of a packed mesh admits a closed form accurate to 0.007 absolute, with a volumetric analogue. A naive area ratio misses a ceiling: while agents stay at least 2r apart, no mesh exceeds 90.69% coverage in the plane or 74.05% in a volume. This ceiling belongs to the separation constraint, not to the problem - permitting overlap substitutes Kershner's thinnest-covering density and removes it, the better regime above a crossover at p=0.80. Hexagonal packing is optimal among five arrangements; the centroidal-Voronoi configuration coverage control supplies reaches only half its coverage at small budgets. The Gaussian assumption is conservative under heavy tails and anisotropy but fragile under bias: a one-sigma aim-point offset costs 14 points of coverage. A repositioning team must also choose a commit time, and the admissible window can be empty. Against the applicable ceiling, analytic placement attains 85% of what the configuration permits while centralised PPO averages 15% over three trainer seeds, with a paired bootstrap against a null policy containing zero at every seed. We argue such ceilings should be computed before any learning experiment and quoted alongside every result.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  We introduce $Z$-sight, a unified robot foundation model that couples vision-language-action (VLA) and generative latent world modeling. - **[Facet-SLAM: Real-Time Triangle Splatting SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23935)**  `arXiv:2611.23935`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Edward Beng Wai Tan, Siew-Kei Lam_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Dense SLAM systems based on 3D Gaussian splatting build maps that render photorealistically, but their surface geometry is poor, and obtaining a mesh requires extraction or fusion stages. Triangle splatting avoids this with surface primitives whose vertices can be triangulated directly into a connected mesh, yet SLAM with this representation has so far relied on photometric tracking, which is too slow for localization at sensor rate. We present Facet-SLAM, the first real-time triangle splatting SLAM system for RGB-D cameras. Fast geometric registration is the natural alternative, but it is not sufficient alone, since the mapper's optimization displaces triangles from the observed surface and makes them unreliable registration targets. We therefore separate the two roles: the tracker sets each triangle's centroid from fused depth and registers frames to these centroids by GICP, while the mapper is restricted to non-translating updates via a change of variables. Our system achieves competitive tracking accuracy at around 100 FPS on a desktop GPU and runs at sensor rate on a Jetson AGX Orin. Compared with the photometric approach, it is over 20 times faster, matches rendering quality, and improves surface accuracy, reducing the chamfer distance of the Delaunay mesh on Replica from 1.55 to 0.86 cm. - **[RecallFlow: Episodic Memory as the Source Distribution of Generative Robot Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23731)**  `arXiv:2611.23731`  `cs.RO`  
  _Xuji Zhang, Qilin Zhong, Zichen Zhang, Wei Zhai, Jian Di, Kun Li, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Diffusion and flow-matching policies have become a standard choice for visuomotor imitation learning, owing to their ability to model multimodal action distributions. In memory-dependent manipulation, the robot must act on information that is no longer visible. Existing methods feed the observation history to the policy as an additional condition, while action generation still starts from Gaussian noise. However, the policy must then pick out the relevant target from a long and redundant history, leading to spurious correlations and unreliable decisions as the memory load grows. Additionally, the initial noise carries no information about what the robot has seen, leaving generation without guidance toward the remembered target. To address these issues, we propose RecallFlow, a flow-policy framework that builds the source distribution from episodic memory. Recall-Relocalization relates the memory to the current view through a few recall slots, so that the source reflects where the remembered targets are now. The transport network then completes the action from the current observation alone, keeping inference nearly as cheap as a memoryless policy. We further build a suite of memory-dependent tasks grouped by memory load and compare all policies under a matched protocol. Extensive experiments show that RecallFlow achieves a state-of-the-art average success rate of 80.4% on the public MIKASA-Robo memory benchmark, and outperforms the strongest baseline by 20.3 points on our suite and by 18.8 points on four real-robot tasks.
