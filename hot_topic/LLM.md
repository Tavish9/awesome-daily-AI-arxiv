- **[What Can a VLA Learn from the Agent That Calls It? Task-Level Experience Distillation for Embodied Navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16246)**  `arXiv:2611.16246`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Jinze Tao, Yuxiao Han, Kangkang Dong, Yancong Wei, Xiaojun Zhu, Bokui Chen, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-language-action (VLA) models drive the robot directly from camera views, yet a VLA alone shows three typical failures. It walks past the goal, stops before reaching it, and rarely recovers after going off track. Pairing a VLA with a large language model (LLM) planner fixes these failures, but queries the LLM throughout every episode, adding token costs and latency. We introduce Harness-to-Executor (H2E), a task-level distillation method that pairs an LLM planner and a frozen VLA as a heterogeneous training agent, keeps only sessions that reach the goal, and then trains a VLA alone on them so no LLM is used at test time. In these sessions, the planner gives sub-instructions and moves the robot with its own tools, detours are common, and the planner decides when to stop. H2E therefore labels every motion as a VLA action for the original task, whoever made it. It masks the loss on steps moving away from the goal and assigns end labels to states near it, so the VLA learns where the task ends. H2E sets a new state of the art on REVERIE-CE and NavRAG-CE. It raises an 8B VLA's success rate by 15.6% and 11.1% respectively, surpassing a method trained on 10M samples. The distilled VLA exceeds its agent on unseen episodes with zero LLM tokens and runs 3.7 times faster.
  </details>

  _Jiarui Guo, Saining Zhang, Jiaying Wan, Jiawei Weng, Junhao Chen, Rui Zhang, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Large-scale robotic simulation requires diverse deformable objects with executable physical properties and realistic interaction behaviors. However, existing 3D asset collections primarily emphasize visual diversity, while scalable physical construction and verification remain challenging. We introduce RFW-S, a large-scale dataset of 83,365 simulation-verified assets spanning 485 everyday categories across five structural families: soft solids, cloth sheets, shells, garments, and bags. We further evaluate robotic grasping in terms of both task success and object integrity, and demonstrate the physical realism of our simulated objects through sim-to-real replay of fragile-object manipulation. RFW-S provides a scalable foundation for physically grounded simulation and embodied robotic learning.

- **[RoboTeam: Benchmarking Vision-Based Multi-Agent Control of Many Robot Arms in Real Time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22138)**  `arXiv:2611.22138`  `cs.RO` `cs.AI`  
  _Changxuan Fan, Zhongwei Xie, Boyi Zhang, Shaoli Hu, Chang Xu, Xiyu Ren, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Multimodal agents can now control robot arms by calling tools and writing code. Existing benchmarks fall short in three ways: most test one or two arms on short tasks, many pause the world while the agent thinks, and many give it ground-truth object positions. We present RoboTeam, a simulated benchmark where agents control 4 to 16 robot arms in real time from camera images alone. Its 36 tasks are long jobs that the arms must do together, such as passing timbers or building a picture from hundreds of cubes. We start from the most flexible agent system, the adaptive system, where one agent decides when to fork delegates. We then compare it with three fixed systems: single-agent, hierarchical, and decentralized. All four complete a similar number of tasks but differ in time and tokens. The hierarchical and decentralized systems are faster but use up to 1.9 times the tokens, and the single agent uses fewer tokens but takes 1.6 times as long, so the adaptive system lies between them. Even so, no model or system is yet fast, cheap, and reliable at once. RoboTeam measures these limits where earlier benchmarks cannot: long multi-arm jobs, camera images only, and a world that never pauses.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>

Can sharing evidence of what is available let a group make full use of its resources after capacity changes? We tested this with language-model agents choosing between two simulated servers. - **[Ambient LLMs: Data-Efficient Supervised Fine-tuning via Temperature Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23229)**  `arXiv:2611.23229`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `cs.CL`  
  _Giannis Daras, Marios Papachristou, Adam Klivans, Antonio Torralba, Constantinos Daskalakis_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Collecting large-scale, diverse, and high-quality post-training datasets for autoregressive language models is challenging. Consequently, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is typically applied to datasets significantly smaller than those used for pre-training, which leads to overfitting and the catastrophic forgetting of the tails of the data distribution. To prevent this collapse, we introduce Ambient SFT, a principled framework for leveraging imperfect predictions to augment the fine-tuning set. - **[The Hidden Cost of MoE Diffusion Decoding: Profiling and Fixing the Sampler](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21852)**  `arXiv:2611.21852`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `cs.CL`  
  _Harshit Samrat, Ramakrishna Paramahamsa Kompella_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Masked diffusion language models (dLLMs) decode by iteratively denoising a full canvas of tokens, so every forward pass runs a per-token sampling and stopping pipeline over the entire canvas-by-vocabulary logits tensor. We profile single-user decoding of a 26B-parameter diffusion Mixture-of-Experts model on a bandwidth-bound edge accelerator (NVIDIA GB10) and report a result that is easy to miss: after weight quantisation, this logits pipeline, not the expert GEMMs, is the single largest consumer of decode time. We confirm the finding on a second, independently built diffusion MoE (LLaDA2.0-mini: pipeline 28.8% of decode, fused W4A16 3.3 times at accuracy parity) and show it disappears on a dense diffusion model (3.6%). The cost is specific to MoE diffusion, where quantisation makes the backbone cheap. Code and quantised weights are released.
