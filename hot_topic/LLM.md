- **[Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18562)**  `arXiv:2611.18562`  `cs.RO` `cs.LG`  
  _Jiaming Tang, Zhuoyang Zhang, Junxian Guo, Fangzhou Zhao, Yilong Zhao, Zekai Li, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Robot-use agents (RUAs) have emerged as a promising approach to general-purpose robot control. However, strong performance has so far been achieved only by frontier proprietary models such as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2, whereas open-weight models remain substantially less capable. We show that an open-weight 27B reasoning model can be trained to use real robots natively through reinforcement learning, entirely in simulation and without demonstrations. Like a computer-use agent (CUA), the model observes the environment, reasons in a chain of thought, and generates the next end-effector pose, without any harness. We build a Post-training Engine for Agentic Robotics (PEAR) and use it to train the model on simulated robot tasks at scale with robot-use reinforcement learning (RURL), mirroring how frontier models acquire computer use. We find that the skills acquired in simulation generalize well beyond the training tasks: they transfer to an unseen robot, to the real world, and to novel instructions. After training on LIBERO and RoboDojo in simulation, the model succeeds in 60% of our trials on a real dual-arm robot, whereas the base model succeeds in none. On LIBERO, training raises the success rate from 1.6% to 74.4%, approaching GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol at maximum reasoning effort. Compared with $\pi_{0.5}$, a vision-language-action model fine-tuned on demonstrations, our model is substantially more robust to changes in the task itself. On LIBERO-Pro, $\pi_{0.5}$ succeeds in only 0.75% of episodes when assigned a new task in a familiar scene whereas our model succeeds in 57.4%. Finally, the model retains its general capabilities, with no measurable degradation on nine reasoning, coding and vision benchmarks. These results highlight robot-use reinforcement learning as a promising component of foundation-model post-training.

  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Frontier large language models (LLMs) have enabled vision-and-language navigation (VLN) through direct closed-loop perception and action. However, navigation performance depends strongly on the harness governing LLM-environment interactions, while existing harnesses largely rely on manually designed navigation tools, memory, and agent roles. We introduce NavRSI, a harness-centric recursive self-improvement framework that optimizes the navigation behavior of frozen LLMs through closed-loop experience. In each round, NavRSI iteratively collects navigation trajectories and failure diagnostics, employs a designer agent to rewrite the harness, and retains the revisions that prove effective, while the designer accumulates experience from previous revisions so that the harness and this experience co-evolve. - **[ManipScope: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Instruction Following and Generalization in Robotic Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27305)**  `arXiv:2611.27305`  `cs.RO`  
  _Jiacheng Li, Yaping Li, Congsheng Xu, Qingchen Liu, Haonan Wang, Zhijie Deng, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Completing a manipulation task does not establish that a robot follows the requested goal rather than a familiar routine. ManipScope is an evaluation-only diagnostic benchmark for robotic instruction following under changed task requirements. Built on RoboTwin 2.0's simulation resources, it specifies 48 held-out scenes across six task families with explicit source-task links; policies are trained on unmodified RoboTwin 2.0 demonstrations, and no demonstrations of the scenes are provided, so success requires reusing skills under a changed request rather than fitting supplied examples. In a feasibility test, letting an agent select the target and the policy execute partly restores instruction following. Case studies with future-video and internal-intervention probes further show that forecasts and execution can agree on the wrong target and that action changes need not transfer the goal.   _Lifan Hu, Qian Yu, Zhaomin Wu, Bingsheng He_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Behavioral fingerprints identify a large language model (LLM) from its responses to a shared set of prompts, enabling applications such as verifying whether a deployed model corresponds to the checkpoint it claims to be. Deployed models, however, are commonly served with stochastic decoding, under which the same model may produce different responses to an identical prompt, depending on an unknown temperature and top-$p$ setting. Existing representations such as LLM DNA provide limited treatment of this variation. Experiments across 115 model checkpoint entries demonstrate that DistDNA improves identification accuracy over the strongest baseline, LLM DNA, by 10.36%.
