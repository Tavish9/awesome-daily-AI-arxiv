- **[SCALE: Real-Time Latent Communication for Cyber-Physical Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08390)**  `arXiv:2611.08390`  `cs.RO` `cs.MA`  
  _Shanghao Shi, Jian Xiao, Ching-Hsiang Chan, Sanjoy Baruah, Sastry Kompella, Yevgeniy Vorobeychik, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Latent communication enables agents to collaborate efficiently by directly exchanging intermediate model representations rather than raw sensor data or text messages. Existing approaches, however, are primarily designed for LLM agents and do not consider the bandwidth constraints and heterogeneous task deadlines faced by cyber-physical agents. In this work, we propose SCALE, a real-time latent communication method tailored for cyber-physical agents. Motivated by the spatio-temporal continuity of the physical world, SCALE matches tokens across consecutive latent messages using similarity scores to identify reusable information and avoid redundant transmission. It then profiles the semantic gain and communication cost of transmitting different amounts of latent information. Based on these profiles, SCALE employs a real-time scheduler that jointly determines which pending messages to transmit and how many latent tokens to send, subject to available bandwidth and task deadlines. We evaluate SCALE across three cyber-physical benchmarks and a physical spider robot case study under diverse network conditions and heterogeneous task deadlines. Our results show that SCALE significantly improves both task performance and on-time delivery in bandwidth-constrained networks. Our code is available at:this https URL.

- **[T3R: Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement Towards Efficient and Robust VLA Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08413)**  `arXiv:2611.08413`  `cs.RO` `cs.CV`  
  _Mohammed Hassan, Zhenyang Chen, Zheng Wang, Zhixin Zhu, Tianlong Chen, Yingyan, et al._
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models enable end-to-end robot control, but inference is increasingly expensive. Each decision processes hundreds of visual tokens, often across multiple cameras, leading to substantial computation while allowing irrelevant background content to distract action prediction under distribution shift. We argue that these issues stem from two complementary forms of redundancy: many visual tokens are spatially irrelevant, while attention over the remaining tokens is often misallocated. Based on this observation, we propose T3R, a Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement framework. In the first stage, segmentation-guided token pruning removes irrelevant visual patches to reduce computation and eliminate distracting context. In the second stage, saliency-guided attention biasing reallocates attention toward action-critical visual regions and language instructions within the retained tokens. Together, pruning produces a cleaner candidate set, while attention biasing further emphasizes the most decision-relevant evidence, yielding a better accuracy-efficiency frontier than either stage alone. On LIBERO-Spatial, T3R removes 85% of scene-camera visual tokens while essentially matching the unpruned success rate, and improves success by up to 7 percentage points under out-of-distribution object-placement perturbations. The timing comparison attributes 7.8 ms per inference call to pruning and attention-bias application. As input resolution increases, LLM-forward profiling shows up to a 3.3x speedup over the unpruned baseline. Evaluations across LIBERO, SIMPLER, and RoboTwin with three VLA backbones show the strongest accuracy-efficiency benefits in multi-camera settings. These results demonstrate that training-free token refinement is a practical approach to efficient and robust VLA inference.

  Personal AI agents often act on underspecified instructions and draw on context such as their user's messages and calendar to decide how to proceed. This context can contain private preferences that interfere with the task, with other people's interests, or with confidentiality norms. Prior work has studied deception under goal conflicts and the leakage of private information, but largely as separate problems. We study both problems together in a simulated company Slack workspace in which four employees delegate sprint staffing to personal agents. - **[Localization Does Not Imply Steerability: A Mechanistic Study of Sign Errors in LLM Mathematics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07478)**  `arXiv:2611.07478`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `cs.CL`  
  _Shradha Agarwal, Tariq J., Deepak Rajbhar_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  When large language models do multi-step mathematics, a characteristic failure recurs: the model writes one wrong sign token mid-computation -- a cofactor sign, the minus of a recursive integration-by-parts step -- then executes the rest of the arithmetic correctly, and the answer comes out wrong. We ask where inside the network that sign is written. Across six open models spanning 14B to 675B parameters, a per-layer readout shows the wrong sign is committed late -- by MLP layers in the last quarter of the network (70-100% per model and task), a pattern present, where tested (Qwen), in the base model before instruction tuning. The mechanism varies: some models carry fixed "minus-stamp" layers that push a minus regardless of the right answer, others carry amplifiers of whichever sign was already chosen, and Qwen adds a downstream rescue layer that opposes the stamp. The circuit is causal: subtracting a single sign direction from the residual stream flips 13 of 14 held-out minus-written integration-by-parts errors on Llama-3.3-70B, with flips replicating on Qwen, Gemma, and Mistral-Small (24-70% per cell), and with representation-level specificity -- the same dose breaks correctly-minus answers while sparing correct-plus answers where the direction is surgical (0/72 on Llama), and matched random-direction and control-layer interventions are null. Core claims were preregistered with pass criteria frozen in git before held-out data was seen: 23 of 29 checks passed, all misses reported. But causality is not uniform: Phi-4 implements an equally well-localized circuit at the same late depth, yet the identical intervention flips almost nothing -- the sign is written redundantly across ~15 layers with margins several times larger (median 17.8 logits). Localization, we conclude, does not imply steerability: two models can share a circuit location yet differ enormously in how overturnable it is.

  _Hong kyu Lee, Kai Shu, Li Xiong_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Large vision-language models (LVLMs) are vulnerable to compositional harm: an image and a prompt that are benign in isolation become harmful through the model's cross-modal reasoning. Recent alignment methods report strong scores on compositional safety benchmarks, but an LLM judge only measures observed behavior and cannot reveal how robustly safety is integrated into the model. We argue that un-alignment provides a complementary audit of alignment robustness. We first show that minimal supervised fine-tuning reaches an LLM-judge score comparable to a multi-stage reinforcement-learning pipeline, so similar benchmark scores cannot establish robustness by themselves.   Data engineering for large language model (LLM) reinforcement learning (RL) decides which samples to train on, when to present them, and which to batch together. Existing methods mostly rely on external feedback, such as pass rates or difficulty labels, which observes only the outcome on each sample. We argue that model internals offer richer and more model-intrinsic feedback that can guide all three decisions. - **[FILO: A Unified Fisher-Geometric Framework for Low-Rank Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08734)**  `arXiv:2605.08734`  `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `cs.CL`  
  _Ziyun Liu, Fengmiao Bian, Jian-Feng Cai_
  <details open><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) reparameterizes a weight matrix $W$ as a product of two low-rank factors, but the Jacobian $J_G$ of the generator mapping the factors to the weight matrix is rank-deficient, so the factor-space preconditioner $J_G^* F_t J_G$ induced by any $W$-space preconditioner $F_t$ is singular, and consequently the standard chain rule cannot be uniquely inverted to map a preconditioned $W$-space direction back to a factor-space update. Therefore, we introduce FILO, a unified Fisher-geometric framework for low-rank adaptation that enables factor updates without directly inverting this singular operator and casts existing LoRA optimizers by their factor-space inversion strategy and $W$-space preconditioner $F_t$. Existing methods occupy four families along these axes: factor-space adaptive updates, block-diagonal surrogates for $J_G^* J_G$, Frobenius-residual pseudoinverse methods, and Riemannian manifold methods. Within this design space, a gradient-statistics-aware $F_t$ paired with a closed-form factor-space solve at $O((m+n)r)$ memory remains underexplored. We instantiate FILO as FILO-Adafactor by adopting the Adafactor diagonal Kronecker preconditioner $H_t$ on $W$ and selecting from the resulting factor-space solution family the element minimizing an $H_t$-weighted imbalance between the two factor contributions. By construction, the induced weight update is the closest LoRA approximation to the preconditioned $W$-space direction under the $H_t$-weighted norm. Across GPT-2 on E2E, Mistral-7B and Qwen2-7B on GLUE, ARC, and GSM8K, and diffusion-model personalization, FILO-Adafactor and its momentum variant are competitive with or improve over a representative set of LoRA optimizers while keeping peak GPU memory at the LoRA optimizer level.
