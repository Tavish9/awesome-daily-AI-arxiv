- **[Learning Reusable Agent Subroutines from Experience](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02994)**  `arXiv:2606.02994`  
  _Zhihan Lei, Jiarui Yan, Joshua Momo, William W. Cohen_
  <details><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Existing methods induce various forms of knowledge from agent traces, from sample workflows to programmatic skills. We propose ARISE (Agent Routine Induction from Successful Experience), which identifies recurring procedures in agent traces and synthesizes them into a reusable library of agent subroutines. Across five tasks in narrative deduction, rule application, and constraint planning, it is shown to be a more effective learning strategy than prompting methods and prose and code artifact induction methods. ARISE outperforms chain-of-thought in all ten model-task settings of the main evaluation. Compared to state-of-the-art methods in agent experience induction, our method scores 6.1 percentage points over the strongest evaluated baselines, and 11.5 percentage points over code-skill induction. In our controlled experiment, ARISE also improves average accuracy by up to 15.6 percentage points compared to formatting the induced knowledge as skills and by up to 19.1 points over rewriting it as code tools.

- **[C-MIG: Multi-view Information Gain-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Clinical Diagnosis Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27860)**  `arXiv:2605.27860`  
  _Yuwei Miao, Gen Li, Yunsheng Zeng, Xiandong Li, Yujin Wang, Siyu Chen, et al._
  <details><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) trained with reinforcement learning (RL) is a promising way to ground large language models in medical evidence for clinical diagnosis. However, existing RAG-RL methods face two problems: (1) their process reward is largely singleview, as most methods inherited from opendomain multi-hop QA reward only the quality of the retrieved information; (2) assigning a reliable process reward is hard, as hard matching gives zero to clinically correct but non-verbatim steps such as "Allergic Sinusitis" versus "Allergic Rhinitis", and the alternative of strong-teacher guiding is bounded by the teacher's own medical competence. To address these issues, we propose C-MIG, a Multiview Information Gain-based framework for Clinical diagnosis. We identify two complementary and crucial intermediate steps in clinical diagnosis: what to retrieve and how to refine. We reward both by the information gain each brings, measured as the increase in the predicted likelihood of the ground-truth answer under a base, frozen reference model. The frozen scorer yields a stable and reproducible reward that needs no extra reward model or teacher. Experiments on four medical benchmarks show that C-MIG outperforms RAGRL baselines in and out of domain and stays competitive with far larger medical-specialist and general-purpose LLMs, at only about 3.4% training overhead and no inference-time cost.

- **[Towards Security-Auditable LLM Agents: A Unified Graph Representation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06812)**  `arXiv:2605.06812`  
  _Chaofan Li, Lyuye Zhang, Jintao Zhai, Siyue Feng, Xichun Yang, Huahao Wang, et al._
  <details><summary>Abstract</summary>
  LLM-based agentic systems are rapidly evolving to perform complex autonomous tasks through dynamic tool invocation, stateful memory management, and multi-agent collaboration. However, this semantics-driven execution paradigm creates a severe semantic gap between low-level physical events and high-level execution intent, making post-hoc security auditing fundamentally difficult. Existing representation mechanisms, including static SBOMs and runtime logs, provide only fragmented evidence and fail to capture cognitive-state evolution, capability bindings, persistent memory contamination, and cascading risk propagation across interacting agents. These results demonstrate that Agent-BOM provides a unified and auditable foundation for root-cause analysis and security adjudication in complex agentic ecosystems.

- **[Reward Design for Physical Reasoning in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13993)**  `arXiv:2604.13993`  
  _Derek Lilienthal, Manisha Mukherjee, Sameera Horawalavithana_
  <details><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Physical reasoning over images requires integrating perception, domain knowledge, and multi-step symbolic inference, yet Vision-Language Models (VLMs) fall far short of human performance on physics benchmarks.   <details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Existing RL policy optimization frameworks rely on final-answer correctness as feedback signals and rarely capture the internal logical structure of the reasoning process.   _Aryan Kasat, Smriti Singh, Aman Chadha, Vinija Jain_
  <details><summary>Abstract</summary>

  <details><summary>Abstract</summary>
  Long chain-of-thought~(CoT) reasoning improves the problem-solving capability of large reasoning models~(LRMs), but often incurs substantial inference cost and overthinking. Existing efficiency methods primarily rely on prompting, post-hoc compression, or length-regularized optimization; these approaches can be sensitive to model scale, depend on external estimators, or under-allocate reasoning to difficult instances.
